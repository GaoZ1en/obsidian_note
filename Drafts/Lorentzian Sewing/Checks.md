# Selected checks and their scope

These are short tests of conventions and examples used in the manuscript. Each can be followed from the indicated sections without a computer. They are not premises of the reconstruction theorems.

- **Open variation (Chs. 4–6).** **Verified:** expansion of the covariant scalar and Maxwell first variations into the Euler term and the signed divergence gives zero residual in xAct, as does the scalar Green identity. **Assumptions:** fixed smooth metric, Levi-Civita derivative, scalar Hessian $J=\Box-m^2$ and Maxwell Lagrangian $-F_{ab}F^{ab}/4$. **Not verified by that calculation:** the existence of a causal inverse or a conserved presymplectic form on a particular boundary domain.
- **Which surface is characteristic? (§7.3).** **Verified:** differentiating $P(X)=X+\beta X^2$ twice in $(u_t,u_x)$ in Mathematica gives diagonal entries $1+3\beta v^2$ and $-(1+\beta v^2)$ at $u=vt$. Contracting with $d(t-x)$ gives $2\beta v^2$, not zero. **Assumptions:** flat two-dimensional metric of signature $(-,+)$, $X=(u_t^2-u_x^2)/2$, $\beta>0$ and $v\ne0$. **Not verified by that calculation:** existence of half-solutions from arbitrary trace data. Theorem 7.1 starts with half-solutions smooth up to the face on one invertible branch.
- **A pulse crossing a timelike cut (§8.3 and App. B).** **Verified:** the transformed massless Dirichlet mismatch is $-e^{-az}$ for an impulse a distance $a>0$ from the cut; the corrected transmitted field has zero residual against the uncut retarded impulse solution. The fixed tangential-mode massive kernel $\tfrac12\Theta(t)J_0(t\sqrt{|k|^2+m^2})$ has the Laplace transform used in the book (Mathematica). **Assumptions:** flat planar interface, initially zero data, $\operatorname{Re} z>0$, positive-real-part square root. **Not verified by the transform alone:** support in the full spacetime light cone; Appendix B supplies a separate energy argument.
- **Counting seam choices (§13.5).** **Verified:** enumeration in Sage of the two signs on each of two seams gives two orbits distinguished by their product, with the diagonal $\mathbb Z_2$ as stabilizer at each object. **Assumptions:** constant $\mathbb Z_2$ re-markings on each connected strip and fixed base identifications. **Not fixed by counting:** whether to work in one orbit or to sum amplitudes and, in the latter case, what physical weights to assign.
- **Fish extension (§13.2 and App. C).** **Verified:** away from the singular cone, the scalar differential identity used in the extension has zero symbolic residual in Mathematica. **Assumptions:** four flat dimensions, $s=-t^2+|\mathbf{x}|^2$, massless field. **Not verified by an ordinary derivative:** the contact term at the origin. The text obtains that term from the distributional normalization $\Box G_F=i\delta^{(4)}$, independently checked there from the jump of the Fourier mode derivative.

**Appendix D — reconstruction from two strips.** **Verified:** Mathematica differentiated the nonlinear jet example in both charts, transformed the moving-cut wave equation and normal momentum, and checked the transfer-matrix ODE, composition, both return determinants and the zero-wave-number limit. For equal half-lengths in the twisted sector the data $(1,k)$ return to $(1,k)$ only if both the value and the slope carry the frame sign. Restricting the two-arc electric two-form gives the one-dimensional kernel generated by the relative phase shift. In the bundle examples Sage enumerated the two sign orbits and their stabilizers; a noncommuting connection law was checked on an explicit parallel column. The null conormal and oriented-flux contractions were also checked in components and by tensor identities. **Assumptions:** smooth closed half-charts; common collar and transitions; fixed positive strip lengths, time-aligned flat metrics and real sign representations in the frequency problem. **Not verified by symbolic algebra:** general separation or smooth descent; the quotient and derivative-pasting arguments in the appendix prove those statements under their stated hypotheses. The mode calculation classifies separated modes, not completeness of a spectral expansion.
