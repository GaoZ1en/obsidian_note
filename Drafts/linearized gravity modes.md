# Operator-Level Projection In The $\xi_a\xi_b$ Basis

Consider the branch

$$\begin{align}
\varepsilon _{\mu}^{~\nu \rho}\nabla ^{(0)}_{\nu}h_{\rho \sigma}+h_{\mu \sigma} & =0
\end{align}$$

with the Killing-basis expansion

$$\begin{align}
h_{\mu \nu} & =h_{ab}\xi _{a,\mu}\xi _{b,\nu}, \qquad a,b\in\{-1,0,1\}.
\end{align}$$

Write

$$\begin{align}
L_{a} & \equiv \mathcal{L}_{\xi _{a}}.
\end{align}$$

The TT conditions give

$$\begin{align}
h_{ab} & =h_{ba}, \\
h_{1,-1}-\frac{1}{4}h_{00} & =0, \\
L_{1}h_{11}+L_{0}h_{10}+L_{-1}h_{1,-1} & =0, \\
L_{1}h_{10}+L_{0}h_{00}+L_{-1}h_{0,-1} & =0, \\
L_{1}h_{1,-1}+L_{0}h_{0,-1}+L_{-1}h_{-1,-1} & =0.
\end{align}$$

With the orientation fixed by

$$\begin{align}
\nabla _{\mu}\xi _{a,\nu} & =-\varepsilon _{\mu\nu\rho}\xi _a^{\rho},
\end{align}$$

the strict projection of the first-order equation on the first tensor index is, up to the common overall sign chosen below,

$$\begin{align}
E_{11}&\equiv (2iL_{0}-4)h_{11}+iL_{-1}h_{10}=0, \\
E_{10}&\equiv (2iL_{0}-4)h_{10}+iL_{-1}h_{00}=0, \\
E_{1,-1}&\equiv (2iL_{0}-2)h_{1,-1}+iL_{-1}h_{0,-1}-\frac{1}{2}h_{00}=0, \\
E_{0,1}&\equiv 2iL_{-1}h_{1,-1}-4h_{10}-2iL_{1}h_{11}=0, \\
E_{00}&\equiv -2iL_{1}h_{10}-4h_{1,-1}-3h_{00}+2iL_{-1}h_{0,-1}=0, \\
E_{0,-1}&\equiv -2iL_{1}h_{1,-1}-4h_{0,-1}+2iL_{-1}h_{-1,-1}=0, \\
E_{-1,1}&\equiv -iL_{1}h_{10}-(2+2iL_{0})h_{1,-1}-\frac{1}{2}h_{00}=0, \\
E_{-1,0}&\equiv -iL_{1}h_{00}-(4+2iL_{0})h_{0,-1}=0, \\
E_{-1,-1}&\equiv -iL_{1}h_{0,-1}-(4+2iL_{0})h_{-1,-1}=0.
\end{align}$$

These equations should be treated as operator equations. They do not by themselves algebraically set all coefficients except $h_{11}$ to zero.

## What The Equations Actually Eliminate

The trace condition gives

$$\begin{align}
h_{1,-1} & =\frac{1}{4}h_{00}.
\end{align}$$

Then $E_{0,1}$ gives

$$\begin{align}
h_{10} &=\frac{i}{2}L_{-1}h_{1,-1}-\frac{i}{2}L_{1}h_{11} \\
&=\frac{i}{8}L_{-1}h_{00}-\frac{i}{2}L_{1}h_{11}.
\end{align}$$

Thus a nonzero $L_{1}h_{11}$ sources a nonzero $h_{10}$. This is not an inconsistency; it is the expected mixed descendant sector.

For example, define

$$\begin{align}
h_{+,0} &=\frac{e^{-4it}}{(1+r^{2})^{2}}\xi _{1}\xi _{1}.
\end{align}$$

Then $\mathcal{L}_{\xi _{-1}}h_{+,0}$ still satisfies the TT constraints and the first-order equation. In the same $\xi_a\xi_b$ basis it has nonzero coefficients

$$\begin{align}
h_{11} &=-4i\,\frac{r e^{-5it+i\phi}}{(1+r^{2})^{5/2}}, & h_{10} &=2i\,\frac{e^{-4it}}{(1+r^{2})^{2}},
\end{align}$$

with the other coefficients in this first descendant vanishing. Therefore the full local first-order system contains solutions with $h_{10}\neq 0$.

## Brown-Henneaux Chiral Representative

The one-coefficient form is obtained after choosing a Brown-Henneaux chiral representative. For the present branch this means keeping the $(0,2)$ tower and quotienting the mixed proper-gauge sector generated by the opposite copy. This is a representative choice in the gauge-equivalence class.

In this representative sector we set the mixed coefficients to zero,

$$\begin{align}
h_{00}&=h_{1,-1}=h_{0,-1}=h_{-1,-1}=h_{10}=0.
\end{align}$$

These conditions fix the representative after the proper-gauge mixed sector is removed. Substituting them into $E_{0,1}$ gives

$$\begin{align}
L_{1}h_{11} & =0.
\end{align}$$

The remaining equation $E_{11}$ gives

$$\begin{align}
L_{0}h_{11} & =-2ih_{11}.
\end{align}$$

Thus the selected representative is

$$\begin{align}
h_{\mu\nu} & =h_{11}\xi _{1,\mu}\xi _{1,\nu}, \\
L_{1}h_{11} & =0, \\
L_{0}h_{11} & =-2ih_{11}.
\end{align}$$

## Solving The Surviving Equation

Let

$$\begin{align}
w & =\frac{r e^{-i(t+\phi)}}{\sqrt{1+r^{2}}}, & A & =\frac{e^{-4it}}{(1+r^{2})^{2}}.
\end{align}$$

Then

$$\begin{align}
L_{1}w&=0, & L_{0}w&=0, \\
L_{1}A&=0, & L_{0}A&=-2iA.
\end{align}$$

Hence

$$\begin{align}
h_{11}(t,r,\phi) & =\frac{e^{-4it}}{(1+r^{2})^{2}}G(w).
\end{align}$$

Single-valuedness in $\phi$ and regularity at $r=0$ give

$$\begin{align}
G(w) &=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\tilde c_n w^n, \\
h_{11}(t,r,\phi) &=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\tilde c_n \frac{r^n e^{-i(n+4)t-in\phi}}{(1+r^{2})^{2+n/2}}.
\end{align}$$

These are the $\bar{\xi}_{-1}$ descendants of the $(0,2)$ Brown-Henneaux representative. The descendants generated by $\xi_{-1}$ belong to the mixed sector described above, and are not removed by the first-order equations alone.

## General Descendant-Module Treatment

The invariant organization is to solve the module generator and let the Killing descendants carry the remaining coefficient structure. Define

$$\begin{align}
D_{\pm}h_{\mu\nu} &\equiv \varepsilon _{\mu}^{~\rho\sigma}\nabla ^{(0)}_{\rho}h_{\sigma\nu} \pm h_{\mu\nu}.
\end{align}$$

For any Killing vector $\displaystyle{K\in\{\xi_a,\bar{\xi}_a\}}$, the AdS background is invariant under $\displaystyle{\mathcal{L}_K}$. Hence

$$\begin{align}
D_{\pm}\mathcal{L}_{K}h &=\mathcal{L}_{K}D_{\pm}h, \\
\operatorname{tr}(\mathcal{L}_{K}h) &=\mathcal{L}_{K}\operatorname{tr}(h), \\
\nabla ^{(0)\mu}(\mathcal{L}_{K}h)_{\mu\nu} &=\mathcal{L}_{K}\left(\nabla ^{(0)\mu}h_{\mu\nu}\right).
\end{align}$$

Therefore the TT conditions and the first-order equation are preserved by all Killing descendants. For the present branch one may solve the generator

$$\begin{align}
h^{\mathrm{gen}}_{\mu\nu} &=H(t,r,\phi)\xi _{1,\mu}\xi _{1,\nu}.
\end{align}$$

Substitution of this generator into the projected equations gives

$$\begin{align}
L_{1}H&=0, \\
L_{0}H&=-2iH.
\end{align}$$

Thus

$$\begin{align}
H(t,r,\phi) &=\frac{e^{-4it}}{(1+r^{2})^{2}}G(w), & w&=\frac{r e^{-i(t+\phi)}}{\sqrt{1+r^{2}}}.
\end{align}$$

The function $\displaystyle{G(w)}$ is a generating function for the $\displaystyle{\bar{\xi}_{-1}}$ descendants. Choosing $\displaystyle{G(w)=1}$ isolates the primary generator

$$\begin{align}
h^{\mathrm{prim}}_{\mu\nu} &=\frac{e^{-4it}}{(1+r^{2})^{2}}\xi _{1,\mu}\xi _{1,\nu}.
\end{align}$$

The full descendant module generated by this primary is

$$\begin{align}
h_{\mu\nu} &= \sum_{m,n\geq 0}c_{mn} \mathcal{L}_{\xi _{-1}}^{m} \mathcal{L}_{\bar{\xi}_{-1}}^{n} h^{\mathrm{prim}}_{\mu\nu}.
\end{align}$$

For $\displaystyle{m>0}$, the expansion of $\displaystyle{h_{\mu\nu}}$ in the $\displaystyle{\xi_a\xi_b}$ basis generally contains nonzero mixed coefficients such as $\displaystyle{h_{10}}$. These coefficients are determined by the Lie action on the primary; they are not independent algebraic data to eliminate before constructing the descendant module.

Completeness is a representation-theoretic input. With regularity, single-valuedness, and Brown-Henneaux falloff, the spectrum is organized into highest-weight modules of $\displaystyle{\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})\times\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})}$. Repeated action by the raising operators reaches a generator, and the lowering operators generate the allowed descendants. If the boundary conditions are changed, the generator classification must be repeated.
