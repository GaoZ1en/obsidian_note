# BMS Group and Extended BMS Group in Gravitational Physics

## Core Definition of BMS Group

The Bondi-Metzner-Sachs (BMS) group is the asymptotic symmetry group of asymptotically flat spacetimes at null infinity in general relativity. First identified by Bondi, Metzner, and Sachs in the 1960s (^cite{BondiMetznerSachs1962}), it represents a profound enlargement of the expected Poincaré symmetry.

Mathematically, the BMS group is a semi-direct product:

$$\text{BMS} = \text{SO}(3,1) \ltimes \mathcal{S}$$

where $\mathcal{S}$ is the infinite-dimensional abelian group of supertranslations.

## Structure of the BMS Group

### Supertranslations
Supertranslations extend ordinary translations to angle-dependent translations along null generators of the asymptotic null hypersurface $\mathscr{I}^+$ ("scri-plus"). They can be parameterized as:

$$T(\theta, \phi) = \sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\sum_{m=-l}^{l} T_{lm} Y_{lm}(\theta,\phi)$$

where $Y_{lm}$ are spherical harmonics. The $l=0,1$ modes correspond to ordinary translations, while higher modes are proper supertranslations.

### Group Composition
The group operation combines Lorentz transformations $\Lambda$ and supertranslations $T$:

$$({\Lambda}_1, T_1) \circ ({\Lambda}_2, T_2) = ({\Lambda}_1 {\Lambda}_2, T_1 + {\Lambda}_1 \cdot T_2)$$

where $\Lambda \cdot T$ denotes the action of the Lorentz transformation on the supertranslation.

## Extended BMS Group

The extended BMS group (EBMS) enlarges the original BMS group by replacing the Lorentz group with a larger group of transformations of the celestial sphere.

### Structure of EBMS
In 4D spacetime, the EBMS has the structure:

$$\text{EBMS} = \text{Diff}(S^2) \ltimes \mathcal{S}$$

or alternatively:

$$\text{EBMS} = \text{Conf}(S^2) \ltimes \mathcal{S}$$

where:
- $\text{Diff}(S^2)$ is the group of diffeomorphisms of the 2-sphere
- $\text{Conf}(S^2)$ is the group of conformal transformations, isomorphic to the Virasoro group

### Superrotations
Superrotations extend the standard rotation group with angle-dependent generalizations. They can be viewed as singular, locally defined conformal transformations of the celestial sphere. In terms of the stereographic coordinate $z = e^{i\phi}\tan(\theta/2)$, they are generated by vector fields:

$$Y_n = z^{n+1}\partial_z, \quad \bar{Y}_n = \bar{z}^{n+1}\partial_{\bar{z}}$$

where $n \in \mathbb{Z}$.

## Representations of the BMS Group

### Induced Representations
The representations of the BMS group can be constructed using Wigner's method of induced representations:

1. Start with a representation of the little group (stabilizer subgroup)
2. Induce a representation on the full BMS group

For massive particles, the little group is SO(3), while for massless particles, it's ISO(2) (Euclidean group in 2D).

### Goldstone Modes
Soft gravitons arise as Goldstone modes associated with spontaneously broken BMS symmetry. These correspond to:
- Soft graviton theorems (Weinberg's soft theorem)
- Memory effects in gravitational wave detection

### BMS Particle Multiplets
Particles transform in multiplets under the BMS group, with supertranslation generators connecting states with different momenta but the same spin.

## Physical Significance

The BMS group has profound implications for:
- Infrared structure of quantum gravity
- Black hole information paradox through soft hair on black holes [[Black Hole Information Paradox]]
- Holographic aspects of flat space quantum gravity
- Celestial holography, which relates scattering amplitudes to correlators in a 2D CFT on the celestial sphere

The representation theory of BMS continues to be an active research area, with connections to [[Celestial CFT]] and [[Asymptotic Symmetries]] in gauge theories.

# BMS Group and Extended BMS Group

## Bondi-Metzner-Sachs (BMS) Group

The BMS group is the asymptotic symmetry group of asymptotically flat spacetimes at null infinity ($$\mathcal{I}$$). It was discovered in the 1960s by Bondi, Metzner, and Sachs during their analysis of gravitational waves in general relativity.

### Structure
The BMS group has the structure of a semi-direct product:

$$\text{BMS} = \mathcal{S} \rtimes \text{SO}(1,3)$$

where:
- $$\mathcal{S}$$ is the infinite-dimensional abelian group of **supertranslations**
- $$\text{SO}(1,3)$$ is the Lorentz group

Supertranslations generalize the standard 4-dimensional translations of the Poincaré group. While ordinary translations shift all points by the same amount, supertranslations allow angle-dependent translations along the null direction at $$\mathcal{I}$$. They can be parametrized by functions on the celestial sphere $$S^2$$.

Explicitly, supertranslations act on the Bondi coordinates $$(u,r,z,\bar{z})$$ as:
$$u \rightarrow u + f(z,\bar{z})$$
where $$f$$ is an arbitrary function on $$S^2$$ (often expanded in spherical harmonics).

## Extended BMS Group

The extended BMS group (EBMS) further enlarges the symmetry structure by including additional transformations.

### Structure
The extended BMS group also has a semi-direct product structure:

$$\text{EBMS} = \mathcal{S} \rtimes \mathcal{G}$$

where:
- $$\mathcal{S}$$ remains the supertranslation group
- $$\mathcal{G}$$ extends the Lorentz group to a larger group of transformations

Different extensions have been proposed:

1. **Virasoro extension**: $$\mathcal{G} = \text{Diff}(S^1) \times \text{Diff}(S^1)$$, replacing the Lorentz group with two copies of the diffeomorphism group of the circle

2. **Superrotation extension**: $$\mathcal{G} = \text{Diff}(S^2)$$, the full diffeomorphism group of the sphere

3. **Newman-Unti group**: An alternative extension that includes additional transformations

## Representations

The representation theory of the BMS group is rich and complex due to its infinite-dimensional nature:

### 1. Induced Representations
Since BMS is a semi-direct product, Wigner's method of induced representations applies:
- Start with representations of the supertranslation subgroup
- Identify orbits under the Lorentz group action
- Find little groups (stabilizers) for representatives of each orbit
- Induce representations from representations of these little groups

### 2. Unitary Representations
These are particularly important for applications in quantum theory:
- Characterized by "BMS mass" (related to eigenvalues of the Casimir operators)
- Include massless and massive representations
- Connect to soft theorems in quantum field theory

### 3. Celestial Representations
A modern approach views BMS representations in terms of conformal field theory on the celestial sphere:
- Soft gravitons appear as primary operators in a 2D CFT
- Superrotations connect to Virasoro generators
- Supertranslations correspond to Kac-Moody currents

## Physical Significance

The BMS group has gained renewed importance due to connections with:

- **Soft theorems**: Weinberg's soft graviton theorem is equivalent to supertranslation invariance
- **Memory effects**: Gravitational wave memory corresponds to supertranslation shifts
- **Black hole information**: Supertranslations may store information in "soft hair" on black holes
- **Holography**: BMS symmetries suggest a potential holographic description of asymptotically flat spacetimes
