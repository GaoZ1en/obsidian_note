# Nonunitary R pair observables and ordinary fermions

## Result and scope

The pair-creation candidate studied here does **not** supply the requested inequivalent relativistic theory. Its density and pair observables have the same finite-mode algebra, compatible inclusions, and cyclic vacuum representation as corresponding observables of ordinary two-component fermions. This remains true for interactions formed from these observables.

The result concerns the algebra generated by the displayed density and pair operators, with the compact physical adjoint specified below. For the explicit family, an additional calculation identifies all polynomial operators satisfying the source's stronger locality condition on one mode for every $m\geq3$, and on up to three modes at $m=3$; they give no enlargement. The corresponding statement for arbitrarily large regions and a continuum limit of the charged fields remain unproved. In particular, nonunitarity of an auxiliary $R$ matrix is not by itself failure of positivity of the physical observable theory.

The source family is Example 4 of [Wang–Hazzard, §§II A and IV A](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26351v1). The reduction below uses its Fock basis and pair algebra, corrects one index in that algebra, and then applies finite-dimensional symplectic representation theory.

## 1. An explicit candidate

Let $m\geq2$ and use complex matrices $\Lambda,\Xi$ satisfying

$$
\Lambda\Xi=-I_m,
\qquad
\operatorname{Tr}(\Lambda\Xi^T)=2.
$$

Define

$$
R^{ab}_{cd}
=\Lambda_{ab}\Xi_{cd}-\delta_{ac}\delta_{bd}.
$$

These conditions imply the involutive Yang–Baxter relations. In terms of $T=R+I$, they give $T^2=2T$ and the corresponding Temperley–Lieb relations, from which the braid equation for $R=T-I$ follows.

An explicit choice for every integer $m\geq2$ is

$$
\begin{align}
\Lambda_m
&=
\begin{pmatrix}
\frac{i}{2}\sqrt{m-2}&\frac12\sqrt{m+2}\\
-\frac12\sqrt{m+2}&\frac{i}{2}\sqrt{m-2}
\end{pmatrix}
\oplus I_{m-2},\\
\Xi_m&=-\Lambda_m^T.
\end{align}
$$

Indeed, $\Lambda_m\Lambda_m^T=I_m$ and $\operatorname{Tr}\Lambda_m^2=-2$. At $m=2$ this is the antisymmetric matrix

$$
\epsilon=
\begin{pmatrix}
0&1\\
-1&0
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
\Lambda_2=\Xi_2=\epsilon,
\qquad R_2=-F,
$$

so the reference case is ordinary two-component fermions. At $m=3$ the same construction gives a nonunitary $9\times9$ involutive $R$.

For $N$ modes, use the algebraic creation and annihilation symbols $\psi^+_{i,a},\psi^-_{i,a}$ with

$$
\begin{align}
\psi^-_{i,a}\psi^+_{j,b}
&=\sum_{c,d}R^{ac}_{bd}\psi^+_{j,c}\psi^-_{i,d}
+\delta_{ij}\delta_{ab},\\
\psi^+_{i,a}\psi^+_{j,b}
&=\sum_{c,d}R^{cd}_{ab}\psi^+_{j,c}\psi^+_{i,d},\\
\psi^-_{i,a}\psi^-_{j,b}
&=\sum_{c,d}R^{ba}_{dc}\psi^-_{j,c}\psi^-_{i,d}.
\end{align}
$$

The superscripts are algebraic labels: $\psi^+$ is **not** assumed to be the physical adjoint of $\psi^-$ when $m>2$.

Let $\Omega$ be annihilated by every $\psi^-_{i,a}$. The ordered Fock basis has occupation multiplicities $1,m,1$ at each mode, for occupation numbers $0,1,2$. Thus

$$
z_R(t)=1+mt+t^2,
\qquad
\dim\mathscr F_{m,N}=(m+2)^N.
$$

The basis property, including for nonunitary $R$, is an algebraic input from the ordered construction in Wang–Hazzard §II D. It does not assert orthogonality in a product inner product.

## 2. The pair algebra

Define

$$
\begin{align}
E_{ij}&=\sum_a\psi^+_{i,a}\psi^-_{j,a},\\
P_{ij}&=\sum_{a,b}\Lambda_{ab}\psi^+_{i,a}\psi^+_{j,b},\\
M_{ij}&=\sum_{a,b}\Xi_{ba}\psi^-_{i,a}\psi^-_{j,b}.
\end{align}
$$

The pair operators are symmetric in their mode labels. Their commutators are

$$
\begin{align}
[E_{ij},E_{kl}]
&=\delta_{jk}E_{il}-\delta_{il}E_{kj},\\
[E_{ij},P_{kl}]
&=\delta_{jk}P_{il}+\delta_{jl}P_{ki},\\
[M_{kl},E_{ij}]
&=\delta_{ki}M_{jl}+\delta_{li}M_{kj},\\
[P_{ij},P_{kl}]&=[M_{ij},M_{kl}]=0,\\
[P_{kl},M_{ij}]
&=\delta_{ik}E_{lj}+\delta_{jl}E_{ki}
+\delta_{jk}E_{li}+\delta_{il}E_{kj}\\
&\quad-2(\delta_{jk}\delta_{li}+\delta_{ik}\delta_{jl})I.
\end{align}
$$

The first term in the last line of Wang–Hazzard Eq. (101) is printed as $\delta_{ik}E_{jl}$. In these conventions it must be $\delta_{ik}E_{lj}$. For example,

$$
[P_{11},M_{12}]=2E_{12},
$$

whereas the printed expression would give $E_{21}+E_{12}$. This discrepancy already occurs for ordinary two-component fermions. The corrected index also follows from the mode-number weight of the commutator: setting $i=k$ leaves weight $e_l-e_j$, which is the weight of $E_{lj}$.

With

$$
X_{ij}=E_{ij}-\delta_{ij}I,
$$

the constant term disappears and these are the generators of $\mathfrak{sp}_{2N}(\mathbb C)$. In particular, operators supported on disjoint sets of mode labels commute. Interpreting the labels as spatial sites therefore gives a local finite-mode observable algebra; a relativistic spacetime net is additional structure.

Choose the physical adjoint on the observable generators to be

$$
E_{ij}^*=E_{ji},
\qquad
P_{ij}^*=M_{ij}.
$$

This is the compact real form. Every finite-dimensional representation of this complex semisimple Lie algebra can be made unitary for its compact group. Concretely, after integrating the representation to $D_N$ of $\operatorname{USp}(2N)$, a positive metric is obtained by averaging any positive starting metric:

$$
G_N=\int_{\operatorname{USp}(2N)}
D_N(g)^\dagger G_{N,0}D_N(g)\,dg.
$$

Here $\dagger$ is the matrix adjoint in the starting coordinates; $*$ is the resulting physical adjoint. This proves finite-mode positivity of the observable representation. It does not turn the $\psi^\pm$ into mutually adjoint fields.

## 3. Why a product metric cannot repair the auxiliary R

There is no positive one-particle metric $G$ for which $R$ is unitary in $G\otimes G$ when $m>2$, for this family.

Suppose such a metric existed, and change the one-particle basis to make it the identity. The transformed matrices still satisfy

$$
\Lambda'\Xi'=-I_m,
\qquad
\operatorname{Tr}(\Lambda'\Xi'^T)=2.
$$

Since $R'$ is now both unitary and involutive, $(I+R')/2$ is an orthogonal rank-one projector. Its rank-one form then requires

$$
\Xi'=c\,\overline{\Lambda'},
\qquad
c=\frac{2}{\|\Lambda'\|_{\mathrm F}^2}>0.
$$

Consequently,

$$
c\,\Lambda'\overline{\Lambda'}=-I_m.
$$

Cauchy–Schwarz for the matrix entries gives

$$
m
=c\left|\operatorname{Tr}
(\Lambda'\overline{\Lambda'})\right|
\leq c\|\Lambda'\|_{\mathrm F}^2
=2.
$$

This contradicts $m>2$. The positive observable metric in §2 therefore cannot be interpreted as a factorized one-particle metric making this auxiliary exchange unitary. Rejecting the observable theory solely because the displayed $R$ is nonunitary would nevertheless be incorrect: §2 supplies its finite-mode positive realization.

### Independent-particle inner products

The obstruction persists if a product metric is required only in a limit of widely separated charged wave packets. This is an additional test of the charged-particle interpretation, not an assumption about the observable representation already constructed.

Let $V$ carry a fixed positive one-particle metric $G$. For two normalized, separated mode wave packets, let

$$
\Gamma_{12,L},\Gamma_{21,L}:V\otimes V\longrightarrow\mathcal H
$$

create the two ordered charged states; $L$ denotes their separation. The creation relation requires

$$
\Gamma_{12,L}=\Gamma_{21,L}R.
$$

After normalizing the one-particle metric by $G^{1/2}$, write $R'$ for the resulting exchange matrix and $H_{12,L},H_{21,L}$ for the two Gram matrices. Then

$$
H_{12,L}=R'^\dagger H_{21,L}R'.
$$

Independence of the two charged preparations, for every internal vector, would require

$$
H_{12,L}\longrightarrow I,\qquad
H_{21,L}\longrightarrow I
\qquad(L\to\infty).
$$

Since $m<\infty$, convergence of all matrix elements is equivalent to norm convergence. Taking the limit in the Gram identity would make $R'$ unitary, which §3 excludes.

For this family there is a uniform quantitative bound. The transformed pair matrices satisfy $\Lambda'\Xi'=-I$ and $\operatorname{Tr}(\Lambda'\Xi'^T)=2$. Define

$$
\mathcal L:=\|\Lambda'\|_{\mathrm F}\|\Xi'\|_{\mathrm F}.
$$

Cauchy–Schwarz gives

$$
m=|\operatorname{Tr}(\Lambda'\Xi')|
\leq\mathcal L.
$$

The rank-one operator $R'+I$ has norm $\mathcal L$ and trace two. On its nontrivial two-dimensional reducing subspace, a unitary basis change puts $R'$ in the form

$$
\begin{pmatrix}1&t\\0&-1\end{pmatrix},
\qquad t=\sqrt{\mathcal L^2-4}.
$$

It acts as $-I$ on the orthogonal complement. Consequently

$$
s:=\|R'\|
=\frac{\mathcal L+\sqrt{\mathcal L^2-4}}2.
$$

Set

$$
\delta_L
=\max\{\|H_{12,L}-I\|,\|H_{21,L}-I\|\}.
$$

The Gram identity implies

$$
\begin{align}
R'^\dagger R'-I
&=(H_{12,L}-I)
-R'^\dagger(H_{21,L}-I)R',\\
s^2-1
&\leq\delta_L(1+s^2).
\end{align}
$$

Therefore, at every separation,

$$
\boxed{
\delta_L\geq
\frac{s^2-1}{s^2+1}
=\sqrt{1-\frac4{\mathcal L^2}}
\geq\sqrt{1-\frac4{m^2}}>0
\qquad(m>2).
}
$$

For $m=3$, even the metric-independent lower bound is $\sqrt5/3$. The failure of product independence cannot be made arbitrarily small by separating the packets.

This remains compatible with positivity. For any such involution, the two-particle metric

$$
H_*=2(I+R'R'^\dagger)^{-1}
$$

is positive and satisfies $R'^\dagger H_*R'=H_*$. It saturates the first bound when both ordered Gram matrices equal $H_*$. Positivity alone therefore does not supply independent charged-particle normalization.

The conditional conclusion is precise: this auxiliary family cannot retain its exchange relation and simultaneously describe all $m$ labels as charged particles with the stated asymptotic product inner products. Observable spacelike commutativity by itself has not been shown here to imply that charged-state condition. A proposal with confined, inaccessible or nonindependently preparable labels must specify a different physical sector construction; it cannot simply assume this limit. No general ban on positive local observable theories follows.

## 4. Exact decomposition of the observable representation

Let $L_N(\omega_k)$ be the fundamental $\mathfrak{sp}_{2N}$ module, with $\omega_k=e_1+\cdots+e_k$ for $1\leq k\leq N$, and let $L_N(\omega_0)=\mathbb C$. The Cartan generators are $X_{ii}=E_{ii}-I$.

The joint occupation multiplicities give the character

$$
\chi_{m,N}(x_1,\ldots,x_N)
=\prod_{i=1}^N(x_i^{-1}+m+x_i).
$$

Define integer polynomials

$$
u_0(m)=1,\qquad u_1(m)=m,\qquad
u_{r+1}(m)=m u_r(m)-u_{r-1}(m).
$$

Then the full representation decomposes as

$$
\boxed{
\mathscr F_{m,N}
\simeq
\bigoplus_{k=0}^N
L_N(\omega_k)\otimes\mathbb C^{u_{N-k}(m)}.
}
$$

Here the multiplicity space carries no action of the density or pair generators.

To prove the character identity, let $e_k$ denote the elementary symmetric polynomial in $x_1,x_1^{-1},\ldots,x_N,x_N^{-1}$, with $e_{-1}=e_{-2}=0$. The primitive exterior-power decomposition gives

$$
\chi_{\omega_k}=e_k-e_{k-2}
\qquad(0\leq k\leq N).
$$

This is the usual symplectic contraction decomposition: the primitive part of $\wedge^k\mathbb C^{2N}$ is $L_N(\omega_k)$. A primary account of the classical decomposition and its dual action is [Bodish–Tubbenhauer, §3.1](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00209-025-03680-3).

Set $m=t+t^{-1}$. The recurrence yields $u_r(m)=t^r+t^{r-2}+\cdots+t^{-r}$. Using $e_{2N-k}=e_k$,

$$
\begin{align}
\sum_{k=0}^N(e_k-e_{k-2})u_{N-k}(m)
&=e_N+\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}
e_k(t^{N-k}+t^{-N+k})\\
&=t^{-N}\prod_{i=1}^N
(1+t x_i)(1+t x_i^{-1})\\
&=\prod_{i=1}^N(m+x_i+x_i^{-1}).
\end{align}
$$

This is a polynomial identity in $m$. Complete reducibility and independence of irreducible characters give the displayed module decomposition. For $m\geq2$, the recurrence gives positive integers $u_r(m)$; at $m=2$, $u_r(2)=r+1$.

For example,

$$
\begin{align}
\mathscr F_{3,2}
&\simeq L_2(\omega_2)
\oplus3L_2(\omega_1)\oplus8L_2(\omega_0),\\
25&=5+3\cdot4+8\cdot1,\\
\mathscr F_{2,2}
&\simeq L_2(\omega_2)
\oplus2L_2(\omega_1)\oplus3L_2(\omega_0),\\
16&=5+2\cdot4+3\cdot1.
\end{align}
$$

The irreducible observable blocks are the same. Only their multiplicities differ.

## 5. Ordinary observables, vacuum and interactions

Let $\mathcal O_{m,N}$ be the unital associative algebra generated by $E,P,M$ on the full Fock space. Since every block in §4 occurs with positive multiplicity,

$$
\mathcal O_{m,N}
\simeq
\bigoplus_{k=0}^N
\operatorname{End}L_N(\omega_k).
$$

Equivalently, the kernel of the enveloping-algebra representation is the intersection of the same finite list of primitive annihilator ideals for every $m\geq2$. Therefore the correspondence that fixes the generator labels defines a faithful $*$-isomorphism

$$
\mathcal O_{m,N}\longrightarrow\mathcal O_{2,N}.
$$

The target is explicitly an algebra of ordinary two-component fermions: take their CAR operators $c_{i,a}$ and use

$$
E^F_{ij}=\sum_{a=1}^2c_{i,a}^\dagger c_{j,a},
\qquad
P^F_{ij}=\sum_{a,b=1}^2\epsilon_{ab}
c_{i,a}^\dagger c_{j,b}^\dagger,
\qquad
M^F_{ij}=(P^F_{ij})^\dagger.
$$

These are invariant under the ordinary color $\operatorname{SU}(2)$ action.

The empty vacuum has Cartan weight $(-1,\ldots,-1)$. Only $L_N(\omega_N)$ contains that weight, and it occurs once. Hence the cyclic observable vacuum space is precisely $L_N(\omega_N)$ for every $m$. With $\|\Omega\|=1$ and the compact adjoint, its inner product is unique, so the isomorphism preserves the vacuum state.

The old density and pair operators commute with the creation operators of a newly added mode. Using the ordered basis, restriction of the $(N+1)$-mode Fock representation to the old observable algebra is therefore a direct sum of $m+2$ copies of the $N$-mode representation. No old algebraic identity is lost, and the observable embeddings are faithful.

The maps for different $N$ agree on every shared generator. They consequently identify the finite-mode observable inclusions and their vacuum inductive limit. This is an algebraic identification; it is not an existence proof for a relativistic continuum limit.

Interactions do not remove this reduction. For example, any self-adjoint polynomial Hamiltonian of the form

$$
\begin{align}
H_{m,N}
&=\sum_{i,j}h_{ij}E_{ij}
+\frac12\sum_{i,j}
\left(\Delta_{ij}P_{ij}
+\overline{\Delta_{ij}}M_{ij}\right)\\
&\quad+\sum_{i,j}g_{ij}E_{ii}E_{jj},\\
h^\dagger&=h,\qquad
\Delta_{ij}=\Delta_{ji},\qquad
g_{ij}=g_{ji}\in\mathbb R,
\end{align}
$$

maps to the identical polynomial in ordinary fermion observables. The quartic term can produce interacting dynamics, but its observable algebra and vacuum representation have not acquired new statistics.

Here $\Omega$ is the empty algebraic reference state, not automatically a ground state of the interacting Hamiltonian. Any ground state selected for the latter maps to a corresponding ordinary observable state. Its existence and continuum properties require a separate construction.

If corresponding local continuum limits of these observable algebras and dynamics exist with the identification preserved, the resulting observable nets are equivalent. This statement requires the specified observables and compatible limits; it does not identify arbitrary extra charged-field operators or prove their continuum existence.

### Additional local operators

For the explicit matrices $\Lambda_m,\Xi_m$ in §1, **every one-mode polynomial operator satisfying Wang–Hazzard's locality condition belongs to the density/pair algebra when $m\geq3$**. At $m=3$, the same statement holds for regions containing two or three modes, with arbitrary polynomial degree.

The locality condition used here is

$$
\mathcal L_{m,N}
=\left\{
O\in\mathfrak A_{\{1,\ldots,N\}}:
[O,\psi^\pm_{j,a}]=0
\text{ for every }j\notin\{1,\ldots,N\}
\right\},
$$

where $\mathfrak A_S$ is the algebra generated by the fields with labels in $S$, acting on the ordered Fock representation. This is [Wang–Hazzard, Definition II.2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26351v1). It is stronger than requiring only commutativity between observables in disjoint regions. Observable commutativity alone does not impose this stronger condition.

Put an external mode after the $N$ modes of the region. On one internal site, write its occupation space as

$$
K_m=\mathbb C|0\rangle\oplus V\oplus\mathbb C|p\rangle.
$$

Moving an external creation operator through that site gives matrices

$$
\begin{align}
T_{ab}
&=\delta_{ab}\oplus t_{ab}\oplus\delta_{ab},\\
(t_{ab})_{rs}
&=\Lambda_{rb}\Xi_{as}-\delta_{ra}\delta_{sb}.
\end{align}
$$

The two scalar blocks follow because the empty state and the local pair are transparent to the external field. The transport matrices for several sites are defined recursively by

$$
T^{(1)}_{ab}=T_{ab},
\qquad
T^{(N+1)}_{ab}=\sum_c T^{(N)}_{ac}\otimes T_{cb}.
$$

Taking the external mode from its vacuum to its one-particle subspace in $[O,\psi^+_{j,a}]=0$ gives the necessary condition

$$
[O,T^{(N)}_{ab}]=0
\qquad\text{for all }a,b.
$$

Consequently,

$$
\mathcal O_{m,N}
\subseteq\mathcal L_{m,N}
\subseteq
\operatorname{Comm}\{T^{(N)}_{ab}\}.
$$

The first inclusion follows from the density and pair commutators with external fields. The second requires only external creation operators, so it remains a valid upper bound when annihilation commutators are imposed too.

For one site, use the third basis vector $e_3$, which exists for every $m\geq3$. The displayed $\Lambda_m$ gives

$$
t_{33}=-2|e_3\rangle\langle e_3|,
\qquad
t_{a3}e_3=-e_a\quad(a\ne3).
$$

If $X$ commutes with all $t_{ab}$, the first identity implies $Xe_3=\kappa e_3$; the second then implies $Xe_a=\kappa e_a$ for every $a$. Hence their commutant on $V$ is scalar. There is also no intertwiner between $V$ and the trivial transport representation: $t_{33}$ has eigenvalues $0,-2$, whereas its value on that representation is $1$. Thus

$$
\operatorname{Comm}\{T_{ab}\}
=\operatorname{End}\!\left(
\mathbb C|0\rangle\oplus\mathbb C|p\rangle
\right)\oplus\mathbb C I_V
=\mathcal O_{m,1}.
$$

This proves the one-mode assertion for all $m\geq3$. At $m=2$, $t_{ab}=-\delta_{ab}I_2$ instead; the full local algebra contains color operators and has dimension $8$, while the chosen $\operatorname{SU}(2)$-invariant density/pair algebra has dimension $5$. The distinction is why the nonunitary case needs its own calculation.

For two and three modes at $m=3$, an exact finite rank calculation gives

$$
\begin{array}{c|ccc}
N&1&2&3\\ \hline
\dim\operatorname{Comm}\{T^{(N)}_{ab}\}
&5&42&429\\
\dim\mathcal O_{3,N}
&5&42&429.
\end{array}
$$

Here is the rank argument, including its scope. The matrices have entries in $\mathbb Z[\zeta_{20},1/2]$. Reduce them modulo $101$, with $\zeta_{20}$ mapped to a primitive twentieth root. For the transport representations $V^{\otimes r}$, $0\leq r\leq3$, the matrix of intertwiner-space dimensions over this finite field is

$$
H_{rs}=
\begin{pmatrix}
1&0&1&0\\
0&1&0&2\\
1&0&2&0\\
0&2&0&5
\end{pmatrix}.
$$

Since $K_3=\mathbf1\oplus V\oplus\mathbf1$, its $N$-fold tensor product contains
$c_{N,r}=\binom Nr 2^{N-r}$ copies of $V^{\otimes r}$. The modular commutant dimension is $c_N^T Hc_N$, yielding $5,42,429$. A nonzero minor after reduction was nonzero before reduction, so these are **upper bounds** on characteristic-zero nullities. The lower bounds are

$$
\dim\mathcal O_{3,N}
=\sum_{k=0}^N
\left[
\binom{2N}{k}-\binom{2N}{k-2}
\right]^2,
\qquad \binom{2N}{j}=0\quad(j<0),
$$

from §4. They match the upper bounds, proving equality and therefore

$$
\boxed{\mathcal L_{3,N}=\mathcal O_{3,N}
\quad\text{for }N=1,2,3.}
$$

The finite computation is reproducible in [the accompanying Sage calculation](parastatistics_local_observables.sage). It checks exact ranks, not floating-point tolerances.

In particular, arbitrary polynomial interaction terms supported on up to three modes and satisfying this stronger locality condition are already polynomials in the ordinary density/pair observables. This closes that route to a new interaction for the explicit $m=3$ model. It does not prove the equality for $N\geq4$, classify models using only observable commutativity, or construct a relativistic limit.

## 6. Reconstruction and verification

This family fails unitarity of the **auxiliary product-space** $R$. That is not a demonstrated failure of a DHR–DR hypothesis for the physical observable net. With the compact observable adjoint and the density/pair algebra used here, the observable construction already has an ordinary fermionic realization. No new local relativistic charge sector or inequivalence invariant has been produced.

An escape would require additional local observables or a different physical sector construction whose positivity, localization, covariance and exchange are established independently. Retaining only the present algebra cannot provide the requested inequivalence.

**Verified:** Mathematica checked the explicit matrix family and recurrence identities. Sage checked the $m=3$ involutive Yang–Baxter matrix, and all defining creation, mixed and annihilation relations in the two-mode $m=2,3$ representations. It verified the corrected pair commutator for all two-mode indices. The character identity was checked for $C_1,\ldots,C_5$ and $m=2,3,4,5$; the generating-function argument above is the proof for arbitrary $N,m$.

Mathematica also checked the rank-one involution's singular-value formula, the metric-defect identity and the positive saturating metric. For the explicit family at $m=3,4,5$, its largest squared singular values agreed with the exact formula. The bound for every $m>2$ and every positive one-particle metric follows from the rank-one normal form and Cauchy–Schwarz argument, not those finite examples.

For the additional-local-operator calculation, Sage verified the external transport identity in the exact two-mode Fock matrices, the one-mode commutants over $\mathbb Q(\zeta_{20})$, and the modular rank bounds through three modes. It checked the matrix identities used in the general one-mode proof at $m=3,\ldots,8$; the argument with $e_3$ proves that statement for every $m\geq3$.

For two modes, Sage found the same cyclic vacuum Gram matrix and the same matrices for every density and pair generator at $m=2,3$. In the ordered vacuum basis

$$
\Omega,\quad P_{11}\Omega,\quad P_{12}\Omega,\quad
P_{22}\Omega,\quad P_{11}P_{22}\Omega,
$$

the Gram matrix is $\operatorname{diag}(1,4,2,4,16)$. A positive metric on the full $25$-dimensional $m=3$ representation was also constructed by congruence from positive diagonal metrics on its $5$, $4$, and $1$ dimensional blocks; every required compact adjoint identity passed.

**Assumptions:** The ordered Fock basis, the displayed class of observables, the compact observable adjoint, and the standard primitive symplectic representation theorem. The additional-local-operator result uses commutation with every external field, not merely other observables. The assertion about continuum nets is conditional on compatible limits existing. No product positive metric for the charged fields is assumed in the observable-algebra reduction. The separate charged-state test explicitly imposes asymptotic product inner products and proves their incompatibility with this exchange family.

**Not verified:** The full local-operator equality for arbitrary numbers of modes; a relativistic continuum field construction for the nonunitary $\psi^\pm$; physical charge fusion or exchange beyond the identified observable algebra; or inequivalence to ordinary boson/fermion sectors. The displayed finite-mode interaction is not itself a completed relativistic QFT.

The source definitions, locality condition, ordered basis, and printed pair commutator were inspected on rendered PDF pages 7, 9, 11 and 24. The correction to Eq. (101) was independently checked, rather than inherited from the source.
