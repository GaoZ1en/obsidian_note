# Complete field nets and R-parastatistics

## Result

The identity-net argument does not establish the required no-go. A complete comparison parent must create the specified physical charges from one vacuum using fields localized in bounded spacetime regions.

There is a genuine obstruction to such a parent: **a charged representation disjoint from the vacuum on every spacelike exterior cannot be created by any relatively local field net**. This applies to larger parents and to both Bose and Fermi fields. The proof below needs no reconstruction theorem.

Broken-phase kink sectors in $1+1$ dimensions illustrate the obstruction in an interacting local relativistic theory. They refute a dimension-independent assertion that positivity, Poincaré covariance and observable locality always imply a complete ordinary field extension containing every physical sector. They do not establish new involutive $R$-parastatistics, nor refute a theorem restricted to $3+1$ dimensions. The original $R$ construction problem remains unresolved.

## 1. Complete comparison

Let $O\mapsto\mathcal A(O)$ be the observable net, $\pi_0$ its vacuum representation on $\mathcal H_0$, and $\{\pi_s\}$ the specified physical charge representations. A comparison parent consists of a graded-local net $\mathcal F$ acting on a positive Hilbert space $\mathcal H_F$, with a vacuum $\Omega_F$ and compatible, faithful inclusions

$$
\iota_O:\mathcal A(O)\longrightarrow\mathcal F_{\mathrm{even}}(O).
$$

The inclusions preserve region labels and spacetime covariance. In the parent vacuum representation, require observable-sector isometries

$$
\begin{align}
V_s&:\mathcal H_s\longrightarrow\mathcal H_F,
&
V_s^\dagger V_s&=I,\\
\pi_F(\iota(A))V_s&=V_s\pi_s(A),
&
U_F(g)V_s&=V_sU_s(g),\\
V_0\Omega_0&=\Omega_F.
\end{align}
$$

Write $\widehat A=\pi_F(\iota(A))$. Each $V_s\mathcal H_s$ is reducing for the represented observable algebra: applying the intertwining relation to $A^\dagger$ also gives

$$
V_s^\dagger\widehat A=\pi_s(A)V_s^\dagger.
$$

Completeness at least requires every sector to be reached by local fields. A sufficient explicit formulation is

$$
\overline{\operatorname{span}
\left\{V_s^\dagger F\Omega_F:
F\in\mathcal F(O),\ O\text{ bounded}\right\}}
=\mathcal H_s.
$$

The obstruction below already rules out the weaker requirement that one of these vectors be nonzero. Multiplicities are allowed by applying the condition to each sector copy. Merely extending $\pi_s$ to a separate representation of $\mathcal F$ does not satisfy this definition.

## 2. Exterior intertwiners

For bounded $O$, let $\mathcal A(O')$ be the $C^*$-algebra generated by observable algebras in its spacelike complement.

**Proposition.** If

$$
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal A(O')}
\left(\pi_0,\pi_s\right)=\{0\}
$$

for every bounded $O$, then no parent defined above is complete for sector $s$.

**Proof.** For $F\in\mathcal F(O)$ define the bounded operator

$$
T_{s,F}=V_s^\dagger F V_0.
$$

Every observable is even, so graded locality implies ordinary commutation with $F$ at spacelike separation, regardless of the parity of $F$. Thus for $A\in\mathcal A(O')$,

$$
\begin{align}
\pi_s(A)T_{s,F}
&=V_s^\dagger\widehat A F V_0\\
&=V_s^\dagger F\widehat A V_0\\
&=T_{s,F}\pi_0(A).
\end{align}
$$

The disjointness assumption forces $T_{s,F}=0$. In particular,

$$
V_s^\dagger F\Omega_F
=T_{s,F}\Omega_0=0.
$$

This holds for every bounded region and every bounded local field, contradicting completeness. $\square$

The proof excludes **any** support-preserving parent with these sector and vacuum intertwiners. Extra species, an enlarged gauge group, and tensor factors cannot change the conclusion while preserving those data.

It also applies to a closed local field $X$ affiliated with $\mathcal F(O)$ when $\Omega_F\in\operatorname{Dom}X$. Use the weakly closed parent local algebras for this statement; taking these closures preserves relative locality. The bounded truncations $X\,1_{[0,n]}(|X|)$ belong to $\mathcal F(O)$ and converge on $\Omega_F$ to $X\Omega_F$. Every truncated vector has zero $s$ component, so the limit does too. A charged state obtained as a weak limit of states with a compensating charge sent to infinity need not be a vector in this vacuum Hilbert space.

This is a sufficient obstruction. Failure of the DHR selection criterion by itself is weaker than the vanishing of all exterior intertwiners and is not substituted for it.

## 3. An asymptotic observable test

An explicit way to prove the needed disjointness is to find uniformly bounded observables $B_n$ such that, for every bounded $O$, eventually $B_n\in\mathcal A(O')$, and

$$
\begin{align}
\mathrm{s}\!-\!\lim_{n\to\infty}\pi_0(B_n)&=b_0I,\\
\mathrm{s}\!-\!\lim_{n\to\infty}\pi_s(B_n)&=b_sI,
&
b_s&\ne b_0.
\end{align}
$$

If $T$ intertwines the two exterior representations, then

$$
\begin{align}
\pi_s(B_n)T&=T\pi_0(B_n),\\
(b_s-b_0)T&=0,
\end{align}
$$

where the second line follows by strong convergence on each vector and boundedness of $T$. Therefore $T=0$.

A scalar expectation-value difference alone is insufficient. The limits must separate the representations on their full Hilbert spaces, as above, or another proof of disjointness must be supplied. Bounded functions of an asymptotic flux can be used instead of an unbounded charge operator, but their limits must actually be proved in the model.

## 4. Broken-phase kink sectors

Take the full local scalar observable net $\mathcal A$ of the $1+1$-dimensional $\phi^4_2$ theory in its $\mathbb Z_2$-broken phase. The sign reversal $\beta$, with $\beta^2=\mathrm{id}$, is a global physical symmetry. The order parameter is included among the observables; this is not the fixed-point subnet under that symmetry.

The construction uses two pure, clustering vacuum states $\omega_+$ and $\omega_-=\omega_+\circ\beta$. Their existence and the pure-phase clustering input come from the constructive phase-transition and cluster-expansion results of [Glimm–Jaffe–Spencer (1975)](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/BF01608328) and [Glimm–Jaffe–Spencer (1976), Part II](https://doi.org/10.1016/0003-4916(76)90027-0). Parameters must lie in that established broken-phase regime; no assertion is made for every quartic coupling.

[Jaffe, Theorem VIII.2](https://www.arthurjaffe.com/Assets/pdf/Quantum-Theory_Relativity.pdf) explicitly records the two broken-symmetry phases satisfying the Wightman and Haag–Kastler axioms. In that source's fixed Gaussian-mass normalization they occur at sufficiently large quartic coupling. This provides the relativistic local vacuum theories used here, independently of the later charged-sector construction.

For this net, [Müger, Theorems 3.14 and 4.4](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-th/9803057v2) supplies a locally normal soliton automorphism $\rho$ and a positive-energy Poincaré-covariant representation $\pi_s=\pi_+\circ\rho$. For a bounded transition region $O_*$, choose the left-handed convention

$$
\rho\big|_{\mathcal A(W_L^{O_*})}=\beta,
\qquad
\rho\big|_{\mathcal A(W_R^{O_*})}=\mathrm{id}.
$$

Here $W_L^{O_*}$ and $W_R^{O_*}$ are the two components of $O_*'$. The two vacua appear on opposite sides of the kink. Since $\rho$ is an automorphism and $\pi_+$ is irreducible, $\pi_s$ is irreducible. This use of the soliton theorem includes boosts and the forward spectrum condition, not only covariance under translations.

### A bounded separating sequence

Because the two states differ, there is a bounded local self-adjoint $B$ with

$$
\beta(B)=-B,
\qquad
a=\omega_+(B)\ne0.
$$

To obtain one, take a self-adjoint local $C$ on which the states differ and set $B=(C-\beta(C))/2$. Fix a spatial length $\ell>0$, denote translation by $(0,x)$ by $\tau_x$, and define averages far to the left:

$$
B_N=\frac1N\sum_{j=N+1}^{2N}\tau_{-j\ell}(B),
\qquad
\|B_N\|\leq\|B\|.
$$

Every summand is spacelike to any fixed bounded region once $N$ is sufficiently large. The sequence can therefore test every exterior algebra.

Here is the strong-limit argument. Set

$$
C_k=\omega_+\!\left(B\,\tau_{k\ell}(B)\right)-a^2.
$$

Vacuum clustering gives $C_k\to0$ as $|k|\to\infty$, and $C_{-k}=\overline{C_k}$. Translation invariance yields

$$
\begin{align}
\|(\pi_+(B_N)-aI)\Omega_+\|^2
&=\frac1{N^2}\sum_{k=-(N-1)}^{N-1}(N-|k|)C_k\\
&\leq\frac{|C_0|}{N}
+\frac2N\sum_{k=1}^{N-1}|C_k|
\longrightarrow0.
\end{align}
$$

The last limit is Cesàro convergence; an exponential decay rate is unnecessary. For each local $D$, locality eventually gives $[B_N,D]=0$, hence

$$
\|(\pi_+(B_N)-aI)\pi_+(D)\Omega_+\|
\leq
\|\pi_+(D)\|\,
\|(\pi_+(B_N)-aI)\Omega_+\|
\longrightarrow0.
$$

These vectors are dense, and $\|\pi_+(B_N)-aI\|\leq\|B\|+|a|$. Thus

$$
\mathrm{s}\!-\!\lim_{N\to\infty}\pi_+(B_N)=aI.
$$

For large $N$, all summands lie in $W_L^{O_*}$, where $\rho=\beta$. Consequently

$$
\pi_s(B_N)
=\pi_+(\rho(B_N))
=-\pi_+(B_N)
\ \longrightarrow\ -aI
\qquad\text{strongly}.
$$

The asymptotic test applies with $b_0=a$ and $b_s=-a$. No bounded-region Bose/Fermi field net preserving this observable theory, its $+$ vacuum and its kink sector can locally create that sector from the vacuum.

### What fails, and what does not follow

The failed DHR hypothesis is **double-cone localization relative to the chosen vacuum**: the exterior representations are disjoint, rather than equivalent. Positivity, observable locality, and relativistic covariance are retained. The existence of an inverse soliton automorphism does not repair the localization failure.

This is a counterexample to complete local-field reconstruction for all physical sectors, when $1+1$-dimensional topological sectors are included. It is an application of known interacting models and soliton theorems, not a newly constructed continuum theory.

The original scalar model already has an ordinary bosonic field description. Its local scalar fields do not create a single kink from a fixed vacuum. Thus exclusion of a **complete locally creating** parent is a different statement from exclusion of every ordinary bosonic description.

No finite-dimensional involutive Yang–Baxter matrix has been identified here as the intrinsic exchange of these kink sectors. A soliton scattering matrix or the identity $\rho^2=\mathrm{id}$ would not supply that identification. This example therefore does not solve the requested $R$-parastatistics construction.

## 5. Consequence for the R problem

A valid inequivalence argument can now use a concrete test: exhibit an actual charged representation of the interacting $R$ theory and prove that it is disjoint from the vacuum on every spacelike exterior. The above proposition then excludes all complete relatively local Bose/Fermi parents, including larger ones.

What remains missing is a single model that combines this obstruction with the required physical involutive $R$ exchange and relativistic interactions. The matrix alone does not define a charge sector or its exterior representation. The finite-crossing obstructions in the companion construction notes remain conditional and do not follow from the three observable axioms.

## Verification

**Verified:** The exterior-intertwiner and strong-limit arguments above are general operator proofs. Mathematica checked the block-commutator and scalar-tail identities with arbitrary $2\times2$ entries, and the pair-counting identity in the discrete variance sum for $N=1,\ldots,8$. The general multiplicity $N-|k|$ follows by counting pairs with fixed difference, not from those finite checks.

**Assumptions:** Observable inclusions preserve localization and are even; the parent vacuum restricts to the specified vacuum; physical sectors occur as reducing observable subrepresentations; and completeness requires local creation. The kink application uses the constructed broken phase, pure-vacuum clustering, local Fock property, and the stated soliton theorem as source inputs. It keeps the full order-parameter observable algebra.

**Source inspection:** Müger v2 was read at the soliton definition, hypotheses, covariance proof and application to local Fock models; the definition and Theorems 3.14 and 4.4 were visually confirmed on PDF pages 3, 14–15 and 17. Jaffe's Theorem VIII.2 and its Wightman/Haag–Kastler conclusion were read and visually confirmed on PDF page 28. The original phase-transition and cluster-expansion results were checked at their primary abstracts, not independently rederived. Schlingemann's related construction was also read, but its translation-covariance statement is not used as a substitute for boost covariance.

**Not verified:** A new interacting $R$ model; intrinsic nonordinary permutation statistics for the kink example; a $3+1$-dimensional counterexample; a universal no-go for $R$-parastatistics; explicit kink scattering amplitudes; or a fresh proof of the cited constructive existence theorems.
