# R-parastatistics, crossing, and local continuum construction

## Result

The construction below supplies an explicit rapidity-dependent $R$-matrix and a positive relativistic Fock representation. For the completed cyclic family, an explicit analytic intertwiner gives nontrivial local algebras in sufficiently large double cones, using the stated inverse-scattering estimates. It does **not** supply the requested new physical statistics: the constructed field net is Bose-local, and restricting its compact internal symmetry gives an ordinary-field realization by construction.

There is also a general algebraic obstruction in this ansatz. A finite-dimensional unitary involutive $R$ satisfying constant crossing symmetry has a natural nondegenerate particle–antiparticle contraction. Its generated symmetric $C^*$ tensor category is rigid. Thus lack of conjugates cannot be its reconstruction loophole.

Enlarging the antiparticle space does not remove this obstruction for a finite particle family closed under exchange. In a unitary involutive crossing-symmetric completion, even an infinite-dimensional one, that family and its antiparticles close inside a finite-dimensional space. The proof below does not assume beforehand that the particle-type projection is a categorical morphism.

The crossing obstruction also applies to an involutive high-rapidity limit of any finite-dimensional regular scattering matrix satisfying the conditions below; scalar factorization is unnecessary for that conclusion. Neither result proves a reconstruction theorem for every possible relativistic completion of an exchange algebra. The broader construction status is recorded in [R-parastatistics and local relativistic interactions](R-parastatistics%20and%20local%20relativistic%20interactions.md).

## 1. Definitions

Use $1+1$-dimensional Minkowski spacetime, $\eta=\operatorname{diag}(+,-)$, and $\hbar=c=1$. All particle labels have the same mass $\mu>0$. Let

$$
\begin{align}
V&=\mathbb C^d,
&\mathcal H_1&=L^2(\mathbb R,d\theta)\otimes V,\\
p(\theta)&=\mu(\cosh\theta,\sinh\theta),
&(U_1(a,\chi)\psi)(\theta)
&=e^{ip(\theta)\cdot a}\psi(\theta-\chi).
\end{align}
$$

The scalar product is antilinear in its first entry. An antiunitary involution $C:V\to V$, $C^2=1$, specifies antiparticles. A $C$-fixed orthonormal basis exists; unless stated otherwise, use this basis, so $C$ is ordinary complex conjugation.

Let

$$
\begin{align}
R&:V\otimes V\longrightarrow V\otimes V,\\
R^\dagger R&=I,\qquad R^2=I,\\
R_{12}R_{23}R_{12}&=R_{23}R_{12}R_{23}.
\end{align}
$$

Write $R_{ab,cd}=\langle e_a\otimes e_b,R(e_c\otimes e_d)\rangle$, with outgoing labels first. The constant crossing condition used here is

$$
\begin{align}
\langle u_1\otimes u_2,R(v_1\otimes v_2)\rangle
&=\langle Cv_1\otimes u_1,R(v_2\otimes Cu_2)\rangle,\\
R_{ab,cd}&=R_{ca,db}
\qquad\text{in a $C$-fixed basis}.
\end{align}
$$

This is the constant specialization of the scattering crossing convention in [Lechner–Scotford, Definition 18](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.13237), and of condition (S5) in [Bostelmann–Cadamuro–Mandrysch](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00023-023-01409-8). In particular, the ordinary flip $F$ satisfies it; $I_{V\otimes V}$ does not when $d>1$.

Crossing is an additional condition. The Yang–Baxter equation and positivity of the exchange Fock space alone do not imply it.

## 2. Crossing supplies conjugates

Define the evaluation and coevaluation

$$
\begin{align}
e:V\otimes V&\longrightarrow\mathbb C,
&e(v\otimes w)&=\langle Cv,w\rangle,\\
c=e^\dagger:\mathbb C&\longrightarrow V\otimes V,
&c(1)&=\sum_{a=1}^d e_a\otimes e_a.
\end{align}
$$

Both are bounded because $d<\infty$. Their conjugate equations are

$$
(e\otimes I)(I\otimes c)=I,
\qquad
(I\otimes e)(c\otimes I)=I.
$$

The substantive point is compatibility with the exchange. Three successive applications of crossing give

$$
R_{ad,bk}=R_{ba,kd}=R_{kb,da}=R_{dk,ab}.
$$

Consequently

$$
\begin{align}
\sum_{a,b}R_{ab,ij}R_{ad,bk}
&=\sum_{a,b}R_{dk,ab}R_{ab,ij}\\
&=(R^2)_{dk,ij}\\
&=\delta_{di}\delta_{kj}.
\end{align}
$$

In operator form,

$$
(e\otimes I)(I\otimes R)(R\otimes I)
=I\otimes e.
$$

This is naturality of the evaluation. Taking adjoints and using $R^\dagger=R$ supplies the corresponding coevaluation identity. The Yang–Baxter equation supplies naturality between crossings themselves.

Therefore the concrete tensor category generated by $V$, $R$, $e$, $c$, and their tensor products, compositions and adjoints is a rigid symmetric $C^*$ tensor category after closing under finite sums and subobjects. Its unit is simple because its endomorphisms are scalar maps on $\mathbb C$. The [Doplicher–Roberts categorical theorem](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/BF01388849) applies:

$$
\mathcal C_R\simeq_{\mathrm{sym},*}\operatorname{Rep}_{\mathrm{fin}}(G,k).
$$

This proves a categorical conclusion. Identifying $\mathcal C_R$ with the physical charge category of a spacetime net is a separate task. Conversely, if it is that physical category and the net reconstruction hypotheses hold, this $R$ cannot evade reconstruction through absent conjugates.

### A counting consequence

The displayed duality is standard: its left and right traces on $\operatorname{End}_{\mathcal C_R}(V)$ are the ordinary matrix trace. Thus the symmetric unitary fiber functor preserves the dimension $d$. Write its image as a super Hilbert space of dimensions $p|q$, with $p+q=d$.

For the constant exchange algebra, let

$$
P_n^R=\frac1{n!}\sum_{\sigma\in S_n}\rho_R^{(n)}(\sigma).
$$

The functor maps this projection to the graded symmetrizer and preserves its rank. Hence

$$
z_R(x):=\sum_{n\geq0}\operatorname{rank}(P_n^R)x^n
=\frac{(1+x)^q}{(1-x)^p}.
$$

In particular,

$$
\operatorname{Tr}_{V\otimes V}R=p-q,
\qquad
p=\frac{d+\operatorname{Tr}R}{2},
\qquad
q=\frac{d-\operatorname{Tr}R}{2}.
$$

The trace identity follows already from equality of the ranks of the two-particle symmetrizers, since $\operatorname{rank}(P_2^R)=(d^2+\operatorname{Tr}R)/2$. Thus unusual single-mode counts such as $1+dx$, $d>1$, cannot occur with all the hypotheses above. This counting result is weaker than an equivalence of local theories. It is also not the thermodynamic partition function of a rapidity-dependent interacting model.

### Enlarging the antiparticle space

Let $V$ be a finite-dimensional particle space, $\dim V=m$, embedded isometrically in a Hilbert space $\mathcal K$ of arbitrary dimension. Suppose a proposed completion has an antiunitary involution $C$ on $\mathcal K$ and a bounded operator $\widehat R$ satisfying unitarity, involutivity, Yang–Baxter and the crossing identity of §1. Require that the original particle family remain closed:

$$
\widehat R(V\otimes V)=V\otimes V,
\qquad
\widehat R|_{V\otimes V}=R.
$$

Then

$$
\mathcal K_0:=V+CV,\qquad
\dim\mathcal K_0\leq2m
$$

is invariant under $C$, and $\mathcal K_0\otimes\mathcal K_0$ is invariant under $\widehat R$. Thus any such completion already contains a finite crossing-symmetric completion.

To prove the mixed-space assertion, take an orthonormal basis $e_a$ of $V$ and write $f_a=Ce_a$. Let $B$ be the compression of $\widehat R$ from $V\otimes CV$ to $CV\otimes V$. Crossing gives

$$
\begin{align}
B_{ca,db}
&:=\langle f_c\otimes e_a,
\widehat R(e_d\otimes f_b)\rangle\\
&=\langle e_a\otimes e_b,
\widehat R(e_c\otimes e_d)\rangle
=R_{ab,cd}.
\end{align}
$$

As a compression of a unitary, $B$ is a contraction. But its Hilbert–Schmidt norm is

$$
\operatorname{Tr}(B^\dagger B)
=\sum_{a,b,c,d}|R_{ab,cd}|^2
=m^2,
$$

because $R$ is unitary on the $m^2$-dimensional space $V\otimes V$. All $m^2$ singular values of $B$ are at most one, and their squares sum to $m^2$. Every singular value is therefore one. For any $v\in V\otimes CV$,

$$
\|\widehat Rv\|=\|Bv\|=\|v\|,
$$

so the orthogonal component outside $CV\otimes V$ vanishes. Hence

$$
\widehat R(V\otimes CV)=CV\otimes V.
$$

Involutivity supplies the reverse inclusion and equality. Twice applying crossing and using $\widehat R^\dagger=\widehat R$ gives

$$
J_2\widehat R J_2=\widehat R,
\qquad
J_2=F(C\otimes C).
$$

It follows that $CV\otimes CV$ is invariant too. The four tensor products of $V$ and $CV$ span $\mathcal K_0\otimes\mathcal K_0$, proving the claim. This argument permits $V\cap CV\neq0$ and does not use a global coevaluation vector on infinite-dimensional $\mathcal K$.

There is a direct diagnostic for $R=\varepsilon I$, $\varepsilon=\pm1$. In the coefficient bases above,

$$
B=\varepsilon|\Omega_m\rangle\langle\Omega_m|,
\qquad
\Omega_m=\sum_{a=1}^m|a,a\rangle,
\qquad
\|B\|=m.
$$

For $m>1$ this contradicts the compression bound $\|B\|\leq1$. Thus no number of additional species can complete this closed finite block under the stated unitarity and crossing condition. This direct contradiction does not even require Yang–Baxter or global involutivity.

### A criterion from symmetric and alternating counts

The same completion result constrains every finite-dimensional unitary involutive $R$. Define

$$
\begin{align}
P_n^+(R)&=\frac1{n!}\sum_{\sigma\in S_n}\rho_R^{(n)}(\sigma),\\
P_n^-(R)&=\frac1{n!}\sum_{\sigma\in S_n}
\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma)\rho_R^{(n)}(\sigma),\\
H_R^\pm(x)&=\sum_{n\geq0}\operatorname{rank}P_n^\pm(R)\,x^n.
\end{align}
$$

[Lechner–Pennig–Wood, Theorem 4.8 and Eqs. (5.6)–(5.7)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1707.00196v3) give finite lists of positive integers $a_i,b_j$, with $\sum_i a_i+\sum_j b_j=m$, such that

$$
H_R^+(x)=\frac{\prod_j(1+b_jx)}{\prod_i(1-a_ix)},
\qquad
H_R^-(x)=\frac{\prod_i(1+a_ix)}{\prod_j(1-b_jx)}.
$$

Suppose the preceding crossing-symmetric completion exists. Restrict it to $\mathcal K_0$, with resulting matrix $\widehat R_0$. The inclusion $V^{\otimes n}\hookrightarrow\mathcal K_0^{\otimes n}$ intertwines every permutation, so

$$
\operatorname{rank}P_n^\pm(R)
\leq\operatorname{rank}P_n^\pm(\widehat R_0).
$$

The finite crossing result gives integers $p,q\geq0$, $p+q=\dim\mathcal K_0$, and

$$
H_{\widehat R_0}^+(x)=\frac{(1+x)^q}{(1-x)^p},
\qquad
H_{\widehat R_0}^-(x)=\frac{(1+x)^p}{(1-x)^q}.
$$

Both coefficient sequences grow at most polynomially in $n$; when a denominator is absent the corresponding sequence eventually vanishes. In contrast, if $a_i\geq2$, positivity of all coefficients gives

$$
\operatorname{rank}P_n^+(R)\geq a_i^n.
$$

If $b_j\geq2$, the same argument gives $\operatorname{rank}P_n^-(R)\geq b_j^n$. Either exponential bound contradicts the preceding polynomial bound. Therefore a necessary condition for a crossing-symmetric completion is

$$
\boxed{a_i=b_j=1\quad\text{for every nonzero parameter}.}
$$

Equivalently, the original $R$ must have the same permutation characters, at every particle number, as a graded flip with finitely many Bose and Fermi labels. For $R=-I$ the alternating rank is $m^n$, explaining why inspecting only its symmetric series $1+mx$ misses the growth obstruction.

This equivalence is at the level of permutation representations: it allows a separate intertwining unitary at each $n$. It neither proves a coherent equivalence of local field theories nor identifies the physical observable algebra. The criterion is necessary, not asserted sufficient for a completion. Its hypotheses also require a fixed closed particle subspace and a unitary, globally involutive, crossing-symmetric exchange. Observable locality alone has not been proved to supply those hypotheses for every charged-field realization.

### A rapidity-dependent completion does not evade this limit

Consider a finite-dimensional matrix function $S(\zeta)$ which is analytic and bounded on an enlarged strip

$$
-\kappa<\operatorname{Im}\zeta<\pi+\kappa,
\qquad \kappa>0.
$$

Assume real-axis unitarity, Hermitian analyticity $S(-\theta)=S(\theta)^\dagger$, the spectral Yang–Baxter equation, and crossing with the same fixed antiparticle involution $C$ as in §1. Suppose the norm limit

$$
R_+=\lim_{\theta\to+\infty}S(\theta)
$$

exists and is involutive. Then $R_+$ satisfies every constant-$R$ hypothesis in §2, including crossing. In particular, a regular momentum-dependent completion cannot have $R_+=\pm I$ with $d>1$.

Here is the analytic step. For any sequence $t_j\to+\infty$, the translated functions $S_j(z)=S(t_j+z)$ form a bounded normal family on the enlarged strip, entry by entry. Any subsequential analytic limit equals $R_+$ on the real line, which is inside the strip. The identity theorem makes that limit constant throughout the strip. Since every convergent subsequence has this same limit, convergence is locally uniform:

$$
\lim_{\theta\to+\infty}S(\theta+z)=R_+
$$

for each fixed $z$ in the strip, including $z=i\pi$. The identical argument works at negative infinity.

Real-axis unitarity and Hermitian analyticity give

$$
R_+^\dagger R_+=I,
\qquad
R_-:=\lim_{\theta\to-\infty}S(\theta)=R_+^\dagger.
$$

The assumed involutivity therefore implies $R_-=R_+$. In a $C$-fixed basis, passing to the limit in crossing gives

$$
\begin{align}
S_{ab,cd}(\theta)&=S_{ca,db}(i\pi-\theta),\\
(R_+)_{ab,cd}&=(R_-)_{ca,db}
=(R_+)_{ca,db}.
\end{align}
$$

Taking both rapidities in the spectral Yang–Baxter equation to $+\infty$ supplies the constant equation for $R_+$. Thus §2 applies. These limits are motivated by the scaling-limit discussion in [Lechner–Scotford, §6](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.13237); the normal-family argument above states the additional hypotheses used here.

This is a result about the limiting exchange category. It does not identify that category with all physical sectors or prove equivalence of the interacting local nets. It also does not apply when the norm limit fails to exist, the enlarged-strip bound is absent, the antiparticle space changes with rapidity, or the limiting matrix is noninvolutive. In particular, one must not substitute $S(0)$ for $R_+$: crossing relates $S(0)$ to $S(i\pi)$, and does not generally make $S(0)$ a constant crossing-symmetric solution.

## 3. An explicit interaction factor

Choose a dimensionless parameter $0<g<1$ and set

$$
\begin{align}
s_g(\zeta)&=\frac{\sinh\zeta-ig}{\sinh\zeta+ig},\\
S_g(\zeta)&=s_g(\zeta)R.
\end{align}
$$

This is an inverse-scattering construction; no local polynomial Lagrangian is assumed. Directly,

$$
\begin{align}
s_g(\theta)s_g(-\theta)&=1,
&\overline{s_g(\theta)}&=s_g(-\theta),\\
s_g(i\pi-\zeta)&=s_g(\zeta),
&s_g(0)&=-1,\\
s_g'(\theta)&=\frac{2ig\cosh\theta}{(\sinh\theta+ig)^2}.
\end{align}
$$

Here $\theta\in\mathbb R$ in the unitarity identities. The scalar factors commute, so the spectral Yang–Baxter equation for $S_g$ reduces to the constant equation for $R$. Crossing reduces to the condition in §1. Twice applying constant crossing, together with $R^\dagger=R$, gives the required PCT identity.

There are no poles in $0<\operatorname{Im}\zeta<\pi$: writing $\zeta=x+iy$,

$$
\operatorname{Im}(\sinh\zeta+ig)
=\cosh x\sin y+g>0.
$$

Moreover,

$$
|\sinh\zeta+ig|^2-|\sinh\zeta-ig|^2
=4g\cosh x\sin y,
$$

so $|s_g|\leq1$ on the closed physical strip. It is analytic and bounded on every enlarged strip

$$
-\kappa<\operatorname{Im}\zeta<\pi+\kappa,
\qquad 0<\kappa<\arcsin g.
$$

The nonconstant factor gives nontrivial scattering once existence of the local model is established. Its algebraic properties alone would not establish that existence.

### The Fock and wedge construction

On $\mathcal H_1^{\otimes2}$ define

$$
(T_g\Psi)(\theta_1,\theta_2)
=S_g(\theta_2-\theta_1)\Psi(\theta_2,\theta_1).
$$

This formula swaps rapidity arguments; the internal operator $S_g$ is displayed explicitly. Unitarity and Hermitian analyticity give $T_g^\dagger=T_g$ and $T_g^2=I$. The spectral Yang–Baxter equation therefore defines a unitary permutation representation $D_n^{S_g}$. Let

$$
\mathcal H_{S_g}
=\bigoplus_{n\geq0}P_n^{S_g}\mathcal H_1^{\otimes n},
\qquad
P_n^{S_g}=\frac1{n!}\sum_{\sigma\in S_n}D_n^{S_g}(\sigma).
$$

The creation operators are

$$
z^\dagger(f)\Psi_n
=\sqrt{n+1}\,P_{n+1}^{S_g}(f\otimes\Psi_n),
\qquad z(f)=z^\dagger(f)^\dagger.
$$

The tensor Poincaré representation restricts to this Fock space and has positive energy and a unique zero-energy vector $\Omega$.

For definiteness choose the left wedge $W_L=\{x^1<-|x^0|\}$. Its one-particle real standard space can be described by Hardy boundary values on $0<\operatorname{Im}\theta<\pi$ with

$$
\psi(\theta+i\pi)=C\psi(\theta).
$$

Its modular operators in this convention are $(\Delta^{it}\psi)(\theta)=\psi(\theta-2\pi t)$ and $(J\psi)(\theta)=C\psi(\theta)$. The wedge algebra is generated by the closures of $z^\dagger(h)+z(h)$ for $h$ in this real space. Crossing supplies the reflected commuting wedge algebra. Translated opposite wedges define the double-cone algebras by intersection.

For compatible twists, crossing and Yang–Baxter characterize the separating-vacuum condition in [Correa da Silva–Lechner, Theorems 3.12 and 3.22](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.02298). This controls wedge algebras. Nontriviality of their bounded-region intersections still needs the estimate below.

## 4. A completed cyclic family

Take $m\geq2$ and $d=2m$, with labels $(q,a)\in\{+1,-1\}\times\mathbb Z_m$. Define

$$
\begin{align}
R\bigl(|q,a\rangle\otimes|r,b\rangle\bigr)
&=|r,b+q\rangle\otimes|q,a-r\rangle,\\
C|q,a\rangle&=|-q,a-q\rangle,
\end{align}
$$

where $C$ is extended antilinearly. The map $R$ is a permutation matrix. Applying it twice gives the identity. Both sides of Yang–Baxter send

$$
((q,a),(r,b),(s,c))
\longmapsto
((s,c+q+r),(r,b+q-s),(q,a-r-s)).
$$

Also $C^2=I$. In the crossing equation, if the left-hand matrix element is nonzero, its labels obey

$$
(A,B;D,E)
=((r,b+q),(q,a-r);(q,a),(r,b)).
$$

The right-hand matrix element is then nonzero with the same coefficient one; reversing this implication proves crossing. Thus $S_g=s_gR$ meets all the algebraic and strip conditions of §3.

The sector $q=r=+1$ restricts to the cyclic matrix used in the earlier interacting construction. Including the negative-charge labels and the stated $C$ is essential; the positive-charge block alone is not the complete crossing problem.

### An ordinary permutation intertwiner

For a tensor word, define

$$
W_n|(q_1,a_1),\ldots,(q_n,a_n)\rangle
=|(q_1,a_1),\,
(q_2,a_2+q_1),\ldots,
(q_n,a_n+\sum_{j<n}q_j)\rangle.
$$

This is a unitary permutation of the basis and

$$
W_nR_i=F_iW_n.
$$

To check the identity, write the prefix charge before positions $i,i+1$ as $Q$. Their two labels after $W_nR_i$ are

$$
(r,b+q+Q),\qquad(q,a-r+Q+r)=(q,a+Q),
$$

which are exactly the labels after $F_iW_n$. Other positions have unchanged prefix charge. This proves the identity at every $m,n$.

In a Fourier basis for $a$, the same $R$ is a phase times the ordinary flip:

$$
\begin{align}
|q,k\rangle
&=\frac1{\sqrt m}\sum_{a\in\mathbb Z_m}\zeta^{-ka}|q,a\rangle,
&\zeta&=e^{2\pi i/m},\\
R(|q,k\rangle\otimes|r,l\rangle)
&=\zeta^{ql-rk}|r,l\rangle\otimes|q,k\rangle.
\end{align}
$$

The labels carry the ordinary characters $(q,k)$ of $U(1)\times\mathbb Z_m$. Antiparticle labels are $(-q,-k)$; the phase in their antiunitary map can be removed by rephasing the basis. The displayed scattering matrix commutes with this compact internal group.

### The bounded-region estimate

For this family, the analytic intertwiner can be given explicitly. Choose $\kappa<\arcsin g$ and the analytic square root

$$
\rho(\zeta)=-s_g(\zeta),\qquad
h(\zeta)^2=\rho(\zeta),\qquad h(0)=1
$$

on a sufficiently small strip around the real axis. It is zero-free there, $|h(\theta)|=1$, and $h(-\zeta)=h(\zeta)^{-1}$. Set

$$
I_n(\boldsymbol\zeta)
=\prod_{i<j}h(\zeta_i-\zeta_j),
\qquad
J_n(\boldsymbol\zeta)=W_n I_n(\boldsymbol\zeta).
$$

For an adjacent swap $\tau_i$, with $\delta=\theta_i-\theta_{i+1}$,

$$
\frac{I_n(\boldsymbol\theta)}
{I_n(\tau_i\boldsymbol\theta)}
=-s_g(\delta),
\qquad
[-s_g(\delta)]s_g(-\delta)=-1.
$$

Hence multiplication by $J_n$ intertwines the $S_g$-symmetric space with the ordinary **antisymmetric** space:

$$
J_nD_n^{S_g}(\tau_i)
=D_n^{-F}(\tau_i)J_n.
$$

This antisymmetric auxiliary space is used for a norm estimate; it does not assign physical Fermi statistics to the model.

The required bounds are exponential in $n$. Indeed,

$$
\frac{h'(\zeta)}{h(\zeta)}
=\frac{ig\cosh\zeta}{\sinh^2\zeta+g^2}.
$$

On $|\operatorname{Im}\zeta|\leq\kappa/2$, the denominator is nonzero and this expression tends uniformly to zero as $|\operatorname{Re}\zeta|\to\infty$. Thus

$$
M_\kappa:=
\sup_{\substack{x\in\mathbb R\\|y|\leq\kappa/2}}
\left|\partial_y\log|h(x+iy)|\right|<\infty.
$$

Since $\log|h(x)|=0$, on the tubes

$$
\left|\operatorname{Im}\zeta_j+\frac\pi2\right|
<\frac{\kappa}{2n}
$$

each pairwise imaginary difference has modulus less than $\kappa/n$. For $n\geq2$ this is at most $\kappa/2$, and hence

$$
\begin{align}
\log\|J_n(\boldsymbol\zeta)\|
&\leq \binom n2\frac{M_\kappa\kappa}{n}
=\frac{M_\kappa\kappa}{2}(n-1),\\
\|J_n(\boldsymbol\zeta)\|
&\leq\gamma^n,\qquad
\gamma=e^{M_\kappa\kappa/2}.
\end{align}
$$

For $n=1$ the product is empty. On the common shifted real boundary, every difference is real, so $J_n$ is unitary and its inverse has norm one.

Here is the precise use of the inverse-scattering estimates. Use the reflected right-wedge algebra for the modular map, as in that reference. [Alazzawi–Lechner, Proposition 4.5](https://arxiv.org/abs/1608.02359) supplies Hardy-space bounds for every regular $S$. Write its $n$-particle modular map as $\Xi_n=X_n\Upsilon_n$. Because all masses are equal, $X_n$ is scalar on the internal indices. With $J_{n,\partial}$ the boundary multiplier,

$$
J_{n,\partial}\Xi_n
=P_n^-X_nJ_n\Upsilon_n.
$$

This identity uses the range of $\Xi_n$ in the $S_g$-symmetric space. It does not assert that $X_n$ maps arbitrary Hardy functions into that space. Their Lemma 5.1 bounds $P_n^-X_n$, gaining the factorial from ordinary antisymmetrization. The displayed factorization therefore gives the estimate

$$
\|\Xi_n(\ell)\|_1
\leq C_1(\ell)
\left(\frac{C_2e^{-C_3\ell}}{\sqrt\ell}\right)^n
\frac{n^n}{n!}.
$$

The constants are independent of $n$. This is the proof mechanism of their Theorem 5.3, with the explicit $J_n$ replacing its intertwiner onto the $S(0)=-F$ space. It is not an application of that theorem while ignoring its $S(0)=-F$ hypothesis: here $S_g(0)=-R$, and the preceding factorization is the replacement argument.

Since the ratio of consecutive $n^n/n!$ tends to $e$, the estimate is summable for sufficiently large wedge separation $\ell$. Modular nuclearity then gives type III local algebras with cyclic vacuum in sufficiently large double cones, and the inverse-scattering construction gives the nonconstant factorizing scattering matrix. The analytic existence and scattering implications are theorem inputs from [Alazzawi–Lechner, Theorems 3.4 and 3.10 and §3.1](https://arxiv.org/abs/1608.02359).

This establishes the stated large-region result. It does not establish cyclicity or nontriviality at every arbitrarily small double-cone scale.

## 5. Why this does not give the requested statistics

Call the constructed local field net $\mathcal F$. Its bounded local operators commute at spacelike separation. Cyclicity in a double cone implies that the one-particle projections of $\mathcal F(O)\Omega$ are dense in $\mathcal H_1$. The isolated mass shell then permits the usual Haag–Ruelle construction from these operators.

For wave packets with disjoint asymptotic velocities, compactly localized approximants have spacelike-separated supports at sufficiently large times; the discarded tails vanish in the scattering limit. Their commutators therefore tend to zero, and the scattering limits satisfy

$$
\Psi^{\mathrm{in/out}}(f_1,f_2)
=\Psi^{\mathrm{in/out}}(f_2,f_1).
$$

These are ordinary bosonic asymptotic particles in the vacuum representation of $\mathcal F$. The nontrivial $R$ in the ordered scattering description is not their physical permutation-statistics operator.

If the compact internal symmetry is treated as gauge and observables are restricted to

$$
\mathcal A(O)=\mathcal F(O)^{\,U(1)\times\mathbb Z_m},
$$

then $\mathcal F$ is already a Bose-local parent field net. Group Fourier components of local operators give local charged fields with ordinary spacelike commutation. This is an ordinary-field realization in the algebraic sense relevant to DHR–DR, whether or not a canonical polynomial Lagrangian is available. It is disqualified by the stronger inequivalence requirement.

No failed DHR–DR assumption has been established for a new physical charge theory here. The full sector category of the constructed observable subnet has not been classified. The conclusion needed for this candidate is narrower and explicit: its apparent $R$-statistics can be placed in a Bose-local field net, while its finite crossing category has conjugates.

## 6. Verification and scope

**Verified:** Sage checked the crossing-only polynomial contraction identity at $d=2,3$ using indeterminates for all crossing orbits. The component derivation in §2 proves it for all finite $d$. Sage checked unitary involutivity, Yang–Baxter, crossing, both the contraction slide and conjugate identity for the completed cyclic family at $m=2,3,4$. It checked $W_nR_i=F_iW_n$ at $m=2,3,4$ and $n=2,3,4$; the prefix-charge calculation proves the general identity.

For the completion test, Sage checked the mixed compression and exact invariant-subspace identity in the completed cyclic examples at $m=2,3,4$.
