# A gauge-completed selected Kerr coupling

## Result

**A fixed angular-projection waveform coefficient has a well-defined metric-source/CPS construction on the stated outgoing analytic domain.** Its gauge completion is essential. Neither the raw quadratic amplitude-equation coefficient nor an uncorrected pairing with a sourced metric has this property.

The construction below uses one radial Green problem and one adjoint metric test. It does not assume completeness of QNMs or existence of a pointwise convergent infinite daughter-metric expansion. This distinction matters when auditing the original card: a selected response functional and a complete metric reconstruction are different mathematical outputs.

## 1. Fix the observable and its domain

Fix a background Kerr metric, an asymptotic frame and polarization, time origin, outgoing strain convention, and nonzero normalized parent amplitudes. Choose one simple bilinearly normalized daughter angular branch at
$$
\Omega=\omega_b+\omega_c,\qquad m_Q=m_b+m_c.
$$
The angular extraction is fixed as part of the observable. Spherical extraction is another fixed choice, related numerically by angular mixing; it is not silently substituted for the selected spheroidal projection.

Use the common radial covering and outgoing sector of the Green-integral note. Require a nonzero Wronskian $W_\ell$, the stated strict horizon bounds, and the source infinity powers established for the DD benchmark. This defines a nonresonant response. A simple-pole residue has the separate normalization in the metric-CPS note; exact resonance requires the corresponding secular response.

Let $c_\Omega$ be the fixed linear conversion from the outgoing coefficient of
$\bar\Gamma^4\delta\Psi_4$ to the chosen strain amplitude. For a metric variation $B$, write
$$
\mathcal W_{1,\ell}(B)
=c_\Omega\,\operatorname{Out}_\ell(T'B).
$$
Let $\mathcal W_{2,\ell}(h,h)$ denote the order-$\varepsilon^2$ quadratic extraction term for a family
$g+\varepsilon h+\varepsilon^2k$; it includes the factor $1/2$ in the second derivative of the observable. It is not the mixed-parameter convention.

Define the source projection
$$
\Pi_\ell(F)=
\frac1{W_\ell}
\int_{\mathcal C}u_\ell\Delta^{-2}Q_\ell[F]\,dr.
$$
For the selected DD representative, the [normalized metric-source identity](metric-cps-normalization.md) proves
$$
\Pi_\ell(F)=
\frac1{W_\ell}
\int_{\mathcal C\times S^2}\Sigma\,H_\ell^{ab}F_{ab}\,
dr\,d\Omega_{\rm ang},
\qquad H_\ell=S'^\dagger\eta_\ell.
$$
All factors in $\eta_\ell$ are fixed there. The source-current endpoints have been estimated, not deleted by assuming source-free conservation.

The completed observable response is
$$
\boxed{
\mathcal A_{\ell;hh}
=c_\Omega\Pi_\ell\!\left(-\tfrac12D^2G[h,h]\right)
+\mathcal W_{2,\ell}(h,h).
}
$$
Divide by the square of the fixed linear parent strain amplitude to define a dimensionless identical-parent coupling. For two distinct parents use the mixed source $-D^2G[h_b,h_c]$, the mixed extraction term and the product of their fixed amplitudes.

## 2. The key identity for an equation-exact change of source

Let $B$ be a metric variation with the same frequency and azimuthal number, regular at the prescribed horizon end and outgoing at infinity. Require that its extracted curvature has the usual outgoing radiative leading order or faster decay, and that the Green boundary terms obey the same analytic prescription.

From $S'E=O'T'$ and the checked separation normalization,
$$
Q_\ell[EB]=L_{\Omega,\ell}Y_B,\qquad
Y_B=\operatorname{Proj}_\ell(\bar\Gamma^4T'B).
$$
The radial Green identity gives
$$
\Pi_\ell(EB)
=\frac1{W_\ell}
\left[\Delta^{-1}(u_\ell Y_B'-Y_Bu_\ell')\right]_{\mathscr H}^{\infty}
=\operatorname{Out}_\ell(T'B).
$$
The horizon expression vanishes; the outgoing-end expression is the outgoing coefficient times $W_\ell$. Therefore
$$
\boxed{c_\Omega\Pi_\ell(EB)=\mathcal W_{1,\ell}(B).}
$$
This identity is the precise replacement for a claim that a source projection annihilates every equation-exact source. It need not annihilate one: the boundary waveform of $B$ is retained.

The admissible source domain can therefore include the DD source and its changes by $EB$ for such variations $B$, with the corresponding quadratic observable completion. The construction is not restricted to gauge transformations that leave the chiral representative unchanged.

## 3. Gauge independence

For a first-order proper diffeomorphism generated by $\xi$, a single-parameter metric family transforms as
$$
h\longmapsto h+\mathcal L_\xi g,
$$
$$
k\longmapsto k+B,\qquad
B=\mathcal L_\xi h+\tfrac12\mathcal L_\xi^2g+\mathcal L_\zeta g.
$$
The second-order vector $\zeta$ is independently proper. Naturality of the Einstein tensor, with the linear parent on shell, gives
$$
F\longmapsto F+EB.
$$
Naturality of the fixed physical extraction gives
$$
\mathcal W_{2,\ell}\longmapsto
\mathcal W_{2,\ell}-\mathcal W_{1,\ell}(B).
$$
Combining these changes with section 2 proves
$$
\delta\mathcal A_{\ell;hh}=0.
$$
The corresponding mixed formula follows with $B=\mathcal L_\xi h_b$ when varying the other parent, as derived and component-checked in the gravitational-response note.

“Proper” here means that the diffeomorphism preserves the prescribed physical frame and data. It must also preserve the declared analytic source/response domain so that all displayed boundary limits exist. This is a precise domain condition, not a claim about arbitrary coordinate changes with uncontrolled endpoint growth.

The linear parent amplitudes used for normalization are invariant under the same proper transformations. Hence the dimensionless coupling is invariant as well.

## 4. Reconstruction and hypersurface independence

Two parent reconstructions representing the same physical parents and differing by an admissible proper gauge transformation are covered by section 3. A Kerr-parameter change, an added physical radiative parent or a change of extraction frame is different input and is not declared an ambiguity.

The coefficient is defined directly by the source projection and the quadratic extraction. It therefore does not require selecting a second-order metric reconstruction. Whenever a compatible sourced reconstruction exists, the normalized Green/CPS relation proves that its extracted selected coefficient equals this same functional. The [outgoing residual-kernel theorem](chiral-reconstruction-proof.md) also rules out hidden chiral Einstein residuals once the outgoing curvature response exists in its class.

A spacetime coordinate change to a hyperboloidal slice pulls back the test, source and density together. Their opposite mode phases cancel. For $t=\tau+h(r)$ and the corresponding azimuthal shift, the per-unit-time volume pairing
$\Sigma H^{ab}F_{ab}\,dt\,dr\,d\Omega_{\rm ang}$
has the same radial integral. In the current formulation, the source term and any side flux are included as in the gravitational-response note. The result does not assert conservation of a bare $\Omega_\Sigma(H,k)$ for $Ek=F$.

Within the common analytic covering, contour deformations preserve the numerator: the source-current connector terms vanish by the endpoint estimates, and the incoming Green-kernel tail is integrable. Thus the selected functional is independent of the admitted slice/contour representation, not of arbitrary finite endpoint subtractions.

These are distinct observables and must not be equated. The public comparison is numerical evidence with angular/radial refinement, not a certified infinite-sum theorem.

## 6. Selection rules and the remaining stronger problem

Axial covariance gives $m_Q=m_b+m_c$ in this complex channel. Conjugate channels use the corresponding signed frequencies and azimuthal numbers. Equatorial reflection requires matching the physical reflection eigenvalue of the completed observable to the product of the parent eigenvalues; it exchanges spin-weighted strain with its conjugate, so a scalar even-$\ell$ mnemonic is insufficient. Rotation does not impose a spherical angular-momentum triangle rule.

The selected coefficient is now constructed, with its source normalization and invariances proved under stated domain conditions. A **full metric as a convergent infinite daughter expansion** is a stronger existence problem. It remains unproved here. It should not be confused with a missing definition of the selected functional above, nor should this selected result be promoted to that stronger theorem.
