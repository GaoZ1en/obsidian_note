# A common analytic domain for local QNM products

## Result

Finite differential products of selected outgoing QNMs admit a common decaying complex contour under the explicit frequency conditions below. This proves a useful closure and endpoint lemma. It does not, by itself, construct the inverse Einstein operator or prove convergence of an infinite angular-mode sum.

The distinction is necessary: a source can satisfy these bounds while an inverse introduces an incoming homogeneous branch, a zero mode, or a nonlocal reconstruction term requiring a separate boundary analysis.

## 1. Domain and finite differential algebra

Set $M=1$, $|a|<1$, $r_\pm=1\pm\sqrt{1-a^2}$. Work on the logarithmic cover of a complex radial neighbourhood with $\operatorname{Re}r>1$, avoiding $r_+$ itself. This region excludes the inner horizon and the zeros $r=\pm ia\cos\theta$ of the Kerr angular denominators for real $\theta$.

Near $z=r-r_+=0$, take modes with Frobenius form
$$
R_j=z^{-s_j-i\sigma_j}H_j(z),\qquad
\sigma_j=\frac{2r_+\omega_j-am_j}{r_+-r_-},
$$
where $H_j$ is holomorphic there. Finite radial or angular derivatives, multiplication, and contractions with the Kerr metric or tetrad produce finite sums of terms bounded by
$$
C|z^{-P-i\Sigma}(\log z)^q|,
\qquad \Sigma=\sum_j\sigma_j,
$$
for finite integers $P\geq0$, $q\geq0$. The local finite-order Einstein source and CCK differential operators have this property when applied in a regular angular patch. Smooth spin-weighted sections must be treated in two angular patches; a coordinate factor $\csc\theta$ alone is not evidence of a physical pole.

The statement concerns a fixed finite list of differentiated fields, currents and superpotentials. It is not a bound uniform in the number of derivatives or in all QNM labels.

## 2. Horizon estimate

Along the spiral
$$
z=\rho e^{(1+i\kappa)u},\qquad u\longrightarrow-\infty,
\quad 0<\rho<\sqrt{1-a^2},
$$
the logarithm is continued as $\log z=\log\rho+(1+i\kappa)u$. Hence
$$
|z^{-P-i\Sigma}|=C_\rho
\exp\{(-P+\operatorname{Im}\Sigma+\kappa\operatorname{Re}\Sigma)u\}.
$$
An endpoint term vanishes if
$$
\operatorname{Im}\Sigma+\kappa\operatorname{Re}\Sigma>P.
$$
An integral containing $dr$ converges already when the right side is $P-1$. Logarithms contribute only a polynomial in $|u|$, so strict inequalities suffice.

If every phase sum in a finite list has $\operatorname{Re}\Sigma>0$, choose
$$
\kappa>\max_{\text{list}}
\frac{P-\operatorname{Im}\Sigma}{\operatorname{Re}\Sigma}.
$$
All of its endpoint terms then vanish on one contour. A radial derivative raises a finite pole order; it does not invalidate the existence of a common $\kappa$.

For the $a=0.3$ parent computed in [spin2-benchmark.md](spin2-benchmark.md), the double-parent phase is
$$
2\sigma=1.08964937968116646221-0.35938907471554720609i.
$$
Thus a source term of pole order $P$ is controlled by
$\kappa>(P+0.359390)/1.089649$, with rounded constants used conservatively. The successful $\kappa=3$ radial-norm calculation must not be reused for an arbitrary higher-order source without checking $P$.

## 3. Infinity and deformation

In a sector on which the chosen analytic outgoing branches have the form
$$
R_j=e^{i\omega_jr}r^{\nu_j}
\left(\sum_{k=0}^{N}a_{jk}r^{-k}+O(r^{-N-1})\right),
$$
a finite differential product is an exponential with frequency sum $W=\sum_j\omega_j$, times finite powers and logarithms. On $r=R_0+iy$, it decays exponentially if $\operatorname{Re}W>0$. This dominates every finite power generated by the local differential operators.

For holomorphic integrands satisfying these estimates uniformly during a contour deformation, Cauchy's theorem applies first at finite endpoints. The connecting pieces then vanish in the limit. At the spiral end, their winding length adds at most another power of $|u|$; the strictly positive exponential rate still controls it. The same logarithmic continuation must be used throughout.

This proves contour independence inside this admissible class. It does not identify contours with different monodromy, justify taking complex conjugates at complex $r$, or permit crossing a singularity. Nor does it prove that regularity of an outgoing field on a real hyperboloid makes its reflected bilinear pairing integrable there.

## 4. What the lemma closes and what it leaves open

The local quadratic Einstein source inherits the finite-pole property from reconstructed first-order modes. Pairings with finite differential test modes are controlled when their phase sums satisfy the two positivity conditions. Integration by parts between local differential expressions is then legitimate, provided all terms being integrated are included in the finite list. The source-current correction in [gravitational-response.md](gravitational-response.md) is still present; endpoint decay cannot remove that bulk current.

The radial Green kernel has both outgoing and incoming components away from a pole. In products with an outgoing source, their exponentials can cancel. The resulting algebraic tail must be checked from the actual source, rather than by the exponential estimate alone. A source-dependent metric corrector is also obtained by transport integration, not merely by taking finite derivatives.

[Casals--Hollands--Pound--Toomani, Theorem 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.15468v2) constructs an Einstein Green operator with its corrector for conserved sources supported to the future or past of a compact set. The theorem does not directly cover eternal exponentially growing QNM sources. Their remarks also identify non-Bondi asymptotics of the retarded radiation-gauge metric. Consequently that theorem cannot replace the continuation and waveform-extraction checks needed here.

The [rotating source calculation](kerr-quadratic.md) adds an exact regularized form for the DD source. The outer-horizon singularity cancels in the conjugated radial differential operator, and its remaining rational angular coefficients have a uniformly convergent series for $|x|\leq1$ at subextremal spin. This is an explicit source construction, still distinct from a bound on the nonlocal sourced metric reconstruction.

**Verified:** the finite differential closure and decay estimates follow from the displayed local forms; Mathematica checks the complex horizon exponent. The exact Schwarzschild source cancellation is separately checked in `verification/schwarzschild-source-horizon.wl`; the rotating source's prefactor and generic algebraic identities are checked in the linked follow-up.

**Assumptions:** fixed finite derivative order; smooth angular sections; holomorphic radial coefficients with only finite poles; specified sectorial outgoing branches; positive real phase sums; fixed logarithmic continuation.

The [Hertz inverse](hertz-metric-reconstruction.md) now extends the endpoint analysis to a specific transport inverse for each separated DD daughter. The [chiral identity](chiral-source-identity.md) allows its source corrector to vanish. These statements do not provide an estimate uniform in every angular daughter.

**Not verified:** an untruncated sourced Kerr metric satisfying every Einstein component globally; a completed normalized metric-CPS/Teukolsky map on that domain; an infinite-mode completion or the full hyperboloidal Hilbert domain. This lemma is progress on the original rr-004 domain gate, not its replacement by a weaker success criterion.
