# Maxwell--Gravity Corner Comparison

## Direct Verdict

The structural analogy is useful but not an identification:

$$\begin{align}
(E_\perp,\lambda_{\mathrm{rel}})_{\mathrm{Max}well} \quad\leftrightarrow\quad (\text{area density},\text{relative boost})_{\mathrm{gravity}}
\end{align}$$

only at the level “matching charge paired with a relative frame.” The actions, gauge groups, constraints and physical extensions are different.

## Maxwell Side

- charge density: electric flux $E_\perp$;
- frame: relative $U(1)$ gauge trivialization $\lambda_{\mathrm{rel}}$;
- origin: integration by parts of the null Maxwell potential plus diagonal cut-gauge reduction;
- raw smooth bulk: no additional corner oscillator;
- fixed-flux sector: the pair drops out of the reduced two-form.

## Gravity Side

Chandrasekaran--Flanagan construct a phase space on two intersecting null surfaces and, after extending the initial data by half-sided boosts, obtain a relative boost angle canonically paired with an area variable. Their extension can include Weyl shocks and is not a mere rewriting of a globally smooth metric sector.

Ciambelli--Freidel--Leigh instead construct a kinematical single-null gravitational Poisson structure before imposing the Raychaudhuri and Damour constraints. Their result organizes spin-0, spin-1 and spin-2 null data but does not supply the finite two-surface composition theorem needed here.

## Safe Correspondence

| Maxwell | Gravity | precise common role |
|---|---|---|
| $U(1)$ frame | null-normal boost frame | relational choice between two sides |
| electric flux | area/boost charge density | Hamiltonian moment paired with frame shift |
| Gauss transport | Raychaudhuri/Damour transport | null constraints with corner integration data |
| flux matching | corner geometric matching | necessary composition constraint |

## Non-Correspondences

- Maxwell electric flux is not gravitational area.
- A smooth Maxwell gauge transition need not create a shock; a half-sided gravitational boost may enlarge the solution class to include Weyl shocks.
- Maxwell has a linear Abelian Gauss law; gravity has field-dependent diffeomorphism symmetry and nonlinear null constraints.
- The Maxwell moment map calculation does not prove the gravitational corner symplectic form.

## Research Use

The Maxwell result supplies a controlled diagnostic for gravity:

1. derive the raw null gravitational potential;
2. identify relative normal frames only after splitting the sheets;
3. determine which matching equation is a moment map;
4. retain all additional metric compatibility equations;
5. state whether the relative boost belongs to the smooth sector or an extended shock sector.

Gravity remains outside the present main calculation.
