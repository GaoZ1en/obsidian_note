# Canonical, Weyl and Fock Quantization

## 1. Direct Verdict

The benchmark has two distinct quantum layers:

1. a representation-independent reduced Weyl/CCR algebra for the radiative master solution space;
2. a stationary Fock representation selected by the positive spectrum of \(\partial_t\) on the complete ultrastatic background.

The finite characteristic diamond supplies neither a new vacuum nor two sheetwise Fock spaces. The global stationary complex structure is pulled back nonlocally to the compatible two-sheet data.

The main global Fock theorem is restricted to

$$
\boxed{Q_E=0,\qquad n=0.}
$$

Its Hilbert space is the symmetric Fock space of the two \(\ell\geq1\) polarization towers. A fixed nonzero \((Q_E,n)\) may be discussed only as a fluctuation theory around a non-finite-energy background, after an explicit background-energy subtraction; it is not a normalizable vector in the main radiative vacuum representation. An optional \(\ell=0\) frame--flux representation is a separate extended theory, not part of the radiative oscillator tower.

## 2. Abstract Reduced Weyl Algebra

Let \(\mathcal S_E^{\mathrm{rad}}\) be the real radiative finite-energy
solution space in the zero-flux/zero-Chern sector, equation (2.5) of
`12-global-characteristic-completion.md`, with Schwartz spectral core, and let \(\Omega\) be the
global/half-cross form proved equal there.

The abstract Weyl algebra is generated by \(W(s)\), \(s\in\mathcal S_E^{\mathrm{rad}}\), with

$$
W(s)^*=W(-s),
$$

$$
W(s)W(s')
=\exp\!\left[-\frac i2\Omega(s,s')\right]W(s+s').
\tag{2.1}
$$

Equivalently one may label the algebra by compactly supported master test functions and use the Peierls causal form derived in `10-peierls-and-propagator-check.md`. The two presentations are isomorphic through the causal propagator.

This B1 algebra needs no vacuum, complex structure, density matrix, or tensor-product factorization of the two sheets.

## 3. Stationary Complex Structure

For the mode algebra, $m$ may be read as an orthonormal real degeneracy label. In the conventional complex $Y_{\ell m}$ basis the equivalent reality relation is imposed and full kernels contain $Y_{\ell m}(x)Y_{\ell m}^*(x')$. The positive-frequency modes are

$$
U^P_{k\ell m}
=\frac{e^{-i\omega_{k\ell}t+ikz}}{\sqrt{4\pi\omega_{k\ell}}}
Y_{\ell m},
\qquad
\omega_{k\ell}=\sqrt{k^2+\lambda_\ell}.
$$

With the Lee--Wald convention \(\Omega(U,U^*)=-i\), the positive compatible complex structure is

$$
J U^P_{k\ell m}=-iU^P_{k\ell m},
\qquad
J U^{P*}_{k\ell m}=+iU^{P*}_{k\ell m}.
\tag{3.1}
$$

Then

$$
\mu(s,s'):=\Omega(s,Js')
\tag{3.2}
$$

is positive on real nonzero radiative solutions.

Let \(T_X\) and \(S_X\) be the direct sums of the explicit two-sheet analysis
and synthesis maps (2.1), (3.1)--(3.2) of
`13-characteristic-complex-structure.md`.  On the classical energy profile
space the characteristic complex structure is the literal operator

$$
\boxed{J_{X,E}=S_X(-i)T_X=R_XJR_X^{-1}.}
\tag{3.3}
$$

It is nonlocal along and between the two sheets. If

$$
a_I=-i\Omega_X(U_I^*,\Phi),
$$

then its mode action is

$$
(J_{X,E}\Phi)|_X
=\sum_{P,\ell,m}\int dk\left[
-ia_I(U_I|_X)+ia_I^*(U_I^*|_X)
\right].
\tag{3.4}
$$

The explicit profile action is

$$
T_X(J_{X,E}\Phi)=-iT_X\Phi,
\tag{3.5}
$$

and, sector by sector,

$$
\begin{aligned}
(J_{X,E}h)_r(s)
=\sum_{q=\pm}\int_0^\infty dy\int_{\mathbb R}
\frac{dk}{2\pi\omega_k}\big[&
\gamma_q\sin(\gamma_qy-\gamma_rs)h_q(y)\\
&+\cos(\gamma_qy-\gamma_rs)\partial_yh_q(y)
\big].
\end{aligned}
\tag{3.6}
$$

Here \(h_- = f\), \(h_+=g\), \(\gamma_-=\alpha\), and \(\gamma_+=\beta\).
The oscillatory kernel and its endpoint interpretation are derived on the
Schwartz/classical energy core in calculation 13.  In particular, the
off-diagonal \(q\neq r\) terms mix the two sheets; (3.3) is a literal
profile-space formula on that domain.

The quantum one-particle space is instead the abstract completion transported
from \(L^2(dk)\).  If \(U_{X,1}\) denotes the completed core synthesis
isometry, then

$$
\boxed{J_{X,1}=U_{X,1}(-i)U_{X,1}^{-1}.}
\tag{3.7}
$$

Formula (3.6) determines this bounded operator by one-particle-norm
continuity; it is not interpreted as an ordinary distributional
sheet-by-sheet kernel action on every one-particle vector.  Generic vectors
need not possess ordinary distribution-valued individual sheet profiles, and
the corner condition \(f(0)=g(0)\) belongs only to the smooth/classical energy
domain.  Tangential derivative and gauge-invariant radiative/field-strength
profiles have improved low-null-frequency behavior, consistently with the
observable choice in this quantization; no additional Sobolev claim is made.

## 4. Creation and Annihilation Operators

Quantize the stationary coefficients by

$$
\widehat\Phi
=\sum_{P,\ell,m}\int dk\left[
a^P_{k\ell m}U^P_{k\ell m}
+a^{P\dagger}_{k\ell m}U^{P*}_{k\ell m}
\right].
\tag{4.1}
$$

The action-derived projection formulas are

$$
\boxed{
a_I=-i\Omega_X(U_I^*,\widehat\Phi),
\qquad
a_I^\dagger=i\Omega_X(U_I,\widehat\Phi).}
\tag{4.2}
$$

Together with (5.8) of the normalization note, the CCR are

$$
[a^P_{k\ell m},a^{P'\dagger}_{k'\ell'm'}]
=\delta_{PP'}\delta_{\ell\ell'}\delta_{mm'}\delta(k-k'),
\tag{4.3}
$$

$$
[a_I,a_J]=[a_I^\dagger,a_J^\dagger]=0.
$$

Classically, with the inverse of the project convention \(\Omega=\delta\pi\wedge\delta q\),

$$
\{a_I,a_J^*\}=-i\delta(I,J),
\tag{4.4}
$$

and (4.3) follows from \([\ ,\ ]=i\{\ ,\ \}\).

## 5. One-Particle and Fock Spaces

For the main sector \(Q_E=n=0\), the one-particle Hilbert space is

$$
\mathcal H_1
=\bigoplus_{P=E,B}\bigoplus_{\ell=1}^{\infty}
\bigoplus_{m=-\ell}^{\ell}L^2(\mathbb R,dk).
\tag{5.1}
$$

The Hamiltonian domain carries the additional \(\omega_{k\ell}\) weight. The radiative Hilbert space is

$$
\boxed{\mathcal H_{\mathrm{rad}}=\Gamma_s(\mathcal H_1).}
\tag{5.2}
$$

This direct sum is over angular momentum and polarization, not over the two null sheets. Both sheets are already present in each normalized mode.
Equation (5.1) is the spectral realization of the one-particle space; it does
not assert that every vector has two ordinary distribution-valued sheet
profiles.

## 6. Hamiltonian and CPS Generator Check

The reduced Noether energy is

$$
H_{\partial_t}^{\mathrm{rad}}
=\frac12\sum_{P,\ell,m}\int dz\left[
(\partial_t\Phi^P_{\ell m})^2
+(\partial_z\Phi^P_{\ell m})^2
+\lambda_\ell(\Phi^P_{\ell m})^2
\right].
\tag{6.1}
$$

The on-shell dispersion relation cancels the \(aa\) and \(a^*a^*\) terms, giving classically

$$
H_{\partial_t}^{\mathrm{rad}}
=\sum_{P,\ell,m}\int dk\,
\omega_{k\ell}a^{P*}_{k\ell m}a^P_{k\ell m},
\tag{6.2}
$$

exactly in the orthonormal real harmonic convention used for the action reduction. Quantum mechanically,

$$
:\widehat H_{\partial_t}^{\mathrm{rad}}:
=\sum_{P,\ell,m}\int dk\,
\omega_{k\ell}a^{P\dagger}_{k\ell m}a^P_{k\ell m}.
\tag{6.3}
$$

With \(\Omega=\delta\pi\wedge\delta q\), the CPS Hamilton equation is

$$
\iota_{\delta_{\partial_t}}\Omega=-\delta H_{\partial_t}.
\tag{6.4}
$$

Indeed,

$$
\{a_{k\ell m},H_{\partial_t}\}
=-i\omega_{k\ell}a_{k\ell m},
$$

which is exactly the active \(\partial_t\) action on the positive-frequency mode coefficient. This verifies that (6.1) is the CPS generator, not merely a diagonal quadratic form guessed from the field equation.

## 7. Vacuum and Two-Point Functions

The stationary vacuum is defined by

$$
a^P_{k\ell m}|0\rangle=0.
$$

For master coefficients in the orthonormal real harmonic basis,

$$
\langle0|\widehat\Phi^P_{\ell m}(t,z)
\widehat\Phi^{P'}_{\ell'm'}(t',z')|0\rangle
=\delta_{PP'}\delta_{\ell\ell'}\delta_{mm'}
 W^+_{\lambda_\ell}(t-t',z-z').
\tag{7.1}
$$

In the standard complex basis, the equivalent full angular kernel is

$$
\sum_{m=-\ell}^{\ell}
Y_{\ell m}(x)Y_{\ell m}^*(x')W^+_{\lambda_\ell}(t-t',z-z').
\tag{7.1a}
$$

Here

$$
W^+_\lambda(\tau,\xi)
=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{dk}{4\pi\sqrt{k^2+\lambda}}
e^{-i\sqrt{k^2+\lambda}(\tau-i0)+ik\xi}
$$

$$
=\frac1{2\pi}K_0\!\left(
\sqrt\lambda\sqrt{\xi^2-(\tau-i0)^2}
\right).
\tag{7.2}
$$

The Maxwell field-strength Wightman functions follow by applying the differential maps (5.3)--(5.4) of `06-s2-harmonic-master-reduction.md` to both arguments. This gives a gauge-independent answer.

The commutator is

$$
[\widehat\Phi(x),\widehat\Phi(x')]
=\int dk\,[U_k(x)U_k^*(x')-U_k^*(x)U_k(x')],
\tag{7.3}
$$

and is compared with the causal propagator in the next note.

## 8. Regional B1 Versus Global B2

The finite characteristic diamond supports the restriction of the global Weyl algebra and the pullback of the global quasifree state. It does not choose \(J\) by itself. Equations (3.3)--(3.6) use the full stationary background and therefore contain B2 state input.

Precisely:

- the abstract Weyl algebra (2.1) is B1 and needs only the real symplectic space;
- the Fock representation, vacuum and Wightman function need the global stationary \(J\);
- restricting the vacuum to a finite characteristic region does not produce a sharp Hilbert tensor factorization;
- regional marginals cannot reconstruct arbitrary global cross covariance.

## 9. Zero-Mode Alternatives

In the main global finite-energy theorem,

$$
\boxed{Q_E=n=0,
\qquad
\mathcal H_{0,0}=\mathcal H_{\mathrm{rad}}.}
$$

Nonzero constant electric flux and nonzero magnetic Chern background both have nonzero constant energy density along the noncompact \(z\)-line, hence infinite global energy. If those backgrounds are retained in an appendix, the radiative fluctuation algebra may be represented after subtracting the background energy and keeping the sector label explicit. A formal direct integral

$$
\int_{\mathbb R}^{\oplus}dQ_E\,\mathcal H_{Q_E,n},
$$

without introducing a conjugate frame operator is then extra representation input, not part of the main Fock theorem. If an extended relative frame is deliberately retained, its Schrödinger/angle--flux representation is described in `zero-modes-and-superselection.md`. Neither choice changes the radiative operators (4.1), and neither background is a normalizable vector obtained by acting on \(|0\rangle\).

## 10. Weyl Composition Corollary

The weak symplectic three-cell reconstruction of `14-three-cell-symplectic-composition-check.md` induces, functorially,

$$
W(c)\longmapsto W(Gc)
$$

as a canonical \(*\)-isomorphism between the Weyl algebra of the reduced matched profile space and the Weyl algebra of the glued master solution space. The two parenthesizations induce the same map because their reconstruction maps agree. This is a B1 algebraic statement, not a Hilbert tensor-product factorization and not a claim of Fock implementability; no Shale--Stinespring condition is asserted.
