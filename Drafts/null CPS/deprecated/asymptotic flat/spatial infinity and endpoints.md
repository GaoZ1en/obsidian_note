# Spatial infinity，null endpoints and timelike data

## 0. Scope and verdict

Stage 7 is not another radial order。It is a second renormalization problem at the corners

$$\begin{align}
\mathscr I^+_-\sim i^0\sim\mathscr I^-_+, \qquad \mathscr I^+_+\sim i^+, \qquad \mathscr I^-_-\sim i^-.
\end{align}$$

The sharp conclusions are：

1. Separate Bondi expansions at fixed $u$ and separate late-time expansions at fixed $r$ do not define a corner space。A jointly polyhomogeneous blown-up space is required。

2. The generic gravitational tail

$$\begin{align}
C_{AB} = C_{AB}^{\pm} + \frac{C_{AB}^{(1)\pm}}{u} + O\left(\frac{\log|u|}{u^2}\right)
\end{align}$$

   leaves the fundamental Ashtekar--Streubel two-form and energy flux finite。

3. The same tail makes the superrotation soft moment

$$\begin{align}
\int du\,uN_{AB}
\end{align}$$

   logarithmically divergent。It can be minimally subtracted only after choosing an endpoint scale and tail corner data。The finite result is scheme dependent by the logarithmic soft coefficient。

4. Antipodal matching is not a consequence of the null equations alone。For Maxwell and ordinary gravitational BMS data it follows from：
   - an Ashtekar--Hansen-type extension through spatial directions；
   - specified regularity at the null boundaries of the $i^0$ cylinder；
   - the field equations；
   - a totally fluxless restriction。

5. A Lagrange-multiplier corner action can impose matching，but then matching is an added boundary equation。It does not explain why the physical scattering sector should satisfy the required regularity。

6. Massive data live on the timelike hyperboloids $H^3_\pm$。A massive final state produces a matter-dependent logarithmic drag contribution to the asymptotic symplectic structure。It cannot be completed by a purely null gravitational corner scheme。

Thus there is a well-defined conditional massless matched sector，but no action-first construction presently proves a maximal generic gravity--massive-matter scattering phase space。The precise unresolved object is a coupled

$$\begin{align}
i^-\cup\mathscr I^-\cup i^0\cup\mathscr I^+\cup i^+
\end{align}$$

relative action whose corner first variation is finite and whose boundary equations generate，rather than assume，the matching conditions。

## 1. Blow up the corner before taking limits

### 1.1 Boundary defining functions

Near a null--spatial corner introduce

$$\begin{align}
\Omega=\frac1r, \qquad \rho=\frac1{|u|}.
\end{align}$$

The compactified quadrant has boundary faces

$$\begin{align}
\{\Omega=0,\rho>0\}=\mathscr I, \qquad \{\rho=0,\Omega>0\}=\text{spatial approach},
\end{align}$$

meeting at

$$\begin{align}
\{\Omega=\rho=0\}.
\end{align}$$

The real blow-up replaces the pointwise corner by its space of approach directions。In the Ashtekar--Hansen description the spatial directions form a timelike unit hyperboloid，with null boundaries corresponding to the limiting cuts of $\mathscr I^\pm$。See [Prabhu--Shehzad](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.04305) for the covariant spatial-infinity phase-space construction。

### 1.2 Joint polyhomogeneous histories

At every fixed bidegree window define

$$\begin{align}
\boxed{ \Phi \sim
\sum_{(p,q,k,\ell)\in\mathcal E_{\rm corner}} \Omega^p\rho^q (\log\Omega)^k (\log\rho)^\ell \Phi_{p,q,k,\ell}(x).
}
\end{align}$$

The index family must satisfy：

1. local finiteness below every pair of real weights；
2. finite log caps at each $(p,q)$；
3. closure under lowering $k,\ell$；
4. closure under the Minkowski sums generated by the nonlinear field equations；
5. conormal control by

$$\begin{align}
\Omega\partial_\Omega, \qquad \rho\partial_\rho, \qquad D_A；
\end{align}$$

6. the same family for tangent variations；
7. uniform remainders，not merely pointwise asymptotic series on each face。

The full asymptotic analysis of linear radiation fields on blown-up asymptotically Minkowski spaces supplies the relevant analytic model；see [Baskin--Vasy--Wunsch](https://arxiv.org/abs/1212.5141)。

### 1.3 Why two separate expansions are insufficient

Consider

$$\begin{align}
f(\Omega,\rho) = \frac{\Omega}{\Omega+\rho}.
\end{align}$$

Then

$$\begin{align}
\lim_{\rho\to0}
\lim_{\Omega\to0}
f =0, \qquad \lim_{\Omega\to0} \lim_{\rho\to0} f =1.
\end{align}$$

Thus each one-variable limit exists，but the corner restriction does not。This function depends nontrivially on the projective ratio $\Omega/\rho$ and is not jointly polyhomogeneous on the unblown corner with a common coefficient system。

### Proposition 1.1

Let $\Phi$ have a joint expansion with nonnegative leading real weights and a remainder uniform under the two Euler vector fields。If the face restrictions exist，then

$$\begin{align}
\boxed{ \iota_{\rho=0}^* \iota_{\Omega=0}^*\Phi = \iota_{\Omega=0}^* \iota_{\rho=0}^*\Phi = \Phi_{0,0,0,0}.
}
\end{align}$$

This is coefficientwise and does not use the EOM。Terms with negative weights or nonzero logs at bidegree $(0,0)$ must first be renormalized；their finite parts can depend on the order and scale。

## 2. Endpoint index family

At each end of null infinity introduce a sign label $\sigma=\pm$ and require

$$\begin{aligned}
C_{AB}(u,x) &= C_{AB}^{\sigma}(x) + \frac{C_{AB}^{(1)\sigma}(x)}{u} + O_{\rm con} \left( \frac{\log|u|}{u^2} \right), \\
N_{AB}(u,x) &= -\frac{C_{AB}^{(1)\sigma}(x)}{u^2} + O_{\rm con} \left( \frac{\log|u|}{u^3} \right), \\
\varphi(u,x) &= \varphi^\sigma(x) + \frac{\varphi^{(1)\sigma}(x)}{u} + O_{\rm con} \left( \frac{\log|u|}{u^2} \right), \\
a_A(u,x) &= a_A^\sigma(x) + \frac{a_A^{(1)\sigma}(x)}{u} + O_{\rm con} \left( \frac{\log|u|}{u^2} \right), \\
F_{uA}^{(0)} &= -\frac{a_A^{(1)\sigma}}{u^2} + O_{\rm con} \left( \frac{\log|u|}{u^3} \right), \\
M(u,x) &= M^\sigma(x)+O_{\rm con}(|u|^{-1}\log^K|u|).
\end{aligned}$$

The constants

$$\begin{align}
C_{AB}^+-C_{AB}^-, \qquad \varphi^+-\varphi^-, \qquad a_A^+-a_A^-
\end{align}$$

are memory data。They are not set to zero。

All endpoint coefficients are independent off shell。The displayed derivative relations for $N_{AB}$ and $F_{uA}^{(0)}$ are kinematical definitions，not equations of motion。

The $1/u$ shear is the tail-to-memory behavior used by Geiller--Laddha--Zwikel to describe generic four-dimensional scattering without peeling；their analysis shows that the flux remains finite while the soft superrotation moment develops a logarithm。[Geiller--Laddha--Zwikel](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.07978)

## 3. Endpoint finiteness of the fundamental radiative sector

### 3.1 Gravity

The gravitational radiative form is

$$\begin{align}
\Omega_{\mathscr I}^{\rm grav} = \frac1{32\pi G} \int_{\mathscr I} du\,d^2x\sqrt q\, \delta N^{AB}\wedge\delta C_{AB}.
\end{align}$$

At either endpoint，

$$\begin{align}
\delta N^{AB}\wedge\delta C_{AB} = O(|u|^{-2}\log^K|u|).
\end{align}$$

Therefore

$$\begin{align}
\int^{\infty}du\, \delta N^{AB}\wedge\delta C_{AB}
\end{align}$$

converges。The energy flux is even smaller：

$$\begin{align}
\int^\infty du\,N_{AB}N^{AB} = O(U^{-3}\log^K U).
\end{align}$$

### 3.2 Scalar and Maxwell

Likewise，

$$\begin{align}
\delta\dot\varphi\wedge\delta\varphi = O(|u|^{-2}\log^K|u|),
\end{align}$$

and

$$\begin{align}
\delta F_u^{A(0)}\wedge\delta a_A = O(|u|^{-2}\log^K|u|).
\end{align}$$

Hence the complete massless form

$$\begin{align}
\Omega_{\mathscr I}^{\rm coupled} = \Omega_{\mathscr I}^{\rm grav} +\Omega_{\mathscr I}^{\phi} +\Omega_{\mathscr I}^{\rm M}
\end{align}$$

is finite on the endpoint class of section 2。

### 3.3 Radial counterterms at the endpoint

The Stage 3 gravitational divergent counterterms vanish after the displayed radial Einstein equations are imposed。The Stage 6 scalar radial logarithm and its cut counterterm cancel coefficientwise before the $u$ limit。For a joint polyhomogeneous family with uniform remainders，Proposition 1.1 then permits the radial renormalized finite part to be restricted to $\rho=0$。

This proves：

> The generic $1/u$ tail does not by itself make the fundamental null radiative action variation or symplectic form divergent。

It does not prove that the complete off-shell Einstein--matter action on a neighborhood containing $i^0$ is finite。The missing terms live on the spatial face and at the blown-up corner，which are absent from a single Bondi collar。

## 4. The relative corner variational problem

Let $R$ and $U$ be radial and endpoint regulators。After radial renormalization write

$$\begin{aligned}
\delta S_{R,U}^{\rm ren} =& \int_{M_{R,U}}E\delta\Phi
\\
& +\Theta_{\mathscr I^+_U} +\Theta_{\mathscr I^-_U} +\Theta_{i^0_{R,U}} +\Theta_{i^+_U} +\Theta_{i^-_U}.
\end{aligned}$$

A global action exists only if the limit of the total corner one-form is finite and admits the chosen polarization。Define the residual obstruction class by

$$\begin{align}
\boxed{ \mathfrak O_{\rm corner} := \left[ \lim_{R,U\to\infty} \left( \Theta_{\mathscr I^+_U} +\Theta_{\mathscr I^-_U} +\Theta_{i^0_{R,U}} +\Theta_{i^+_U} +\Theta_{i^-_U} \right) \right] \Big/ \left( \delta B+dC \right).
}
\end{align}$$

The quotient is taken in the declared local corner-counterterm category。The full off-shell variational principle is well posed only if

$$\begin{align}
\mathfrak O_{\rm corner}=0
\end{align}$$

or if its nonzero part is assigned a boundary polarization with additional canonical variables。

The null calculation of section 3 proves only that the radiative restriction of this class is finite。It does not set the spatial or timelike components to zero。

## 5. What antipodal matching actually assumes

Let

$$\begin{align}
\mathcal A:S^2\longrightarrow S^2
\end{align}$$

be the antipodal map connecting a future-directed null direction at $\mathscr I^-$ to the corresponding outgoing direction at $\mathscr I^+$ through $i^0$。

### 5.1 Maxwell

Use an oriented electric density $\mathcal E$ so that no coordinate-sign convention is hidden。The matching condition is

$$\begin{align}
\boxed{ \mathcal E^+_{i^0} = \mathcal A^*\mathcal E^-_{i^0}.
}
\end{align}$$

Prabhu's construction does not infer this from an arbitrary pair of null solutions。It：

1. enlarges spatial infinity to a manifold of spatial and null directions；
2. imposes regularity of the rescaled Maxwell field at the null boundaries of that manifold；
3. uses Maxwell equations there；
4. restricts to the totally fluxless symmetry algebra。

Under those conditions the relevant fields and charges match。[Prabhu](https://arxiv.org/abs/1808.07863)

### 5.2 Gravitational supermomentum

The analogous datum is the relevant electric component of the rescaled Weyl tensor，denoted schematically by $\mathcal E_{\rm W}$：

$$\begin{align}
\boxed{ \mathcal E_{{\rm W},i^0}^+ = \mathcal A^* \mathcal E_{{\rm W},i^0}^-.
}
\end{align}$$

Again the logical chain is

$$\begin{align}
\text{Ashtekar--Hansen extension} + \text{null-direction regularity} + \text{Einstein equations} + \text{fluxless sector}
\Longrightarrow
\text{matching}.
\end{align}$$

This is the content of the supermomentum theorem of [Prabhu](https://arxiv.org/abs/1902.08200)。

### 5.3 Lorentz charges

Lorentz matching requires additional asymptotic conditions beyond the basic Ashtekar--Hansen definition。Prabhu--Shehzad prove it for an explicitly restricted class，and state separately the additional requirements at timelike infinity needed for a complete scattering conservation law。[Prabhu--Shehzad](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.04900)

Therefore：

$$\begin{align}
\boxed{ \text{antipodal matching is a conditional regularity theorem，not a universal null EOM identity}.
}
\end{align}$$

## 6. Three inequivalent ways to use matching

### 6.1 Restrict the solution space

Define

$$\begin{align}
\mathcal S_{\rm match} = \left\{ (\Phi_-,\Phi_+)
\in \mathcal S_{\mathscr I^-} \times \mathcal S_{\mathscr I^+} : \Phi_+^{i^0} = \mathcal A^*\Phi_-^{i^0} \right\}.
\end{align}$$

This is a symplectic reduction only when the total symplectic flux through spatial infinity vanishes。

### 6.2 Add a boundary equation

Introduce a corner multiplier $\Lambda$ and write schematically

$$\begin{align}
S_{i^0}^{\rm glue} = \int_{S^2} \Lambda \left( \Phi_+^{i^0} -\mathcal A^*\Phi_-^{i^0} \right).
\end{align}$$

Then

$$\begin{align}
\frac{\delta S_{i^0}^{\rm glue}}{\delta\Lambda}=0
\end{align}$$

imposes matching。Variation with respect to the two face data supplies the opposite corner momenta。

This makes matching an action-derived equation，but only because a new corner field and a new term were added。It does not derive the regularity assumptions that motivated the equation。

### 6.3 Treat matching as a scattering map

One may instead seek a PDE scattering map

$$\begin{align}
\mathscr S: \mathcal D_{\mathscr I^-} \longrightarrow \mathcal D_{\mathscr I^+}
\end{align}$$

whose asymptotics automatically satisfy the matching relation。This is analytically strongest and presently not available for the full generic Einstein--massive-matter class。

The three constructions must not be conflated。

## 7. The logarithmic superrotation moment

For

$$\begin{align}
C_{AB} = C_{AB}^+ + \frac{C_{AB}^{(1)+}}u+\cdots,
\end{align}$$

one has

$$\begin{align}
N_{AB} = -\frac{C_{AB}^{(1)+}}{u^2}+\cdots.
\end{align}$$

The fundamental radiative integral converges，but

$$\begin{align}
\boxed{ \int^Udu\,uN_{AB} = -C_{AB}^{(1)+}\log U+O(1).
}
\end{align}$$

### 7.1 Minimal subtraction

For a superrotation parameter $Y^A$ let

$$\begin{align}
\mathcal Q_Y^{\log}
\end{align}$$

denote the sphere pairing of $Y^A$ with the tail coefficient that multiplies this logarithm。A renormalized moment can be defined by

$$\begin{align}
H_Y^{\rm ren}(\mu) = \lim_{U\to\infty} \left[ H_Y(U) -\mathcal Q_Y^{\log}\log(U\mu) \right].
\end{align}$$

Changing scale gives

$$\begin{align}
\boxed{ H_Y^{\rm ren}(\mu') -H_Y^{\rm ren}(\mu) = -\mathcal Q_Y^{\log} \log\frac{\mu'}{\mu}.
}
\end{align}$$

Thus the subtraction leaves a logarithmic anomaly/finite-part ambiguity。The coefficient is physical data controlled by the logarithmic soft theorem。

### 7.2 Why $i^0$ alone cannot solve it

Spatial infinity touches only

$$\begin{align}
\mathscr I^+_- \quad\text{and}\quad \mathscr I^-_+.
\end{align}$$

The future endpoint

$$\begin{align}
\mathscr I^+_+
\end{align}$$

belongs to $i^+$，and the past endpoint $\mathscr I^-_-$ belongs to $i^-$。Therefore one $i^0$ corner term cannot cancel all four endpoint logarithms in a scattering problem。

There are two viable choices：

1. exclude the $1/u$ tail by stronger endpoint falloffs；
2. enlarge every relevant endpoint by tail variables and specify a scale-dependent corner polarization。

The first excludes the generic logarithmic-soft sector。The second is a new completion，not a property of the minimal Ashtekar--Streubel phase space。

## 8. Massive data and the timelike obstruction

### 8.1 Timelike hyperboloid phase space

Inside the future light cone use

$$\begin{align}
ds^2 = -d\tau^2+\tau^2h_{ab}(y)dy^ady^b.
\end{align}$$

For a real massive scalar，

$$\begin{align}
\phi_m = \tau^{-3/2} \left[ b(y)e^{-im\tau} +\overline b(y)e^{im\tau} \right] +O(\tau^{-5/2}).
\end{align}$$

Assume $b$ belongs to a weighted $L^2(H^3)$ class for which the integral below converges。Without this hyperbolic decay condition even the free timelike symplectic form is not defined。

The leading Klein--Gordon form is

$$\begin{align}
\boxed{ \Omega_{i^+}^{m} = 2im \int_{H^3} d^3y\sqrt h\, \delta\overline b\wedge\delta b, }
\end{align}$$

up to the overall orientation convention。This is the finite timelike canonical pair。The universal decay and hyperbolic scattering data are described in [Liu--Long](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.15619)。

### 8.2 Coulombic drag

When the final mass aspect is nonzero，the true retarded time differs from the flat one。For isotropic endpoint mass $M_+$，

$$\begin{align}
u = t-r-2GM_+\log(r-2M_+).
\end{align}$$

Consequently the finite Cauchy-slice potential acquires

$$\begin{align}
-2GM_+\, \theta_{\rm div}^r
\log t.
\end{align}$$

This is the mechanism isolated by Geiller--Laddha--Zwikel。They emphasize that in a generic massive scattering process the coefficient depends on the matter sector at $i^+$ and cannot be cancelled by a purely gravitational celestial counterterm at $\mathscr I^+_+$。[Geiller--Laddha--Zwikel](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.07978)

The required cancellation，if it exists，must be a coupled descent involving

$$\begin{align}
\Theta_{\mathscr I^+} +\Theta_{i^+}^{m} +\delta S_{\mathscr I^+\cap i^+}.
\end{align}$$

No such full off-shell corner action is derived here。Accordingly，

$$\begin{align}
\boxed{ \text{generic massive scattering is a genuine open action-level obstruction}.
}
\end{align}$$

It is not evidence that the local field theory is inconsistent；it means that the null-only asymptotic variational problem is incomplete。

The analytic situation is now more precise than “nobody has calculated it”。

On nontrapping asymptotically Minkowski spaces，Baskin--Vasy--Wunsch prove full asymptotic expansions of forward wave solutions in all asymptotic regimes。This validates the use of a blown-up joint asymptotic space for linear radiation。[Baskin--Vasy--Wunsch](https://arxiv.org/abs/1212.5141)

### 9.2 Quasilinear and vacuum gravity

Kádár--Kehrberger construct semiglobal solutions near spatial infinity from past to future null infinity，prove propagation of polyhomogeneity，and include an Einstein-vacuum application in harmonic gauge。[Kádár--Kehrberger](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.09814)

This supplies actual nontrivial two-ended polyhomogeneous solutions，but it does not yet provide：

- the fixed-frame Bondi-gauge action dictionary of Stages 3--4；
- the coupled massless and massive matter system used here；
- a proof that the action/corner counterterms are continuous in their PDE norms；
- a maximal characteristic-data theorem；
- a coupled $i^\pm$ symplectic gluing theorem。

Thus the actual-solution gate is partially open for vacuum/quasilinear waves and remains open for the complete action-first coupled scattering class。

## 10. Stage 7 theorem and obstruction statement

### Theorem 10.1

Assume：

1. a jointly polyhomogeneous normal-crossing completion at $\mathscr I^\pm\cap i^0$；
2. the endpoint tail class of section 2；
3. fixed round celestial metric and the radial counterterm scheme of Stages 3--6；
4. Ashtekar--Hansen regularity and totally fluxless matching conditions for the ordinary Maxwell/BMS corner data；
5. no massive final data and vanishing endpoint mass aspect at $i^\pm$。

Then the massless radiative two-form is finite on the full null boundaries，and the antipodally matched subspace is a consistent conditional global classical sector。The $1/u$ tail and memory are retained。

However：

1. smooth-superrotation soft moments require a logarithmic subtraction and have a scale-dependent finite part；
2. the matching conditions are regularity/scattering restrictions unless an explicit gluing field is added；
3. with massive final data，a matter-dependent logarithmic timelike contribution remains outside the null-only counterterm complex。

Therefore no theorem in the present category establishes a maximal generic Einstein--matter global action domain。The first missing arrow is

$$\begin{align}
\boxed{ \text{joint Einstein--matter corner histories} \longrightarrow
S_{\rm ren}^{\mathscr I^\pm\cup i^0\cup i^\pm}
\text{ off shell and }C^2.
}
\end{align}$$

This is a sharper result than simply declaring $i^0$ “subtle”：the null radiative sector is finite，the ordinary matching theorem is conditional but available，and the remaining obstructions are isolated to tail-generator renormalization and coupled timelike corners。

### Assumptions

- fixed conformal frame and round celestial metric；
- finite polyhomogeneous bidegree windows；
- generic $1/u$ gravitational tail but no slower tail；
- Ashtekar--Hansen regularity for the conditional matched sector；
- vanishing timelike endpoint mass for the conditional massless theorem；
- local finite-jet corner terms unless explicitly enlarged by a multiplier/edge variable。

### Not proved

- completeness or maximality of the two-variable index family；
- a full nonlinear Bondi-gauge PDE realization；
- a coupled massive $i^\pm$ action；
- uniqueness of the superrotation finite part；
- quantum Ward identities。

These limitations determine the precise conditional scope of Stage 8。

## 12. Correct corner completion and the no-maximum theorem

### 12.1 The product ansatz misses the front face

Section 1 used $\Omega$ and $\rho$ as if the resolved corner still had only two boundary hypersurfaces。That is insufficient。The real blow-up is

$$\begin{align}
X_{\rm c} = \left[ [0,\epsilon)_\Omega \times [0,\epsilon)_\rho ;
\{(0,0)\} \right].
\end{align}$$

On the projective chart define

$$\begin{align}
r_{\rm c}=\Omega+\rho, \qquad z=\frac{\Omega}{\Omega+\rho}.
\end{align}$$

Then

$$\begin{align}
\Omega=r_{\rm c}z, \qquad \rho=r_{\rm c}(1-z).
\end{align}$$

There are three boundary hypersurfaces：

$$\begin{aligned}
F_{\rm ff}&=\{r_{\rm c}=0,\ 0<z<1\}, \\
F_{\mathscr I}&=\{z=0\}, \\
F_{\rm sp}&=\{z=1\}.
\end{aligned}$$

The old counterexample is now diagnostic rather than pathological：

$$\begin{align}
\frac{\Omega}{\Omega+\rho}=z
\end{align}$$

is smooth on $X_{\rm c}$。It cannot be represented by an unblown double series with coefficients depending only on $x^A$，but it is an ordinary front-face coefficient。

The two Euler fields lift to smooth $b$-vector fields：

$$
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
\Omega\partial_\Omega
&=
z\,r_{\rm c}\partial_{r_{\rm c}}
+z(1-z)\partial_z,
\\
\rho\partial_\rho
&=
(1-z)\,r_{\rm c}\partial_{r_{\rm c}}
-z(1-z)\partial_z.
\end{aligned}
}
$$

Thus the correct off-shell corner class is

$$\begin{align}
\boxed{ \Phi \in \mathcal A_{\rm phg}^{ \left( E_{\rm ff}, E_{\mathscr I}, E_{\rm sp} \right)
} \left( X_{\rm c}\times S^2 \right).
}
\end{align}$$

Near the front face this means

$$\begin{align}
\Phi \sim
\sum_{(\alpha,k)\in E_{\rm ff}} r_{\rm c}^{\alpha} (\log r_{\rm c})^k \Phi_{\alpha,k}(z,x),
\end{align}$$

where $\Phi_{\alpha,k}$ is itself polyhomogeneous as $z\to0,1$ with index sets $E_{\mathscr I}$ and $E_{\rm sp}$。The two corner restrictions

$$\begin{align}
F_{\rm ff}\cap F_{\mathscr I}, \qquad F_{\rm ff}\cap F_{\rm sp}
\end{align}$$

must have the same iterated coefficients whether expanded first at the front face or at the side face。That compatibility，not equality of two unrelated one-variable series，is the resolved-corner matching condition。

### 12.2 Minimal saturation relative to the operations

Fix a radial/endpoint order window $N$ and let $\mathfrak O_N$ be the finite list of operations actually used：

1. multiplication up to the polynomial degree occurring in the truncated EOM and action；
2. $b$-differentiation；
3. finite Neumann expansion of inverse metrics after the nonzero leading background is factored out；
4. restriction to faces and corners；
5. eikonal，generator，radial-gauge and normal homotopy primitives；
6. field-space variation up to degree two；
7. pushforward along a regulated normal fiber。

Let $\mathbf E_{\rm seed}$ contain the leading Minkowski，Coulomb，radiative，memory and $1/u$-tail exponents。Define

$$\begin{align}
\boxed{ \operatorname{Sat}_N \left( \mathbf E_{\rm seed} \right) := \bigcap \left\{ \mathbf F: \mathbf E_{\rm seed}\subseteq\mathbf F, \quad \mathfrak O_N\mathbf F\subseteq\mathbf F \right\}.
}
\end{align}$$

The intersection is taken componentwise over the three faces and uses extended union whenever a normal primitive is resonant。It has three immediate properties：

1. it is the unique smallest index triple closed under $\mathfrak O_N$；
2. every coefficient generated by the finite-order EOM，gauge map，action or its first two variations lies in it；
3. if the seed sets are locally finite with finite log caps and all nonlinear perturbations have nonnegative weight after the background is factored out，then $\operatorname{Sat}_N(\mathbf E_{\rm seed})$ has the same properties。

The proof is algebraic：arbitrary intersections preserve closure，while finite sums，finite extended unions and the finite resonance operation preserve local finiteness in a fixed order window。This is the precise sense in which the index family is **complete**。It is completeness relative to a declared operation set，not an assertion that every asymptotically flat solution belongs to it。

### 12.3 Why no maximal unrestricted family exists

Let

$$\begin{align}
w_\ast(N)
\end{align}$$

be the largest weight that can contribute to the action，its first variation or its Hessian in the chosen finite-order problem。Take any admissible locally finite index triple $\mathbf E$。Choose a real number

$$\begin{align}
P>w_\ast(N)
\end{align}$$

which is absent from $E_{\rm ff}$，and add

$$\begin{align}
r_{\rm c}^{P}f(z,x)
\end{align}$$

with arbitrary smooth $f$ vanishing to the required orders at $z=0,1$。Saturating this new seed under $\mathfrak O_N$ produces a strictly larger locally finite triple $\mathbf E'$。Every new contribution to the regulated action and its first two variations is

$$\begin{align}
O \left( \epsilon_{\rm c}^{P-w_\ast(N)}
|\log\epsilon_{\rm c}|^K \right),
\end{align}$$

so the same counterterms and polarization remain admissible。

The argument can be repeated at arbitrarily large real weights。Therefore：

$$
\boxed{
\begin{gathered}
\text{in the unrestricted locally finite index-set poset，}
\\
\text{no finite-order admissible family is maximal，and no maximum exists.}
\end{gathered}
}
$$

At all orders the same conclusion follows by adjoining a positive irrational exponent not in the locally finite set and then its discrete saturation。A maximality theorem becomes meaningful only after fixing a discrete candidate lattice，a coefficient category and a bounded weight/log window。Within such a lattice one may enumerate maximal elements，but the answer is lattice dependent。

### Theorem 12.1

with the orientation of $S_{ij}$ fixed by the ordered outward normals。Higher intersections continue by the same incidence rule。This is the manifold-with-corners extension of the relative bicomplex used by [Margalef-Bentabol--Villaseñor](https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.01842)。

## 15. Can antipodal matching be a boundary Euler--Lagrange equation?

### 15.1 Independence lemma

Let the off-shell traces

$$\begin{align}
\Phi_+^{i^0}, \qquad \Phi_-^{i^0}
\end{align}$$

be independent，and suppose the regulated local action has no cross-corner field or coupling：

$$\begin{align}
S = S_+[\Phi_+] +S_-[\Phi_-] +S_{\rm sp}[\chi].
\end{align}$$

For tangent variations with disjoint support，

$$\begin{align}
\boxed{ \delta_+\delta_-S=0.
}
\end{align}$$

The boundary Euler--Lagrange locus is therefore locally a product

$$\begin{align}
\mathcal Z_+\times\mathcal Z_-,
\end{align}$$

whereas antipodal matching is the diagonal graph

$$\begin{align}
\mathcal G_{\mathcal A} = \left\{ \Phi_+=\mathcal A^*\Phi_- \right\}.
\end{align}$$

Except in the trivial case where both factors contain only one point，a product cannot equal this graph。Thus no block-diagonal local variation can generate antipodal matching。

### 15.2 The three possible mechanisms

There are exactly three logically distinct mechanisms：

1. **Common spatial field.**
   A field $\chi$ on the blown-up spatial face has boundary traces

$$\begin{align}
\Phi_\pm=\iota_\pm^*\chi.
\end{align}$$

   Smoothness and the chosen parity/fluxless class imply the antipodal relation。Matching is then built into the configuration space；variation derives the spatial EOM and momentum balance，not the trace identity。

2. **Explicit gluing equation.**
   Add

$$\begin{align}
S_{\rm glue} = \int_{S^2} \Lambda \left( \Phi_+-\mathcal A^*\Phi_- \right).
\end{align}$$

   Variation of $\Lambda$ derives matching，and variation of $\Phi_\pm$ gives opposite corner momenta。The relation is action-derived because a new field and coupling were deliberately added。

3. **PDE scattering map.**
   Solve a global problem whose image lies in $\mathcal G_\mathcal A$。Then matching is a theorem about the solution map，not a boundary equation of an unrestricted off-shell action。

The Ashtekar--Hansen/Prabhu results use the first and third mechanisms：extension，regularity，EOM and the totally fluxless sector。They do not show that an unrestricted bulk variation dynamically selects the regularity class。

### Theorem 15.1

Antipodal configuration matching cannot emerge from the local corner variation when the two null traces are independent and no cross-corner sector is present。A global spatial action can derive momentum/flux gluing，but configuration matching is either a trace regularity condition，an explicitly added multiplier equation，or a property of the scattering map。

This is the requested independence/no-go result。
