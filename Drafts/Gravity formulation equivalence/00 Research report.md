# When do different formulations of gravity define equivalent theories?

## Main result so far

The first benchmark establishes **precise sectorwise classical equivalence, not unrestricted equivalence**. The local Einstein–Palatini–CS dictionary is exact, but a complete equivalence also depends on the admissible nondegenerate domain, the full proper gauge relation, boundary/corner data, and quantum choices.

Five concrete results sharpen that statement:

1. The relative symplectic obstruction has an exact formula. If $F^*\theta_2-\theta_1=\delta B+dY$, then
   \[
   \boxed{F^*\Omega_2-\Omega_1=\int_{\partial\Sigma}\delta\bigl(Y-F^*C_2+C_1\bigr).}
   \]
   Vanishing of this corner integral, together with a genuine smooth global proper-gauge orbit equivalence, is sufficient for a reduced symplectomorphism. For that fixed correspondence it is also necessary for preservation of the integrated form.
2. The global AdS3 vacuum projective orbit is selected by **degree one as well as central monodromy**. The potentials $\mathcal L=-1/4$ and $-9/4$ both have Hill monodromy $-I$, but their projective degrees are one and three; only the former has a smooth degree-one center with the fixed angular period.
3. Smoothness of the AdS3 metric does **not** by itself force the global CS gauge group to be PSL rather than SL. An explicit smooth flat SL pair reconstructs global AdS. The usual $-I$ polar matrix transport must be distinguished from genuine disk holonomy including the polar spin-frame closing transition. This is a framing qualification of a commonly used argument, not a proof that arbitrary SL and PSL theories are equivalent.

4. For the independently fixed standard chiral CS and metric Einstein–GHY–counterterm prescriptions, the exact Bañados-collar wall defect is $-(k/\pi R^2)\mathcal L_+\mathcal L_-\tau$, with $\tau=-dt\wedge d\phi$. The actual corner difference vanishes on the fixed-normal radial slice. Their pulled-back asymptotic CPS forms therefore agree; finite-wall Dirichlet and chiral variational problems remain different.

5. A full classical reduced symplectomorphism is now **proved for the geodesically complete geometric vacuum sector**, with fixed compatible full spin/frame transitions and collar-controlled smooth structure. The independent metric and CS definitions both reduce to the degree-one projective product. Completeness promotes the local developing map to a global isometry and matches the full finite proper relations. A nondegenerate CS gauge path reaching an incomplete, non-GH metric shows why the unrestricted statement is false.

## 1. Seven logically different equivalence questions

A formulation here includes fields, action/variational principle, admissible solutions, proper gauge transformations, asymptotic conditions, corner prescription, and, when relevant, quantization data. A proposed equivalence must name the map and which of these structures it preserves.

| Level | Required mathematical content | What it does not establish by itself |
|---|---|---|
| Equation-level | A bidirectional correspondence of the specified classical solution domains, or a clearly stated local/on-shell correspondence | Proper gauge groups, symplectic normalization, global exhaustiveness |
| Gauge | Correspondence of full gauge equivalence relations; for the stronger groupoid notion also stabilizers and their actions | Equality of action-derived regional forms |
| Phase-space | A bijection, preferably a smooth diffeomorphism, of declared reduced spaces | Preservation of the two-form |
| Symplectic | A diffeomorphism $\Phi$ with $\Phi^*\Omega_2=\Omega_1$ | Preservation of an arbitrarily named Hamiltonian or symmetry dictionary |
| Hamiltonian symmetry | Matching symmetries, moment maps, reference constants, central extensions, and charges | Completeness of a restricted list of observables; off-shell equivalence |
| Action/variational | An off-shell correspondence of bulk, boundary, and joint actions, with permitted endpoint generating terms and the same allowed variations | Quantum measure, global bundle identifications, or anomalous phases |
| Quantum | An isomorphism/intertwiner of specified observable algebras and representations; matching dynamics, states/partition functions when claimed | An automatic consequence of any one classical row |

A regular invertible off-shell field redefinition preserving the complete relative action and proper gauge data is a strong route to classical symplectic equivalence. Auxiliary-field elimination requires a controlled unique branch and its boundary variational analysis; it is not an invertible field redefinition before reduction. Local equality of equations is much weaker than either statement.

The correct implication is conditional:

\[
\text{relative action/potential matching + global gauge-orbit equivalence}
\Longrightarrow \text{reduced symplectomorphism}
\Longrightarrow \text{smooth phase-space equivalence}.
\]

The first arrow uses the [relative-CPS theorem](</Users/koishi/Documents/Note/Drafts/Gravity formulation equivalence/03 Relative CPS equivalence theorem.md>). Preservation of Hamiltonian symmetries additionally requires a symmetry dictionary and charge normalization. Quantum equivalence needs extra data. The reverse arrows fail in general; explicit counterexamples are given in that theorem report.

## 3. The benchmark map and the remaining mathematical interfaces

For a metric in the declared complete domain choose a global orientation/time-orientation-compatible coframe that also matches the prescribed exact DS collar and full spin/frame transitions, then take its torsion-free connection. Such an admissible coframe exists by pulling back the known same-stress filling's coframe along the unique proper isometry. Arbitrary boundary Lorentz frames are not admissible choices for this map. The field map is

\[
[g]\mapsto[(A,\bar A)]
=\left[\left(\omega(e)+e/\ell,\omega(e)-e/\ell\right)\right].
\]

Its inverse on a geometric CS representative is $e=\ell(A-\bar A)/2$, $g=2\operatorname{tr}(e\otimes e)$. Both arrows require a common diagonal bundle identification. The [complete-sector theorem](</Users/koishi/Documents/Note/Drafts/Gravity formulation equivalence/06 Complete vacuum sector theorem.md>) proves these are mutually inverse smooth symplectic maps on its independently specified complete quotients, using the actual standard boundary prescriptions.

The following distinctions explain its scope and what cannot be inferred from a perturbative dictionary:

- Arbitrary CS gauge transformations, even supported away from the boundary, can make the triad degenerate. Therefore the nondegenerate domain and allowed finite gauge paths/groupoid cannot be left implicit.
- A near-boundary Bañados gauge can fail in the interior without the metric being singular. Global fillings must be constructed in regular representatives rather than inferred from a radial chart.
- A single exponential of an autonomous vector field is not a global parametrization of circle diffeomorphisms. The existing metric all-order formula is valuable input but not a global-completeness proof.
- The projective quotient removes parametrization redundancy of the reference vacuum. It does not declare all PSL asymptotic transformations proper gauge on every excited state.
- The standard chiral CS boundary action has now been compared with the metric Einstein–GHY–counterterm/Harlow–Wu action on the explicit collar slice. The calculation retains the nonzero finite-wall source, and does not identify finite Dirichlet with Brown–Henneaux conditions or discard cap/overlap phases.

## 4. Quantum implications that are already justified

A classical symplectomorphism alone does not imply quantum equivalence. The particle-on-a-circle flat-line-bundle counterexample is in the [relative theorem](</Users/koishi/Documents/Note/Drafts/Gravity formulation equivalence/03 Relative CPS equivalence theorem.md>); the [new benchmark](</Users/koishi/Documents/Note/Drafts/Gravity formulation equivalence/10 Vacuum quantum equivalence benchmark.md>) proves explicit prequantum/polarized transport conditions and a smooth infinite-mode squeeze obstruction. A connection lift, polarization and half-form pairing, and transported closed operator domains are independent hypotheses.

For the smooth vacuum orbit, the equivariant determinant question is now constructive, not settled merely by contractibility.
