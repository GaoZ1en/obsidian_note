## 1. Finite-cutoff action/corner comparison

This comparison is now performed in the [standard chiral calculation](</Users/koishi/Documents/Note/Drafts/Gravity formulation equivalence/05 Standard chiral boundary comparison.md>). With $\tau=-dt\wedge d\phi$, its exact outer-wall defect is $-(k/\pi R^2)\mathcal L_+\mathcal L_-\tau$. The metric wall source is $+(k/\pi R^2)\delta(\mathcal L_+\mathcal L_-)\tau$, while the chiral CS boundary variation vanishes. Both differences disappear in the bounded-profile Brown–Henneaux limit, but not generally at finite cutoff.

The actual corner difference is $C_g-\alpha=2\gamma *_\Gamma(E_a\delta N^a)$, hence vanishes on a fixed-internal-normal slice. This proves equality of the pulled-back integrated two-forms for the independently chosen actions, not only existence of a manufactured prescription. Normal–tangent Lorentz variations provide explicit counterexamples outside that slice.

The remaining boundary questions concern global cap/overlap action phases, arbitrary varying boundary sources, and other boundary polarizations; finite-wall Dirichlet is not the same ensemble as fixed leading Brown–Henneaux source.

## 2. Global vacuum filling and gauge relation

The [complete-sector theorem](</Users/koishi/Documents/Note/Drafts/Gravity formulation equivalence/06 Complete vacuum sector theorem.md>) now gives this construction and classification. Uniformly controlled nonautonomous collar isotopies produce every degree-one point; an independent exact FG-preserving generator gives locally smooth common-collar sections. The developing map of any complete nondegenerate flat pair is a global AdS isometry. Complete metrics with equal stresses differ by a proper global diffeomorphism, and equal-collar flat pairs by a unique proper gauge pair. Thus the full finite endpoint relations agree in that domain.

Two restrictions are substantive. Nondegenerate CS paths can reach incomplete, non-GH metrics without changing the boundary at all; completeness of the spacetime flow is not implied by pointwise invertibility. Separately, moving a nontrivial boundary deformation arbitrarily far out gives compact-open convergence to vacuum with fixed nonvacuum stress, so an ordinary compact-open quotient cannot be the Hausdorff orbit manifold. The theorem uses explicit collar-controlled functional diffeology and proves its quotient is the usual smooth projective orbit.

The open question is whether a precisely defined maximal GH vacuum domain coincides with this complete sector, and what changes under weaker temporal/support conditions, nontrivial topology, or different full boundary framing. These are not answered by the complete-sector theorem.

## 3. Beyond the vacuum

For other Virasoro orbits, the degree and monodromy together are natural classifiers to test, but global fillings also require topology and causal regularity. Higher exceptional constants already show a smoothness obstruction. Hyperbolic/BTZ sectors have noncontractible cycles and can carry additional holonomy moduli or two-boundary constraints; their symplectic form cannot be inferred by copying a disk argument. Defects require puncture/source actions and their corner contributions.

A productive next non-vacuum benchmark would hold a hyperbolic conjugacy class fixed on an annulus and calculate the remaining conjugate zero modes explicitly. This should be attempted after the disk sector is closed, not substituted for it.

## 7. General theorem extensions worth testing

The relative-CPS criterion currently assumes fixed boundaries, globally defined representatives or explicit patch data, and regular smooth quotients. Its most useful extensions are:

- a stratified/groupoid version preserving stabilizers rather than only the coarse reduced space;
- explicit Čech/relative cocycles for patchwise transgression and nontrivial bundles;
- moving or null boundaries with embedding contributions;
- a BV–BFV comparison in which auxiliary-field elimination and boundary reduction are compatible;
- quantization transport with an actual connection-preserving prequantum lift and implementability conditions.

Each extension should be driven by a counterexample or a computable gravitational model. None is being asserted here as a completed universal theorem.
