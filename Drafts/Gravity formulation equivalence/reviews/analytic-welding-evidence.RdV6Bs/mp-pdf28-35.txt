3.4   Equivalent descriptions of locality

Generally, the sewing isomorphisms of an R+ -extension can depend on the boundary

3.6     Modular and crossing invariance properties

In this section, we discuss several properties of local Fr-smooth R+ -extensions that serve
as intermediate steps in the proof of Theorem 1.1, also possibly interesting by their own
right. The real determinant line bundle satisfies all of these properties (Theorem 3.21).
We prove these properties for a general local Fr-smooth R+ -extension in Section 5.3, by
relating them to the real determinant line bundle in genus 0. Before the definitions, let us
briefly describe each property.
 1. Flat modular invariance (Definition 3.16). Sewing the two boundary components of
    an annulus with the standard parametrization and cylindrical metric results in a torus
    also with a flat metric, and the seam on the torus is a geodesic. If the same torus is
    obtained from two different annuli, the geodesic seams may not be homotopic. Flat
    modular invariance asserts that certain cocycles still agree in this case.
 2. Crossing invariance (Definition 3.18). A hyperbolic surface in Mhyp
                                                                     0,4 with four bound-
    ary components may be decomposed into two hyperbolic pairs of pants in several ways,
    changing the homotopy class of the seam. Crossing invariance asserts that it is possible
    to trivialize E(M0,3 ) such that any pants decomposition along hyperbolic geodesics
    induces a trivialization of E(M0,4 ) independent of the homotopy class of the seam.
 3. Hyperbolic modular invariance (Definition 3.20). Similarly, a handle in M1,1 may be
    decomposed into a pair of pants along a hyperbolic geodesic in a chosen homotopy class.
    Hyperbolic modular invariance asserts the existence of a trivialization of E(M0,3 ) com-
    patible with the self-sewing isomorphisms of hyperbolic pairs of pants. Together with
    crossing invariance, this implies invariance under S- and A-moves between hyperbolic
    pants decompositions, as defined by Hatcher and Thurston [HT80, Hat99].
