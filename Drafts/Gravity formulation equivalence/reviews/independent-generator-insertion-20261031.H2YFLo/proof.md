# Independent follow-up: the linear-antifield normalized generator insertion

## Result and scope

At isotopy time \(t=0\), for the stated right-action cubic generator

\[
\Gamma_3=\int\left[R\,\iota_\xi w+T\,\iota_\xi f
+\kappa^{-1}W\wedge\iota_\xi E\right],\qquad
\xi=e_0^{-1}\zeta,
\]

the contribution to the normalized one-loop generator insertion obtained from the **actual UV free Gaussian** is

\[
\boxed{\left.\gamma_{1,\Gamma_3}\right|_{\text{external degree }1}
=g_\Lambda(\epsilon)\int_{\mathbb H^3}e_0^a\wedge E_a,
\qquad g_\Lambda(\epsilon)=-\frac{2}{\kappa}a_1(\epsilon,\Lambda).}
\tag{1}
\]

There is no linear W, R, or T term. Here E is the **coframe antifield**, W is the connection antifield, and \(a_1\) is the per-fiber-component diagonal of

\[
F_0=e^{-\epsilon R_1}R_1^{-1}1_{(\Lambda^2,\infty)}(R_1)
\]

on one rank-three parameter bundle. The heat convention is one factor \(e^{-\epsilon\Delta}\) on a contracted propagator, equivalently \(e^{-\epsilon\Delta/2}\) on each leg. It is the same heat time as in the frozen linear-wheel calculation; applying a full heat factor separately to both legs would replace \(\epsilon\) by \(2\epsilon\).

Explicitly, with

\[
I_m=\frac1{2\pi^2}\int_{b_m}^\infty
\frac{\lambda^2+m^2}{\lambda^2+5-m^2}
 e^{-\epsilon(\lambda^2+5-m^2)}d\lambda,
\quad b_m=\sqrt{\max(\Lambda^2-5+m^2,0)},
\]

\[
\boxed{g_\Lambda(\epsilon)=-\frac{2}{3\kappa}(I_0+2I_1).}
\tag{2}
\]

For fixed \(\Lambda\), its small-heat behavior is

\[
\boxed{g_\Lambda(\epsilon)
=-\frac1{2\kappa\pi^{3/2}\sqrt\epsilon}
+g_{\Lambda,\mathrm{FP}}
-\frac3{2\kappa\pi^{3/2}}\sqrt\epsilon+O(\epsilon),}
\tag{3}
\]

where

\[
\boxed{g_{\Lambda,\mathrm{FP}}=
\frac{\sqrt5+3}{6\kappa\pi}
+\frac{b_0+2b_1-\sqrt5\arctan(b_0/\sqrt5)-3\arctan(b_1/2)}
 {3\kappa\pi^2}.}
\tag{4}
\]

The exact coefficient (2) is negative for \(\kappa>0\). The divergent coefficient in (3) is negative, while the displayed heat finite part (4) is positive. In contrast to the frozen tadpole, this insertion has an uncancelled \(\epsilon^{-1/2}\) divergence. Formula (4) specifies the finite part in this heat prescription, not a universal finite counterterm.

Equations (1)–(4) are ordinary smeared coefficients for smooth compactly supported external test antifields. For genuinely retained antifields, the constant smearing \(e_0\) is not an L2 covector and can be undefined. No retained Ward identity and no action of the sharp L2 projector on this constant have been inferred.

## 1. Which part of the normalized insertion is being computed

The conventions are those of [note19:31–78](</Users/koishi/Documents/Note/Drafts/Gravity formulation equivalence/19 Retained BV canonical transport on complete AdS.md#L31-L78>) and [note20:87–127](</Users/koishi/Documents/Note/Drafts/Gravity formulation equivalence/20 One-loop BV transport and complete AdS trace obstructions.md#L87-L127>). In particular, \((q,p)=1\), \(QF=(S,F)\), \(\partial_tS_t=(S_t,\Gamma_t)\), and coefficients are written first and commute with ordinary differentials. The external antifields E,W are odd two-forms; R,T are even three-forms. The normalized insertion is \(\gamma=I(\Gamma\rho)/I\rho\).

We compute the contribution generated by \(\Gamma_3\) itself, with no independently added source quantum generator \(\Gamma_{1,0}\). If a quantum lift has such a term, its linear restriction must be added; it is not fixed by this calculation. At \(t=0\), the local source half-density is one, so its log amplitude is zero.

At external homogeneous degree one, only the quadratic UV Taylor term of \(\Gamma_3\) contributes. The term \(-\langle\Gamma^{[1]}S^{[3]}\rangle\) in the normalized-insertion formula starts at external degree two, because a cubic \(\Gamma_3\) with one UV entry has two external entries. Stationary graph corrections also start at degree two. Disconnected denominator corrections therefore do not modify the coefficient being computed.

## 2. The actual odd free Gaussian and its sign

On the UV Lagrangian, write

\[
c=(\eta,\zeta),\qquad
\Psi_2=H_1G\bar c,\qquad
p=(W,E)=-\kappa *G\bar c,
\tag{5}
\]

with c and \(\bar c\) in the actual UV parameter band. The complete proper generator is

\[
G=\begin{pmatrix}d&-j\\j&d\end{pmatrix},
\quad d=D_{\omega_0},\quad j=[e_0,\cdot],\quad
M=G^\dagger G=R_1\oplus R_1.
\]

The free ghost action is exactly

\[
S_{\mathrm{gh},2}=-\kappa\langle\bar c,Mc\rangle.
\tag{6}
\]

The fixed linear reparametrization (5) has a field-independent Gaussian normalization; it cancels in the normalized insertion. It does not change the covariance sign.

For one pair with action \(-\kappa r\bar c c\), direct finite Berezin integration gives

\[
\langle\bar c c\rangle=\frac1{\kappa r},\qquad
\langle c\bar c\rangle=-\frac1{\kappa r}.
\tag{7}
\]

These are the signs of the Euclidean weight \(e^{-S/\hbar}\); the unit-width formulas use \(\hbar=1\). In the full kernel, with the stated heat prescription,

\[
\langle\bar c(x)c(y)\rangle_\epsilon=\kappa^{-1}F_0(x,y),\qquad
\langle c(x)\bar c(y)\rangle_\epsilon=-\kappa^{-1}F_0(x,y),
\tag{8}
\]

with the indices transposed as appropriate. The sharp projector appears once since both UV legs are projected and the projection is idempotent. Restoring \(\hbar\), the contraction carries one factor \(\hbar\), whose coefficient is \(\gamma_1\).

## 3. Coincident covariance and the external-antifield contraction

By the point-symmetry argument in the frozen proof, both first covariant derivatives of the full spin-one spectral kernel vanish at coincidence on H3, including the actual sharp UV exclusion. Isotropy gives \(F_0(x,x)=a_1 1_3\). This is a statement about the full spectral kernel, not a principal-symbol approximation.

Write \(W_i^a=(*W)^a_i\), \(E_j^a=(*E)^a_j\), and identify \(\xi^k=\zeta^k\) in a background orthonormal coframe. The algebraic parts of (5) are

\[
W_i^a=\kappa\epsilon_{iba}\bar\zeta^b
\quad\text{and}\quad
E_i^a=-\kappa\epsilon_{iba}\bar\eta^b.
\]

The omitted terms are the d-terms, whose coincident covariances vanish. It follows that

\[
\boxed{\langle W_i^a\zeta^k\rangle_\epsilon=a_1\epsilon_{ika},
\qquad \langle\zeta^kE_i^a\rangle_\epsilon=0.}
\tag{9}
\]

The first two generator terms require a ghost–physical covariance if their external entry is R or T. The free even and odd Gaussians are block diagonal, so these covariances vanish. There is no UV R or T coordinate on \(\mathcal L_0\).

For the last generator term, the form identity is

\[
(*e^i)\wedge\iota_k(*e^j)=\epsilon_{jki}\,dV.
\]

Keeping the coefficient order exactly as written in \(W\wedge\iota_\xi E\), its external-E part is

\[
\kappa^{-1}W_i^a\zeta^kE_j^a\epsilon_{jki}\,dV.
\]

Moving the odd external E coefficient to the front crosses two odd UV variables and produces no sign. Using (9),

\[
\frac{a_1}{\kappa}\sum_{i,k}\epsilon_{jki}\epsilon_{ika}E_j^a
=-\frac{2a_1}{\kappa}\sum_jE_j^j.
\tag{10}
\]

The external-W part vanishes because it contracts \(\zeta\) with E. Finally \(\sum_jE_j^j dV=e_0^a\wedge E_a\), proving (1). In particular, dropping the odd Wick sign or treating the odd external E as commuting would be unsafe.

## 4. Spectral evaluation and heat subtraction

The vector parameter bundle is the full spin-one bundle, not just its transverse helicities. Its HC decomposition is the signed \(m=0,\pm1\) sum with per-helicity density \((\lambda^2+m^2)d\lambda/(2\pi^2)\) and energy \(\lambda^2+5-m^2\). Therefore

\[
a_1=\frac13(I_0+2I_1).
\]

The factor \(1/3\) is the per-component isotropic diagonal, not a removed internal copy. The J/P copy choice was already accounted for in the actual ghost contraction (9).

For the unexcluded integrals,

\[
I_0^{\rm all}=\frac{e^{-5\epsilon}}{4\pi^{3/2}\sqrt\epsilon}
-\frac{\sqrt5}{4\pi}\operatorname{erfc}(\sqrt{5\epsilon}),
\]
\[
I_1^{\rm all}=\frac{e^{-4\epsilon}}{4\pi^{3/2}\sqrt\epsilon}
-\frac3{8\pi}\operatorname{erfc}(2\sqrt\epsilon).
\]

The actual sharp UV integrals subtract the finite pieces from 0 to \(b_0,b_1\). Their \(\epsilon\to0\) primitives are

\[
J_0=b_0-\sqrt5\arctan(b_0/\sqrt5),\qquad
J_1=b_1-\frac32\arctan(b_1/2).
\]

Substitution gives (3)–(4). Since \(a_1>0\), the exact unsusbtracted coefficient is negative. Since \(J_0,J_1\ge0\), the specified finite part is positive. No finite-part prescription is being confused with an actual finite \(\epsilon\to0\) limit.

## 5. Compact test antifields versus actual retained antifields

For a compactly supported smooth external E, the integral in (1) exists. Such a test insertion should not silently be called genuinely retained: sharp spectral projection is nonlocal and generally destroys compact support.

For \(\Lambda^2>5\), choose \(b^2<\Lambda^2-5\) and let

\[
E=\vartheta\,\phi_b(r)*e_0,\qquad
\phi_b(r)=\frac{1-\cos br}{r\sinh r},
\]

where \(\vartheta\) is one odd external coefficient. This E is genuinely in the retained upper-degree trace band and belongs to every Sobolev space. However, the ordinary coefficient of \(\vartheta\) in

\[
\int e_0^a\wedge E_a=3\vartheta\int\phi_b\,dV
\]

is infinite. Thus (1) is not a finite functional on the full retained antifield Hilbert/Sobolev sector even at positive heat time. For \(\Lambda^2\le5\), the retained trace-spin channel on H3 is absent (there is no L2 threshold eigenvector), so the displayed linear term vanishes on that retained channel by the **spin spectral decomposition**, not by projecting a constant non-L2 function.

The functional derivative of the formal local expression (1) is a constant smearing proportional to \(e_0\), which is not L2. The sharp L2 operator \(P_\Lambda\) is not automatically defined on this constant. Although the formal local differential equation \(R_K e_0=4e_0\) holds, it is not an L2 spectral-eigenvector statement and does not license writing \(P_\Lambda e_0=1_{4\le\Lambda^2}e_0\). In particular, no retained antibracket or Ward identity is established by manipulating that expression.

A source quantum-generator choice, a spatial extension/subtraction, and a compatible retained BV calculus are extra inputs. This calculation fixes the free Gaussian insertion and its signs, not those additional data.
