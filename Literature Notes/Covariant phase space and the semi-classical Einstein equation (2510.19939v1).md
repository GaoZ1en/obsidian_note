abstract: "The covariant phase space formalism in general relativity is a covariant method for constructing the symplectic two-form, Hamiltonian and other conserved charges on the phase space of solutions to the Einstein equation with classical matter. In this note, we consider a generalization of this formalism to the semi-classical Einstein equation coupled to quantum matter. Given a family of solutions in semi-classical gravity, we define the semi-classical symplectic two-form -- a natural generalization of the classical sympelctic two-form -- as the sum of the gravitational symplectic form and the Berry curvature associated to the quantum state of matter. We show that the semi-classical symplectic two-form is independent of the Cauchy slice, and satisfies the quantum generalization of the classical Hollands-Iyer-Wald identity. For small perturbations, we also extend our discussion to gauge-invariantly defined subregions of spacetime, where the quantum contribution is replaced by the Berry curvature of certain special purifications involving the Connes cocycle. In the AdS/CFT context, the semi-classical symplectic form defined here is naturally dual to the Berry curvature in the boundary CFT."

consider a spacetime $\displaystyle{M}$ with a fixed background metric $\displaystyle{g_{\mu \nu}^{(0)}}$. let $\displaystyle{D(r)}$ and $\displaystyle{D(\bar{r})}$ be two complementary subregions, defined in a gauge invariant way. we could take $\displaystyle{D(r)}$ to be the entanglement wedge of the boundary region $\displaystyle{R}$. let $\displaystyle{\Sigma}$ be a Cauchy slice that passes through the *quantum extremal surface*(?)) torresponding to $\displaystyle{R}$, and let $\displaystyle{r\subseteq \Sigma}$ be part of $\displaystyle{\Sigma}$ in $\displaystyle{D(r)}$. let $\displaystyle{\mathcal{M}_{r}}$ be the von Neumann algebra of observables associated to the domain of dependence $\displaystyle{D(r)}$. let $\displaystyle{\bar{r}=\Sigma-r}$. let $\displaystyle{\Omega}$ be a cyclic and separating state with respect to $\displaystyle{\mathcal{M}_{r}}$ (if $\displaystyle{M}$ is a AdS black hole, we can take $\displaystyle{\Omega}$ to be the Hartle-Hawking state). the global Hilbert space factorizes into the Hilbert space of $\displaystyle{r}$ and $\displaystyle{\bar{r}}$ as

$$\begin{align}
\mathcal{H} & =\mathcal{H}_{r}\otimes \mathcal{H}_{\bar{r}}
\end{align}$$

let $\displaystyle{\psi}$ be some global excited state and let $\displaystyle{\rho _{\psi}}$ be the corresponding density matrix on $\displaystyle{r}$. construct a purification of $\displaystyle{\rho _{\psi}}$ which we can use to define a subregional Berry connection. for this, the purification should be such that on $\displaystyle{D(\bar{r})}$. Connes-Størmer theorem states that quantum field theory of algebra type III$_{1}$, the Connes-Størmer construction gives a canonical way to construct such purifications. the Connes cocycle

$$\begin{align}
\hat{\psi}_{s} & =u'_{\Omega|\psi}(s)\psi
\end{align}$$

where

$$\begin{align}
u'_{\Omega|\psi}(s) & =\Delta ^{'is}_{\Omega|\psi}\Delta ^{'-is}_{\psi}
\end{align}$$

is the Connes-cocycle. here $\displaystyle{\Delta'_{\psi}=\rho ^{-1}_{\psi}\otimes \rho ^{'}_{\psi}}$ where $\displaystyle{\rho _{\psi}}$ is the density matrix of $\displaystyle{\psi}$ on $\displaystyle{r}$, while $\displaystyle{\rho'_{\psi}}$ is the density matrix on $\displaystyle{\bar{r}}$. similarly, $\displaystyle{\Delta'_{\Omega|\psi}=\rho ^{-1}_{\psi}\otimes \rho'_{\Omega}}$ is the relative modular operator, and so

$$\begin{align}

 & =\mathbb{1}\otimes (\rho _{\Omega}')^{is}(\rho _{\psi}')^{-is}
\end{align}$$

using standard properties of modular flow, it is easy to check that

$$\begin{align}
\braket{ \hat{\psi}_{s}|a|\hat{\psi}_{s} }  & = \braket{ \psi|a|\psi }  \\
\braket{ \hat{\psi}_{s}|a'|\hat{\psi}_{s} }  & = \braket{ \psi|\Delta ^{-is}_{\Omega}a'\Delta ^{is}_{\Omega}|\psi }
