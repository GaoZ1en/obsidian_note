## The three papers worth reading closely

### 1. IIB scalar/form dynamics from a boundary polarization

[2611.08841v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08841v1), Euihun Joung and J. A. Rosabal — **Type IIB Forms and Scalars from an SL(2,R) Topological Field Theory with Boundary**. Detailed note: [[Type IIB Forms and Scalars from an SL(2,R) Topological Field Theory with Boundary (2611.08841v1)]]. Primary hep-th.

The strongest immediate construction today: conjugate pairs of eleven-dimensional topological forms, a boundary functional selecting their duality polarization, and a flat SL(2,R) connection encoding scalar monodromy. The note reconstructs the action variation, the residual SO(2) boundary condition, scalar/frame normalization, pseudo-action reduction and J-fold holonomy. Reproduced all eleven algebraic Bianchi residuals, the important matrix/kinetic identities and local polarization coefficients. Useful for CPS and sewing because it separates the constraint surface, boundary polarization and global holonomy sector. **Do not yet use it as a finished AdS on-shell-action or quantum-sewing prescription:** quantized sources/lattices and the needed corner term are not constructed.

### 2. A boundary-current obstruction in Maxwell–Chern–Simons AdS3/BCFT2

[2611.08934v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08934v1), Hikaru Toya and Satoshi Yamaguchi — **AdS3/BCFT2 correspondence and 't Hooft anomaly of U(1) symmetry**. Detailed note: [[AdS3 BCFT2 correspondence and t Hooft anomaly of U(1) symmetry (2611.08934v1)]]. Primary hep-th.

A particularly transparent test for boundary-condition and source/response bookkeeping. A finite-cutoff magnetic corner condition relates the two radial branches; the apparently vanishing growing-branch coefficient leaves a finite normal BCFT current. The note derives that limit and reconstructs the generic Legendre/Bessel massive-vector modes from the first-order helicity equations. Reproduced the geometry, arbitrary-function solutions, both cutoff Dirichlet conditions, flux/current substitutions and mode residuals. Useful for the current AdS quantization work, but the renormalized source polarization is a quoted input, and the extra separated modes have **not** had their physical boundary-condition spectrum or norm determined. The current example tests the stated boundary conditions, not every possible brane theory.

### 3. Higher-rank edge currents, canonical reduction and a pure-gauge mixed kernel

[2611.09786v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09786v1), Erica Bertolini, Matteo Carrega, Nicola Maggiore, Daniel Sacco Shaikh and Maria Florencia Semino — **Edge dynamics of covariant higher-rank Chern-Simons theory of fractons**. Detailed note: [[Edge dynamics of covariant higher-rank Chern-Simons theory of fractons (2611.09786v1)]]. Primary hep-th; cross-listed cond-mat.mes-hall, cond-mat.str-el, gr-qc.

The most directly reusable charge objects are the two normal moments of one scalar functional relation, their ordinary/higher-rank central terms, and the normalized tensor-sector canonical pair. The full note covers §§1–10, including auxiliary elimination, Luttinger versus chiral reduction, boundary matching, field-strength response and non-planar projections. Reproduced the variational coefficients, moments, Hamiltonian branch and Hessian-kernel cancellation. **A real limitation was found:** the claimed sufficient chiral-matching relations omit the remaining first boundary equation's compatibility condition. An exact positive-energy counterexample and a compatible example are given in the note. Use the canonical and gauge-invariant-response constructions, but retain the additional condition before borrowing the two matched chiral modes. This does not require discarding the conditional co-propagation sign argument.

### 1. Discreetly Conformal: Higher-Order Chiral Scalars and the Discrete Series

[2611.08581](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08581), Calvin Y.-R. Chen, Kurt Hinterbichler, Samanta Saha and Thomas Yan; hep-th. The question is how gauging shift symmetries changes the massless scalar's phase space. The authors generalize its chiral/antichiral Floreanini–Jackiw decomposition to larger gauged shift sectors, obtaining higher-spatial-derivative chiral actions and an explanation of hidden conformal symmetry, including discrete-series scalars in de Sitter space. Useful beside today's edge note for distinguishing a gauge quotient from a chiral phase-space description. It remains a sketch because the immediate project use is a comparison model, rather than a supplied gravitational regional-sewing or AdS boundary-domain construction.

### 2. Carrollian minimal massive gravity and the fate of third-way consistency

[2611.09209](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09209), Büşra Dedeoğlu, Özgür Sarıoğlu and Utku Zorba; primary hep-th, cross gr-qc. The authors study a singular Carroll contraction of minimal massive gravity: although an algebra bracket diverges, its direction is null in the contracted bilinear form, so the Chern–Simons-like action remains finite. They report an invertible field redefinition removing the third-way deformation on generic parameters, a canonical replacement for a degenerate standard charge formula, a bms3 algebra, and a constraint count with no local graviton. This is an especially useful **next deep-read candidate** for degenerate flavor metrics and surface-charge definitions. It stays medium today because the three selected boundary constructions already have more immediate uses; the 53-page charge/constraint argument and two thermodynamic ensembles have not been independently checked.

### 3. Generalized Schwarzian thermodynamics from gravitational perturbations of near-extremal Kerr

[2611.09757](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09757), Marcela Cárdenas, Benjamín Cuevas-Tapia and Jessica Trespalacios; primary gr-qc, cross hep-th. The question is whether generalized Schwarzian thermodynamics follows directly from four-dimensional near-Kerr perturbations. The source constructs a boundary-deforming nonnormalizable mode and a horizon-supported normalizable mode, reduces their equations to a JT scalar equation, and claims that field-dependent isometry conditions and the vacuum equations independently select the stationary KdV hierarchy; Euclidean Hamiltonian actions give odd-power near-extremal entropy corrections. Useful for separating horizon and asymptotic response data before computing thermodynamic generators. Another strong follow-up candidate, kept medium because a reconstruction of the perturbative phase space, regularity and Euclidean boundary terms is still needed before transferring these results to the vault's CPS conventions.

### 4. Bondi-Sachs formalism on past light cones and an analogue of the integral mass formula

[2611.08958](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08958), Matheus G. Barbosa; primary gr-qc, cross math-ph. This extends characteristic Bondi–Sachs evolution to past light cones of an accelerating observer with a rotating tetrad, matter and cosmological constant, without a symmetry assumption. Vertex and axial regularity fix integration constants in the hierarchical equations; the source reports a first-proper-time-order matter-independent evolution property and an integral-mass-formula analogue for an observer in matter. It offers useful finite/null-boundary data and regularity prescriptions, rather than simply an asymptotic Bondi expansion. Medium because no explicit CPS charge or regional symplectic matching is established at sketch depth. The 42-page paper overlaps the author's 2609.25477 but advertises improved regularity and field-equation treatment; it is a separate new ID, not automatically a tracked-version update.

### 5. Fermionic Kaluza-Klein Reductions

[2611.08584](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08584), Francesco Bertucci; primary hep-th, cross gr-qc. The paper derives free spin-1/2 and spin-3/2 Kaluza–Klein reductions on a general product spacetime in arbitrary dimension at the action level. Internal Dirac and Rarita–Schwinger eigenvalues determine the lower-dimensional mass towers; a Hodge-like decomposition organizes the spin-3/2 ansatz and the fate of its gauge symmetry. Potentially valuable for normalized fermionic mode expansions and supergravity testbeds, since action-level reduction can retain constraints lost by reading off equations alone. Medium because it supplies a compactification/spectral dictionary, not the particular AdS boundary conditions and symplectic normalization currently being constructed.

### 6. Standard Subspaces and Modular Theory — General Theory and Applications to QFT

[2611.08668](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08668), Ricardo Correa da Silva, Gandalf Lechner and Roberto Longo; primary math-ph, cross math.OA. This 143-page work-in-progress monograph chapter develops real standard subspaces and their modular data through KMS conditions and reflection positivity, then treats symplectic structures/polarizers, direct sums/integrals/tensor products, free U(1) currents, Klein–Gordon localization and entropy operators. It is a promising reference for moving from real solution spaces to localized quantum algebras and for understanding which operations genuinely preserve regional structure. The current source is only the first chapter, not the promised complete monograph. Medium/reference placement is deliberate: this run did not decompose 143 pages or assert that its abstract-level tensor-product operations already solve gauge-theory sewing.

### 7. NLO corrections to charged-current correlators near extremality: non-Abelian hydrodynamics meets AdS2

[2611.08628](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08628), Matti Järvinen, Elias Kiritsis, Francesco Nitti, Andrea Olzi and Edwan Préau; primary hep-th, cross cond-mat.str-el and gr-qc. The authors analytically obtain a longitudinal charged-current correlator around a doubly charged near-extremal AdS5 Reissner–Nordström brane, expanding frequency, momentum, temperature and isospin chemical potential relative to the larger quark chemical potential. The claimed NLO result captures a hydrodynamic-like pole interacting with the AdS2 infrared tower; nonzero isospin lifts a collision present at zero isospin, with numerical comparisons supporting the spectrum and spectral function. Useful as a matched-region correlator/pole calculation and for diagnosing nonuniform hydrodynamic limits. Medium because this is not yet a normalized global AdS mode or boundary-domain calculation for the current fields; the extensive analytic/numerical comparison was not reproduced.

### 8. A Song of Spice and Ice: A diagonal dual to dS3 & its Galilean flat space limit

[2611.08600](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08600), Arjun Bagchi, Sharang Rajesh Iyer and K. Narayan; hep-th. The proposal combines a future-boundary holomorphic sector with a past-boundary antiholomorphic sector into a diagonal dS3 dual with equal and opposite imaginary central charges. A stress-tensor prescription built from the future wavefunction and its conjugate is used in Cardy entropy comparisons for dS3 and complex Kerr–dS3, followed by a Galilean rather than Carrollian flat limit and a BMS–Cardy comparison. Worth reading for how conjugation, boundary choice and contraction affect charge normalization. Medium because the diagonal holographic identification and complex-saddle/state-counting interpretation are proposals to be checked, not an already established CPS equivalence or a real Lorentzian black-hole quantization.

### 9. Natural potentials for TT tensors, divergence-free symmetric tensors and linearised relativistic initial data

[2611.09303](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09303), Erwann Delay; primary math.DG, cross gr-qc and math-ph. Starting from a natural tensor vanishing at a background metric, the source uses the adjoint of its linearization to construct local potentials for divergence-free tensors, and TT tensors when conformal directions are also annihilated. It relates the Einstein-background linearized Bach operator to massless/partially-massless spin-two factors and extends the construction, with Fredholm properties, to linearized constraint data on hypersurfaces of Minkowski, dS and AdS. Useful for generating constrained compactly supported perturbations and for distinguishing local surjectivity from finite-dimensional obstructions on closed spaces. Medium because the exact symbol, boundary-domain and global hypotheses must be read before using it as a completeness statement for the present AdS phase space.

### 10. Zak Phase, Pole–Zero Invariants and the Maslov Index of Bloch Cauchy Lines in One-Dimensional Periodic Media

[2611.09419](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09419), Didier Felbacq; math-ph. The paper organizes Berry–Zak phase, surface-impedance poles/zeros and interface states through the line of field/flux Cauchy data at an inversion center. Its Maslov winding is related to band-edge parities, while monotonic rotation of gap-decaying Cauchy lines turns interface states into crossings and gives an at-most-one-state criterion in the stated one-dimensional setting. A useful geometric model for matching boundary data and tracking spectral flow without confusing a coordinate impedance pole with a physical state. It remains medium because the result is a specialized periodic-medium spectral problem, not a field-theory symplectic sewing map; higher-dimensional extension is explicitly unfinished.
