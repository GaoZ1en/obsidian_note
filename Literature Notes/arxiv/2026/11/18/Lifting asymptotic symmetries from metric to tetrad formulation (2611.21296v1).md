The useful object is a **choice of lift**, attached to a choice of reference tetrad. It removes a vertical term in the generator bracket; it does not prove that a Hamiltonian exists or that its charge algebra has no extension. This distinction matters when the corner carries Lorentz charge. All references are to arXiv v1; PDF page = printed page + 1 (title page unnumbered).

## Residual transformations as projectable field-space vectors (Secs. 2–3.2; PDF pp. 4–10)

Use oriented, time-oriented tetrads on a spacetime patch, $g=e^T\eta e$, with torsion-free $\omega[e]$ on the vacuum Cartan–Palatini solution space. The local gauge generator is $X=(\xi,\lambda)$ with $\delta_X e=\mathcal L_\xi e+\lambda e$ and $\delta_X\omega=\mathcal L_\xi\omega-D\lambda$. The ordinary bracket has Lorentz part $\xi_1[\lambda_2]-\xi_2[\lambda_1]+[\lambda_1,\lambda_2]$. Treat the parameters as functionals of the fields when evaluating the adjusted generator bracket,

$$[X_1,X_2]_*=[X_1,X_2]-\delta_{X_1}X_2+\delta_{X_2}X_1,\qquad [\delta_{X_1},\delta_{X_2}]=-\delta_{[X_1,X_2]_*}.$$

The minus in the second identity is for the paper's pullback convention: at fixed parameters $\delta_{X_1}$ acts on $D_{X_2}e$ by $D_{X_2}D_{X_1}e$, and varying the parameter adds $D_{\delta_{X_1}X_2}e$. This identity proves closure on the conditions that define the residual solution space, without using any surface-charge bracket.

Let $S_g$ be vacuum metrics satisfying specified boundary and gauge conditions. In $S_e^H$ impose additional tetrad conditions whose intersection with each metric fiber is a free, transitive orbit of the same residual local group $L_H$. The on-shell connection is determined by $e$ here; a theory with additional independent connection data would require rechecking the fiber hypothesis. The metric projection $\pi(e)=g[e]$ descends on projectable pairs, i.e. $\xi[e]=\xi[g[e]]$. This makes $\delta_{(0,\mu)}\xi=0$. Thus the kernel $C^\infty(S_e^H,\mathfrak l_H)$ of generator projection is an ideal, regardless of whether $H$ is normal in the Lorentz group. The ordinary commutator of Lorentz matrices need not preserve $\mathfrak l_H$ alone; the **adjusted** bracket of tangent vectors does. A naive choice $\lambda=\sigma[\xi]$ solving the extra gauge conditions generally has a vertical defect $\beta=[\hat\xi_1,\hat\xi_2]_*^L-\sigma[[\xi_1,\xi_2]_*]$. Changing $\sigma$ by a vertical $\tau$ adds the two mixed adjusted brackets and $[\tau_1,\tau_2]_*^L-\tau[[\xi_1,\xi_2]_*]$ (Eq. 3.14), so testing $\beta$ for just one lift is not a test of a non-split extension.

## Hold the comparison frame fixed (Secs. 3.3–3.5, App. A; PDF pp. 10–14, 24–26)

Choose a smooth reference section $e_0[g]\in S_e^H$ over the patch of solution space under consideration. Define $\Lambda=e e_0^{-1}\in L_H$ so that $(g,\Lambda)$ are product coordinates. Since $d\pi(\delta_\xi e_0)=d\pi(\mathcal L_\xi e_0)=\mathcal L_\xi g$, their difference is vertical in the **unrestricted** tetrad space. In matrix notation

$$\rho_0[\xi]=(\delta_\xi e_0-\mathcal L_\xi e_0)e_0^{-1},\qquad \delta_X\Lambda=\lambda\Lambda-\Lambda\rho_0[\xi]+\xi[\Lambda].$$

The second formula follows from varying $e e_0^{-1}$ with $\delta e_0^{-1}=-e_0^{-1}\delta e_0 e_0^{-1}$, and separating the common Lie derivative of the two tetrads. Set $\delta_X\Lambda=0$, giving $s[\xi]=\operatorname{Ad}_\Lambda\rho_0[\xi]-\xi[\Lambda]\Lambda^{-1}$ (Eq. 3.20). This is tangent to the gauge slice, since it moves on the family $\Lambda e_0[g']$ with $\Lambda$ fixed. Both lifted vectors annihilate $\Lambda$, hence so does their commutator. Projectability fixes the vector part of that bracket, so uniqueness of $s$ fixes its Lorentz part. Decomposition into this lift plus a vertical generator is unique. For a pure vertical $\mu$, $\delta_\mu\Lambda=\mu\Lambda$, $\delta_\mu\rho_0=0$ and $\delta_\mu s=[\mu,s]-\xi[\mu]$. Substitution into the adjusted bracket cancels both explicit spacetime derivative and matrix commutator, leaving $[\hat\xi,(0,\mu)]_*=(0,-\delta_{\hat\xi}\mu)$. This is the semidirect action; for field-independent vertical parameters it vanishes, giving a direct sum at the parameter-algebra level when the metric parameterization closes with field-independent structure.

If $e_0'=he_0$ for $h[g]\in L_H$, then $\Lambda'=\Lambda h^{-1}$ and $\rho'_0=\operatorname{Ad}_h\rho_0+(\delta_\xi h-\xi[h])h^{-1}$. Inserting both expressions gives $s'-s=\operatorname{Ad}_\Lambda(h^{-1}\delta_\xi h)$: only field dependence of the change matters. For a Killing vector $\delta_\xi g=0$, $\delta_{\hat\xi}e=0$ and the unique compensator is $-(\mathcal L_\xi e)e^{-1}$, the Kosmann lift at that metric, independently of $h$. For general $\xi$ the usual Kosmann prescription projects the frame variation onto its metric-induced symmetric part and need not be a Lie algebra homomorphism; the section lift agrees only on isometries in general. Appendix A distinguishes this local tangent-space argument from lifting diffeomorphisms globally as principal-bundle automorphisms: the chosen field-space section need not exist globally.

## BMSW-u: the compensator is not the raw dyad angle (Sec. 4, App. B; PDF pp. 15–22, 27–30)

For $ds^2=e^{2B}(V/r)du^2-2e^{2B}du\,dr+\gamma_{ab}(dx^a-U^adu)(dx^b-U^bdu)$, fix $g_{rr}=g_{ra}=0$, $\partial_r\ln(\det\gamma/r^4)=0$, and impose $(g_{uu},g_{ur},g_{ua},g_{ab})=(O(r),-1+O(r^{-2}),O(1),O(r^2))$. Leading angular data are $q_{ab}$ and the shear coefficient $C_{ab}$ with $q^{ab}C_{ab}=0$. The hypersurface Einstein equations determine the radial coefficients (e.g. $B_2=-C_{ab}C^{ab}/32$, $U_2^a=-D_b C^{ab}/2$), leaving the mass and angular momentum aspects. The leading trace-free angular equation sets $(\partial_u q)_{\langle ab\rangle}=0$, so only the conformal scale evolves: $q_{ab}=e^{\psi(u,x)}Q_{ab}(x)$. Appendix B.1 organizes the subleading angular evolution and remaining mass/aspect balance laws; B.2 expands the Lie variations used below, B.3 gives the torsion-free spin connection and B.4 illustrates a different phase choice for the dyad. The full nonlinear evolution hierarchy is not required for the lift.

For $\xi^u=f$, $\mathcal L_\xi g_{rr}=0$ first gives $\partial_r f=0$; $\mathcal L_\xi g_{ra}=0$ gives $\partial_r\xi^a=e^{2B}\gamma^{ab}\partial_b f$, hence $\xi^a=Y^a+I^a$, $I^a=-\int_r^\infty e^{2B}\gamma^{ab}\partial_b f\,dr'$. Tracing $\mathcal L_\xi\gamma$ and using the determinant constraint gives $\xi^r=r(U^a\partial_a f-2W-D_a I^a)/2$; the falloffs set $W=\partial_u f$ and $\partial_uY^a=0$. The resulting modified bracket acts on the free parameters by $Y_{12}=[Y_1,Y_2]$, $f_{12}=Y_1[f_2]-Y_2[f_1]+f_1\partial_u f_2-f_2\partial_u f_1$. These are the boundary Lie bracket on $(u,x)$; the field variations correct the subleading metric-dependent completion. An explicit component calculation in the xAct kernel reproduced the $rr$, $ra$ and angular Lie-derivative equations, without imposing the Einstein hierarchy.

Use $\ell=\partial_r$, $n=e^{-2B}(\partial_u+\frac V{2r}\partial_r+U^a\partial_a)$ and an angular orthonormal dyad. The internal metric has $\eta_{01}=-1$ and $\eta_{22}=\eta_{33}=1$; the real dyad is related to its triangular reference $\underline E$ by $R(\phi)=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\cos\phi&\sin\phi\\-\sin\phi&\cos\phi\end{smallmatrix}\right)$. With $\ell,n$ fixed by the gauge, $\phi=O(1)$ is the remaining local $U(1)$ angle. The three families of fixed coefficients are $\lambda^{01}=-\partial_r\xi^r$, $\lambda^{0i}=e^{2B}E_i^a\partial_a f$ and $\lambda^{1i}=E_i^a(\partial_a\xi^r-\tfrac V{2r}\partial_a f)$; setting the undetermined $\lambda^{23}$ to zero is not the same as freezing $\phi$. The angle variation follows by varying $E=R(\phi)\underline E[\gamma]$ and projecting $\delta R\,R^{-1}$ onto the rotation matrix:

$$\delta_X\phi=\xi[\phi]-A_\xi+\lambda^{23},\qquad A_\xi=\underline E_2^b(\mathcal L_{Y+I}\underline E_{3b}-\partial_b f\,U^c\underline E_{3c}).$$

Thus $\lambda^{23}=A_\xi-\xi[\phi]+\mu^{23}$ gives $\delta_X\phi=\mu^{23}$. Since independent $\mu^{23}$ are constant in field space, the commutator fixes the comparison angle and has $\mu^{23}_{12}=0$, giving the direct sum with the $U(1)$ parameter algebra. Alternatively calculate the Lorentz coefficient of $\delta_\xi e_0-\mathcal L_\xi e_0$ at $\phi=0$ (Eq. 4.34); conjugating rotates the dyad and the inhomogeneous term is precisely $-\xi[\phi]$. Mathematica reproduced the triangular dyad inverse metric, its linearized metric relation, and the sign of $\partial_\phi R\,R^{-1}$.

**Two equation-level caveats in v1.** (1) The all-orders determinant assertion of Eq. (4.3), PDF p. 15, does not follow with independent trace-free $E^1$ as written. With $q=I$ at a point and $t=1/r$, the normalized determinant of $\sqrt{1+t^2\operatorname{tr}(C^2)/2}\,I+tC+t^3 E^1$ has coefficient $-\operatorname{tr}(CE^1)$ at $t^4$ (Sage polynomial check). To enforce the determinant identically the square root can instead involve the square of the **full** trace-free matrix $T=tC+t^3E^1+\cdots$. Use the exact determinant gauge (4.2) for the lift; do not take the printed all-orders ansatz or dependent subleading coefficients as verified. (2) Eq. (4.30), PDF p. 20, appears to have the opposite overall sign from the $23$ component of the commutator in (2.1) with the index convention of (2.2), using $\lambda^I{}_J=\lambda^{IK}\eta_{KJ}$ and $[L,M]=LM-ML$. Sage gives $[L_1,L_2]^{23}=\lambda_1^{02}\lambda_2^{13}+\lambda_1^{12}\lambda_2^{03}-(1\leftrightarrow2)$, and when both $23$ parameters are identically zero the field-variation terms cannot contribute to that component. The nonvanishing defect and the horizontal cancellation argument do not depend on this sign, but verify it before substituting the printed defect into a charge convention.

## Use for first-order regional charges (Sec. 5, PDF pp. 22–23)

Choose a reference coframe on the overlap and carry the comparison frame as sewing data (or state why its vertical transformations are degenerate). At fixed comparison frame, vary only $g$ to build $\hat\xi$, then insert it into the CPS charge construction with the field-dependent parameter subtraction. In the vault's convention $\omega=\delta\theta$, fix the boundary/corner improvement of $\theta$ separately and check integrability and flux. In an algebra of the form $\{Q_1,Q_2\}=Q_{[1,2]_*}+K_{12}$ the section eliminates the *extra Lorentz generator* $Q_{(0,\beta)}$, not the charge cocycle $K_{12}$. A useful test is to change the reference by $h[g]$ on one side only: the shift should be measured by the vertical generator $\operatorname{Ad}_\Lambda(h^{-1}\delta_\xi h)$, and cancels only if the matching prescription or the Lorentz charge makes it so. The paper discusses standard Palatini versus Holst-area charges for these tangent rotations, but does not compute the full first-order charge algebra. That calculation, generic nonlinear Bondi evolution, and global compatibility of reference sections were not checked here.
