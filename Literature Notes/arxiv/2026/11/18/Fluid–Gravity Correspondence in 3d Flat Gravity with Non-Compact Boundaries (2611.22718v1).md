The split of the strip symplectic form into time cuts and spatial endpoints is the directly reusable result. The local oscillator algebra does not quantize the endpoint sector. The radial map, although exact, is a response map on admissible cutoffs, not a map between different bulk solutions. Below, PDF and printed pages agree (p. 1 unnumbered), arXiv v1.

## From a flat connection to two cut currents (Secs. II–VIII, pp. 3–7)

The gauge algebra is $\mathfrak{isl}(2,\mathbb R)$ with $[L_m,L_n]=(m-n)L_{m+n}$, $[L_m,M_n]=(m-n)M_{m+n}$ and $[M_m,M_n]=0$. Work in radial gauge $A=b^{-1}(d+a)b$ with fixed $b=e^{r(M_1-M_{-1})/2}$; flatness descends to $da+a\wedge a=0$. Keep only $a=a^L L_0+a^M M_0$, with $a^L=2\mu\,dt+\mathcal L\,dx/2$, $a^M=2\nu\,dt+\mathcal P\,dx/2$. Their Cartan generators commute, so $\partial_t\mathcal L=4\partial_x\mu$, $\partial_t\mathcal P=4\partial_x\nu$ (not evolution equations for the sources). Locally $a^L=dT$, $a^M=dX$. The reconstruction gives $ds^2=dr^2-r^2dT^2+dX^2$. It is our checked locally flat metric: the only nonzero connection coefficients in $(r,T,X)$ are $\Gamma^r_{TT}=r$ and $\Gamma^T_{rT}=\Gamma^T_{Tr}=1/r$, with zero Riemann tensor for $r>0$. In boundary labels the Jacobian is $\mu\mathcal P-\nu\mathcal L$; require it nonzero. This allows physically different boundary data even when the local bulk curvature is the same.

For $\epsilon=2\zeta L_0+2\xi M_0$, $\delta_\epsilon a=d\epsilon$ gives $\delta_\epsilon\mathcal L=4\partial_x\zeta$, $\delta_\epsilon\mathcal P=4\partial_x\xi$. The time component gives $\delta_\epsilon\mu=\partial_t\zeta$, $\delta_\epsilon\nu=\partial_t\xi$. At fixed smearing, contracting the renormalized symplectic current with the gauge action makes it exact on shell since $D\delta a=0$; Stokes gives the cut variation and the spatial-end terms (Eqs. 13–15). With the **reduced** normalization of Eqs. (21)–(22), the charge is $\int(\xi\mathcal L+\zeta\mathcal P)\,dx$. Using $\{Q_1,Q_2\}=\delta_{\epsilon_2}Q_1$ gives $\{\mathcal L(x),\mathcal P(y)\}=4\partial_x\delta(x-y)$ and vanishing like-like brackets, for compact support or matched endpoints that kill the boundary term. Define $J_\pm=(\mathcal P\pm\mathcal L)/2$, $\mu_\pm=\mu\pm\nu$. Both orders of the mixed bracket have the same coefficient multiplying $\partial_x\delta(x-y)$, by antisymmetry and $\partial_y\delta=-\partial_x\delta$. Consequently $\{J_\pm(x),J_\pm(y)\}=\pm2\partial_x\delta(x-y)$ and $\{J_+,J_-\}=0$, while $\partial_tJ_\pm=\pm2\partial_x\mu_\pm$.

**Normalization to fix before transport to another CS phase space.** Direct evaluation in Mathematica of Eq. (14) with $\langle L_0,M_0\rangle=1/4$ as printed in Eq. (4), $k=2\pi$, and Eq. (17) gives **one quarter** of Eq. (22). The same factor enters going from Eq. (11) to Eq. (21). The displayed reduced formulas are mutually consistent with effective pairing one in those charge/potential expressions, or a compensating overall prefactor; this note keeps that reduced convention and does not infer its physical units from Eq. (4). Likewise, with $\kappa^{mn}$ the ordinary inverse of Eq. (4), direct contraction of the translation components in Eq. (23) gives $[dr^2-r^2(a^L)^2+(a^M)^2]/8$ (exact Sage check), not Eq. (25) without an additional constant metric normalization. Local flatness is unaffected, and the overall factor drops out if the induced metric is normalized by its own $xx$ component. Separately, the claim of uniqueness of the invariant form up to scale should be read as a choice, since an invariant Lorentz–Lorentz pairing can also be added to the mixed one; that is not included in this reduced model.

## The strip integral is not the equal-time form (Sec. XI, pp. 9–10)

In the reduced normalization, using $T,X$ and dropping field-space exact terms, the strip current is $-d(\delta T\wedge\delta X)$. Orient the strip by $dt\wedge dx$ on $[t_i,t_f]\times\mathbb R$. Stokes then gives

$$\begin{aligned}
\Omega_{\rm strip}&=\Omega_\Sigma(t_f)-\Omega_\Sigma(t_i)+\Omega_{\rm end},\quad \Omega_\Sigma(t)=-\tfrac12\int\delta T\wedge\delta\mathcal P\,dx,\\
\Omega_{\rm end}&=2\int_{t_i}^{t_f}\big[\delta T\wedge\delta\nu\big]_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\,dt.
\end{aligned}$$

Here the wedges between variations are field-space wedges. $L_0^{\rm cut}=2[T]_{-\infty}^{+\infty}$ and $P_0=2[X]_{-\infty}^{+\infty}$ are conserved when $\mu,\nu$ have matching values at the two ends. They are preserved by transformations whose smearings also match at the ends, but need not be fixed across all solutions. Conservation permits replacing $\Omega_{\rm end}$ by $-\delta X_0\wedge\delta L_0^{\rm cut}/2$, with $X_0=X(t_f,+\infty)-X(t_i,+\infty)$. Indeed $[\delta T\wedge\delta\nu]_{-\infty}^{+\infty}=(\delta L_0^{\rm cut}/2)\wedge\delta\nu(+\infty)$ and $\partial_t X=2\nu$. Fixing $X_0$ kills this term; just fixing the equality of endpoint source values does not. Shifting $\widetilde T=T-T(+\infty)$ moves $+\delta T(+\infty)\wedge\delta P_0/2$ into each cut form and the opposite time difference into the spatial-end form, leaving the strip unchanged. Inverting the local cut pairing gives $\{\widetilde T(x),\mathcal P(y)\}=2\delta(x-y)$ in the paper's order; then $\mathcal L=2\partial_x\widetilde T$ reproduces the current bracket on the local smearings. It is not a proof that the same inverse applies to constant smearings or independent endpoint variables.

## Source variations, fluid energy and allowed ensembles (Secs. IX–X, XIII–XIV, pp. 7–9, 13–14)

Normalize the induced metric by $W=(\mathcal P^2-r^2\mathcal L^2)/4>0$ so its spatial component is 1; take the orientation of the clock for $N>0$. Then $\sigma=-N^2dt^2+(dx+N^xdt)^2$ with $N=4r(\mu\mathcal P-\nu\mathcal L)/(\mathcal P^2-r^2\mathcal L^2)$ and $N^x=4(\nu\mathcal P-r^2\mu\mathcal L)/(\mathcal P^2-r^2\mathcal L^2)$. In the timelike/spacelike orthonormal frame write $T^{ab}=\rho u^au^b+p s^as^b+2j u^{(a}s^{b)}$. Its variation against the normalized ADM metric is $-\sqrt{-\sigma} T^{ab}\delta\sigma_{ab}/2=\rho\delta N-j\delta N^x$. With $\rho=(\mathcal P^2+r^2\mathcal L^2)/(8r)$ and $j=-\mathcal P\mathcal L/4$, Mathematica gave exactly $(\mathcal P\delta\mu+\mathcal L\delta\nu-\nu\delta\mathcal L-\mu\delta\mathcal P)/2$, the representative in Eq. (21a). Pressure is not fixed by varying only a unit spatial metric; the paper uses stress conservation and its constitutive choice $p=\rho$, giving a traceless fluid. Do not identify the sign convention of (52) with the vault's Hilbert variation without comparing the metric variable and whether the density is a boundary response or a bulk tensor.

At $r=1$, $\mathcal E=H_++H_-$, $\mathcal J=-H_++H_-$ and $H_\pm=\int J_\pm^2dx/4$. This proves $\mathcal E\ge|\mathcal J|$ algebraically; the strict regular branch requires $W=J_+J_->0$ pointwise in the chart, which is stronger than the integrated bound. $L^1\cap L^2$ is a robust sufficient condition for finite zero modes and energies, not a proof that all convergent improper integrals are absolutely convergent. $\dot H_\pm=\pm\int J_\pm\partial_x\mu_\pm dx$ need not vanish for general sources.

* Heisenberg-like: take spatially and temporally constant $\mu,\nu$ on each solution. The currents are stationary but the constants may vary between solutions. Taking $(\mu,\nu)=\nabla F(P_0,L_0^{\rm cut})$ closes the source one-form by equality of mixed derivatives. The particular potential representative (21a) vanishes if $d(P_0\mu+L_0^{\rm cut}\nu-2F)=0$; setting its integration constant to zero gives the degree-two formula in the paper and $\delta F=\mu\delta P_0+\nu\delta L_0^{\rm cut}$.
* Dirichlet-like: $\mu_\pm=v_\pm J_\pm/2$ with $v_\pm=N_\circ\pm N^x_\circ$ constant on each solution, giving $J_\pm(x\pm v_\pm t)$. The time derivative of $H_\pm$ is now an endpoint term proportional to $[J_\pm^2]_{-\infty}^{+\infty}$. The source form is $N_\circ\delta\mathcal E-N^x_\circ\delta\mathcal J$; expressing it as $\delta S/(2\pi)$ makes the associated flux vanish. With Eq. (21a) on this family, its representative is proportional to $\mathcal E\delta N_\circ-\mathcal J\delta N^x_\circ$; degree-one $S=\mathcal E h(\mathcal J/\mathcal E)$ solves the vanishing condition up to a constant convention for $S$. The first-law form is $\delta\mathcal E=(2\pi N_\circ)^{-1}\delta S+(N^x_\circ/N_\circ)\delta\mathcal J$ when $N_\circ\ne0$. This is a variational first law, not a count of microscopic states.

## Scattering and what the Weyl algebra leaves out (Secs. XII, XIV–XV, pp. 10–19)

For Schwartz $J_\pm$ the spatial scattering operator is $S_\pm=-\partial_x^2+(12\pi/c_\pm)J_\pm$ with real $c_\pm\ne0$. Writing $(\psi,\psi')$ makes the connection matrix traceless, hence the Wronskian and the determinant of transport are preserved. On the line compare coefficients at the two ends rather than taking a periodic monodromy: $\Xi=\lim M(x_R)^{-1}U(x_R,x_L)M(x_L)$ for the two free-wave columns in $M$. For real nonzero wave number, real-potential flux conservation gives $|T_{\rm sc}|^2+|R_{\rm sc}|^2=1$ in flux normalization. At wave number $i\kappa$, $\kappa>0$, the growing left $e^{-\kappa x}$ and growing right $e^{\kappa x}$ coefficients must vanish (so the coefficient-basis element $\Xi_{22}$ vanishes for a bound state in the paper's order). At zero wave number the plane-wave basis degenerates. The Dirichlet-like flow has $\partial_t S_\pm=\pm[v_\pm\partial_x,S_\pm]$ just by differentiation of the translating multiplication potential; this is a translation of the full-line operator, not a general assertion of isospectrality for arbitrary source histories.

One chirality at a time, set $j_0=\int Jdx\ne0$, $H>0$, $a=12H/j_0^2$ and $\Phi=C+j_0\tanh(az)/4$. The current $J=2\partial_z\Phi$ is $6H\,\mathrm{sech}^2(az)/j_0$; using $y=\tanh(az)$ gives $\int Jdz=j_0$ and $\int J^2dz/4=H$ (both checked in Mathematica). Taking $j_0=-\beta H^{1/3}$ and $c=\pi\beta^3/[2\ell(\ell+1)]$ with $\beta>0$ gives $a^{-2}(12\pi/c)J=-\ell(\ell+1)\,\mathrm{sech}^2 Z$, $Z=az$. The finite nonzero $c$ formula covers nonzero $\ell(\ell+1)$; the free case can be taken separately. Mathematica also reproduced the intertwining $H_\ell A_\ell^\dagger=A_\ell^\dagger H_{\ell-1}$ for $A_\ell^\dagger=-\partial_Z+\ell\tanh Z$. Applying it successively to a free plane wave yields only the original propagation direction at each end for positive integer $\ell$; generic strengths are not claimed reflectionless.

For local quantum observables smear with real compactly supported tests. $\sigma(\eta,\chi)=2\int(\eta_+\partial_x\chi_+-\eta_-\partial_x\chi_-)dx$ is antisymmetric by parts and has no kernel on that test space. The Weyl relations are $W(\eta)W(\chi)=e^{-i\sigma(\eta,\chi)/2}W(\eta+\chi)$ and $[Q(\eta),Q(\chi)]=i\sigma(\eta,\chi)$ in a regular representation. For Fourier convention $J_p=\int e^{-ipx}J(x)dx$, the derivative of the delta gives $[J_p^\pm,J_q^\pm]=\mp4\pi p\delta(p+q)$. Thus $a_p^\pm=J_{\mp p}^\pm/\sqrt{4\pi p}$ is the annihilator choice for $p>0$, with $[a_p,a_q^\dagger]=\delta(p-q)$. In normalized boundary time the positive generator is $d\Gamma(p\oplus p)$ on two chiral Fock spaces, with vacuum covariance the real part of the one-particle pairing and imaginary part $\sigma/2$. In Eq. (159), PDF p. 19, the second term prints a duplicate plus sector; it should be the minus-sector number operator to agree with (158). No constant smearing belongs to $C_c^\infty(\mathbb R)$: the zero modes in the strip analysis need their own algebra or sector prescription, not a Fock $p=0$ oscillator. No such extension or quantum cutoff flow is proved.

## The finite radial map and a sewing test (Sec. XVI–XVII, pp. 19–22)

Hold the CS fields and sources fixed and vary $r>0$. Write $s=(\mathcal P^2-r^2\mathcal L^2)/(8r)$ and $\tau=\ln r$. In the traceless orthonormal stress matrix, $\det T_{\rm frame}=\rho^2-j^2=s^2$. On the regular spacelike-cut branch $W>0$, $s=\sqrt{\det T_{\rm frame}}$. Differentiation gives $(\partial_\tau\rho,\partial_\tau s)=(-s,-\rho)$, $\partial_\tau j=0$: equivalently $\rho+s=\mathcal P^2/(4r)$ scales as $e^{-\tau}$ and $\rho-s=r\mathcal L^2/4$ as $e^\tau$. This integrates to the paper's boost with diagonal $\cosh\tau$ and off-diagonal $-\sinh\tau$ in $(\rho,s)$, preserving $\rho^2-s^2=j^2$. Mathematica reproduced ADM data, variations, invariant and integrated flow for this branch. The boost of the signed $s$ may continue algebraically beyond $W=0$, but there the ADM representative is singular and the positive root is $|s|$, not $s$. It is not a canonical statement about changing the cutoff of the full, unrestricted gravity theory.

For the vault, keep $\omega=\delta\theta$ and use the strip only to relate oriented cut forms plus end terms. First try sewing two finite intervals with the same $(T,\mathcal P)$ on the common end: their end integrals cancel by orientation if the endpoint clock data and allowed variations also agree. If matching only currents, keep the integration constants of $T,X$ and check the residual pairing rather than assuming cancellation. Only after that step reduce and quantize local modes. The paper's representative (21a), the transport of stress at fixed solution and the global choice of corner variables are three separate inputs; none proves the Harlow–Wu boundary prescription for a different variational problem.
