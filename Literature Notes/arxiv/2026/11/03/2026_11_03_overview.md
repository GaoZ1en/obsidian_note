### M3. Mutual Information to All Orders from Modular Flow: a Replica-Free Approach

[2611.03630v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03630v1) — **T1-boundary; T2-dS-BH-holography. Source-derived.** The source develops a long-distance expansion of vacuum mutual information between two disjoint CFT balls without replica-number analytic continuation. This is useful for distinguishing a controlled correlation expansion between separated regions from a canonical sewing map of phase spaces. Medium placement: it does not supply the current gravitational regional algebra or its continuum quantization, and neither the all-orders formula, analytic assumptions nor numerical comparisons were independently checked here.

### M4. First Law of Thermodynamics for Interacting Charged Black Holes and Applications

[2611.02663v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.02663v1) — **T1-charge; T1-boundary; T2-dS-BH-holography. Source-derived.** The question is how a mass-variation identity accounts for several electrically charged horizons and finite conical axis singularities. The authors work with stationary, axisymmetric, asymptotically flat Einstein–Maxwell families of fixed horizon type, explicitly retaining asymptotic and parameter-regularity assumptions. In the all-degenerate sector they define a corrected ADM-mass function whose angular derivatives are horizon angular velocities, report a positive angular Hessian at Majumdar–Papapetrou, and combine the variation identity with a separate conical-sign theorem to obtain a conditional monotonicity along increasing Weyl-gap paths. This offers a comparison for boundary/defect work terms and the assumptions behind integrating a first law. Medium placement: the multi-horizon/conical problem is less immediate than the selected single-family generator reconstruction; the Hessian and monotonicity claims remain unverified source results.

### M7. The Fluctuation-Dissipation Theorem and Dynamical Love Number in Schwarzschild Black Holes

[2611.00545v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.00545v1) — **T2-spectral; T2-dS-BH-holography. Source-derived.** The authors study a Schwarzschild black hole in the Hartle–Hawking state driven by an external massless scalar. A complex radial contour implements the Schwinger–Keldysh time path, and the source claims a multipole-by-multipole fluctuation-dissipation relation at arbitrary real frequency. Low-frequency matching then relates logarithmic conservative running at order omega squared to leading absorption at order omega; KMS analyticity fixes thermal noise, while the radial near-zone equation and matching determine the conservative logarithm. This is a useful comparison for separating retarded response, state-dependent noise and renormalized conservative data in the current mode-to-response chain. Medium placement: the problem is a thermal open Schwarzschild system rather than the present AdS closed-system spectrum; the contour, coefficients and matching have not been independently verified.

### M8. Spectral Instability of Charged Scalar Quasinormal Modes and Superradiance: Stabilizing the Unstable

[2611.03070v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03070v1) — **T2-spectral; T2-model. Source-derived.** The source compares quasinormal-mode migration with real-frequency superradiant amplification for charged massless scalars on RN and RN–dS. It moves a prescribed positive potential bump outward while retaining the radiative boundary conditions; smallness is stated in mass-scaled height. The reported RN least-damped positive-frequency branch can enter the superradiant band while staying damped, with weak changes to scattering amplification, whereas selected unstable dS monopoles become damped without immediately suppressing a large amplification peak. The useful distinction is between pole motion, modal stability and energy amplification; none can be inferred from another without the scattering problem and norm. Medium placement: these are open radiative BH spectra and a prescribed perturbation, not the current AdS canonical spectrum. The mode searches and numerical scattering results remain source claims.

The likely use is a bookkeeping reference when a regional cut moves independently of the physical matter flow: a change of integrated charge includes transport as well as physical flux. Medium placement: a vector-calculus transport classification is not yet a covariant gravitational symplectic potential or corner algebra.
