# Sewing a full defect theory to the local gauge sector

The concrete example is a relation between a $\mathbb Z_4$ BF theory and a $\mathbb Z_2$ BF theory, not a matching of equal labels across a cut. In a folded description the allowed electric labels and magnetic labels are mutual annihilators. The wall has no independent cohomological interface sector in the model, but fusing it with the physical boundary leaves an edge field. This is a useful finite test for separating **matching constraints**, **independent interface variables**, and **boundary gauge parameters that become physical**. All locators below are to [arXiv v1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.25012v1), with PDF page = printed page.

## From torsion to the modified physical boundary (§§1–2, pp. 3–20)

A $d$-dimensional relative theory sits on the physical end of a $(d+1)$-dimensional SymTFT slab. Choosing the other (topological) boundary determines the absolute theory. A genuine charged operator needs an allowed endpoint on both sides. An operator prevented from ending on the physical boundary leaves a dual symmetry generator with nothing on which to act. In bottom-up language one gauges a subgroup in a half-slab and fuses the resulting interface with the **physical**, not the symmetry, boundary. An ordinary boundary of the smaller SymTFT then becomes a modified boundary of the larger one (1.1).

For a four-dimensional internal $X$, first distinguish compactly supported $H^2(X,\partial X)$ from ordinary $H^2(X)$; use $j^*$ for the map between them. Compact support supplies dynamical Cartan fluxes; free ordinary classes modulo compactly supported classes give backgrounds for the center. If $\operatorname{Tor}H_1(X)\ne0$, UCT gives $\operatorname{Tor}H^2(X)=\operatorname{Ext}(H_1(X),\mathbb Z)$, dual by torsion linking to the relative $H^3(X,\partial X)$ in the stated setups. In §2.3 the authors keep differential characters rather than just curvature forms, since torsion curvature is invisible in real de Rham cohomology. With dual generators $\tau_2,\sigma_3$ of order $n$, choose $L(\tau_2,\sigma_3)=1/n$ modulo integers. Then $\check G_4$ has terms $\check C_2\star\check\tau_2$ and $\check b_1\star\check\sigma_3$, and $\check G_8$ terms $\check C_6\star\check\tau_2$ and $\check b_5\star\check\sigma_3$. The product of complementary classes followed by integration over $X$ and taking the cochain descendant gives (2.41):

$$S_7/(2\pi)=\frac1n\int_{M_7}(C_2\cup\delta b_4+b_1\cup\delta C_5).$$

The lower-case fields $b_1,b_4$ are summed over; $C_2,C_5$ are backgrounds before coupling to the slab. This is the paper's reduction prescription and assumes a suitable relative differential-character pairing (p. 17 n. 10); it is not a closed-manifold de Rham calculation. The orbifold excision example in §2.2 also gives the usual common-boundary path integral $Z_{\hat X}=\int D\phi_{\partial X}\,Z_{X_{\rm loc}}[\phi]Z_X[\phi]$; resolving the boundary label is extra work, not implied by that formal product.

When $H^2(X,\partial X)$ is free, its image lies in a chosen free part, and the discriminant form on the resulting finite $\mathbb D$ is nondegenerate, orthogonal complementation splits $H^2(\partial X)=\mathbb D\oplus\mathbb T$ (2.42–2.47). On the complement the restricted absolute torsion generators $t_k$ and lifts $s_\ell$ of relative classes pair by $\kappa_{k\ell}$. With $E$ the discriminant field and $C,B$ the respective $t,s$ fields, the 8d kinetic-sector action is $2\pi\int(D_{ab}E_2^a\delta E_5^b+\kappa_{k\ell}(B_2^\ell\delta C_5^k+C_2^k\delta B_5^\ell))$ in a choice of generators/transgression. Vary the combined bulk and physical boundary action in the $E$ and $C$ polarizations of §2.4: $E$ is matched to Cartan field strengths (via $D^{-1}$ in (2.55)); $B_2,B_5$ restrict to $\delta b_1,\delta b_4$. These fields admit endpoints on the boundary holonomies; there is no analogous condition for $C$. Changing polarization on the other end changes which of the corresponding symmetries acts trivially. In particular, a decoupled topological sector may still affect which defects have endpoints.

## How to compute a wall from $B$ (§3, pp. 21–25)

Cut $X=X_L\cup B\cup X_A$, where $X_L$ surrounds the singular locus and $X_A$ is the cylindrical end. $\partial_A B$ and $\partial_L B$ supply the two slab theories and $B$ compactifies to their common interface. Track the *maps*, not just group orders:

$$H^{n-1}(\partial B)\xrightarrow{d_{n-1}} H^n(B,\partial B)\xrightarrow{\iota^n} H^n(B)\xrightarrow{r_n} H^n(\partial B).$$

Tangential boundary data must lie in $\mathrm{im}\,r_n$. For the normal data, extend a boundary cocycle and differentiate the extension; this represents $d_{n-1}$. For a closed form the collar formula is $d(\chi(r)\alpha)=\chi'(r)dr\wedge\alpha$, with inward $r$ and $\chi=1$ at the boundary, zero off the collar. Two extensions differ by a relative cochain and produce cohomologous images. For torsion use the integral connecting map, not the real form alone. This is why contraction with $\partial_r$ and pullback to the interface are different matching operations in (3.8–3.9); the restriction arrow is from the bordism to its boundary as in (3.5). Exactness makes the unfixed groups $\ker r_n=\operatorname{im}\iota^n\cong H^n(B,\partial B)/\operatorname{im}d_{n-1}$. Zero image means pure matching; torsion can yield topological degrees of freedom; free classes can yield propagating or additional sectors, depending on the compactification and what survives the physical limit. This is a classification of the retained cohomological sectors, not a theorem about all local degrees of freedom on an arbitrary wall.

## The split example: the punctured toroidal orbifold (§4, pp. 25–30)

Excising the 16 $A_1$ neighborhoods from $T^4/\mathbb Z_2$ gives the space $X$ with 16 $\mathbb RP^3$ boundaries. Its six free two-cycles pair as three $U(2)$ lattices with matrix $\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&2\\2&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)$. The local model is three neighborhoods of the pairs of tori; each pair intersects twice and retracts to $T^2\vee T^2\vee S^1$. The local boundaries supply the discriminant $\mathbb D=(\mathbb Z_2)^6$ and the exterior boundaries have $(\mathbb Z_2)^{16}$ in $H^2$. With the maps of (4.18–4.22), the orthogonal complement $\mathbb T$ has rank ten over $\mathbb F_2$ (five restricted torsion classes paired with five relative lifts). The inverse hyperbolic block has off-diagonal $1/2$. Sage reproduced the Smith entries $(2,2)$ and the inverse, hence $\mathbb D$ and its pairing for all three blocks. The off-diagonal torsion pairing is also $1/2$ in dual bases; the self-pairing of the lifts in this example is absorbed by shifting $C_5$ (p. 29 n. 17), not assumed intrinsically zero.

The paper uses Mayer–Vietoris for $B\cup X_L$ (4.10), with $H_2(X_L)\to H_2(X)$ an isomorphism, then duality/UCT and boundary restriction to conclude $\iota^n=0$. The two boundaries of the bordism therefore transmit $\mathbb D$; $\mathbb T$ is present on the asymptotic side alone and the fused theory has the asymmetric $B,C$ endpoints above. This is a split kinetic-sector example. The ranks and restrictions were taken from the source geometry, not reconstructed from a triangulation; the existence of the proposed large-volume/low-energy limit yielding a local 7d QFT is open in the paper itself.

## The nonsplit example: frozen $D_5$ on a circle (§5.1 and App. B–C, pp. 31–37, 52–62)

Use the dual geometry $X=(Y_{I_2}^{\rm ell}\times S^1)/\mathbb Z_2$ (half-shift on $S^1$ combined with elliptic monodromy). The $A_1$ locus supports local $\mathfrak{sp}(1)\simeq\mathfrak{su}(2)$; resolve the exceptional curve to work with Cartan fluxes on $\hat X$, keeping the boundary unchanged. App. B tracks cycles by the mapping torus (coinvariants $\mathrm{coker}(f_*-1)$ and cycles fibred over the circle from $\ker(f_*-1)$), uses Mayer–Vietoris after excision, then converts homology to cohomology by Poincaré–Lefschetz duality. The key compact-to-ordinary map follows by chasing the relative boundary: with its cycle names (B.10–B.12), $\partial(a\tau(D)+b\tau(C)+LW)=a\tau(\gamma)+(b+2L)\tau(\alpha)$, with $\tau(\alpha)$ of order four. The kernel in these generators requires $a=0$, $b+2L=0\pmod4$; hence its generator is $2\tau(C)+W$. Dually this is $j^*_2(\omega_2)=2\eta_2+\tau_2$, with $2\tau_2=0$. In the quotient $\tau_2=-2\eta_2$ gives $4\eta_2=0$: the relation matrix has Smith form $(1,4)$, not $(2,2)$ as it would without the torsion component. Thus the local electric sector is a quotient $\mathbb Z_2$ of the $\mathbb Z_4$ label group, not a free choice of a second $\mathbb Z_2$. The degree-three sequence has $j^*_3(\omega_3)=2\eta_3$, with $r(\eta_3)=2x_3$ and connecting map $x_3\mapsto\sigma_4$ of order two. The local magnetic labels give an embedded $\mathbb Z_2$.

The paper chooses $\int\omega_2\eta_3=\int\omega_3\eta_2=1$, so the free intersection has $\int\omega_2\omega_3=2$ and its inverse gives $1/2$ for $\eta_2\eta_3$. With $f_2,f_4$ the dynamical Cartan fluxes, $\tilde E_2,\tilde E_4,\tilde C_2$ the boundary backgrounds, and $k_3$ the discrete relative-torsion field from reducing $dG_7$ on $\sigma_4$, this yields

$$S_6/(2\pi)=\int_{M_6}(2 f_2 f_4+\tilde E_2 f_4+\tilde E_4 f_2+\tfrac12\tilde E_2\tilde E_4+\tfrac12\tilde C_2\,d k_3).\tag{5.20}$$

Products in the finite sector denote cochain products with the indicated lift convention. Free classes removed from this calculation (5.6; App. C) include the twisted circle, generic fiber and associated fibred cycles; the source argues they do not pair in the quadratic kinetic reduction with the retained torsion. This does not exclude cubic anomalies: App. C identifies a possible instanton background coupling through the M-theory cubic term and leaves the mixed free/torsion pairing unresolved.

The pairing on the asymptotic torsion is $L(x_2,x_3)=1/4\pmod1$. Put $A_2=E_2+2C_2$ and $A_4=2E_4+B_4$, using integral lifts of the $\mathbb Z_2$ digits. Multiplying $A_2\delta A_4/4$ gives

$$\frac12 E_2\delta E_4+\frac12 C_2\delta B_4+\frac14 E_2\delta B_4 \pmod{\mathbb Z}.\tag{5.27}$$

The remaining term $C_2\delta E_4$ is integral. The digits are **not independent $\mathbb Z_2$ gauge fields**: flatness of $A_2$ requires $\delta E_2=0$ mod 2 and $\delta C_2=-\delta\tilde E_2/2$ mod 2 with integer lift $\tilde E_2$ in this last expression (not the background notation in (5.20)). This is the Bockstein/carry; adding low digits $1+1$ changes the high digit. App. A uses a lift with the opposite sign for the low magnetic digit (A.8–A.11); it also explains half-gauging, which fixes the anomalous combination rather than gauging two independent factors. Sage reproduced the mod-one polynomial for all digits, including the order-four pairing phase on the two unit labels. For the coupled boundary variation one absorbs the pure $\tilde E_2\tilde E_4/2$ contact into the bulk transgression in the authors' scheme. Keeping their orientation, the $E_4,E_2,C_2$ variations respectively set $E_2=-2f_2$, $E_4+B_4/2=2f_4$, $B_4=dk_3$ at $M_6$ (5.29). These are in the paper's continuum shorthand; the integral-lift/endpoint equations, not a free division by two modulo 2, should be used for discrete sewing. $C_2$ has no endpoint condition of the analogous kind. The $B_4$-endpoint phase is stored in the topological sector.

## The folded relation and fusion (§§5.2–5.3, pp. 37–46)

Excise a tube around the singular circle; its link is $(S^3/\mathbb Z_2\times S^1)/\mathbb Z_2$. In App. B, $H_1(B)$ has order-four generator $\beta$ and the local boundary torsion circle maps to $2\beta$; the asymptotic one maps to $\beta$. A degree-two torsion character evaluating $1/4$ on $\beta$ then restricts to $1/4$ and $1/2$, respectively. For degree three the relevant $H_2(B)$ torsion is order two, and the character of value $1/2$ restricts to the doubled asymptotic degree-three character and the generator on the local side. With boundary order $(A,L)$ the two restrictions in Table 2 are therefore

$$r_2(a)=(a,a\bmod2),\qquad r_3(b)=(2b,b).$$

The connecting maps in the same bases are $d_2(a,c)=a+c\pmod2$, $d_3(a,c)=a+2c\pmod4$. Thus the tangential $A'_2=A_2\bmod2$ and $A_4=2A'_4\pmod4$ follow from membership in the images of $r_2,r_3$; primes label local fields. The normal coefficients of (5.58) are determined by $d_2,d_3$ and the inward collar convention. The bulk/relative expansions use the same fields restricted from both ends, not a separate Hilbert space for each coefficient on the interface. Sage checked the displayed finite exact sequences and the following folded pairing (opposite orientation for the local side):

$$L_{\rm fold}((a,c),(b,d))=ab/4-cd/2\pmod1.$$

Indeed $L_{\rm fold}(r_2(a),r_3(d))=(a-(a\bmod2))d/2$ is integral. Testing $a=1$ gives the full annihilator condition $b=2d\pmod4$; testing $d=1$ yields $c=a\bmod2$. The product has order $4\cdot2=8$, maximal isotropic in the order-64 folded charge group. This explicitly checks compatibility of the two Lagrangian halves, independently of computing the free-cohomology sector. For the other degrees the paper's zero-image result (5.45) uses the geometrical maps of App. B. Charge labels of surface **characters** must use the dual maps: pullback of the nontrivial local $A'_2$ character gives the asymptotic character of charge $2$; restriction of asymptotic $A_4$ characters gives charge mod 2, so even ones are trivial on the local side. This is the precise nontrivial transmission statement behind the prose on p. 43.

Upon fusion, $A'_2=E_2, A'_4=E_4$ and the low digit $B_4$ satisfies Dirichlet while $C_2$ does not. Allow a boundary gauge parameter $k_3$ rather than setting it to zero: in a representative, $B_4|=dk_3$. Insertion into the boundary transgression of $C_2\delta B_4/2$ produces the same $C_2 dk_3/2$ term as the reduction. There is no conflict between an interface with zero independent cohomological fields and this edge degree of freedom after choosing/fusing a boundary condition. The boundary-condition variation and interpretation of the transgression are source results; the discrete pairing above establishes only the matching and annihilator statement.

At the other end of the slab (Table 3) electric fixes $(E_2,C_2)$, magnetic fixes $(E_4,B_4)$, and mixed fixes $(E_2,B_4)$. Electric Wilson lines see only the even characters of $\mathbb Z_4$, so the kernel of the one-form action is the order-two subgroup, with effective $\mathbb Z_2$. Integrating over the symmetry-boundary multiplier $\lambda_3$ gives $\delta A_2=0$ modulo 4, not two flat independent digits. Magnetic polarization gauges the full one-form group and uses the kernel $\exp(2\pi i\int A_2\cup A_4/4)$ (5.68); the resulting three-form symmetry is the full dual $\mathbb Z_4$. Sage verified $F_4 F_4^\dagger=4\mathbf1$ for $F_4(a,b)=i^{ab}$ and the order-four phase $i$ on unit labels, absent in a product of two untwisted order-two Fourier kernels. Gauging just the trivially acting subgroup gives the mixed case: both residual order-two factors act effectively, but their anomaly records the nonsplit extension. The finite checks concern labels and the local kernel, not manifold-dependent normalization factors or a full partition function on an arbitrary $M_6$.

## What to borrow for regional CPS sewing

With the vault's convention $\Omega=\delta\Theta$, opposite boundary orientations give $\Omega_A-\Omega_L$ on folded product data; here its discrete analogue is a difference of linking pairings. The test is to compute the matching relation and its annihilator before choosing a polarization or summing over common labels. Imposing separate $\mathbb Z_2$ sums would give the right number of elementary labels but the wrong kernel. For a continuous CPS application this is only a model: one would still have to derive the two cut potentials from the action and their corner terms, impose the correct matching and quotient genuine gauge degeneracies. The source's scope is the finite kinetic BF sector (not a full M-theory action or a gravitational seam); the relative differential-cohomology refinement, a proof of all geometric decoupling limits, and the cubic mixed anomaly are not independently established here.
