## Poisson bracket and L-infinity algebras

- arXiv: [2606.30630v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.30630v1)
- Categories: primary `hep-th`; cross-listed `gr-qc`, `math-ph`
- Reason codes: `T1-symplectic`, `T1-Wald-CPS`, `T1-charge`, `T3-math`
- Technical relevance: high. Author-signal relevance: none.
- Detailed note: [[Poisson bracket and L_infinity algebras (2606.30630v1)]]

Source claim: the paper rewrites the Peierls bracket of Lagrangian field theory in an $L_\infty$ language and shows that the causal propagator inverts the sigmoid-smeared symplectic operator on cohomology. The core formulas are the CPS operator

$$\begin{align}
\Omega=\frac12\omega\!\left(\delta\Phi,[Q_\Phi,\sigma]\delta\Phi\right),
\end{align}$$

the observable-to-Hamiltonian map

$$\begin{align}
\mathcal L_{\xi_F}\Phi=\Delta_\Phi^{\mathrm{causal}}f_\Phi,
\end{align}$$

and the Peierls bracket

$$\begin{align}
[F,G]=\omega\!\left(f_\Phi,\Delta_\Phi^{\mathrm{causal}}g_\Phi\right).
\end{align}$$

Main technical mechanism: observable characteristics $f_\Phi$ are localized $Q_\Phi$-closed states; $\Delta_\Phi^R-\Delta_\Phi^A$ turns them into Hamiltonian vector fields. Conversely, a symmetry $\xi_\Phi$ gives an observable characteristic $[Q_\Phi,\sigma]\xi_\Phi$, and the resulting charge algebra closes with central term

$$\begin{align}
C_{\xi,\gamma}=\omega(\xi_\Phi,[Q_\Phi,\sigma]\gamma_\Phi)\big|_{\Phi=0}.
\end{align}$$

Likely use case: this is directly reusable as a Peierls/CPS dictionary for notes where a smoothed Cauchy-slice operator replaces a sharp surface integral. The particle example also gives a compact central-extension model for charge-algebra checks.

Verification status: Mathematica checked the integration-by-parts identities in the Galilean example that give $[X^i(0),P^j]=\delta^{ij}$ and $[P^i,B^j]=m\delta^{ij}$ under $\int\sigma'=1$ and $\int t\sigma''=-1$. The full Claim 1 contraction, cyclicity signs, Peierls-complex homotopy, and $p$-adic contour obstruction remain source-derived.

## Asymptotic boundary structure of Lagrangian gauge theories

- arXiv: [2606.29587v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.29587v1)
- Categories: primary `hep-th`; cross-listed `math-ph`
- Reason codes: `T1-boundary`, `T1-Wald-CPS`, `T1-symplectic`, `T2-celestial-carrollian`, `T3-math`
- Technical relevance: high. Author-signal relevance: none.
- Detailed note: [[Asymptotic boundary structure of Lagrangian gauge theories (2606.29587v1)]]

Source claim: a local gauge theory with an asymptotic boundary defines a boundary gauge theory whose renormalized presymplectic and anomaly structures are extracted from a finite-order-pole bulk $Q$-cocycle. The two central maps are

$$\begin{align}
\mathcal R(\tilde\alpha)=b^*\frac{1}{p!}L_\nabla^p\alpha,\qquad \mathcal A(\tilde\alpha)=b^*\frac{(-1)^{\mathrm{tot}(\tilde\alpha)}}{(p-1)!}L_\nabla^{p-1}L_{\mathfrak d}\alpha,
\end{align}$$

where $\alpha=\Omega^p\tilde\alpha$ extends smoothly to the $Q$-boundary.

Main technical mechanism: $\mathcal R$ gives a finite renormalized boundary BFV structure, while $\mathcal A$ extracts the logarithmic/anomaly cocycle. For a bulk compatible presymplectic structure $\tilde\omega=d\tilde\chi$, the induced structures are

$$\begin{align}
\omega^{\mathcal R}=d\mathcal R(\tilde\chi),\qquad \omega^{\mathcal A}=d\mathcal A(\tilde\chi),
\end{align}$$

with corresponding boundary Hamiltonians obtained from $\mathcal R(\tilde H)$ and the explicit anomaly Hamiltonian. The examples cover AdS scalar/Maxwell anomaly actions and null-infinity magnetic Carrollian towers.

Likely use case: this is the most direct fit for asymptotic-boundary CPS notes. It gives a reusable distinction between counterterm-renormalized boundary phase space and the anomaly cocycle left by the logarithmic piece.

Verification status: source structure and definitions were checked against the TeX source. No xAct or Mathematica reproduction of the $R/A$ theorem, AdS scalar recurrence, Maxwell anomaly action, or null-infinity tower was completed in this run; those are recorded in the detailed note as source-derived or blocked.

The boundary term supplies the stationary thermodynamic curvature that the bulk canonical energy misses. Under positivity, properness, and zero-sector rigidity assumptions, path concavity gives the RII-type entropy bound.

Likely use case: this is a useful black-hole thermodynamics template for distinguishing bulk canonical-energy positivity from the asymptotic charge Hessian needed by an ensemble constraint.
