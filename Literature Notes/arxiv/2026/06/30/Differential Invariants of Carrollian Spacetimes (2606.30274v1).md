# Carrollian structure and the screen bundle

A Carrollian spacetime is a triple

$$
(M,g,K),
$$

where $M$ has dimension $d+1$, $g$ is a degenerate nonnegative quadratic form of rank $d$, and

$$
\ker g=\langle K\rangle
$$

is spanned by a nonvanishing vector field. The screen bundle is

$$
\mathcal U=TM/\langle K\rangle.
$$

The degenerate metric $g$ descends to a nondegenerate metric on $\mathcal U$. This is the paper's intrinsic replacement for introducing an ambient Lorentzian connection or an auxiliary rigging.

# Intrinsic torsion and higher endomorphisms

The first intrinsic torsion is

$$
q_1=L_Kg.
$$

It descends to a symmetric form on $\mathcal U$, and raising one index with $g$ gives an endomorphism

$$
g(Q_1X,Y)=q_1(X,Y),
\qquad X,Y\in\mathcal U.
$$

The paper calls $q_1$ nondegenerate when $Q_1$ has a simple spectrum, and strongly nondegenerate when its eigenvalues are also nonzero.

Higher tensors are obtained by repeated Lie derivatives:

$$
q_l=L_K^l g,
\qquad
g(Q_lX,Y)=q_l(X,Y).
$$

Trace invariants are then formed from words in the $Q_l$:

$$
I_\sigma=\operatorname{tr}(Q_\sigma),
\qquad
Q_\sigma=Q_{i_1}\cdots Q_{i_m}.
$$

For powers of $Q_1$, Cayley-Hamilton relations make traces of order above $d$ dependent.

# Canonical screen connection and normal form

Under nondegeneracy, the source constructs a canonical connection on the screen bundle. In dimension three, it is characterized by

$$
\nabla g=0,
\qquad
\nabla q_1=\alpha\otimes g+\beta\otimes q_1,
$$

for uniquely determined 1-forms $\alpha$ and $\beta$. In general dimension, the analogous condition is expressed using the span of powers of $Q_1$.

The paper then uses functionally independent invariants as coordinates. If $I^0,\ldots,I^d$ are independent, the structure can be written in normal form as

$$
g=\sum_{i,j=1}^dG_{ij}(I^0,\ldots,I^d)\,dI^i\,dI^j,
$$

and

$$
K=\sum_{i=0}^dH^i\partial_{I^i}.
$$

This solves the generic local equivalence problem in principle: the rational differential invariants are generated by the invariant coordinates, the metric coefficients, the vector-field coefficients, and the corresponding invariant derivations.

# Dimension three and rational frames

The physically common case of a three-dimensional Carrollian spacetime has $d=2$. The paper computes the number of independent second-order invariants and gives both an eigenframe presentation and a rational canonical frame.

The rational frame is built by choosing $\xi$ such that

$$
\xi\in\ker dI_1^1,
$$

and, in general dimension,

$$
\xi,Q_1\xi,\ldots,Q_1^{d-2}\xi\in\ker dI_1^2,
\qquad
dI_1^2(Q_1^{d-1}\xi)=1.
$$

The resulting frame is

$$
(\xi,Q_1\xi,\ldots,Q_1^{d-1}\xi,K).
$$

For note use, the main point is not the explicit frame but the hierarchy: first fix the screen quotient, then use intrinsic torsion to choose invariant directions, then express all remaining data as scalar invariants.

# Spencer cohomology and fundamental invariants

The final section computes the Spencer cohomology for the Carrollian $G$-structure. The two local roles to remember are:

$$
H^{0,2}=S^2\mathcal U^*,
$$

which is the intrinsic torsion sector, and

$$
H^{1,2},
$$

which contains the intrinsic curvature sector. This makes precise why $q_1=L_Kg$ is a fundamental invariant rather than merely a convenient derivative.

# Local use

For null-boundary and Carroll-frame notes, this paper gives a clean intrinsic ledger:

- start from $(M,g,K)$ rather than an ambient spacetime;
- pass to the screen bundle $\mathcal U$;
- record $q_1=L_Kg$ as intrinsic torsion;
- build higher $q_l$ and $Q_l$;
- use trace invariants and invariant derivations for classification;
- reserve ambient connection data for additional structure, not for the Carrollian structure itself.

This is directly adjacent to horizon/null-cut notes where the choice of $K$, its rescaling, and the screen quotient must be kept separate from extrinsic geometry.
