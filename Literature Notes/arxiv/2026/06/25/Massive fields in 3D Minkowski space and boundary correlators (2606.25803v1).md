# Useful point for this vault

This paper is a compact reference for how massive bulk fields can enter a two-dimensional Carrollian boundary theory at null infinity. The transferable object is not a boundary charge algebra. It is a representation-theoretic and integral-transform pipeline:

1. identify the global BMS$_3$ subgroup with the three-dimensional Poincare algebra;
1. use the Carroll boost charge $\xi$ as the boundary quantum number measuring the bulk mass;
1. construct a massive Carrollian primary wavefunction as a bulk-to-boundary kernel;
1. map momentum-space scattering amplitudes to Carrollian correlators by a generalized modified Mellin transform.

This is medium-priority because it is close to null-boundary/Carrollian holography, but it does not itself construct a CPS form, a boundary charge, or an edge-mode algebra.

# Carrollian primary data and the massive label

The source starts from the BMS$_3$ algebra

$$\begin{align}
[L_m,L_n]&=(m-n)L_{m+n}+\frac{c_L}{12}(n^3-n)\delta_{m+n,0},\\
[L_m,M_n]&=(m-n)M_{m+n}+\frac{c_M}{12}(n^3-n)\delta_{m+n,0},\\
[M_m,M_n]&=0.
\end{align}$$

The global sector generated by $L_{0,\pm1}$ and $M_{0,\pm1}$ is identified with $\mathfrak{iso}(2,1)$:

$$\begin{align}
L_0=J_{12},\qquad L_{\pm 1}=J_{01}\pm iJ_{02},\qquad
M_0=P_0,\qquad M_{\pm 1}=P_1\pm iP_2 .
\end{align}$$

A Carrollian primary on the cylinder is labeled by scaling dimension $\Delta$ and boost charge $\xi$,

$$\begin{align}
[L_0,\mathcal O_{\Delta,\xi}(0,0)]&=\Delta \mathcal O_{\Delta,\xi}(0,0),\\
[M_0,\mathcal O_{\Delta,\xi}(0,0)]&=\xi \mathcal O_{\Delta,\xi}(0,0).
\end{align}$$

The important observation is that the Poincare quadratic Casimir acts on such a primary as

$$\begin{align}
C=P^2=-(M_0^2-M_1M_{-1}),\qquad
[C,\mathcal O_{\Delta,\xi}]=-\xi^2\mathcal O_{\Delta,\xi}.
\end{align}$$

Thus a massive bulk scalar is expected to be represented by a Carrollian primary with nonzero $\xi$. In the local dictionary of this vault, $\xi$ is the boundary-side mass label, not a charge generated by a new boundary current.

# Electric-branch correlators

The paper reviews Carrollian two- and three-point Ward identities and isolates an electric branch that is useful for massive fields. For a two-point function

$$\begin{align}
G^{(2)}(u_1,u_2,\phi_1,\phi_2)
=\langle\mathcal O_{\Delta_1,\xi_1}(u_1,\phi_1)
\mathcal O_{\Delta_2,\xi_2}(u_2,\phi_2)\rangle,
\end{align}$$

the electric branch is localized in the angular difference and has a nontrivial exponential dependence on the Carrollian time difference. The source's two-point amplitude computation later reproduces the structure

$$\begin{align}
A_2(u_1,\phi_1;u_2,\phi_2)
\propto u_{12}^{-(\Delta_1+\Delta_2-1)}e^{-i\xi u_{12}}\delta(\phi_{12}).
\end{align}$$

This is the first place where the paper differs from the purely massless Carrollian amplitude story: nonzero boost charge produces the massive phase factor, while angular support remains ultralocal.

# Massive scalar kernel in Bondi variables

The bulk model is a free massive scalar on three-dimensional Minkowski space,

$$\begin{align}
S=-\frac12\int d^3X\left(\partial_\mu\Phi\partial^\mu\Phi+m^2\Phi^2\right),
\qquad
(\Box-m^2)G_F(X,X')=\delta^{(3)}(X-X').
\end{align}$$

In Bondi-like variables and momentum space the paper uses a massive on-shell parametrization adapted to the null-infinity angle $\phi$. A convenient way to record its structure is

$$\begin{align}
q^\mu(\phi)=(1,\cos\phi,\sin\phi),\qquad
\partial_\phi q^\mu=(0,-\sin\phi,\cos\phi),
\end{align}$$

$$\begin{align}
p^\mu(\omega,\phi)=(\omega+\xi)q^\mu(\phi)+i\xi\,\partial_\phi q^\mu(\phi).
\end{align}$$

With mostly-plus metric, this satisfies

$$\begin{align}
p^2=-\xi^2.
\end{align}$$

The mass is therefore encoded by $\xi$ in the boundary representation. The bulk-to-boundary propagator is obtained by taking one point of the Feynman propagator to future null infinity and rescaling the leading oscillatory behavior.

# Carrollian primary wavefunction

The same kernel is then recovered from symmetry. Let

$$\begin{align}
S=u-t+x\cos\phi+y\sin\phi,\qquad
R=-x\sin\phi+y\cos\phi .
\end{align}$$

The intertwining condition is that the simultaneous bulk Poincare and boundary Carroll action annihilates the kernel:

$$\begin{align}
\left(P_n^{\rm bulk}+M_n^{\rm bdry}\right)\psi_{\Delta,\xi}=0,\qquad
\left(J_n^{\rm bulk}+L_n^{\rm bdry}\right)\psi_{\Delta,\xi}=0,\qquad n=0,\pm1.
\end{align}$$

Solving these first-order equations gives

$$\begin{align}
\psi_{\Delta,\xi}(X;u,\phi)=C_{\Delta,\xi}S^{-\Delta}e^{\xi(R-iS)}.
\end{align}$$

Equivalently, the kernel admits the integral representation

$$\begin{align}
\psi_{\Delta,\xi}(X;u,\phi)
=\int_0^\infty d\omega\,\omega^{\Delta-1}e^{i(\omega+\xi)u}
e^{ip(\omega,\phi)\cdot X}.
\end{align}$$

Using $p^2=-\xi^2$, this wavefunction solves the source-free massive Klein-Gordon equation,

$$\begin{align}
(\Box-\xi^2)\psi_{\Delta,\xi}=0
\end{align}$$

up to the source's sign convention for the d'Alembertian and mass term.

# Boundary correlators from flat-space diagrams

The two-point contact diagram is

$$\begin{align}
A_2(u_1,\phi_1;u_2,\phi_2)
=\int d^3X\,\psi_{\xi_1}(X;u_1,\phi_1)\psi_{\xi_2}(X;u_2,\phi_2).
\end{align}$$

Substituting the integral representation of $\psi$ makes the $X$-integral impose momentum conservation. For a fixed massive label, the source states that this gives the electric-branch Carrollian two-point function.

For the three-point example, the source couples two massless scalars to one massive scalar,

$$\begin{align}
\lambda\int d^3X\,\Phi^2\Phi_m,
\end{align}$$

and computes

$$\begin{align}
A_3(u_i,\phi_i)=\lambda\int d^3X\,
\psi_{\Delta_1,0}(X;u_1,\phi_1)
\psi_{\Delta_2,0}(X;u_2,\phi_2)
\psi_{\Delta_3,\xi}(X;u_3,\phi_3).
\end{align}$$

After Schwinger or Mellin-like integral variables are introduced, the bulk integral again imposes momentum conservation. In angular variables $z_i=e^{i\phi_i}$, the constraints determine the saddle values $\omega_i^\star$ and the Jacobian

$$\begin{align}
J=\frac{(z_1-z_2)(z_1-z_3)(z_2-z_3)}{z_1z_2z_3}.
\end{align}$$

The result matches the electric-branch Carrollian three-point function from the Ward-identity analysis.

# Amplitude transform

The final useful formula is the transform between massive momentum-space amplitudes and Carrollian boundary correlators. Schematically,

$$\begin{align}
\left\langle\prod_i\mathcal O_{\Delta_i,\xi_i}(u_i,\phi_i)\right\rangle
=\int_0^\infty \prod_i d\omega_i\,\omega_i^{\Delta_i-1}
e^{\pm i(\omega_i+\xi_i)u_i}\,
\mathcal M_n(p_1,\ldots,p_n),
\end{align}$$

where the sign depends on whether the external leg is incoming or outgoing. This is the massive generalization of the modified Mellin transform used in the massless Carrollian amplitude literature.
