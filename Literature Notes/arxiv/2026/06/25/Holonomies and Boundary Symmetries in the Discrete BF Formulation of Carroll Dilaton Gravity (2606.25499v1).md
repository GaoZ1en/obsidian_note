# What the paper constructs

The paper gives a holonomy-based discrete BF realization of two-dimensional Carroll dilaton gravity. Its main point is not a new local bulk dynamics: the bulk remains topological. The useful construction is the boundary phase space obtained after the discrete flatness constraint removes local bulk degrees of freedom. Boundary links carry holonomies; residual gauge transformations that act nontrivially on those boundary links become physical boundary symmetries.

The mechanism is parallel to discrete BF/JT logic, but the algebra is the three-dimensional Carroll algebra rather than $\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb R)$. This changes the boundary-current story: the Carroll algebra is nilpotent, the invariant metric issue is avoided by an adjoint-coadjoint pairing, and the Virasoro-type sector is obtained only after reducing to a single effective abelian current.

# Carroll BF data

The finite algebra is generated by a temporal translation ${\tt H}$, a spatial translation ${\tt P}$, and a Carroll boost ${\tt B}$. The only nonzero bracket is

$$\begin{align}
[{\tt B},{\tt P}]={\tt H},\qquad [{\tt B},{\tt H}]=[{\tt P},{\tt H}]=0.
\end{align}$$

The gauge connection is

$$\begin{align}
\mathcal A=\tau\,{\tt H}+e\,{\tt P}+\omega\,{\tt B},
\end{align}$$

where $\tau$ is the temporal vielbein, $e$ is the spatial vielbein, and $\omega$ is the Carroll boost connection. The curvature is

$$\begin{align}
\mathcal F=d\mathcal A+\mathcal A\wedge\mathcal A,
\end{align}$$

with components

$$\begin{align}
\mathcal F_{\tt H}=d\tau+\omega\wedge e,\qquad \mathcal F_{\tt P}=de,\qquad \mathcal F_{\tt B}=d\omega.
\end{align}$$

The dilaton multiplet is

$$\begin{align}
\mathcal X=\mathcal X_{\tt H}{\tt H}+\mathcal X_{\tt P}{\tt P}+\mathcal X_{\tt B}{\tt B}.
\end{align}$$

The source emphasizes that the Carroll algebra is non-semisimple, so the BF action should not be read as relying on a nondegenerate invariant Killing metric. Instead, the action uses a pairing between adjoint curvature variables and coadjoint dilaton variables. In components the continuum action is

$$\begin{align}
\mathcal S =\int\left[ \mathcal X_{\tt H}(d\tau+\omega\wedge e) +\mathcal X_{\tt P}\,de +\mathcal X_{\tt B}\,d\omega \right].
\end{align}$$

Its equations of motion impose flatness of $\mathcal A$ and covariant constancy of $\mathcal X$. Thus the bulk has no local propagating degrees of freedom; the relevant phase-space information must sit at the boundary.

# Lattice holonomies and flatness

For an oriented link $\ell$, the discrete gauge variable is the holonomy

$$\begin{align}
\mathcal U_\ell=\mathcal P\exp\!\left(\int_\ell\mathcal A\right), \qquad \mathcal U_{\ell^{-1}}=\mathcal U_\ell^{-1}.
\end{align}$$

For a plaquette $f$, the discrete curvature is the ordered product around the face,

$$\begin{align}
\mathcal W_f=\prod_{\ell\in\partial f}\mathcal U_\ell.
\end{align}$$

The discrete BF action is

$$\begin{align}
\mathcal S_{\mathrm{disc}} =\sum_f\langle\mathcal X_f,\log\mathcal W_f\rangle +\mathcal S_{\mathrm{boundary}}.
\end{align}$$

The logarithm is taken on the branch connected to the identity. This is a real restriction on the configuration space: it is consistent with the classical flat sector, where plaquette holonomies stay continuously connected to $\mathbbm 1$.

Varying with respect to $\mathcal X_f$ gives

$$\begin{align}
\log\mathcal W_f=0 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \mathcal W_f=\mathbbm 1,
\end{align}$$

which is the lattice version of $\mathcal F=0$. Appendix C also expands a small plaquette:

$$\begin{align}
\mathcal W_f =\mathbbm 1+\int_f\mathcal F+\mathcal O(\varepsilon^3) =\exp\!\left(\int_f\mathcal F+\mathcal O(\varepsilon^3)\right).
\end{align}$$

Variation with respect to link holonomies gives the lattice covariant-constancy equation

$$\begin{align}
\sum_{f\supset\ell}\mathrm{Ad}_{\mathcal U_{\ell,f}}(\mathcal X_f)=0,
\end{align}$$

which reduces in the continuum limit to

$$\begin{align}
D_{\mathcal A}X=0.
\end{align}$$

# Boundary holonomies and affine Carroll algebra

The boundary is a closed chain of links labeled by $n=0,\ldots,N-1$, with spacing $\Delta\tau=2\pi/N$. A boundary link holonomy is

$$\begin{align}
U_n=\mathcal P\exp\!\left(\int_{\tau_n}^{\tau_{n+1}}a_\tau(\tau)\,d\tau\right), \qquad \tau_n=n\Delta\tau.
\end{align}$$

For small lattice spacing the link is written as

$$\begin{align}
U_n=\exp(\Delta\tau\,a_n),\qquad a_n=\frac1{\Delta\tau}\log U_n.
\end{align}$$

The affine boundary condition restricts

$$\begin{align}
a_n=\alpha^I\mathcal L_n^I{\tt T}_I,\qquad I\in\{{\tt H},{\tt P},{\tt B}\}.
\end{align}$$

Gauge transformations act by

$$\begin{align}
U_n\mapsto g_n^{-1}U_n g_{n+1}.
\end{align}$$

For $g_n=\exp(\lambda_n)$, the first-order Baker-Campbell-Hausdorff expansion gives

$$\begin{align}
\delta a_n=\frac{\lambda_{n+1}-\lambda_n}{\Delta\tau}+[a_n,\lambda_n].
\end{align}$$

Writing

$$\begin{align}
\nabla\lambda_n=\frac{\lambda_{n+1}-\lambda_n}{\Delta\tau},
\end{align}$$

the transformation law is the discrete affine-current transformation

$$\begin{align}
\delta a_n=\nabla\lambda_n+[a_n,\lambda_n].
\end{align}$$

For $\lambda_n=\epsilon_n^I{\tt T}_I$, the component transformations are

$$\begin{align}
\delta\mathcal L_n^{\tt H} =\frac1{\alpha^{\tt H}}\nabla\epsilon_n^{\tt H} +\frac{\alpha^{\tt B}}{\alpha^{\tt H}}\mathcal L_n^{\tt B}\epsilon_n^{\tt P} -\frac{\alpha^{\tt P}}{\alpha^{\tt H}}\mathcal L_n^{\tt P}\epsilon_n^{\tt B},
\end{align}$$

$$\begin{align}
\delta\mathcal L_n^{\tt P} =\frac1{\alpha^{\tt P}}\nabla\epsilon_n^{\tt P}, \qquad \delta\mathcal L_n^{\tt B} =\frac1{\alpha^{\tt B}}\nabla\epsilon_n^{\tt B}.
\end{align}$$

The boundary charge is

$$\begin{align}
Q[\epsilon] =\frac{{\tt k}}{2\pi}\sum_n\Delta\tau \left( \alpha^{\tt H}\epsilon_n^{\tt H}\mathcal L_n^{\tt H} +\alpha^{\tt P}\epsilon_n^{\tt P}\mathcal L_n^{\tt P} +\alpha^{\tt B}\epsilon_n^{\tt B}\mathcal L_n^{\tt B} \right).
\end{align}$$

The resulting affine bracket is source-stated as

$$\begin{align}
\{\mathcal L_n^I,\mathcal L_m^J\} =f^{IJ}{}_K\mathcal L_n^K\delta_{n,m} +\frac{{\tt k}}2\eta^{IJ}\nabla\delta_{n,m}.
\end{align}$$

Only the Carroll structure constants

$$\begin{align}
f^{{\tt B}{\tt P}}{}_{\tt H}=1,\qquad f^{{\tt P}{\tt B}}{}_{\tt H}=-1
\end{align}$$

contribute to the nonabelian part. This is the key structural difference from relativistic $\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb R)$ BF/JT: the nontrivial mixing is nilpotent and lands only in the ${\tt H}$ sector.

# Dilaton action under boundary currents

The boundary dilaton is

$$\begin{align}
x_n=\mathcal X_n^I{\tt T}_I.
\end{align}$$

It transforms algebraically,

$$\begin{align}
\delta_\epsilon x_n=[x_n,\lambda_n],
\end{align}$$

so no discrete derivative appears in the dilaton transformation. In components,

$$\begin{align}
\delta\mathcal X_n^{\tt H} =\mathcal X_n^{\tt B}\epsilon_n^{\tt P} -\mathcal X_n^{\tt P}\epsilon_n^{\tt B}, \qquad \delta\mathcal X_n^{\tt P}=0, \qquad \delta\mathcal X_n^{\tt B}=0.
\end{align}$$

The nonzero mixed brackets are

$$\begin{align}
\{\mathcal X_n^{\tt H},\mathcal L_m^{\tt P}\} =\mathcal X_n^{\tt B}\delta_{n,m}, \qquad \{\mathcal X_n^{\tt H},\mathcal L_m^{\tt B}\} =-\mathcal X_n^{\tt P}\delta_{n,m}.
\end{align}$$

Thus the affine Carroll currents act nontrivially only on the ${\tt H}$ component of the dilaton multiplet.

# Conformal reduction and Virasoro-type sector

The conformal reduction fixes

$$\begin{align}
\mathcal L_n^{\tt P}=\mu,\qquad \mathcal L_n^{\tt B}=0,
\end{align}$$

leaving a single dynamical current

$$\begin{align}
\mathcal J_n=\mathcal L_n^{\tt H}.
\end{align}$$

The source current algebra is

$$\begin{align}
\{\mathcal J_n,\mathcal J_m\} ={\tt K}\nabla\delta_{n,m}, \qquad {\tt K}=\frac{k}{2}\eta^{{\tt H}{\tt H}}.
\end{align}$$

The paper stresses that this is not the ordinary nonabelian Sugawara construction on the full Carroll algebra. After reduction, the current sector is effectively abelian, so the stress-tensor-like object is

$$\begin{align}
\mathcal L_n =\frac1{2{\tt K}}\mathcal J_n\mathcal J_n +\beta\nabla\mathcal J_n.
\end{align}$$

The action on the current is

$$\begin{align}
\{\mathcal L_n,\mathcal J_m\} =(\nabla\mathcal J_m)\delta_{n,m} +\mathcal J_m\nabla\delta_{n,m} +\beta K\nabla^2\delta_{n,m}.
\end{align}$$

The Virasoro-type bracket is

$$\begin{align}
\{\mathcal L_n,\mathcal L_m\} =(\mathcal L_n+\mathcal L_m)\nabla\delta_{n,m} +\beta^2{\tt K}\nabla^3\delta_{n,m}.
\end{align}$$

The central term is classical and comes from the improvement term. In the source's OPE language the effective classical central charge is

$$\begin{align}
c=2\beta^2{\tt K},
\end{align}$$

up to conventional normalization after quantization.

The reduced dilaton sector Poisson-commutes with the conformal generators:

$$\begin{align}
\{\mathcal L_n,\mathcal X_m^I\}=0,\qquad I\in\{{\tt H},{\tt P},{\tt B}\}.
\end{align}$$

So the dilaton becomes a conformal singlet after the conformal reduction.

# Local dictionary for current projects

The boundary/CPS analogy is:

1. The flatness constraint $\mathcal W_f=\mathbbm 1$ plays the role of eliminating local bulk gauge curvature, leaving global and boundary data.
1. Boundary holonomies $U_n$ are the actual phase-space coordinates, analogous to boundary connection data or Wilson-line variables in Chern-Simons/BF descriptions.
1. Residual gauge transformations are pure gauge in the bulk but physical at boundary links, matching the edge-mode logic that boundary-nontrivial gauge transformations can carry charges.
1. The affine current algebra is the boundary charge algebra before reduction.
1. The conformal reduction is a discrete Drinfeld-Sokolov-type reduction, but the source explicitly warns that the nilpotent Carroll algebra prevents simply importing the usual $\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb R)$ Sugawara story.
