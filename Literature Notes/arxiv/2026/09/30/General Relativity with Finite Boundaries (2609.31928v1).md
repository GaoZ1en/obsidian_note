# Gravity with a finite conformal boundary

A finite region with fixed boundary conformal class and mean curvature has dynamical boundary data in addition to its propagating gravitational modes. A perturbation locally generated by a diffeomorphism can survive the regional gauge quotient when its normal displacement is nonzero at the boundary. The thesis develops this statement through flat, de Sitter and anti-de Sitter examples, and constructs a Euclidean ensemble whose independent sources are the conformal inverse temperature and mean curvature.

The useful conclusions are conditional and sector dependent. Positive heat capacity is not a proof of Lorentzian stability, Euclidean positivity or nonlinear well-posedness. The saddle product for complementary de Sitter patches is not a quantum sewing theorem. Several printed formulas fail direct checks; these are isolated below rather than silently propagated.

## 1. The regional variational problem

Let a Lorentzian spacetime region $M$ have a timelike boundary $\Gamma$, outward spacelike unit normal $n$, induced metric $h_{mn}$ and
$$
K_{mn}=\frac12\mathcal L_n h_{mn},\qquad K=h^{mn}K_{mn}.
$$
Write $d=D-1$ and $h=e^{2\omega}\bar h$. The sources are $([h],K)$; the Weyl factor $\omega$ is not fixed. For fixed conformal structure an allowed variation is $\delta h_{mn}=2h_{mn}\delta\omega$, with $\delta K=0$.

The Lorentzian action is
$$
I_C=\frac1{16\pi G}\int_M\sqrt{-g}(R-2\Lambda)
 +\frac1{8\pi Gd}\int_\Gamma\sqrt{-h}K.
$$
For $D=4$ this is one third of the usual GHY coefficient. To derive its boundary polarization, start with $I_D$, whose on-shell covariant-metric variation is
$$
\delta I_D=-\frac1{16\pi G}\int_\Gamma\sqrt{-h}
 (K^{mn}-Kh^{mn})\delta h_{mn}.
$$
Since $I_C=I_D-(d-1)(8\pi Gd)^{-1}\int\sqrt{-h}K$, direct variation gives
$$
\delta I_C=-\frac1{16\pi G}\int_\Gamma\sqrt{-h}
 \left(K^{mn}-\frac Kd h^{mn}\right)\delta h_{mn}
 -\frac{d-1}{8\pi Gd}\int_\Gamma\sqrt{-h}\,\delta K.
$$
The first coefficient is trace free, so both terms vanish on the prescribed variations. This identifies the polarization and the volume density conjugate to $K$. It does not remove initial/final caps or settle arbitrary corner terms. The thesis discusses the corner angle and cites a vanishing Hayward coefficient for this boundary prescription; a complete regional corner symplectic construction is not supplied here.

**Checked:** the Weyl coefficients cancel algebraically. **Assumptions:** outward-normal convention above, Einstein action, smooth non-null boundary, variations compatible with the source prescription; caps must be handled separately. **Not verified:** a general corner completion or a BV–BFV boundary action.

The Cauchy data on $\Sigma$ obey the Einstein constraints. At $\partial\Sigma=\Sigma\cap\Gamma$, their compatibility with boundary data includes the corner angle and, in the examples, the pair $(\omega,\partial_t\omega)$. Chapter 1 distinguishes existence and uniqueness from continuous dependence. Its discussion of high tangential momentum instabilities prevents interpreting older conjectural well-posedness language in chapters 3–4 as an established general Lorentzian theorem. Euclidean ellipticity likewise does not imply globally unique saddles for all boundary sources; the thermodynamic chapters explicitly have multiple fillings.

## 2. Linear boundary conditions and the gauge quotient

In Gaussian normal coordinates $ds^2=dx_\perp^2+\bar h_{mn}dx^m dx^n$, let $g=\bar g+\epsilon H$. The conditions are
$$
H_{mn}-\frac1d\bar h_{mn}H^p{}_p=0,
\qquad
2\delta K=\partial_\perp H^m{}_m-2D^mH_{\perp m}-KH_{\perp\perp}=0.
$$
The derivative acts on the trace including the varying inverse background metric. Appendix B derives this from
$$
\delta K_{mn}=\frac12\partial_\perp H_{mn}
-D_{(m}H_{n)\perp}-\frac12K_{mn}H_{\perp\perp},
\quad
\delta K=\bar h^{mn}\delta K_{mn}-K^{mn}H_{mn}.
$$
A locally diffeomorphic perturbation $H=\mathcal L_\xi\bar g$ preserves the sources if
$$
\left(K_{mn}-\frac Kd\bar h_{mn}\right)\xi_\perp
+D_{(m}\xi_{n)}-\frac1d\bar h_{mn}D\cdot\xi=0,
$$
$$
(\partial_\perp K-D^2)\xi_\perp+\xi^mD_mK=0.
$$
An allowed regional gauge diffeomorphism must also preserve the manifold boundary, hence $\xi_\perp|_\Gamma=0$. A source-preserving normal displacement is a candidate physical variation, not an allowed boundary-preserving gauge transformation. Nonzero $\xi_\perp$ alone is insufficient: a Killing vector has $\mathcal L_\xi g=0$ and gives no metric perturbation. This matters in the $l=1$ sectors.

For spherical backgrounds, Appendix D uses
$$
ds^2=g_{ab}(y)dy^ady^b+r(y)^2\sigma_{ij}dx^idx^j.
$$
Scalar harmonics satisfy $\widetilde D^2Y=-l(l+1)Y$, vector harmonics can be built as $\star dY$, and their derived traceless tensors are absent or degenerate at $l=0,1$. The invariant variables $F_a,F,F_{ab}$ reduce propagating modes to scalar/vector master fields. Their gauge invariance under unrestricted local diffeomorphisms means they omit the physical displacement sector of the regional quotient. Low harmonics must therefore be reconstructed separately.

## 3. Flat boundaries, spherical spectra and controllable sources

For a planar Minkowski wall at $x=0$, $K=0$. Harmonic gauge imposes $\partial^\mu H_{\mu\nu}-\tfrac12\partial_\nu H=0$. At nonzero normal momentum $k_x$, solving the boundary and harmonic constraints leaves two transverse-traceless polarizations proportional to
$$
\sin(k_xx)e^{-i\omega t+i\boldsymbol k\cdot\boldsymbol y},
\qquad \omega^2=k_x^2+|\boldsymbol k|^2.
$$
Appendix C explicitly decomposes the symmetric tangential amplitude in a basis consisting of timelike $k_m$ and two spacelike polarization vectors. Gauge fixing removes normal amplitudes; the remaining trace and longitudinal constraints leave plus and cross components. At $k_x=0$, a further normal-displacement mode solves the null tangential wave equation. It is locally pure gauge but survives the boundary-preserving quotient. Compact rectangular boundaries quantize nonzero momenta and add compatibility requirements at their intersections; this construction is not a nonlinear existence theorem for arbitrary corners.

For comparison, Dirichlet data need not determine a regional solution. Appendix K supplies the Rindler perturbation
$$
ds^2=-z^2dt^2+dz^2+dx^2+dy^2,
\qquad H_{tt}=z(z-z_c)f(t),\quad H_{\mu\nu}=0\text{ otherwise}.
$$
It vanishes on $z=z_c$ for arbitrary smooth $f$. Choosing $f$ supported away from the initial slice gives vanishing initial perturbation as well. It is locally diffeomorphic through a vector with nonzero normal boundary value. **Checked with xCoba:** the complete first-order Ricci matrix vanishes for symbolic $f(t)$, and the induced perturbation is zero. This is a concrete linearized nonuniqueness example modulo boundary-preserving gauge. It does not establish the same pathology for every Dirichlet background; planar AdS is treated separately in Appendix K.

For a flat spherical wall of radius $R$, $K=2/R$. The nontrivial $l=0$ displacement satisfies
$$
(\partial_t^2-2/R^2)\xi^r=0,
\qquad \xi^r\propto e^{\pm\sqrt2t/R}.
$$
The regular $l=1$ diffeomorphisms are Killing deformations and give no additional metric perturbations. For $l\ge2$, both master equations are
$$
[-\partial_t^2+\partial_r^2-l(l+1)/r^2]\Phi=0.
$$
Regularity at the origin selects $\Phi\propto r\omega j_l(r\omega)e^{-i\omega t}$, with $j_l$ the **spherical** Bessel function. The source's notation $J_l$ must not be read as ordinary cylindrical Bessel $J_l$.

The vector boundary equation is $\Phi/R+\partial_r\Phi=0$. Setting $x=\omega R$ gives
$$
F_V(x)=(l+2)j_l(x)-xj_{l+1}(x)=0.
$$
The scalar boundary equation, with $L=l(l+1)$, is
$$
\left[\frac{L(2L-3)}{R^4}+\frac{4L-4}{R^2}\partial_t^2+2\partial_t^4\right]\Phi
+\left[\frac{3L-4}{R^2}+2\partial_t^2\right]\frac{\partial_r\Phi}{R}=0.
$$
Equivalently,
$$
\begin{aligned}
F_S(x)={}&[-4-4l+5l^2+7l^3+2l^4-2(l(3+2l)-1)x^2+2x^4]j_l(x)\\
&+x[4-3l(l+1)+2x^2]j_{l+1}(x).
\end{aligned}
$$
**Checked:** Mathematica at 40-digit working precision gives the $l=2$ root
$$
x=2.50459077149771662884+0.41324579233954661142i,
$$
with zero residual to the reported approximately 37-digit absolute accuracy. The real-coefficient/even-frequency structure yields the associated quartet. This reproduces a specific unstable mode, not a complete spectrum or proof of the large-$l$ law. The reported asymptotic imaginary growth $\operatorname{Im}(\omega R)\sim0.34l^{1/3}$ remains **Source-derived**.

Allowing a prescribed $\delta K(t)$ changes the $l=0$ equation to
$$
(\partial_t^2-2/R^2)\xi^r=\delta K(t).
$$
The retarded kernel $R\sinh[\sqrt2(t-s)/R]/\sqrt2$ shows how a pulse can tune a growing coefficient. This is externally controlled boundary data; it is not spontaneous stabilization of the fixed-$K$ theory. In higher harmonics, the forced response has the scalar spectral denominator $F_S$, so contour and initial-data choices matter when growing poles are present.

## 4. The conformal thermal ensemble

After Euclidean continuation, use
$$
I_E=-\frac1{16\pi G}\int\sqrt g(R-2\Lambda)
-\frac1{8\pi Gd}\int\sqrt hK,
\qquad Z(\widetilde\beta,K)\simeq\sum_{g_*}e^{-I_E[g_*]}.
$$
For $h=e^{2\omega}(d\tau^2+R^2d\Omega_{D-2}^2)$, $\widetilde\beta=\beta/R$ is conformally invariant. The scale $e^\omega R$ is determined by the saddle, not fixed as a Dirichlet source. At fixed $K$,
$$
E=\partial_{\widetilde\beta}I_E,
\quad S=\widetilde\beta E-I_E,
\quad C_K=-\widetilde\beta\partial_{\widetilde\beta}S
=-\widetilde\beta^2\partial_{\widetilde\beta}^2I_E.
$$
For $ds^2=e^{2\omega}[f(r)d\tau^2/f(R)+dr^2/f(r)+r^2d\Omega^2]$, smoothness at a simple horizon fixes $\beta=4\pi\sqrt{f(R)}/|f'(r_h)|$. With outward normal toward increasing $r$,
$$
K=e^{-\omega}\left[\frac{f'(R)}{2\sqrt{f(R)}}+\frac{D-2}{R}\sqrt{f(R)}\right].
$$
An exterior cosmic patch uses the opposite normal. Keeping this orientation explicit is essential when comparing or joining patches.

### Flat Schwarzschild box

Let $x=M/R\in(0,1/2)$ in the chapter's metric parameter convention. Then
$$
\widetilde\beta=8\pi x\sqrt{1-2x},\qquad
I_{\rm BH}=-\frac{4\pi x(2-3x)^3}{3GK^2(1-2x)},
\qquad I_{\rm flat}=-\frac{4\widetilde\beta}{3GK^2}.
$$
The turning point is $x=1/3$, $\widetilde\beta_{\max}=8\pi/\sqrt{27}$. Differentiating along a branch gives
$$
E=\frac{(2-3x)^2(3x-1)}{3GK^2(1-2x)^{3/2}},\qquad
S=\frac{4\pi x^2(2-3x)^2}{GK^2(1-2x)},
$$
$$
C_K=-\frac{8\pi x^2(2-3x)(2-8x+9x^2)}{GK^2(1-2x)(1-3x)}.
$$
The small branch has negative heat capacity; the large branch has positive heat capacity. The action crossing is $x=4/9$, $\widetilde\beta_c=32\pi/27$. **Checked:** all derivatives and the crossing by exact Mathematica algebra. The high-temperature result $C_K\sim8\pi^3/(GK^2\widetilde\beta^2)$ is the source's asymptotic interpretation; positive heat capacity alone does not eliminate Euclidean negative modes, as chapter 5 itself emphasizes.

### Three-dimensional de Sitter

Put $A=\sqrt{K^2\ell^2+4}-K\ell$. A pole patch has a regular origin, no horizon, free $\widetilde\beta$, and
$$
I_P(K)=-\frac{\widetilde\beta\ell A}{16G}.
$$
A cosmic patch contains the cosmological horizon and excludes the origin. Its horizon and boundary radii are
$$
r_c=\frac{\pi\ell A}{\widetilde\beta},\qquad
r_t=\frac{\sqrt2\pi\ell}{\widetilde\beta}\sqrt{\frac A{\sqrt{K^2\ell^2+4}}}.
$$
The bare cosmic action is $I_C=-\pi^2\ell A/(4G\widetilde\beta)$. Subtracting the pole patch at the **same** $K$ gives
$$
I_{C,\mathrm{reg}}=-\frac{\ell A}{16G}
\left(\frac{4\pi^2}{\widetilde\beta}-\widetilde\beta\right),
\qquad S=C_K=\frac{\pi^2\ell A}{2G\widetilde\beta}.
$$
**Checked:** the entropy and heat capacity follow exactly from the action. The phase crossing is $\widetilde\beta=2\pi$. A Cardy-form expression uses $c_{\mathrm{conf}}=3\ell A/(4G)$; this algebraic resemblance does not construct a microscopic CFT.

Pure de Sitter occurs at $\widetilde\beta_{dS}=\pi A$, where $r_c=\ell$. A complementary pole patch has **opposite** $K$, unlike the subtraction reference. Using bare actions,
$$
I_C(\widetilde\beta_{dS},K)+I_P(\widetilde\beta_{dS},-K)
=-\frac{\pi\ell}{2G}.
$$
**Checked:** exact symbolic residual zero. Thus the saddle weights multiply to $e^{A_H/(4G)}$ for the matching geometric pieces. No integration over boundary states, measure, determinant or Ward identity has been established. General matching of $([h],K)$ alone also does not identify the complete first and second fundamental forms of arbitrary fillings.

Appendix E compares Dirichlet thermodynamics at fixed physical radius: its cosmic-patch heat capacity is negative. Section 3.3.5 also examines a spherical rather than toroidal boundary; its semiclassical partition function does not simply fit the same effective-central-charge interpretation. That section has a printed normal-normalization problem, so its displayed geometry should be rederived before reuse.

### Four-dimensional de Sitter and Nariai

Use physical radii and $f(r)=1-2m/r-r^2/\ell^2$. For the cosmic patch set $m=r_c(1-r_c^2/\ell^2)/2$, and define
$$
A_t=r_t\ell^2-r_t^3-r_c\ell^2+r_c^3.
$$
Then
$$
\widetilde\beta=\frac{4\pi r_c\ell\sqrt{A_t}}{r_t^{3/2}(3r_c^2-\ell^2)},
\quad
K\ell=-\frac{4r_t\ell^2-6r_t^3-3r_c\ell^2+3r_c^3}{2r_t^{3/2}\sqrt{A_t}},
$$
$$
I_C=-\frac{\pi r_c(4r_t\ell^2-3r_c\ell^2-3r_c^3)}{3G(\ell^2-3r_c^2)}.
$$
The regular pole patch is a separate reference solution at the same $(\widetilde\beta,K)$. Subtraction changes energy by its vacuum term but leaves entropy unchanged. To differentiate without solving the source map explicitly, use
$$
\left.\frac{\partial I}{\partial\widetilde\beta}\right|_K
=\frac{I_{r_c}K_{r_t}-I_{r_t}K_{r_c}}
{\widetilde\beta_{r_c}K_{r_t}-\widetilde\beta_{r_t}K_{r_c}}.
$$
**Checked:** this yields $S=\pi r_c^2/G$ exactly in units $\ell=G=1$, with the dimensional factors restored above. The same Jacobian calculation reproduces the pure-dS heat capacity after taking $r_c=\ell$:
$$
C_K=-\frac{2\pi r_t\ell^2(2\ell^2-r_t^2)}
{G(\ell^3-4r_t\ell^2+2r_t^3)}.
$$
It changes sign through a pole at $r_t/\ell=0.258652022504\ldots$, $K\ell=-7.2015035592\ldots$. It tends to $-4\pi r_t\ell/G$ near the worldline and $2\pi\ell^2/G$ near the horizon. The derivative must be taken in the full fixed-$K$ ensemble **before** imposing the pure-dS curve; differentiating only along that curve gives the wrong heat capacity.

Black-hole patches use the smaller horizon $r_b<\ell/\sqrt3$, the opposite normal and the corresponding positive period. Their entropy is $\pi r_b^2/G$. Small black holes have negative heat capacity; the near-wall large branch has positive heat capacity. Appendices F–H organize algebraic branches, explicit black-hole formulas and numerical phase plots. The source's three-saddle phase diagram is restricted to static spherical fillings; it does not classify all Euclidean saddles.

In the Nariai scaling $r_c=\ell/\sqrt3+\epsilon$, $r_t=\ell/\sqrt3+\epsilon\rho$,
$$
\widetilde\beta_N=2\pi\sqrt{1-\rho^2}
=\frac{2\sqrt3\pi}{\sqrt{K^2\ell^2+3}},
\quad K\ell=\frac{\sqrt3\rho}{\sqrt{1-\rho^2}},
$$
$$
S_N=\frac{\pi\ell^2}{3G},\qquad
C_K=-\frac{2\pi\ell^2}{G(2-9\rho+4\rho^3)}.
$$
**Checked:** the denominator's interior root is $\rho=0.227452045612\ldots$. The numerical transition curves beyond these local checks remain **Source-derived**. The complementary pure-dS$_4$ saddle product is reported as $Z_C(K)Z_P(-K)=e^{\pi\ell^2/G}$; it has the same classical matching and quantum limitations as the dS$_3$ identity.

## 5. Lorentzian de Sitter dynamics

For $f=1-r^2/\ell^2$, the scalar/vector master equations are
$$
\left[f^{-1}\partial_t^2-\partial_r(f\partial_r)+\frac{l(l+1)}{r^2}\right]\Phi=0.
$$
The cosmic-patch solution uses the horizon branch
$$
\Phi=e^{-i\omega t}f^{-i\omega\ell/2}(r^2/\ell^2)^{i\omega\ell/2}
{}_2F_1\left(-l-i\omega\ell,1+l-i\omega\ell;1-i\omega\ell;\frac12-\frac\ell{2r}\right).
$$
The vector condition remains $\Phi/r+\partial_r\Phi=0$. The scalar condition is frequency dependent and includes fourth time derivatives. Write $s=r^2/\ell^2$, $L=l(l+1)$ and
$$
a_1=L(1-s)[3-2s-2L(1-s)],\quad
 a_2=4-2s-4L(1-s),\quad a_3=4-2s-L(3-2s).
$$
Then its boundary operator is
$$
\left(\frac{a_1}{r^4}+\frac{a_2}{r^2}\partial_t^2-2\partial_t^4\right)\Phi
+\left(\frac{a_3}{r^2}-2\partial_t^2\right)f^2\frac{\partial_r\Phi}{r}=0.
$$
**Source-derived:** numerical vector roots are decaying; scalar roots include growing modes. At large positive $K\ell$, the shear and sound branches behave as
$$
\omega_{\rm shear}\ell=-\frac{i[L-2]}{2(K\ell)^2}+\cdots,
\quad
\omega_{\rm sound}\ell=\pm\frac{\sqrt L}{\sqrt2K\ell}
-\frac{i[L-2]}{4(K\ell)^2}+\cdots.
$$
Additional scalar roots approach $\omega\ell=\pm i$. Appendix J relates their leading near-horizon form to locally diffeomorphic Rindler displacements, under an angular-gradient restriction. This suppression is a fixed-angular-scale expansion; chapter 5 describes a distinct high-$l$ sector with growth scaling like $l^{1/3}$. These limits must not be interchanged.

Appendix I finds $\omega R=\pm i\sqrt{2-R^2/\ell^2}$ for the physical $l=0$ displacement. The displayed $l=1$ vectors are Killing, so their imaginary coordinate frequencies do not describe nonzero growing metric perturbations. Pole-patch solutions instead impose interior regularity and have their own discrete spectra. Their full numerical roots were not reproduced in this run.

## 6. AdS boundary response and global modes

In four dimensions the conformal Brown–York tensor, defined relative to $\bar h$, is
$$
T_{mn}=-\frac{e^\omega}{8\pi G}\left(K_{mn}-\frac K3h_{mn}\right).
$$
It is trace free and the momentum constraint gives
$$
\bar D^mT_{mn}=-\frac{e^{3\omega}}{12\pi G}\partial_nK.
$$
Thus $T_{mn}\xi^n$ is conserved for a conformal Killing vector preserving $K$, with appropriate flux conditions at the edges of the charge surface. For $\Lambda=-3/\ell^2$, the Hamiltonian constraint is
$$
\bar D^2\omega+\frac12(\bar D\omega)^2-\frac14\bar R
-16\pi^2G^2e^{-4\omega}T^{mn}T_{mn}
+\frac16(K^2-9/\ell^2)e^{2\omega}=0.
$$
This is a boundary constraint coupled to bulk response, not an independent closed boundary field theory. In the Fefferman–Graham expansion, Appendix L obtains
$$
T_{mn}=\frac{\ell e^{\rho_c}}{8\pi G}
\left(g^{(2)}_{mn}-\frac13\operatorname{tr}g^{(2)}g^{(0)}_{mn}\right)
+\frac{3\ell^2}{16\pi G}g^{(3)}_{mn}+\cdots,
$$
where $g^{(2)}=-\operatorname{Schouten}(g^{(0)})$ and $g^{(3)}$ is transverse traceless. The curvature term generally diverges in the strict asymptotic limit; at a finite boundary it carries response. The inversion $e^{\rho_c}/\ell\sim\sqrt{R[g^{(0)}]/[4(K\ell-3)]}$ requires nonzero curvature and a real compatible branch. It cannot be used indiscriminately for planar $R=0$.

## 7. Planar AdS, black branes and the source for the Weyl mode

For $ds^2=\ell^2z^{-2}(dz^2+\eta_{mn}dx^mdx^n)$ and $z\ge z_c$, the outward normal is $-z\partial_z/\ell$. A normal displacement satisfies
$$
\delta K=\frac{z_c}{\ell}\partial^2\xi^z|_\Gamma,
\qquad \delta\omega=-\xi^z/z_c
$$
when tangential conformal Killing pieces are omitted. Thus fixed $K$ gives a boundary wave equation. The pair $(\delta\omega,\partial_t\delta\omega)$ supplies corner initial data. Appendix K constructs a boundary-vanishing gauge transformation to Fefferman–Graham gauge; gauge fixing does not erase the physical normal displacement.

In this gauge the general linearized metric has
$$
H_{mn}=\frac{\ell^2}{z^2}\left[
\widetilde\gamma_{mn}+\frac13\eta_{mn}\gamma
+\frac16(z^2-z_c^2)\partial_m\partial_n\gamma\right],
\quad \partial^2\gamma=0,
$$
where $\widetilde\gamma$ is transverse traceless, vanishes at $z_c$, and obeys
$$
[\partial^2+\partial_z^2-2z^{-1}\partial_z]\widetilde\gamma_{mn}=0.
$$
Appendix P excludes the polynomial and exponentially growing radial branches by the stated interior regularity requirement. The oscillatory solution combines $(1\pm iqz)e^{\mp iqz}$ to vanish at $z_c$, with $\omega^2=|\boldsymbol k|^2+q^2$. **Checked:** each radial branch solves the ODE exactly. This is a linearized planar result under those interior conditions; global spherical and black-brane instabilities cannot be inferred away from it.

A black brane with $f(z)=1-2GMz^3/\ell^4$ instead has a homogeneous Weyl mode
$$
\ddot\omega=\Omega^2\omega,
\qquad \Omega=\frac{3GMz_c^2}{\ell^2\sqrt{\ell^4-2GMz_c^3}}.
$$
The source gives a nonlinear embedding equation and a nonzero energy for this dressing. It decouples in the $M=0$ limit, and its coordinate growth rate requires time rescaling near the horizon. Appendix M treats the spherical black hole, but its printed linear frequency needs the correction below.

In Euclidean planar AdS, compactly supported or suitably decaying $\delta K$ sources
$$

\int d^3y\frac{\delta K(\boldsymbol y)}{|\boldsymbol x-\boldsymbol y|}.
$$
The sign follows from $\nabla^2(1/4\pi|x|)=-\delta^{(3)}(x)$ and $\delta\omega=-\xi^z/z_c$. Chapter 4.5 obtains a quadratic on-shell functional with an IR-divergent local $\delta K^2$ term and a nonlocal Coulomb kernel. At separated points its second derivative is
