## Medium-priority technical candidates

**[Cylinder Defect Casimir Energy and Weyl Anomalies](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.34529v1)** — primary `hep-th`; cross-list cond-mat.stat-mech, cond-mat.str-el, hep-lat, math-ph. **Source-derived.** The question is which defect anomaly determines a cylindrical Casimir observable. The source distinguishes a circular cylinder in flat space, with a displacement-anomaly logarithm, from the great cylinder produced by ambient radial quantization, whose vacuum energy involves the intrinsic defect anomaly and a fixed local anomaly convention. It tests these relations in free-field and holographic examples. Useful for separating embedding dependence, anomaly scheme and vacuum choice in boundary energy calculations; medium because this is a focused defect observable rather than a regional phase-space construction. `T1-boundary; T2-spectral; T3-math`.

**[Quantum vacuum interaction between semitransparent plates in a P\"oschl-Teller background: an exact relative TGTG formula](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.31866v1)** — primary `hep-th`; cross-list none. **Source-derived.** The paper asks how to define the interaction part of a one-loop vacuum energy in an inhomogeneous background. It constructs a background-dressed TGTG relative determinant for two delta-function plates in a one-soliton Pöschl–Teller potential, subtracting both isolated plate contributions in that same background. The claimed interaction depends on the individual positions as well as separation; for repulsive plates the normalizable zero mode does not produce an infrared divergence in this relative quantity. Useful as an exactly solvable model for subtraction and zero-mode handling; medium because its connection to gravitational boundary quantization is methodological. `T2-spectral; T1-boundary`.

**[The Hamilton--Jacobi and Symplectic Analysis for Extended Ho\v{r}ava Gravity](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.32218v1)** — primary `math-ph`; cross-list none. **Source-derived.** For the linearized non-projectable lambda-R model with the lowest-order BPS extension and a Cotton-tensor-squared term, the authors compare Hamilton–Jacobi and Faddeev–Jackiw reductions. They report matching generalized/fundamental brackets and derive a path-integral measure from the symplectic tensor, treating lambda equal to one third separately from the generic case. Useful for comparing constraint rank, degenerate couplings and the relation between reduced brackets and a measure. Medium because the construction is tied to a linearized preferred-foliation model and the abstract supplies no finite-boundary charge or sewing result. `T1-symplectic; T2-model; T3-math`.

**[dS/CFT Holography including Fermionic Fields](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.34073v1)** — primary `hep-th`; cross-list none. **Source-derived.** The question is how oscillatory late-time Dirac falloffs determine sources and a Bunch–Davies wavefunctional in dS/CFT. The source adds a Dirac boundary term, uses projections onto eigenspaces of the timelike gamma matrix, and reports agreement between path-integral and canonical two-point functions. Its proposed map associates one bulk Dirac field with a pair of boundary spinor primaries; conformal Ward identities and Yukawa couplings to principal- or complementary-series scalars provide further source checks. Useful for fermionic boundary polarization and source conventions. Medium because this concerns asymptotic dS wavefunction data rather than the finite-region gravitational phase space. `T2-spectral; T2-dS-BH-holography; T1-boundary`.

**[Two-horizon sector thermodynamics and NUT bi-hair in Kerr-Newman-NUT spacetimes](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.33032v1)** — primary `gr-qc`; cross-list hep-th. **Source-derived.** The authors test NUT thermodynamic state-space choices by combining inner- and outer-horizon entropies and inverse temperatures into sum and difference sectors. Within their explicitly restricted class of homogeneous state spaces, they report that the sum sector closes with electric and NUT charges, while the difference sector also needs angular momentum and the secondary quantity J_N = M N. Eliminating either NUT variable fails their sector-temperature and scaling tests. Useful as a warning that first-law consistency alone does not uniquely select a state space; J_N is an off-shell thermodynamic variable here, not a newly constructed asymptotic charge. Medium pending an independent CPS interpretation of those variables. `T1-charge; T2-dS-BH-holography`.

**[A gravitational realization of Courant-Hilbert deformations](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35452v1)** — primary `hep-th`; cross-list none. **Source-derived.** The source seeks a gravitational action for general Courant–Hilbert stress-tensor deformations, including T-Tbar and root-T-Tbar families. It proposes a two-dimensional massive-gravity sector depending on the relative-zweibein eigenvalue ratio, rewrites the matter sector through a rank-one spectral projector, and eliminates the projector before the remaining eigenvalue variable to recover the Russo–Townsend flow form. Useful as an auxiliary-field elimination model adjacent to today’s BV pushforward paper. Medium because classical on-shell elimination is the immediate result; a canonical/BV map or quantum measure is not supplied by this abstract. `T2-model; T3-math`.

**[Holography for integrable field theories](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.34909v1)** — primary `hep-th`; cross-list none. **Source-derived.** The paper develops a topological-string dual for integrable field theories constructed from four-dimensional Chern–Simons theory. A planar spectral curve encodes the generalized Calabi–Yau geometry; RG evolution is described as a geometric flow on its moduli. The authors claim an all-orders-in-planar-coupling description and report explicit one- and two-loop matches, including principal chiral models with a WZ term. Useful for connecting a concrete gauge-theory construction to geometric deformation data. Medium because the immediate target is planar integrability/RG flow rather than regional observables; Kevin Costello is a separate author signal, not the technical reason for promotion. `T2-model; T3-math; A-big-name`.

**[Cardy random matrix theory](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.31825v1)** — primary `hep-th`; cross-list none. **Source-derived.** The authors ask how much CFT spectral structure can follow from modular invariance alone in a maximum-ignorance matrix ensemble. They coarse-grain the modular constraint into fixed-width energy windows, construct a matrix potential for primary dimensions, and report Monte Carlo agreement with a Cardy density above the black-hole threshold together with local GUE statistics. The separation between window, intra-window and level-spacing scales is the useful feature for AdS3 spectral comparisons. Medium because this is a constrained ensemble model, not a derivation of a specific CFT spectrum or a completed three-dimensional gravity path integral. `T2-spectral; T2-dS-BH-holography`.

**[Localizing the M-Theory Path Integral](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.31829v1)** — primary `hep-th`; cross-list none. **Source-derived.** The source proposes localization of the local perturbative M-theory path integral to equivariant flux data. Relative equivariant extensions of eleven-dimensional Chern–Simons couplings determine the action, and a tangent–cotangent character supplies an M2-background one-loop determinant. It reports partition-function and twisted-index results for ABJM, an analytic Airy constant for V(5,2), and a Type-IIA all-genus expression. Useful for equivariant boundary data and determinant organization. Medium because the claim is a proposed local perturbative prescription with specialized supersymmetric backgrounds; it should not be read as a nonperturbative definition or general sewing formula. `T3-math; T2-spectral; T2-model`.

**[Perturbations of Kerr-Newman black holes: separation and mode stability](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.33661v1)** — primary `gr-qc`; cross-list none. **Source-derived.** For subextremal Kerr–Newman perturbations, the source addresses the coupling of radiative spin-one and spin-two fields using a matrix integrating factor for transformed first-order systems. After angular separation, it applies a Whiting-type transform to claim absence of real-frequency quasinormal modes for the radial equations. Useful for coupled Einstein–Maxwell spectral reductions. Medium because the asserted result is specifically mode stability on the real axis; it is not, by itself, nonlinear stability, decay, or exclusion of all upper-half-plane modes. `T2-spectral; T2-model; T2-dS-BH-holography`.
