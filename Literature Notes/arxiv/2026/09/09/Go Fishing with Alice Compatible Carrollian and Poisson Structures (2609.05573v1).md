# Why this is high priority

Reason codes: `T1-boundary`, `T1-symmetry`, `T2-celestial-carrollian`, `T3-math`.

The paper gives an intrinsic Poisson-geometric structure on Carrollian manifolds, including future null infinity and black-hole horizons. The anchor-image condition, symplectic-leaf decomposition, and distinction between local Hamiltonian flow and a merely anchor-generated null direction are directly relevant to the vault's boundary and reduced-algebra questions.

# Direct verdict

**Incorrect as a collection of stated results.** Theorem 2.1, claiming that Carrollian one-forms form the sections of a Lie subalgebroid if and only if the Poisson tensor is regular, has a direct smooth counterexample. The Kerr example also uses the wrong outer-horizon radius. These errors do not invalidate the basic definition, global bivector decomposition, local-Hamiltonian theorem on nonzero leaves, or compatible-connection construction, but those surviving results must be cited separately rather than via the paper's complete key-results list.

# Definition and conventions

A Carrollian bundle is a principal $\mathbb R$-bundle

$$
\pi:M\to\Sigma
$$

with a degenerate metric $g$ whose kernel is the vertical line and a clock form $\tau$ with $\tau(\kappa)=1$. The clock gives

$$
TM=\mathsf V M\oplus\mathsf H M,
\qquad
T^*M=\mathsf V^*M\oplus\mathsf H^*M.
$$

A Carrollian--Poisson bundle $(M,g,\tau,P)$ satisfies

$$
\ker_m g\subset\operatorname{Im}P_m^\#
$$

at every point where $P_m^\#$ has nonzero rank. At rank-zero points the condition is deliberately vacuous. If $\mathcal L_\kappa P=0$, the structure is called Poisson-stationary.

This compatibility says the Carroll direction is tangent to every nonzero-dimensional symplectic leaf. It does not by itself say that $\kappa$ is generated by a closed one-form or by a globally defined Hamiltonian.

# Global decomposition

The clock splitting gives a unique decomposition

$$
P=\kappa\wedge E_{\mathsf H}+P_{\mathsf H},
$$

where

$$
E_{\mathsf H}=P^\#(\tau).
$$

Define the Carrollian one-forms

$$
\mathcal A_{\mathsf C}(M)
=\{\eta\in\Omega^1_{\mathsf H}(M):P^\#(\eta)\in\ker g\}.
$$

Then $P_{\mathsf H}^\#(\eta)=0$ for every $\eta\in\mathcal A_{\mathsf C}$. When the base dimension is one or two, this forces $P_{\mathsf H}=0$, so

$$
P=\kappa\wedge E_{\mathsf H}
$$

and the structure is minimal. Its nonzero symplectic leaves are two-dimensional and tangent to $\operatorname{span}\{\kappa,E_{\mathsf H}\}$.

# Carrollian one-forms and the failed iff theorem

The Koszul bracket closes on $\mathcal A_{\mathsf C}$:

$$
[\eta,\theta]
=\mathcal L_{P^\#\eta}\theta-\mathcal L_{P^\#\theta}\eta,
$$

because $P(\eta,\theta)=0$ and vertical vector fields close under Lie bracket. Thus $\mathcal A_{\mathsf C}$ is a Lie--Rinehart module.

Theorem 2.1 then claims that this module is the section module of a Lie subalgebroid $A\subset T^*M$ **if and only if** $P$ has constant rank. The reverse implication is false.

Take

$$
M=\mathbb R^2_{t,x},\qquad
g=dx^2,\qquad
\tau=dt,\qquad
\kappa=\partial_t,
$$

and

$$
P=x\,\partial_t\wedge\partial_x.
$$

This is precisely the paper's Carrollian-plane construction with a coefficient that has zeros. It has rank two for $x\ne0$ and rank zero at $x=0$, so it is not regular. Nevertheless

$$
\mathcal A_{\mathsf C}(M)=C^\infty(M)\,dx=\Gamma(\mathsf H^*M)
$$

everywhere: $P^\#(f\,dx)=xf\,\partial_t$ is vertical, including at $x=0$. Lemma 2.1 supplies a smooth closed Koszul bracket and anchor on this rank-one subbundle. Therefore $A=\mathsf H^*M\subset T^*M$ is a Lie subalgebroid while $P$ is singular.

The proof's step “if $P_m^\#$ is zero, constant fibre dimension implies that $P$ is the trivial Poisson structure” fails because the Carrollian-one-form fibre dimension can remain unchanged across a rank $2\to0$ drop.

# Local Hamiltonianity

For a Poisson-stationary structure, every nonzero-dimensional symplectic leaf $S$ contains the $\mathbb R$ fibre and inherits a symplectic form $\omega_S$. Since

$$
\mathcal L_{\kappa|_S}\omega_S=0,
\qquad d\omega_S=0,
$$

Cartan's formula gives

$$
d(\iota_{\kappa|_S}\omega_S)=0.
$$

The Poincaré lemma therefore makes $\kappa|_S$ locally Hamiltonian. Global Hamiltonianity can still fail by the first cohomology of the leaf. At rank-zero leaves $\kappa$ is not in the anchor image, and the statement does not apply.

# Physical examples and formula errors

For the BTZ horizon $\mathcal H^+\simeq\mathbb R\times S^1$, the paper uses

$$
P=r_+^{-1}\kappa\wedge v^{\mathsf H},
$$

which is symplectic on the two-dimensional horizon.

For the Kerr horizon $\mathcal H^+\simeq\mathbb R\times S^2$, it uses

$$
P=\kappa\wedge u^{\mathsf H},
$$

where $u$ is the axial Killing field. This has rank two away from the poles and rank zero at the poles, so it is a natural singular Carrollian--Poisson example.

**Failed:** the paper writes

$$
r_+=M+\sqrt{M^2+a^2}.
$$

For Kerr, $\Delta(r)=r^2-2Mr+a^2$, and substitution gives

$$
\Delta\left(M+\sqrt{M^2+a^2}\right)=2a^2,
$$

not zero unless $a=0$. The correct outer radius is

$$
r_+=M+\sqrt{M^2-a^2},\qquad |a|\le M.
$$

This correction leaves the intrinsic bivector construction intact but changes the horizon geometry and its normalization.

For future null infinity $\mathscr I^+\simeq\mathbb R\times S^{d-2}$, the model

$$
P=\kappa\wedge L^{\mathsf H}
$$

uses a sum of commuting rotation generators. It is regular of rank two when the chosen Hopf-like field is nowhere zero, and singular at its poles otherwise. This is a selected Poisson structure on $\mathscr I^+$, not one canonically derived from radiative gravity or its covariant phase space.

# Compatible contravariant connections

A Vaisman connection $D$ differentiates one-forms along the Poisson anchor. The paper defines a strong Carrollian--Poisson bundle by requiring

$$
Dg=0,\qquad D\tau=0.
$$

Starting from a metric-compatible affine connection and using the clock splitting, it constructs

$$
D_\alpha\beta
=\nabla_{P^\#\alpha}\beta
-\beta(\kappa)\nabla_{P^\#\alpha}\tau
+B_\alpha^*\beta,
$$

where $B_\alpha$ is horizontally valued and skew with respect to $g$. This proves existence, but the free $B_\alpha$ and the initial affine connection show that the result is noncanonical.

# Contravariant geodesics

An admissible cotangent curve $c(s)$ above $\gamma(s)$ obeys

$$
P^\#(c(s))=\dot\gamma(s),
$$

and is a contravariant geodesic if

$$
D_{c(s)}c(s)=0.
$$

For a strong minimal structure, a nontrivial geodesic remains along a Carroll fibre if and only if its initial anchor velocity is nonzero and vertical and $P_m\ne0$. The minimality condition is essential: with $P_{\mathsf H}\ne0$, membership in $\ker P_{\mathsf H}^\#$ need not propagate from initial data.

# Independent checks

**Checked (rendered PDF, TeX source, Sage, and direct geometry):**

- The rendered page and source agree on the statement of Theorem 2.1 and its regularity iff claim.
- For $P=x\partial_t\wedge\partial_x$, Sage confirms generic rank two and rank zero at $x=0$, while the anchor of $dx$ is vertical for every $x$. This supplies the explicit counterexample above.
- For the claimed Kerr radius, Sage gives the exact residual $2a^2$ in $\Delta$; the standard $M+\sqrt{M^2-a^2}$ gives exact zero.
- In the nonminimal torus example, Sage verifies for odd $n=1,3,5,7,9$ that the alternating covector has zero horizontal-anchor coefficients, reproducing the finite-sum cancellation used in the paper.
- The minimal Poisson condition follows directly from
  $$
  [P,P]_{SN}=-2\kappa\wedge E_{\mathsf H}\wedge[\kappa,E_{\mathsf H}]=0,
  $$
  which is precisely Frobenius closure of the rank-two characteristic distribution away from zeros.

**Source-derived:** the full leaf-bundle proposition, the general compatible-Vaisman parameterization, and propagation argument for minimal contravariant geodesics.

**Not established:** deformation quantization preserving $\kappa$ as an inner derivation, representation theory of the quantized algebra, integrability to a Carrollian symplectic groupoid, or any observable-algebra sewing result.

# Translation to the vault programme

The useful structural distinction is

$$
\kappa\in\operatorname{Im}P^\#
\quad\not\Rightarrow\quad
\kappa=P^\#(dh)\ \text{globally}.
$$

Anchor accessibility, local Hamiltonian generation, and global Hamiltonian generation are separate claims. Likewise, singular rank-zero points cannot be discarded: the Kerr and even-dimensional $\mathscr I^+$ examples place them exactly at geometrically distinguished poles.

Because Theorem 2.1 fails at such rank jumps, no Lie-algebroid or quantization conclusion should be imported solely from regularity language. One must inspect the actual submodule fibres and bracket closure. The paper provides boundary Poisson geometry, not the covariant phase-space bracket of gravity and not a regional observable-algebra construction.
