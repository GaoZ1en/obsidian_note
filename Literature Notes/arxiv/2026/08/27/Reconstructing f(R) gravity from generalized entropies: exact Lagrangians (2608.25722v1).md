# Branch data and notation

The theory is four-dimensional metric (f(R)) gravity with signature and curvature conventions implicit in the standard Schwarzschild-de Sitter form

$$
ds^2=-h(r)dt^2+h(r)^{-1}dr^2+r^2d\Omega^2,
\qquad
h(r)=1-\frac{2Gm}{r}-\frac{Rr^2}{12}.
$$

The horizon equation

$$
\frac{R}{12}r_h^3-r_h+2Gm=0
$$

does not define a unique (A(R)) unless the mass behavior is specified.

| branch | choice | area-curvature map | control |
|---|---|---|---|
| maximally symmetric | (m=0), cosmological horizon | (A=48\pi/R) | exact in (R) and entropy deformation |
| fixed mass | (m=M), expansion near (R=0) | (A=A_0(1+cR)+O(R^2)) | first order in curvature |

For the exact branch,

$$
s\equiv\frac{A}{4G}=\frac{\sigma}{R},
\qquad
\sigma\equiv\frac{12\pi}{G},
\qquad
\frac{ds}{dR}=-\frac{\sigma}{R^2}<0.
$$

For the fixed-mass map,

$$
s=s_0(1+cR),
\qquad
\frac{ds}{dR}=s_0c>0.
$$

This sign is the entire branch dependence of the local stability identity.

# Exact inverse Wald map

The Wald relation is

$$
f_R(R)=\frac{4GS(A)}{A}=\frac{S(s)}{s}.
$$

On (s=\sigma/R), (dR=-\sigma s^{-2}ds), hence

$$
\boxed{
f(R)=-\sigma\int^{\sigma/R}\frac{S(s')}{s'^3}\,ds'+c_1
}
$$

with (c_1) fixed by the Einstein limit. If

$$S(s)=\sum_k a_ks^{q_k},$$

then

$$
f(R)=\sum_{q_k\neq2}
\frac{a_k\sigma^{q_k-1}}{2-q_k}R^{2-q_k}
+\sum_{q_k=2}a_k\sigma\ln R+c_1.
$$

Thus (q=1) gives the Einstein term, (q>1) gives sublinear or inverse-curvature behavior, and (q=2) is logarithmic. This is a statement about the chosen de Sitter branch, not a branch-free entropy theorem.

# Representative exact Lagrangians

For Tsallis-Cirto (S=\gamma s^\delta),

$$
f_T(R)=\frac{\gamma\sigma^{\delta-1}}{2-\delta}R^{2-\delta}-2\Lambda,
\qquad \delta\neq2.
$$

The Barrow case is (gamma=1), (delta=1+\Delta/2):

$$
f_B(R)=\frac{\sigma^{\Delta/2}}{1-\Delta/2}R^{1-\Delta/2}-2\Lambda.
$$

For Renyi (S=\lambda^{-1}\ln(1+\lambda s)),

$$
\begin{aligned}
f_{\mathcal R}(R)
={}&\frac{R^2}{2\lambda\sigma}
\ln\left(1+\frac{\lambda\sigma}{R}\right)
+\frac R2\\
&-\frac{\lambda\sigma}{2}
\ln\left(\frac{R+\lambda\sigma}{\sigma}\right)-2\Lambda.
\end{aligned}
$$

For Kaniadakis (S=\kappa^{-1}\sinh(\kappa s)),

$$
\begin{aligned}
f_K(R)
={}&\frac{R^2}{2\kappa\sigma}\sinh\frac{\kappa\sigma}{R}
+\frac R2\cosh\frac{\kappa\sigma}{R}\\
&-\frac{\kappa\sigma}{2}
\operatorname{Shi}\left(\frac{\kappa\sigma}{R}\right)-2\Lambda,
\end{aligned}
$$

so

$$f_K(R)=R-\frac{\kappa^2\sigma^2}{6R}-2\Lambda+O(\kappa^4).$$

For (S=s+\alpha\ln s),

$$
f_{\log}(R)=R+\frac{\alpha R^2}{4\sigma}
\left(1+2\ln\frac{\sigma}{R}\right)-2\Lambda.
$$

# Stability derivation

Differentiating the master relation gives

$$
\boxed{
f_{RR}=\frac{ds}{dR}\frac{d}{ds}\left(\frac{S}{s}\right)
}.
$$

Therefore,

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
ds/dR<0 &\Longrightarrow f_{RR}>0
\text{ requires }d(S/s)/ds<0,\\
ds/dR>0 &\Longrightarrow f_{RR}>0
\text{ requires }d(S/s)/ds>0.
\end{array}
$$

On the maximally symmetric branch,

$$
f_R-Rf_{RR}
=\frac{S}{s}+s\frac{d}{ds}\left(\frac{S}{s}\right)
=S'(s),
$$

so

$$
m_{\rm sc}^2=\frac{S'(s)}{3f_{RR}}.
$$

The logical boundary is important: (S'(s)>0) does not by itself prove stability. It only converts (f_{RR}>0) into (m_{\rm sc}^2>0). One still needs (S/s>0), (f_{RR}>0), a real constant-curvature root, and the domain assumptions for the chosen entropy.

Examples on the exact branch are:

- Renyi with (lambda>0): (d(S/s)/ds<0), hence (f_{RR}>0), and (S'(s)>0).
- Barrow with (Delta>0): (d(S/s)/ds>0), hence (f_{RR}<0).
- Kaniadakis with (kappa\neq0): (d(S/s)/ds>0), hence (f_{RR}<0).
- Power-law entanglement (S=s(1-\mu s^\beta)): (f_{RR}>0) when (mu\beta>0), with the additional restriction (1-\mu(1+\beta)s^\beta>0).

# Fixed-mass branch and its regulator boundary

The fixed-mass horizon expansion is

$$
r_h=2GM\left(1+\frac{G^2M^2R}{3}\right)+O(R^2),
$$

which gives the stated (A_0) and (c). The main-text Renyi, Barrow, and Kaniadakis functions are resummations selected to reproduce the first nontrivial orders

$$O(\lambda),\qquad O(\Delta),\qquad O(\kappa^2),$$

respectively. Their finite-deformation behavior must not be confused with the exact Appendix-A quadratures

$$
f(R)=\frac{1}{s_0c}\int^{s_0(1+cR)}\frac{S(s')}{s'}\,ds'+c_1.
$$

Those quadratures are exact in the entropy deformation but still inherit the omitted (O(R^2)) terms in the area-curvature map. In particular, an apparent sign recovery in a main-text resummation outside its retained deformation order is not physical evidence against the exact monotonicity criterion.

# Weak-isolated-horizon charge

Introduce

$$
\phi=f_R(\chi),
\qquad
V(\phi)=\phi\chi(\phi)-f(\chi(\phi)),
$$

so the first-order action is

$$
16\pi G\,\mathcal A
=\int_M\phi\Sigma_{IJ}\wedge F^{IJ}
-\frac1{4!}\int_MV(\phi)\epsilon_{IJKL}
e^I\wedge e^J\wedge e^K\wedge e^L.
$$

On the constant-curvature backgrounds used here, (phi=f_R(R_0)) is constant. The paper states that the weak-isolated-horizon symplectic structure yields

$$
H_\eta=\mathcal N\oint_{S_\Delta}\phi\,{}^2\epsilon,
\qquad
\mathcal N=\frac1{4G}.
$$

Then

$$
H_\eta=\frac{A_\Delta}{4G}f_R(R_0).
$$

If the same horizon lies on the reconstruction curve (A=A(R_0)), the master relation makes this exactly (S(A_\Delta)). The displayed Renyi, Barrow, and Kaniadakis expansions are therefore algebraically consistent. The nontrivial Source-derived input is that this integral is the Hamiltonian of the residual boost with the stated phase space and normalization; the equality to entropy is no longer an independent formula check once that input and the inverse Wald relation are fixed.

# Translation into the vault's CPS and regulator language

- The map (S(A)\mapsto f(R)) is not intrinsic until a horizon branch (A(R)) is part of the data. This is analogous to fixing the allowed phase space and boundary conditions before interpreting a charge variation.
- The exact maximally symmetric construction and the fixed-mass construction are different regulated inverse problems. They cannot be compared as successive approximations to one universal (f(R)).
- Appendix A removes the deformation-parameter truncation but not the curvature truncation of the fixed-mass map. "Exact" must always carry that qualifier.
- The boost-charge identity is a finite classical CPS statement. It does not establish a quantum entropy count, edge-mode Hilbert space, or continuum factorization.
- The paper does not derive the generalized entropy microscopically. It asks which (f(R)) function reproduces an entropy supplied as input.
