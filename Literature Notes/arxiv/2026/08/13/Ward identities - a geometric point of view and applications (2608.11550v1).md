# Why this is medium priority

Reason codes: `T1-boundary`, `T1-symmetry`, `T3-math`.

This is directly relevant to the vault's boundary/CPS vocabulary because it isolates a central term as a geometric anomaly of boundary parametrizations. It is not a covariant-phase-space charge construction: the fundamental object is a probabilistic Liouville amplitude in Segal's bordism picture, and the generators act on boundary states. Its most reusable chain is

$$
\text{boundary deformation}
\longrightarrow \delta S_{\rm L}^0
\longrightarrow \text{Schwarzian contour term}
\longrightarrow \text{Virasoro Ward recursion}
\longrightarrow \text{descendant coefficients/BPZ}.
$$

The paper is medium rather than high priority because the active projects can reuse the anomaly and recursion structure, but not the paper's full probabilistic analytic framework without importing substantial Liouville-CFT machinery.

# Conventions and objects

The Liouville parameters are

$$
\gamma\in(0,2),
\qquad
Q=\frac\gamma2+\frac2\gamma,
\qquad
c_{\rm L}=1+6Q^2,
$$

and a vertex operator of momentum $\alpha$ has conformal weight

$$
\Delta_\alpha=\frac\alpha2\left(Q-\frac\alpha2\right).
$$

For an incoming boundary chart $\zeta_k$, a holomorphic boundary vector field is expanded as

$$
\zeta_k^*v
=-\sum_{n\in\mathbb Z}[v_k]_n w^{n+1}\partial_w.
$$

This minus sign is part of the mode convention. All contours in the anomaly formula use the incoming parametrization orientation, opposite to the boundary orientation induced from the surface.

The interacting generators obey

$$
[\mathbf L_n,\mathbf L_m]
=(n-m)\mathbf L_{n+m}
+\frac{c_{\rm L}}{12}(n^3-n)\delta_{n,-m}\,\mathrm{Id},
$$

with an independent commuting anti-holomorphic copy. Generalized primary and descendant states need not lie in the Hilbert space; they are analytic families valued in weighted form-domain duals.

# 1. Schwarzian boundary anomaly

Let $g=e^{\sigma(z,\bar z)}|dz|^2$ and define the projective connection

$$
T_g=\partial_z^2\sigma-\frac12(\partial_z\sigma)^2,
\qquad
\mathcal T_g=T_g(z)\,dz^2.
$$

For a smooth extension $V$ of a holomorphic boundary vector field, the holomorphic combination of anomaly variations is

$$
\mathcal D_VS_{\rm L}^0
:=\frac12\left(D_VS_{\rm L}^0-iD_{iV}S_{\rm L}^0\right).
$$

Theorem 3.1 states

$$
\mathcal D_VS_{\rm L}^0(\Sigma)
-\frac1{24}\sum_{k=1}^b[v_k]_0
=\frac{i}{24\pi}\sum_{k=1}^b
\oint_{\partial_k\Sigma}^{\rm in}
vS_{\zeta_k^{-1}}\,dz
-\frac{i}{24\pi}\int_\Sigma
\langle\bar\partial V,\mathcal T_g\rangle.
$$

If the vector field extends meromorphically to the filled genus-zero surface and is holomorphic near $\overline\Sigma$, then $\bar\partial V=0$ on $\Sigma$ and only the boundary Schwarzian remains.

## Derivation skeleton

The Weyl factor generated by the real flow of $V$ satisfies

$$
\left.(\partial_t\omega_t^V-i\partial_t\omega_t^{iV})\right|_{t=0}
=2\left(\partial_zV+(\partial_z\sigma)V\right).
$$

After differentiating the anomaly functional, the key local identity is

$$
\begin{aligned}
&(\partial_zV+(\partial_z\sigma)V)
\partial_z\partial_{\bar z}\sigma\,dz\wedge d\bar z\\
&\quad=d\left(
V\partial_z\partial_{\bar z}\sigma\,d\bar z
+VT_g\,dz
\right)
+(\partial_{\bar z}V)T_g\,dz\wedge d\bar z.
\end{aligned}
$$

Admissibility makes the metric flat on boundary collars. Stokes' theorem therefore leaves a boundary $vT_g$ integral plus the bulk $\bar\partial V$ correction.

Writing $r_k=\zeta_k^{-1}$, the cylindrical collar metric gives

$$
\sigma=\log|r_k'|^2-\log|r_k|^2,
\qquad
T_g=S_{r_k}+\frac{(r_k')^2}{2r_k^2}.
$$

The second term produces the zero mode,

$$
\oint v\frac{(r_k')^2}{r_k^2}\,dz
=-2\pi i[v_k]_0,
$$

which explains both the $1/24$ subtraction and the projective Schwarzian term.

# 2. Local Ward identity and recursion

The local Ward identity combines four inputs:

1. differentiability of amplitudes under analytic boundary deformation;
2. diffeomorphism covariance;
3. Weyl covariance;
4. the anomaly formula above.

For round incoming circles centered at $x$, define

$$
\mathbf D_{n,\mathbf z,\boldsymbol\alpha}(x)
=\sum_{i=1}^m\left[
-(z_i-x)^{1-n}\partial_{z_i}
+\left((n-1)(z_i-x)^{-n}
-(z_i-x)^{1-n}\partial_{z_i}\sigma_i\right)
\Delta_{\alpha_i}
\right].
$$

Then, for the disk building block,

$$
\mathcal A(\mathbf L_{-n}U)
=\mathbf D_{n,\mathbf z,\boldsymbol\alpha}(x)\mathcal A(U).
$$

For annuli and pairs of pants the same meromorphic vector field has Laurent expansions at the other boundary circles. The Ward relation consequently includes positive-mode insertions there. The sums truncate because

$$
\mathbf L_{k-1}\Psi_{\alpha,\nu,\widetilde\nu}=0
\qquad\text{for }k-1>|\nu|.
$$

This gives a finite recursion in descendant level. For analytic non-round boundaries, annuli are sewn onto round building blocks and the coordinate-change operators transport the recursion.

# 3. Matrix coefficients and vertex operators

When the primary coefficient is nonzero, normalized descendant coefficients factor as

$$
\frac{\mathcal A(\otimes_j\Psi_{\alpha_j,\nu_j,\widetilde\nu_j})}
{\mathcal A(\otimes_j\Psi_{\alpha_j})}
=\omega(\Sigma,\boldsymbol\Delta,\boldsymbol\nu)
\overline{\omega(\Sigma,\boldsymbol\Delta,
\widetilde{\boldsymbol\nu})}.
$$

The holomorphic polynomial is obtained recursively from the Virasoro relations; the anti-holomorphic sector commutes with it. The result is then identified with Shapovalov matrix coefficients of the formal chiral vertex operator. This is an algebraic equality of descendant coefficients after the probabilistic amplitude has supplied the primary normalization; it is not a new construction of the full non-chiral CFT Hilbert space.

# 4. Applications

## Smoothness of correlations

The paper removes a disk around an auxiliary zero-weight insertion $V_0(p)=1$ and rewrites the closed-surface correlation as a one-boundary amplitude evaluated on $\Psi_0$. Riemann--Roch supplies meromorphic vector fields whose values isolate derivatives with respect to selected marked points. The Ward identity turns each derivative into a finite combination of descendant amplitudes.

The iteration closes on the $C^\infty$-module generated by finite descendants. The crucial analytic input is Lemma 5.1: continuity and differentiability of the relevant amplitudes in a weighted space

$$
e^{-\beta c_-}\mathcal D(\mathcal Q),
\qquad
Q<\beta<s_\Sigma.
$$

The paper says this estimate was established implicitly in earlier work and recalls a concrete version. The finite Ward recursion is explicit, but the probabilistic weighted estimate was not independently reproduced here.

## BPZ equations

For

$$
\alpha_{r,s}=(1-r)\frac\gamma2+(1-s)\frac2\gamma,
$$

the singular vector at level $rs$ vanishes in the irreducible Liouville module. At level two,

$$
(\alpha^2\mathbf L_{-2}+\mathbf L_{-1}^2)\Psi_\alpha=0,
\qquad
\alpha\in\{\alpha_{1,2},\alpha_{2,1}\}.
$$

Applying the disk Ward identity yields

$$
\left((\mathbf D_1^{(x)})^2
+\alpha^2\mathbf D_2^{(x)}\right)\mathcal F_\alpha=0.
$$

In a flat affine chart, translation covariance changes $\mathbf D_1$ into $\partial_x$ on the full correlator. Dividing by $\alpha^2$ gives the standard equation

$$
\left[
\frac1{\alpha^2}\partial_x^2
+\sum_{i=1}^N\left(
\frac1{x-x_i}\partial_{x_i}
+\frac{\Delta_{\beta_i}}{(x-x_i)^2}
\right)
\right]\mathcal G_\alpha^{\rm flat}=0.
$$

At general level, the singular-vector polynomial becomes a differential operator of order at most $rs$ with principal part $\partial_x^{rs}$.

# Independent verification

## Checked

- **Mathematica:** using $\sigma_z=r''/r'-r'/r$, reproduced
  $$
  \partial_z^2\sigma-\frac12(\partial_z\sigma)^2
  =S_r+\frac{(r')^2}{2r^2}.
  $$
- **Mathematica:** expanded the differential-form coefficient in equation (3.11); both sides cancel identically after using $\partial_{\bar z}T_g=\sigma_{zz\bar z}-\sigma_z\sigma_{z\bar z}$.
- **SageMath 10.9:** for the level-two Shapovalov matrix in the basis $(L_{-2}v,L_{-1}^2v)$,
  $$
  F^{(2)}=
  \begin{pmatrix}
  4\Delta+c/2&6\Delta\\
  6\Delta&4\Delta(2\Delta+1)
  \end{pmatrix},
  $$
  verified $F^{(2)}(\alpha^2,1)^T=0$ and $\det F^{(2)}=0$ for both $\alpha_{1,2}=-2/\gamma$ and $\alpha_{2,1}=-\gamma/2$, with $c=1+6Q^2$ and the paper's $\Delta_\alpha$.
- **Algebraic consistency:** substituting $n=1,2$ into definition (4.7) reproduces the displayed $D_2^{(x)}$ and the standard flat BPZ operator after the global translation Ward identity.
- **Visual confirmation:** rendered PDF pages 12, 15, 20, 24--26 and checked Theorem 3.1, the round Ward operator, vertex-operator comparison, smoothness argument, equation (5.3), and the standard BPZ equation against the TeX source.

## Failed source check

Equation (5.3) displays

$$
\mathbf D_1^{(x)}
=-\sum_{i=1}^N\partial_{x_i}
+\Delta_{\beta_i}\partial_{x_i}\sigma(x_i,\bar x_i),
$$

where $i$ is free in the second term. More importantly, setting $n=1$ in the defining equation (4.7) instead gives

$$
\boxed{
\mathbf D_1^{(x)}
=-\sum_{i=1}^N
\left(\partial_{x_i}
+\Delta_{\beta_i}\partial_{x_i}\sigma(x_i,\bar x_i)\right)
}.
$$

Thus the printed equation is missing the sum brackets and has the wrong sign for the metric-derivative term. The subsequent flat-coordinate BPZ equation is unaffected because $\partial\sigma=0$ there, but the curved-coordinate form should not be reused as printed.

# Assumptions

- The probabilistic amplitudes exist under the stated Seiberg bounds, and their analytic continuation is used only where the corresponding generalized amplitudes are defined.
- Boundary metrics are admissible and cylindrical on collars; this is what removes the curvature term at the boundary in the anomaly derivation.
- The normalized matrix coefficient is considered only when its primary denominator is nonzero.
- The genus-zero pure-boundary formula requires a meromorphic global vector field that is holomorphic near $\overline\Sigma$; a collar-only field retains the bulk $\bar\partial V$ term.

# Not independently verified

- The GMC construction, sewing theorem, analytic continuation of generalized states, and weighted differentiability estimate in Lemma 5.1 were not computationally reproduced.
- The claimed uniqueness and all-level convergence properties of the formal chiral vertex operator were not independently proved.
- The general $(r,s)$ singular-vector coefficients were not generated beyond the level-two check.
- No claim is made here about a CPS symplectic form, Hamiltonian charge, self-adjointness, positivity, or Hilbert-space unitarity; those are not established by these Ward identities.

# Claim tier and reusable takeaway

**Achieved claim tier:** exact verification of the local Schwarzian identity, the level-two representation-theory input, and the operator mismatch in equation (5.3); source-level reconstruction of the Ward recursion and its stated applications. The global probabilistic analytic estimates remain literature-dependent.

For current work, the useful template is

$$
\text{central term}
=\text{boundary projective connection}
+\text{zero-mode correction}
+\text{bulk extension obstruction}.
$$

This is a good comparison point whenever a boundary charge algebra is said to acquire a Schwarzian cocycle: first identify the projective connection, then track the zero mode and the dependence on how the boundary generator is extended into the bulk.
