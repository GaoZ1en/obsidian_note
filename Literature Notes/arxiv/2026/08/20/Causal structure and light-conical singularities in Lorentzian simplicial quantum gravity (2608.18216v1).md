# Direct verdict

Correct under the following precise conditions: “abundant” and “entropically favored” refer to the probability distribution generated by the paper's constructive algorithm with its stated uniform coordinate/edge-squared samplings, random exploration order, finite cutoffs, fixed triangulations, and exclusion of null subsimplices.

The analytic claims are stronger than the statistical ones:

- a spacelike Regge bone is light-cone regular exactly when its circuit contains four light-ray crossings;
- other crossing counts give an imaginary Regge-action term whose sign depends on the branch/contour prescription;
- explicit three- and four-dimensional refinement sectors can carry yarmulke or trouser singularities on unbounded edge-length regions;
- realizability of individual simplices does not by itself guarantee a global time orientation.

The statement should be rewritten as:

> Light-conical singularities dominate the sampled histograms for the tested Lorentzian triangulations and sampling prescriptions; whether they dominate the Regge path-integral measure or survive a continuum limit remains open.

The source does not prove measure-independent genericity, path-integral divergence, continuum causal pathology, or failure of a continuum limit.

# Regge and causal definitions

A \(d\)-simplex is realizable when its edge data admit an embedding in flat Euclidean or Minkowski space. The recursive Cayley--Menger conditions require the appropriate sign of the signed volume squared and compatible signatures for all subsimplices.

In a Regge triangulation curvature is concentrated on codimension-two bones. With \(8\pi G=1\),

\[
S_R=\sum_b A_b\epsilon_b,
\qquad
\epsilon_b=\theta_{\rm flat}-\sum_{\sigma\supset b}\theta_{\sigma b}.
\]

For a spacelike bone the orthogonal plane is Lorentzian. Each projected wedge contains zero, one, or two light-ray crossings. Let \(\mathcal N_c\) be their total around the circuit:

- \(\mathcal N_c=4\): one future and one past light cone, the regular case;
- \(\mathcal N_c<4\): yarmulke type;
- \(\mathcal N_c>4\): trouser type.

The light-conical imaginary contribution is

\[
S_{\rm sing}
=-i\left(1-\frac{\mathcal N_c}{4}\right)\frac{A_b}{4G}.
\]

For the branch convention adopted in the paper, \(e^{iS}\) enhances yarmulkes and suppresses trousers. Reversing how the contour passes a branch cut can reverse this behavior.

# Constructive realizability algorithm

The algorithm grows a consistent geometry bone circuit by bone circuit.

1. Start with an embedded simplex or fixed boundary data.
2. Choose an explorable bone and cyclically visit its incident four-simplices.
3. When only a shared tetrahedron is known, sample the fifth vertex in Minkowski coordinates.
4. When two adjacent tetrahedra are partly fixed, project orthogonally to the bone and sample the remaining squared edge of the projected triangle inside its realizable interval.
5. Convert that triangle into a dihedral angle and reconstruct the missing four-simplex edge.
6. If previously generated assignments conflict, erase the minimal affected edge stars and revisit them.

This avoids the extremely inefficient rejection of arbitrary edge assignments that violate generalized triangle inequalities.

The algorithm is constructive for realizability, but its output distribution is not canonical. It combines:

- random orderings of bones and simplices;
- uniform vertex-coordinate sampling in \([-L,L]^d\);
- uniform squared-edge sampling in a finite allowed interval or a cutoff interval;
- a distribution over the different generation histories.

The appendix gives Jacobians for converting coordinate sampling to squared-edge measures, but the reported simulations do not reweight to Lebesgue measure. A four-dimensional Regge gravitational measure is itself unsettled.

# Statistical results

## \(\Delta_n\) circuits

The triangulation \(\Delta_n\) glues \(n\) four-simplices around one equilateral spacelike triangle. For ten runs of 1,000 geometrizations at each displayed setting, the histogram of \(\mathcal N_c\) is peaked near \(n\).

Thus, when \(n\) is appreciably larger than four, regular circuits are rare in this sampler and trouser-type circuits dominate. This is relevant to barycentric refinement, which increases the number of simplices incident on inherited bones.

When all tetrahedra are required to be spacelike, the distribution changes substantially and becomes more cutoff-ratio dependent; large sampling boxes can favor yarmulke sectors.

## Subdivided hypercube

For one triangulated four-dimensional hypercube with all edge variables dynamical, the same qualitative result appears: bones with circuit length four or six have crossing histograms centered near that circuit length. This is a finite triangulation statistic, not a continuum measurement.

# Unbounded sectors

## Three dimensions

A spine of \(N\) tetrahedra around a very long spacelike edge has only thin Lorentzian angles and hence a yarmulke singularity. Its leading action is

\[
S=2\pi i\,l+\mathcal O(\log l).
\]

Spike configurations with a large bulk vertex star project to a Lorentzian two-sphere boundary. Gauss--Bonnet forces the total leading imaginary contribution to be yarmulke dominated:

\[
S=4\pi i\,l+\mathcal O(l^0).
\]

Trouser bones can occur locally but are overcompensated when all bulk edges share the same leading length.

## Four dimensions

Four-dimensional spine configurations with one very long edge force the adjacent triangles to be timelike, so those triangles do not carry light-conical singularities.

Four-dimensional spike configurations with large spacelike bulk edges instead project to a Lorentzian triangulation of the boundary three-sphere:

\[
S^{4D}=\frac l2 S^{3D}+\mathcal O(l^0).
\]

Because the three-dimensional boundary action is not topological, explicit boundary geometries can yield yarmulke-only, trouser-only, mixed, or regular sectors. Hence both types can have unbounded support in four dimensions.

This proves existence of unbounded irregular regions. It does not by itself prove divergence: oscillatory integrations over other variables can restrict or cancel the large-\(l\) contribution, and the result may depend on integration order.

# Causal irregularities beyond bones

The paper recalls:

- edge causality: an orthogonal three-dimensional cut around a spacelike edge should contain exactly two light cones with two disk-like components on the link;
- vertex causality: the light cones at a vertex should cut the boundary of its star in two three-balls.

Violations need not produce an imaginary Regge action because the action responds directly to codimension-two deficit angles.

# Pairwise embeddability and chrono-topology

Two individually realizable neighboring simplices need not admit a nonoverlapping glued embedding of a prescribed relative orientation when their shared face is null. For non-null shared faces, reflection across the face supplies both relative orientations.

A triangulation is **pairwise embeddable** when every neighboring pair has a positive-relative-orientation glued embedding. It has a **pairwise embeddable chrono-topology** when these local embeddings also admit a consistent global time orientation.

The paper gives examples where:

- a null shared face obstructs the chosen relative orientation;
- local pairs embed but an odd cycle obstructs global time orientation;
- changing edge causal types at fixed connectivity changes the chrono-topology;
- closed timelike flow appears in one sector and source-to-sink time flow in another.

# Discrete causality data

Each simplex receives its spacelike/timelike type from Cayley--Menger signs. For a spacelike \(k\)-simplex \(\sigma\) bounding a timelike \((k+1)\)-simplex \(\tau\), a sign

\[
\epsilon(\sigma\subset\tau)=\pm1
\]

records whether the outward timelike normal is future or past directed.

Local closure, inheritance, transitive orientability of timelike-edge graphs, and sign reversal across positively oriented shared spacelike faces constrain these data. The signs determine whether each wedge has zero, one, or two light-ray crossings and therefore allow a combinatorial regularity test around bones.

These constraints are necessary but not sufficient for Lorentzian realizability.

# Bottom line

The strongest transferable contribution is the separation of three questions: realizability, local light-cone regularity, and global time orientation. The paper convincingly shows that none follows automatically from the others. Its statistical warning is serious but conditional: causal irregularities dominate the authors' constructive samples, not yet a proven quantum-gravity measure or continuum limit.
