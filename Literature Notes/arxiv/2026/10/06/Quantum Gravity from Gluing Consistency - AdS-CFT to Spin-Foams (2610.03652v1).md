# BF sewing, physical channel data and effective simplicity

This paper supplies a useful separation of **kinematic sewing kernels**, **physical channel data**, and an **effective gravitational realization**. Its concrete spherical construction identifies flat Wilson-network recoupling with conformal partial waves and conformal $6j$ kernels. Its free-field calculation reorganizes thermal one-loop determinants as Wilson towers. Its broader claim—that one defect-closed, noncompact conformal BF calculus bootstraps quantum gravity on a sufficiently rich class of fillings—is a structural proposal, not an established construction.

For regional sewing, the immediate use is to state which state space, pairing, defect data and kernel a cut actually carries, and where independent physical input enters. For CPS, the useful chain is BF boundary momentum $\to$ conserved $B$-current classes $\to$ renormalized stress-tensor charges $\to$ a conditional effective curvature momentum. Equality of asymptotic charges is weaker than a symplectomorphism or an identification of local observable algebras.

Evidence: **Source-derived** reconstruction of the full main text and Appendix A. **Checked** algebraic, character, geometric and normalization tests are listed after the reconstruction. No full noncompact defect quantization, bootstrap solution, or derivation of interacting gravitational dynamics is claimed here.

## 1. BF action, boundary momentum and decorated cuts (§2, Appendix A.1)

Start with
$$
 S_{\rm BF}=\frac1{2\kappa_{\rm BF}}\int_M B_{IJ}\wedge F^{IJ},\quad
 F^{IJ}=dA^{IJ}+A^I{}_K\wedge A^{KJ}.
$$
Its variation in the paper's ordering is
$$
 \delta S_{\rm BF}=\frac1{2\kappa_{\rm BF}}
 \int_M[\delta B_{IJ}\wedge F^{IJ}+\delta A^{IJ}\wedge(D_AB)_{IJ}]
 +\frac1{2\kappa_{\rm BF}}\int_{\partial M}\delta A^{IJ}\wedge B_{IJ}.
$$
Thus
$$
 F=0,\qquad D_AB=0,\qquad
 \Theta_{\rm BF}=\frac1{2\kappa_{\rm BF}}\delta A^{IJ}\wedge B_{IJ}.
$$
For two commuting field variations, the corresponding presymplectic current in this polarization is
$$
 \omega_{\rm BF}(\delta_1,\delta_2)=\frac1{2\kappa_{\rm BF}}
 [\delta_2A^{IJ}\wedge\delta_1B_{IJ}-\delta_1A^{IJ}\wedge\delta_2B_{IJ}].
$$
This last expression uses $\omega=\delta_1\Theta(\delta_2)-\delta_2\Theta(\delta_1)$. Fixing $A$ kills the displayed boundary variation; changing polarization requires the associated boundary term. **Checked:** the four-dimensional abelian component integration-by-parts residual in xAct. The nonabelian statement uses invariance of the bilinear form.

Besides ordinary gauge transformations, $B\mapsto B+D_A\Xi$, $\deg\Xi=d-2$, is a shift symmetry modulo a boundary term. On a contractible source-free patch, gauge-fixing $A=0$ leaves $dB=0$ modulo exact forms. This establishes the absence of local BF bulk modes, while leaving global holonomies, fluxes and boundary data.

The two elementary decorations affect different equations:

- Electric Wilson lines $W_R(\gamma)=\mathcal P\exp\int_\gamma A_R$ carry representation data and source the $B$-Gauss law.
- Codimension-two worldvolumes prescribe meridional $A$-holonomy in a conjugacy class $[g_m]$; additional electric data may live in its centralizer.

A bulk cut inherits marked electric points, magnetic loci and junction data, summarized by $\mathfrak d_Y$. The assumed kinematic space is $\mathcal H_{\rm BF}(Y;\mathfrak d_Y)$. Global flat holonomies not fixed by boundary conditions remain cut variables. Their conjugate $B$ data are implicit in the chosen polarization.

This is not automatically $\mathcal H_{\rm CFT}(C)$. The CFT pairing
$$
 X=X_L\cup_C X_R,\qquad Z[X]=\langle\Psi_R|\Psi_L\rangle_C
$$
is a physical statement about the CFT. The proposal is that BF resolutions and kernels can represent the corresponding amplitudes on an appropriate class of cuts. For nonspherical $C$, radial state–operator correspondence does not provide a known complete physical basis.

## 2. From Wilson intertwiners to a bootstrap equation (§§2.2–2.6)

### Spherical recoupling and the principal-series resolution

In a contractible flat region, a Wilson segment is a representation matrix of a group element relating its endpoints. At a trivalent vertex the intertwiner property cancels the local vertex group element. A four-line graph therefore lies in
$$
 \mathcal V_{1234}=\operatorname{Inv}_G(V_{R_1}\otimes V_{R_2}\otimes V_{R_3}\otimes V_{R_4}).
$$
Factorization through an intermediate representation produces
$$
 \mathcal I^{(s)}_{R,\alpha\beta}
 =C^\alpha_{12R^\vee}\circ C^\beta_{R34},\qquad
 \mathcal I^{(s)}_{R,\alpha\beta}
 =\int d\mu_t(R')\sum_{\gamma\delta}
 \mathsf F^{R',\gamma\delta}_{R,\alpha\beta}[R_i]\,
 \mathcal I^{(t)}_{R',\gamma\delta}.
$$
Multiplicity labels and the dual orientation of the internal line are necessary. $\mathsf F$ is the Racah/$6j$ change of coupling basis. Associativity of repeated decompositions gives pentagon coherence. Noncompact tensor products require direct integrals, measures and possibly isolated/discrete contributions.

For the conformal spherical resolution, choose $\Delta=d/2+i\nu$. Its shadow pairing turns contraction of the intermediate index into
$$
 \Psi^{(s)}_{\Delta,\rho;\alpha\beta}(x_i)\propto
 \int d^dx\,
 \langle\mathcal O_1\mathcal O_2\mathcal O_{\Delta,\rho}(x)\rangle_\alpha
 \langle\widetilde{\mathcal O}_{d-\Delta,\rho^R}(x)\mathcal O_3\mathcal O_4\rangle_\beta.
$$
This is the shadow-symmetric Euclidean partial wave. Its crossing kernel is the conformal $6j$. The identity operator and other isolated pieces need not lie in the principal integral itself. The physical CFT spectrum is not the principal-series continuum.

**Checked, compact illustration only:** for four spin-$1/2$ representations of $SU(2)$, explicit normalized singlet/triplet invariant tensors give
$$
 F=\begin{pmatrix}1/2&\sqrt3/2\\\sqrt3/2&-1/2\end{pmatrix},\qquad FF^T=1,
$$
and reconstruct one pairing basis from the other exactly. This tests the stated recoupling mechanism in a finite example. It does not evaluate a noncompact conformal $6j$ or prove its analytic continuation.

### Physical endpoints and conformal blocks

A physical endpoint carries an analytically continued lowest-weight module. Fixing that external label does not select the internal OPE branch. The additional boundary prescription is
$$
 \mathcal P_\Delta\Psi_{\Delta,J}\propto G_{\Delta,J},
$$
which removes the shadow branch. Equivalently, after quotienting null states, the physical family projector is
$$
 \Pi_{\Delta,J}=\sum_{a,b\in\mathcal V_{\Delta,J}}
 |\Delta,J;a\rangle(G^{-1})^{ab}\langle\Delta,J;b|.
$$
For a complete amplitude, continuation of an individual block is insufficient: the coefficient density, contour deformations and residues must also be specified.

The BRS cap construction supplies a compatible pure-AdS realization. A scalar cap is a stabilizer $SO(d+1)$ singlet, and its primary matrix element is
$$
 \langle\!\langle\Delta|g(\rho,x)|\Delta\rangle
 =e^{\rho\Delta}(1+e^{2\rho}x^2)^{-\Delta}
 =\left(\frac z{z^2+x^2}\right)^\Delta,\qquad z=e^{-\rho}.
$$
**Checked:** in AdS₅ Poincaré coordinates this kernel obeys $(\nabla^2-\Delta(\Delta-4))K=0$ at unit radius. This verifies the local kernel equation, not the entire network/OPE construction. The intrinsic endpoint prescription remains extra boundary data of the proposal.

### Global evidence and the exact content of the bootstrap

For a fixed asymptotic configuration $\mathfrak X=(X,\mathfrak d_\partial)$, an admissible filling/network presentation $\mathfrak p$ gives blocks and a generalized measure. The proposed kernel obeys
$$
 \Psi^{\rm BF}_{\mathfrak p,\lambda}
 =\int d\mu_{\mathfrak p'}(\lambda')
 K^{\rm BF}_{\mathfrak p\to\mathfrak p'}(\lambda,\lambda';\mathfrak X)
 \Psi^{\rm BF}_{\mathfrak p',\lambda'}.
$$
A physical amplitude is
$$
 \mathcal A_{\mathsf D}(\mathfrak X;\mathfrak p)
 =\int d\mu_{\mathfrak p}(\lambda)\rho_{\mathfrak p}[\mathsf D](\lambda)
 \Psi^{\rm BF}_{\mathfrak p,\lambda}.
$$
Where the kernel has the needed invertible distributional continuation, presentation independence becomes
$$
 \rho_{\mathfrak p'}[\mathsf D](\lambda')
 =\int d\mu_{\mathfrak p}(\lambda)\rho_{\mathfrak p}[\mathsf D](\lambda)
 K^{\rm BF}_{\mathfrak p\to\mathfrak p'}(\lambda,\lambda';\mathfrak X).
$$
If $\rho_{\mathfrak p'}$ is merely defined by this transport, the equation introduces no new spectral constraint. It becomes bootstrap when the target presentation has independently specified physical data, or when the same physical data recur in multiple presentations. Locality, OPE convergence, nondegenerate pairing, positivity/unitarity, identity and stress-tensor Ward identities are additional physical requirements.

The higher-topology evidence is assembled from distinct settings. Compact four-dimensional BF state sums demonstrate global recoupling; perturbative CMR/BV–BFV constructions show gluing around a fixed flat connection; noncompact lower-dimensional BF supplies continuous/discrete harmonic-analysis precedents. These are not a combined construction for the present conformal group and all defects.

A useful explicit compact model is the common boundary $S^2\times S^1$. The $D^3\times S^1$ filling prepares Wilson characters $\chi_R(g)$; the $S^2\times D^2$ filling can fix a core-defect monodromy $[h]$. With normalized Haar measure,
$$
 \delta_{\rm cl}(g,[h])=\sum_{R\in\widehat G}\overline{\chi_R(h)}\chi_R(g).
$$
This is a genuine representation/holonomy change of basis in the compact class-function space. Its noncompact conformal analogue, measure, contour and physical defect-channel interpretation remain to be supplied. A noncontractible-cycle holonomy does not itself require a defect; a contractible meridian with nontrivial holonomy does.

## 3. The conformal-Cartan branch and electric charges (§3)

### Flatness and conformal geometry

Use the source's generators
$$
 D=J_{\hat r\bullet},\quad P_i=J_{i\bullet}+J_{i\hat r},\quad
 K_i=J_{i\bullet}-J_{i\hat r},\quad M_{ij}=J_{ij},
$$
$$
 [D,P_i]=-P_i,\quad[D,K_i]=K_i,\quad
 [P_i,K_j]=2(M_{ij}-\delta_{ij}D).
$$
In radial gauge,
$$
 A=b^{-1}(a+d)b,\quad b=e^{\rho D/\ell},\quad a_\rho=0,\quad
 a=\ell^{-1}e^iP_i+\tfrac12\omega^{ij}M_{ij}+\phi D+\ell^{-1}f^iK_i.
$$
Invertibility of $e^i{}_{\mu}$ selects a geometric branch and a parabolic reduction. For $d>2$, a local $K_i$ transformation sets $\phi=0$; the torsion equation fixes $\omega=\omega_{\rm LC}$; the Ricci contraction fixes
$$
 f^i=-\frac{\ell^2}{2}\mathsf P^i,\qquad
 \mathsf P_{ij}=\frac1{d-2}\left(R_{ij}-\frac R{2(d-1)}g_{ij}\right),
$$
$$
 a[g]=\ell^{-1}e^iP_i+\tfrac12\omega_{\rm LC}^{ij}M_{ij}
 -\frac\ell2\mathsf P^iK_i.
$$
The remaining curvature sets Weyl curvature to zero in $d\geq4$, and Cotton curvature to zero in $d=3$. Separately, $F_{\rho\mu}=0$ gives $\partial_\rho a=0$. Thus flatness describes local conformal flatness on this branch; radial gauge alone does not imply radial independence.

For $ds^2=d\tau^2+R_\partial^2d\Omega_{d-1}^2$,
$$
 a_{\rm cyl}=\ell^{-1}(e^0P_0+e^\alpha P_\alpha)
 +\tfrac12\widehat\omega^{\alpha\beta}M_{\alpha\beta}
 +\frac\ell{4R_\partial^2}e^0K_0-\frac\ell{4R_\partial^2}e^\alpha K_\alpha.
$$
**Checked:** xAct computes the $d=3$ cylinder Schouten components
$\mathsf P_{\tau\tau}=-1/(2R_\partial^2)$, $\mathsf P_{\theta\theta}=1/2$, $\mathsf P_{\varphi\varphi}=\sin^2\theta/2$. Exact generator-matrix evaluation then gives zero for all three cylinder Cartan curvature components. These checks support the signs and factors in this representative.

### Wilson state preparation and Gauss law

A half-cylinder with a central Wilson line prepares a marked BF cut state. In a physical lowest-weight representation, Euclidean propagation acts by $e^{TD/R_\partial}$. Dividing out $e^{-T\Delta/R_\partial}$ suppresses a level-$n$ descendant by $e^{-nT/R_\partial}$. The infinite half-cylinder therefore selects the primary subspace when the initial component overlaps it; endpoint polarization resolves spin components.

A coadjoint-orbit line has
$$
 S_{\rm WL}=\int_C\langle\Lambda,u^{-1}(d+A)u\rangle,\quad
 q=u\Lambda u^{-1},\quad J_C=q\,\delta_C,
$$
$$
 F=0,\qquad D_AB+\kappa_{\rm BF}J_C=0.
$$
For a covariantly constant adjoint section $D_A\zeta=0$, integration over a transverse ball gives
$$
 \int_{S^{d-1}_{\rm link}}\langle\zeta,B\rangle
 =-\kappa_{\rm BF}\int_{D^d}\langle\zeta,J_C\rangle.
$$
The representation is measured by linking $B$-flux. For $d>2$, the linking sphere is simply connected, so an electric worldline has no generic meridional $A$-holonomy. Magnetic defects have an $S^1$ meridian and are different objects. A heavy electric label is not yet a backreacted black-hole metric: flat BF remains flat away from magnetic defects.

## 4. Thermal character, Fock space and Wilson towers (§4)

The cylinder thermal connection is
$$
 a_\tau=\ell^{-1}P_0+\frac\ell{4R_\partial^2}K_0
 =R_\partial^{-1}H_\partial.
$$
With $s_\partial=\log(2R_\partial/\ell)$, one has
$$
 e^{s_\partial D}H_\partial e^{-s_\partial D}=X=\tfrac12(P_0+K_0),\qquad
 e^{\frac\pi4(P_0-K_0)}De^{-\frac\pi4(P_0-K_0)}=X.
$$
Hence the thermal holonomy is conjugate to $e^{\beta D/R_\partial}$. **Checked:** exact defining-representation commutators and the rotation conjugacy for $d=3,4,5$. The arbitrary cylinder scale enters through $\beta/R_\partial$, not $\beta/\ell$ until a Weyl frame is chosen.

For a generic long scalar module,
$$
 \chi_{\Delta,0}(q)=\mathcal W_{\Delta,0}(\gamma_\beta)
 =\sum_{n\geq0}\binom{n+d-1}{d-1}q^{\Delta+n}
 =\frac{q^\Delta}{(1-q)^d}.
$$
At $R_\partial=\ell$, bulk scalar energies satisfy $\ell E_{n,s}=\Delta+2n+s$, $m^2\ell^2=\Delta(\Delta-d)$. The harmonic generating function
$\sum_sg_s^{(d)}q^s=(1-q^2)/(1-q)^d$ cancels the radial factor $(1-q^2)^{-1}$, giving the same one-particle trace.

After dividing out the vacuum contribution, bosonic Fock space gives
$$
 \log Z^{(1)}_{\Delta,\rm th}
 =\sum_{k\geq1}\frac1k\frac{q^{k\Delta}}{(1-q^k)^d}
 =\sum_{k\geq1}\frac1k\operatorname{Tr}_V U_\beta^k,
$$
$$
 Z^{(1)}_{\Delta,\rm th}=\sum_{N\geq0}\operatorname{Tr}_{\operatorname{Sym}^N V}U_\beta.
$$
The first is a sum of multiply wound loops; the second decomposes particle-number sectors. The cycle-index formula is
$$
 \operatorname{Tr}_{\mathrm{Sym}^NV}U
 =\sum_{\lambda\vdash N}\frac1{z_\lambda}
 \prod_k(\operatorname{Tr}_VU^k)^{m_k},\qquad
 z_\lambda=\prod_k k^{m_k}m_k!.
$$
For example, $\chi_{\mathrm{Sym}^2V}=(\chi_V^2+\chi_V(U^2))/2$, and the cubic expression is $(\chi_V^3+3\chi_V\chi_V(U^2)+2\chi_V(U^3))/6$. These formulas count states; one still has to decompose them into conformal primary modules.

### Refined double-trace decomposition

Let $r=\lfloor d/2\rfloor$. The long-module descendant factor is
$$
 \mathcal P_d(q,\mathbf y)=\frac1{(1-q)^{d\bmod2}}
 \prod_{a=1}^r\frac1{(1-qy_a)(1-qy_a^{-1})},\qquad
 \chi_{\Delta,J}=q^\Delta\chi_J^{SO(d)}(\mathbf y)\mathcal P_d.
$$
For generic dimensions where all modules in question are long, divide the symmetric-power character by $\mathcal P_d$. In the two-particle sector,
$$
 \mathcal G^{\rm prim}_2
 =\frac{q^{2\Delta}}2
 \left[\mathcal P_d(q,\mathbf y)+\frac{\mathcal P_d(q^2,\mathbf y^2)}{\mathcal P_d(q,\mathbf y)}\right]
 =\frac{q^{2\Delta}}2[\mathcal P_d(q,\mathbf y)+\mathcal P_d(-q,\mathbf y)].
$$
Each denominator factor obeys $(1-qw)/(1-q^2w^2)=1/(1+qw)$, proving the cycle ratio factor by factor. Together with
$$
 (1-q^2)\mathcal P_d(q,\mathbf y)=\sum_{s\geq0}q^s\chi_s^{SO(d)}(\mathbf y),
$$
the even projection gives
$$
 \operatorname{Sym}^2\mathcal V_{\Delta,0}
 =\bigoplus_{n\geq0}\bigoplus_{s=0,2,4,\ldots}
 \mathcal V_{2\Delta+2n+s,s}.
$$
There is one generic double-trace primary per $(n,s\ \text{even})$, not one primary per two-particle state. Replacing each irreducible character with its once-wound loop gives
$$
 \frac12[\mathcal W_{\Delta,0}(\gamma_\beta)^2+
 \mathcal W_{\Delta,0}(\gamma_\beta^2)]
 =\sum_{n\geq0,s\ \mathrm{even}}\mathcal W_{2\Delta+2n+s,s}(\gamma_\beta).
$$
Summing the analogous decompositions over all particle numbers gives the single-winding multi-trace tower representation of the same free determinant.

**Checked:** exact refined cycle ratios in $d=3,\ldots,7$; refined $SO(3)$ double-trace character equality through relative level 10; and cycle-index versus direct symmetric Fock counting for a three-level model through $N=6$. At $d=3$, the primary generating function's unrefined coefficients through relative level 8 are $1,0,6,0,15,0,28,0,45$. These coefficients count spin components, not just irreducible primaries.

For fermions, replace symmetric by exterior powers and insert $(-1)^{k+1}$ in the winding sum. For gauge/short multiplets, first use the physical irreducible character with null descendants removed. Dividing a short-character sum by the generic long factor without treating shortening is not justified. The paper does not perform the complete multi-stress-tensor decomposition or compute interactions. The determinant agreement establishes a free spectral reorganization, not local interacting bulk dynamics.

## 5. Generalized simplicity as boundary-current matching (§5.1)

In a flat Dirichlet Cartan frame $\bar A=dx^aP_a$, decompose $B$ in the dual conformal basis. Locally fixing the shift gauge to remove the rotation, special-conformal and dilatation components leaves $t^aP_a^*$. The remaining equations are
$$
 e^{[a}\wedge t^{b]}=0,\qquad dt^a=0,\qquad e_a\wedge t^a=0.
$$
Identifying
$$
 -\kappa_{\rm BF}^{-1}t^a=\star_\partial\tau^a,\qquad
 \tau^a=e^a{}_{\mu}T^{\mu\nu}dx_\nu,
$$
turns these into symmetry, conservation and tracelessness of $T$. This is a boundary-current identification with a CFT stress tensor; BF equations alone do not select its correlation functions or normalization $C_T$.

For a reference generator $X$, use a parallel section $D_A\zeta_X=0$. On a simply connected patch with $A=u^{-1}du$, $\zeta_X=u^{-1}Xu$. On a region with holonomy, only sections compatible with that holonomy extend globally. Thus an integral charge requires more than a locally chosen $X$.

For the corresponding conformal Killing vector, define
$$
 j_X=T_{\mu\nu}\xi_X^\nu dx^\mu,\qquad\mathcal J_X=\star_\partial j_X.
$$
If $T$ is symmetric, conserved and traceless, then
$$
 \nabla_\mu(T^\mu{}_{\nu}\xi_X^\nu)
 =T^{\mu\nu}\nabla_{(\mu}\xi_{X\nu)}
 =\frac1d(\nabla\cdot\xi_X)T^\mu{}_{\mu}=0.
$$
**Checked with xAct:** the product-rule decomposition and contraction of a symmetric tensor with the conformal-Killing decomposition. On the BF side,
$$
 d\langle\zeta_X,B\rangle=0,\qquad
 \langle\zeta_X,B+D_A\Xi\rangle=
 \langle\zeta_X,B\rangle+d\langle\zeta_X,\Xi\rangle.
$$
The proposed generalized simplicity statement is consequently an equality of current classes,
$$
 \boxed{\left[-\frac1{\kappa_{\rm BF}}\langle\zeta_X,B\rangle\big|_{\partial M}\right]_{\rm imp}
 =[\mathcal J_X[T]]_{\rm imp}.}
$$
Locally, representatives differ by $d\chi_X$. Integrating on a closed cut removes that improvement; an open regional cut retains a corner term. With a Wilson source,
$$
 d[-\kappa_{\rm BF}^{-1}\langle\zeta_X,B\rangle]=\langle\zeta_X,J_C\rangle,
$$
so a small linking integral measures the generator's action on the endpoint representation. Anomaly terms and global extension of $\zeta_X$ must be specified before transporting this relation to a general background.

## 6. EFT momentum, Einstein simplicity and the cosmological term (§5.2, Appendix A.2)

Now assume the physical data already admit a local weakly coupled gravitational EFT: large $C_T$, large-$N$ factorization, sparse light single-trace spectrum, a large higher-spin gap, appropriate Regge behavior and one interacting stress-tensor sector. These conditions are additional input. The source explicitly does not derive this EFT from the Wilson interactions.

The holographic variation is
$$
 \delta W[e_{(0)}]=\int_{\partial M}|e_{(0)}|\,
 \langle T^\mu{}_a\rangle\delta e_{(0)\mu}{}^a.
$$
The stress tensor is finite **renormalized** momentum, after boundary terms and counterterms. For an integrable asymptotic symmetry, a compatible CPS charge representative satisfies
$$
 [q_X^{\rm EFT}|_{\partial M}]_{\rm imp}=[\mathcal J_X[\langle T\rangle]]_{\rm imp}.
$$
Combining this with the BF current dictionary motivates charge matching. It does not determine a unique local bulk $B$, and does not identify the full symplectic structures.

For first-order algebraic-curvature actions $L(e,R,\Phi)$, define
$$
 \mathcal P_{ab}=\frac{\partial L}{\partial R^{ab}},\qquad
 \Theta_{\rm eff}^{(\omega)}=\delta\omega^{ab}\wedge\mathcal P_{ab}.
$$
Comparing with $\Theta_{\rm BF}^{(\omega)}=\delta\omega^{ab}\wedge B_{ab}/(2\kappa_{\rm BF})$ motivates
$$
 B^{ab}_{\rm grav}=2\kappa_{\rm BF}\mathcal P^{ab},\qquad
 B^{a\bullet}_{\rm grav}=0.
$$
This is conditional on a compatible choice of variables, boundary terms and polarization. Torsion terms, curvature derivatives and other connection couplings can introduce additional momenta. Finite-gap corrections and finite-$C_T$ quantum corrections are separate expansions; the latter need not be expressible as a local classical constitutive law.

For Einstein–Cartan gravity,
$$
 \mathcal P^{ab}=\frac1{2\kappa_{\rm grav}(d-1)!}
 \varepsilon^{ab}{}_{c_1\ldots c_{d-1}}e^{c_1}\wedge\cdots\wedge e^{c_{d-1}}.
$$
At equal couplings this reproduces the simplicity form
$$
 B^{ab}_{\rm grav}=\frac1{(d-1)!}\varepsilon^{ab}{}_{c_1\ldots c_{d-1}}e^{c_1}\wedge\cdots\wedge e^{c_{d-1}},
 \quad B^{a\bullet}_{\rm grav}=0.
$$
It is a property of the Einstein EFT representative, not a constraint imposed on every BF block in the proposed sewing calculus.

### Why naive simplicity substitution is not the desired AdS action

Split $A^{ab}=\omega^{ab}$, $A^{a\bullet}=e^a/\ell$. The internal signature gives
$$
 F^{ab}=R^{ab}+\ell^{-2}e^a\wedge e^b,\qquad F^{a\bullet}=T^a/\ell.
$$
Flatness selects locally AdS torsion-free geometry and is stronger than Einstein's equation in bulk dimension greater than three.

Substituting simple $B$ into the pure BF action produces a bracket $R^{ab}+\ell^{-2}e^a\wedge e^b$. Let $n=d+1$. Varying the coframe at fixed independent connection weights the curvature and volume terms by $n-2$ and $n$, respectively. On a constant-curvature ansatz $R^{ab}=c\,e^a\wedge e^b$,
$$
 (n-2)c+n\ell^{-2}=0,
 \quad c=-\frac{d+1}{d-1}\ell^{-2},\quad
 \Lambda_{\rm simpBF}=-\frac{d(d+1)}{2\ell^2}.
$$
Thus $L_{\rm simpBF}=\ell\sqrt{(d-1)/(d+1)}$. The Einstein action whose physical AdS radius is $\ell$ instead requires
$$
 S_{\rm EC}^{(\ell)}=\frac1{2\kappa_{\rm grav}(d-1)!}
 \int\varepsilon_{ab c_1\ldots c_{d-1}}e^{c_1}\wedge\cdots\wedge e^{c_{d-1}}
 \wedge\left(R^{ab}+\frac{d-1}{d+1}\ell^{-2}e^a\wedge e^b\right).
$$
Then $c=-\ell^{-2}$ and $\Lambda=-d(d-1)/(2\ell^2)$. **Checked:** the constant-curvature scaling equations and exterior-algebra contraction coefficients in bulk dimensions 4, 5 and 6. The change is in a nonderivative potential, so the curvature momentum used above is unchanged. This qualification is in the paper itself and is essential when reusing its BF/Einstein comparison.

For an oriented Euclidean codimension-two surface and $n^{ab}n_{ab}=2$,
$$
 \operatorname{vol}_\Sigma=\frac12n_{ab}B^{ab},\qquad
 \operatorname{Area}(\Sigma)=\frac12\int_\Sigma n_{ab}B^{ab}.
$$
**Checked:** the factor $1/2$ in exterior-algebra frames for bulk dimensions 4, 5 and 6. Lorentzian binormal signs require the corresponding continuation; the displayed formula is the Euclidean orientation convention.

## 7. What the outlook does and does not establish (§6)

The BF Fourier kernel is schematically
$$
 \widetilde\Psi[B]=\int\mathcal DA\,K[B,A]\Psi[A],\qquad
 K[B,A]\sim\exp\left[\frac{i}{2\kappa_{\rm BF}}\int_{\partial M}A^{ab}\wedge B_{ab}\right].
$$
A physical transform still needs gauge reduction, measure, domain and boundary conditions. Its canonical pairing alone is not a higher-dimensional modular-invariance theorem.

On a pre-existing regular Euclidean gravitational saddle, the horizon zero-mode projection defines
$$
 \Theta_H=\frac12\oint n_{ab}A^{ab},\qquad
 \Pi_H=\frac1{2\kappa_{\rm BF}}\int_H n_{ab}B^{ab},\qquad
 K_{\rm hor}\sim e^{i\Theta_H\Pi_H}.
$$
With the stated boost/angle continuation and $\Theta_H^E=2\pi$, Einstein simplicity and $\kappa_{\rm BF}=8\pi G_N$ give
$$
 2\pi\Pi_H=\frac{\operatorname{Area}(H)}{4G_N}.
$$
For suitable higher-curvature actions, $B=2\kappa_{\rm BF}\mathcal P$ gives the corresponding Wald expression. **Checked:** the normalization substitution. Smoothness, the gravitational saddle, the constitutive relation and the analytic-continuation prescription are inputs. No microscopic density of states follows from this algebra alone.

The proposed flat-space contraction, dS observer/CRT sewing, supersymmetric extension and Virasoro reorganization are future directions. The dS discussion quotes an anticipated entropy/logarithmic check from forthcoming work; no independent derivation of it is supplied here or verified in this note. The existence of a nonempty physical bootstrap solution space beyond already known boundary constructions remains open.

## Translation to regional observables and CPS

A concrete next use is to formulate a sewing test with the smallest nontrivial electric/magnetic pair of presentations: specify the cut state spaces, both pairings, the shared boundary data and an actual kernel before assigning physical meaning to transported coefficients. This guards against interpreting a formal basis change as a new bootstrap equation.

For regional charges, retain the local representative and its improvement term. The closed-cut identity in §5.1 discards $\int_{\partial\Sigma}\chi_X$; a regional cut with corners does not. Likewise, the BF pairing and charge-matching dictionary are not yet a proof that the gravitational regional presymplectic form, gauge quotient or observable algebra is reproduced. A test would have to compare those structures with the same boundary conditions and counterterms.

For spectral work, the double-trace decomposition is directly reusable as a character benchmark. For dynamical claims, the exact obstruction is the absent interacting Wilson/defect construction that would yield an effective metric action. The paper explicitly assumes ordinary AdS/CFT to enter the EFT regime, so using that regime to claim it has derived AdS/CFT would be circular.
