## Medium-priority technical candidates

### 1. Black Hole Quasinormal Mode Resonances and Reconnections in Coupled Systems (2609.35971v1)

**Source-derived — T2-spectral; T2-model.** The question is how coupled gravitational and scalar QNMs reorganize near resonances. In perturbed dynamical Chern–Simons gravity the authors report exceptional points, avoided crossings, reconnections and double-spiral endpoints whose limiting frequency is independent of perturbation strength in the cases studied; excitation factors show resonant scaling. A coupled square-barrier model reproduces the qualitative crossings and reconnections. This offers a tractable spectral benchmark for mode mixing and sensitivity, but it is an open-system resonance problem without an immediate AdS canonical or boundary-charge construction, so medium placement is appropriate.

Source: [official v1 abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35971v1); primary/cross categories: General Relativity and Quantum Cosmology (gr-qc) ; High Energy Astrophysical Phenomena (astro-ph.HE); High Energy Physics - Theory (hep-th). No independent derivation or CAS verification.

### 2. Spectral instabilities in the time domain (2609.35993v1)

**Source-derived — T2-spectral.** A double Pöschl–Teller barrier model separates spectral sensitivity from when a waveform can reveal it. Successive-scattering analysis places the first secular correction after one causal round trip; before the first echo the authors find unshifted overtone frequencies. In the perturbative regime its linear-in-time coefficient reproduces the frequency-domain shift, while more general finite-time effective frequencies need equal neither exact spectrum. Numerical time evolution supports the analysis. The useful lesson for response and spectral work is to retain the observation-time and causal boundary assumptions; the toy scattering setting has no direct CPS or AdS quantization result.

Source: [official v1 abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35993v1); primary/cross categories: General Relativity and Quantum Cosmology (gr-qc). No independent derivation or CAS verification.

### 3. Signatures of Bulk Topology from the t Hooft Worldsheet (2609.36015v1)

**Source-derived — T1-boundary; T2-dS-BH-holography.** Within a specified ansatz of matrix-quantum-mechanics wavefunctions generating planar diagrams, the authors use replica entanglement to diagnose contractible cycles and changes of dominant bulk topology. The construction interprets diagrams as off-shell string worldsheets, relates replica contractibility to conical punctures, and proposes an open-string edge-mode interpretation of a deconfinement transition. Toy evaporation examples diagnose disconnected/replica-wormhole dominance without ensemble averaging or separately postulating saddles. It supplies a possible microscopic comparison for edge degrees of freedom, but the wavefunction/worldsheet ansatz and candidate edge interpretation do not furnish a regional CPS or sewing theorem; retain medium placement.

Source: [official v1 abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.36015v1); primary/cross categories: High Energy Physics - Theory (hep-th). No independent derivation or CAS verification.

### 4. Deformation Quantization of Schutz Quantum Cosmology: Relational Time, Constraints, and Physical States (2609.37585v1)

**Source-derived — T3-math; T2-model; T2-spectral.** For FLRW gravity with a Schutz fluid clock, the paper compares reduced relational quantization with bilateral star constraints for a deparametrized constraint. The antisymmetric constraint gives Moyal evolution, whereas the symmetric constraint imposes a separate shell condition. With a fixed self-adjoint Hamiltonian, negative-spectral-support density operators receive an explicit distributional extension that preserves coherences and returns the reduced state as its clock-momentum marginal. A flat dust model with the stated ordering and Dirichlet data realizes the construction. This is useful when auditing reduction versus constrained quantization, but the fixed constraint representative, spectral sector and self-adjoint realization are essential; it does not establish general equivalence for gravity or unreduced OFPT.

Source: [official v1 abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.37585v1); primary/cross categories: General Relativity and Quantum Cosmology (gr-qc). No independent derivation or CAS verification.

### 5. Constructing and Probing Three-Boundary Booklet Geometries (2609.37099v1)

**Source-derived — T1-boundary; T2-dS-BH-holography.** The authors propose three-boundary partially entangled thermal states by replacing two-index heavy-shell insertion data with a trivalent junction tensor. Israel junction conditions identify candidate symmetric and mixed-orientation booklet branches; counting in the symmetric all-black-hole sector and a heavy-operator detection estimate provide consistency probes. The proposed ratio is approximately three, compared with three-halves for the two-boundary construction. This is a concrete multiboundary state-preparation example relevant to sewing intuition, but a semiclassical shell ansatz and sector-specific checks do not define the general regional gluing map, so it remains a medium sketch.

Source: [official v1 abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.37099v1); primary/cross categories: High Energy Physics - Theory (hep-th) ; General Relativity and Quantum Cosmology (gr-qc); Quantum Physics (quant-ph). No independent derivation or CAS verification.

### 6. Asymptotics of Killing horizons: A Characterization in de Sitter Spacetime (2609.37427v1)

**Source-derived — T1-symmetry; T1-boundary; T2-dS-BH-holography.** The abstract states a local uniqueness characterization of de Sitter among positive-cosmological-constant vacuum spacetimes whose Killing horizons meet conformal infinity. It relates the intersection to isolated essential zeros of the boundary extension of the Killing field, reconstructs de Sitter Killing fields from boundary values, and distinguishes degenerate versus bifurcate nondegenerate horizons using a conformal invariant that also determines surface gravity. This may supply geometric input for horizon generators and boundary data. The precise regularity and conformal-completion hypotheses require the full theorem before reuse; the proof was not reconstructed in this sketch, and there is no immediate charge calculation, hence medium priority.

Source: [official v1 abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.37427v1); primary/cross categories: General Relativity and Quantum Cosmology (gr-qc) ; Mathematical Physics (math-ph). No independent derivation or CAS verification.

### 7. Logarithmic gravity from a very slow roll limit of inflation (2609.38052v1)

**Source-derived — T2-dS-BH-holography; T2-spectral; A-big-name.** A linear-roll limit sends Newton’s constant to zero at fixed Hubble scale and inflaton-potential slope. Surviving inflaton fluctuations become reheating-surface conformal-factor fluctuations with a Gaussian, log-correlated measure; conformal scattering data determine its quadratic and linear terms, with GJMS and Q-curvature descriptions in even dimensions. The authors argue for one-loop cancellation and explicitly test it in 2+1 dimensions, discuss a finite residual-symmetry Faddeev–Popov factor, and obtain an exponential potential after conditioning against bubble nucleation. This is relevant to boundary conformal measures, determinants and de Sitter quantization. Maldacena is an additional author signal, but the special inflationary limit and absence of an immediate CPS/sewing application keep it medium.

Source: [official v1 abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.38052v1); primary/cross categories: High Energy Physics - Theory (hep-th) ; General Relativity and Quantum Cosmology (gr-qc). No independent derivation or CAS verification.

### 8. Boundaries and defects in conformal field theories at finite temperature (2609.38130v1)

**Source-derived — T1-boundary; T2-spectral.** For Euclidean CFTs at finite temperature and infinite volume, the paper separates defects wrapping the thermal circle from defects localized on it. It classifies scalar, vector and spin-two bulk one-point structures, relates low-temperature expansions to defect data, and derives localized-defect sum rules from periodicity and the defect OPE. Free-field boundary and defect examples provide thermal data, thermodynamic observables and a Hurwitz-zeta decomposition suggestive of dispersion relations. This is a potentially useful reference for boundary correlators and thermal sums. The long reference treatment was not decomposed here: its immediate connection to the current canonical or regional construction is indirect, so length does not justify promotion to the deep queue.

Source: [official v1 abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.38130v1); primary/cross categories: High Energy Physics - Theory (hep-th). No independent derivation or CAS verification.

### 9. Monopoles, duality, and large charge in Chern-Simons QED$_3$ (2609.37795v1)

**Source-derived — T2-model; T2-spectral; T3-math.** The authors compute monopole dimensions through subleading order in large fermion number and Chern–Simons level at fixed ratio, then compare extrapolated values with scalar-QED duality predictions. The reported low-charge values support an emergent SO(3) interpretation, while higher charges agree closely with the proposed dual data. Fits also distinguish the constant large-charge term in fermionic versus scalar Chern–Simons QED. The reusable material is spectral data and a constrained gauge-theory comparison; extrapolation to one flavor and fitted constants are evidence rather than a proof of duality, and there is no direct boundary-charge or regional-action construction.

Source: [official v1 abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.37795v1); primary/cross categories: High Energy Physics - Theory (hep-th). No independent derivation or CAS verification.

### 10. Allowable complex saddles with real charges in the gravitational index (2609.35961v1)

**Source-derived — T2-dS-BH-holography; T2-spectral.** The paper constructs supersymmetric nonextremal complex AdS black-hole saddles with real charges and several complex horizons, then tests admissibility using the Kontsevich–Segal–Witten criterion. It compares the resulting restrictions with convergence domains of the dual superconformal index: asymptotic agreement occurs in most studied cases, while the full bulk condition excludes additional saddles. The useful distinction is between convergence of a boundary generating function and admissibility throughout a bulk saddle. It is medium because its concrete application is the supersymmetric gravitational index, with no direct canonical CPS calculation; the abstract does not support a universal equivalence of the two criteria.

Source: [official v1 abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35961v1); primary/cross categories: High Energy Physics - Theory (hep-th). No independent derivation or CAS verification.
