## `2607.05788v1` - Dipole ghosts and spontaneous symmetry breaking in higher-order Chern-Simons theory

Reason codes: `T2-model`, `T2-spectral`.

Technical relevance: medium. The paper studies dipole ghost sectors in gauge theories and higher-order Chern-Simons theory. The source claim is that double-pole dipole ghosts can be traced to degeneracy of classical equations of motion, and that spontaneous symmetry breaking removes the degeneracy and replaces the dipole sector with ordinary massive gauge excitations.

Main technical mechanism: introduce an auxiliary-field-like dipole structure, compare it with the multiplicity of classical solutions, then track how the constrained higher-order Chern-Simons propagator develops double poles. The one-loop effective potential is the spectral/quantum diagnostic.

Likely use case: a compact model of how degeneracy in the kinetic operator becomes a ghost-sector signal, useful when checking Chern-Simons-like testbeds or higher-derivative gauge reductions.

Why not high today: it is a gauge-model and spectral mechanism paper, not directly a surface-charge/CPS/boundary-symmetry construction.

## `2607.05918v1` - Shifted Poisson unfoldings and quantum anomalies

Reason codes: `T3-math`, `T1-symplectic`.

Technical relevance: medium. The paper builds a deformation-theoretic controller for families of shifted Poisson structures. The source claim is that flat splittings of a Poisson transverse controller $\mathbb U_\pi$ are exactly transversal shifted Poisson unfoldings, and that the $\hbar$-adic lifting problem has degree-two obstruction classes interpreted as transport anomalies.

Main technical mechanism: organize parameter-dependent classical observables by a homotopy stabilizer preserving the Poisson Maurer-Cartan element up to coherent homotopy; then test whether quantized objects admit anomaly-free horizontal transport. The examples include star products, BV observables, factorization algebras, AKSZ theories, and the Poisson sigma model.

Likely use case: background language for Poisson/CPS deformation problems where the question is whether observables can be compared across a parameter family.

Why not high today: mathematically relevant, but not directly tied to current gravitational charge or boundary-condition computations.
