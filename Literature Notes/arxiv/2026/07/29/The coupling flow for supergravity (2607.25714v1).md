The transferable result is not an all-order Nicolai map. It is an all-order flow equation for correlators in the gravitational coupling, together with a leading-order demonstration that the apparently singular multiplicative BRST term can be converted into a derivation only after taking the final free-field expectation value.

## Nicolai maps as coupling flows

After integrating out fermions, ghosts, auxiliaries, and other inner fields $\phi_i$, the effective action for outer bosons $\phi_o$ has the loop expansion

$$
S_g[\phi_o]
=S_g^{(0)}[\phi_o]
+\sum_{r\geq1}\hbar^rS_g^{(r)}[\phi_o].
$$

A Nicolai map $T_g$ is defined by

$$
\langle Y[\phi_o]\rangle_g^{\phi_o}
=
\langle Y[T_g^{-1}\phi_o]\rangle_0^{\phi_o}.
$$

When the integrated-out fields self-interact, as in supergravity, the map itself becomes quantum:

$$
T_g\phi_o
=T_g^{(0)}\phi_o+\sum_{r\geq1}\hbar^rT_g^{(r)}\phi_o.
$$

The coupling-flow method begins from a derivational functional operator $R$,

$$
g\frac{d}{dg}\langle Y\rangle_g
=
\left\langle
\left(g\frac{\partial}{\partial g}+R[g]\right)Y
\right\rangle_g,
\qquad
R[g]=gR_1+g^2R_2+\cdots,
$$

and formally integrates it to

$$
T_g^{-1}\phi_o
=
\left.
\exp\!\left[g\left(\partial_{g'}+\frac1{g'}R[g']\right)\right]\phi_o
\right|_{g'=0}.
$$

This last step requires $R$ to obey the Leibniz rule. A multiplicative contribution cannot simply be included in a field redefinition.

## Why the superconformal formulation helps

The fields are the Weyl multiplet and a chiral compensator:

$$
e^a{}_\mu,\ b_\mu,\ A_\mu,\ \psi_\mu^\alpha;
\qquad
\phi,\ \rho^\alpha,\ F,
$$

with composite Lorentz, special-conformal, and $S$-supersymmetry connections. The key off-shell improvement is

$$
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{SUSY}}
=\delta_\alpha\Delta_\alpha.
$$

In the ordinary Poincaré formulation an auxiliary-field remainder obstructed this identity. Here the compensator makes the complete invariant Lagrangian a supervariation before gauge fixing.

The gauge-fixed action instead has the mixed form

$$
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{SUSY}}+\mathcal L_{\mathrm{GF}}
=
\delta_\alpha\Delta_\alpha+s\Delta_{\mathrm{GF}},
$$

where $s$ is the Slavnov differential. Local supersymmetry and BRST do not anticommute:

$$
\delta_\alpha s\,\Delta_\alpha\neq0,
\qquad
\{\delta_\alpha,s\}\Delta_{\mathrm{GF}}\neq0.
$$

This produces the central obstruction.

## Derivational and multiplicative pieces

Keeping the vierbein as the outer field and integrating over the compensator, gravitino, and ghosts gives

$$
\kappa\frac{d}{d\kappa}\langle Y\rangle_\kappa
=
\left\langle
\left(
\kappa\partial_\kappa-E
+R^{\mathrm{inv}}+R^{\mathrm{gf}}+Z
\right)Y
\right\rangle_\kappa.
$$

Here $E$ is the Euler operator generated by passing from nonperturbative to canonically scaled fields,

$$
e^a{}_\mu=\delta^a_\mu+\kappa\varphi^a{}_\mu,
\quad
\widetilde\psi_\mu=\kappa\psi_\mu,
\quad
\widetilde\Phi=1+\kappa\phi,
$$

$R^{\mathrm{inv}}$ is generated by the supervariation, $R^{\mathrm{gf}}$ by the BRST variation of the outer field, and $Z$ acts multiplicatively. The inner-field Lagrangian is regular at $\kappa=0$, but dividing the flow by $\kappa$ appears to produce

$$
\frac1\kappa
\left(R_0^{\mathrm{inv}}+R_0^{\mathrm{gf}}-E+Z_0\right).
$$

Regularity therefore requires the free-theory expectation-value identity

$$
\left\langle
\left(R_0^{\mathrm{inv}}+R_0^{\mathrm{gf}}-E+Z_0\right)Y
\right\rangle_{\kappa=0}=0.
$$

It does not require the stronger functional-operator identity before the final Wick contractions.

## Wick conversion of the BRST obstruction

The paper first isolates the mechanism in supersymmetric Yang--Mills theory. A multiplicative term containing the gauge-fixing function can be moved, by BRST invariance and Wick's theorem, onto a functional derivative of $Y$. In axial gauge the decisive free relation is the equality between the longitudinal gluon contraction and the differentiated ghost contraction,

$$
\xi\,n^\mu
\langle A_\mu(x)A_\nu(y)\rangle_0
=
\langle\bar c(x)\,\partial_\nu c(y)\rangle_0.
$$

Thus the equality is true inside the final free correlator, not as an identity in the effective bosonic theory. This distinction explains how a multiplicative $Z$ can repair the regularity condition without by itself defining a Nicolai map.

## Free supergravity and the regularity condition

The authors invert the gauge-fixed quadratic action for the coupled graviton, compensator, gravitino, and ghosts. In the Landau limits for the bosonic and $S$-supersymmetry gauges, the invariant part reduces to

$$
R_0^{\mathrm{inv}}
=
\int
\left(
\varphi_{ab}
-\frac14\varphi\,\eta_{ab}
-\frac1{2\xi_Q}\varphi\frac{\partial_a\partial_b}{\Box}
\right)
\frac{\delta}{\delta\varphi_{ab}},
$$

while $R_0^{\mathrm{gf}}$ vanishes on the harmonic gauge slice. The remaining multiplicative term is a quadratic transverse projector:

$$
Z_0
=
\frac{i}{3}\int
\varphi_{ab}
\left(\eta^{ab}-\frac{\partial^a\partial^b}{\Box}\right)
\Box
\left(\eta^{cd}-\frac{\partial^c\partial^d}{\Box}\right)
\varphi_{cd}.
$$

A partial Wick contraction replaces the second graviton by the free propagator and an Euler derivative. After distributing a total derivative with $p+q=1$, its action becomes

$$
Z_0\ \longrightarrow\
\int\varphi
\left[
\frac14\eta_{ab}
+\left(4p-\frac32\right)
\frac{\partial_a\partial_b}{\Box}
\right]
\frac{\delta}{\delta\varphi_{ab}}.
$$

The local $+\frac14$ cancels the trace deficit in $R_0^{\mathrm{inv}}-E$, while an allowed choice of $p$ cancels the nonlocal gauge-dependent term. Hence the apparent $1/\kappa$ singularity vanishes inside all free correlators, up to the stated gauge artifacts.

## Contact with the direct on-shell map

In Landau gauge the leading classical map is

$$
\begin{aligned}
T^{(0,1)}\varphi_{ab}(x)
=\int d^4y\,
\partial^c\partial^d\Box^{-1}(x-y)
\bigl[
&\tfrac12\eta_{cd}\varphi\varphi_{ab}
-\tfrac12\eta_{cd}\varphi_a{}^e\varphi_{eb}\\
&-\varphi_{ab}\varphi_{cd}
+2\varphi_{ac}\varphi_{bd}
\bigr](y).
\end{aligned}
$$

Four further longitudinal structures remain unfixed. Reverse-engineering the displayed map from a standard supervariation-generated flow reproduces the first pair of tensor structures and only an antisymmetric combination of the last pair. The missing symmetric contribution can again arise through a partial Wick contraction. This is evidence for the same conversion mechanism, not an all-order proof.

The paper's precise conclusion is therefore:

- an all-order coupling flow for Poincaré-supergravity correlators exists;
- its gauge-invariant part is generated off shell by superconformal supersymmetry;
- BRST gauge fixing leaves multiplicative terms;
- leading regularity at $\kappa=0$ is restored inside free correlators;
- the existence of an all-order Nicolai map remains open.

## Verification boundary

### Checked

- Mathematica independently solved cancellation of the nonlocal term:
  $4p-\frac32-\frac1{2\xi_Q}=0$ with $p+q=1$ gives
  $p=(1+3\xi_Q)/(8\xi_Q)$ and $q=(5\xi_Q-1)/(8\xi_Q)$.
- The coefficient vectors from the paper's $\check R_1$ and Wick-generated $\bar R_1$ sum exactly to the $(-1,2)$ tensor pair required by the direct on-shell $R_1$.

### Blocked

- The quoted free propagators, gamma-matrix contractions, and the lengthy evaluation of $R_0^{\mathrm{inv}}$ and $Z_0$ were followed from the source but not independently rederived.
- A universal conversion of every multiplicative BRST term into a derivation is not supplied by the paper.

### Failed

- No independently attempted algebraic step failed. The stronger claim “the paper constructs an all-order Nicolai map for supergravity” would be false; the source explicitly leaves that question open.
