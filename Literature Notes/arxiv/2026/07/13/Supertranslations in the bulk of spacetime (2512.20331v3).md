## How to read the Letter and End Matter

The main Letter has four technical stages. The residual-gauge section defines a bulk supertranslation through an eikonal equation for a new null foliation. The Minkowski section solves that equation by characteristics and translates the foliation change into optical memory. The Schwarzschild section identifies a curvature-dependent transition between null geodesics with and without turning points. The final main-text section proposes a light-ray operator as the generator on a fixed null hypersurface.

The End Matter is essential rather than optional. It records the Newman-Unti inverse-metric conditions, extends the characteristic construction to a spherical boundary in generic dimension, and derives the real light-cone coordinate map used for the linearized operator. It also contains a missing factor in Eq. (23), so its formulas cannot be treated as a clean appendix.

## Null foliations and the coordinate-independent definition

In Newman-Unti coordinates $(u,r,x^a)$, the hypersurfaces $u=\mathrm{const}$ are null and $r$ is an affine parameter along their generators. The inverse metric obeys

$$
g^{uu}=0,
\qquad
g^{ur}=-1,
\qquad
g^{ua}=0.
\tag{19}
$$

The normal $\ell=du$ is first a null covector; after raising its index it is tangent to the null generators. A second foliation is described by

$$
\bar u=u+f(r,x^a),
\qquad
\bar\ell=d\bar u.
$$

Imposing $g^{-1}(d\bar u,d\bar u)=0$ gives

$$
g^{rr}(\partial_r f)^2
-2\partial_r f
+2g^{ra}\partial_r f\,\partial_a f
+g^{ab}\partial_a f\,\partial_b f=0.
\tag{1}
$$

The paper defines a residual transformation by a scalar $H$ for which $dH$ is null, and a supertranslation by such a transformation modulo rescaling of the null normal. Boundary data $f_0$ at null infinity or at a finite null surface are extended into the bulk by solving the nonlinear first-order equation for $f$.

This is a geometric definition of a change of foliation. It does not by itself prove that the change is a nontrivial Hamiltonian symmetry of dynamical gravity. Global existence also requires control of caustics, branch points, and the integrability of a position-dependent rescaling of the normal.

## Plane-fronted Minkowski characteristic flow

For

$$
ds^2=-2,du,dr+2r^2dz,d\bar z,
\tag{2}
$$

Eq. (1) reduces to

$$
\partial_r f
=\frac{1}{r^2}\partial_z f\,\partial_{\bar z}f.
\tag{3}
$$

With $p_r=\partial_r f$, $p=\partial_z f$, and $\bar p=\partial_{\bar z}f$, the Hamilton-Jacobi function is

$$
F=p_r-\frac{p\bar p}{r^2}=0.
\tag{4}
$$

The characteristic equations are

$$
\frac{dr}{ds}=1,
\qquad
\frac{dz}{ds}=-\frac{\bar p}{r^2},
\qquad
\frac{d\bar z}{ds}=-\frac{p}{r^2},
\qquad
\frac{dp}{ds}=\frac{d\bar p}{ds}=0.
\tag{5}
$$

Their angular integrals define characteristic labels $(\zeta,\bar\zeta)$,

$$
z=\zeta+\frac{\bar p}{r},
\qquad
\bar z=\bar\zeta+\frac{p}{r}.
\tag{6}
$$

Along the characteristic,

$$
\frac{df}{ds}=-\frac{p\bar p}{r^2},
\qquad
f=f_0(\zeta,\bar\zeta)+\frac{p\bar p}{r},
\tag{7}
$$

and the data at $r\to\infty$ fix $p=\partial_\zeta f_0$ and $\bar p=\partial_{\bar\zeta}f_0$. The resulting finite coordinate relation includes

$$
u=\bar u-f_0
-\frac{\partial_\zeta f_0\partial_{\bar\zeta}f_0}{r},
\qquad
z=\zeta+\frac{\partial_{\bar\zeta}f_0}{r},
\tag{8}
$$

with the complex-conjugate equation for $\bar z$. The boundary datum $f_0$ labels a cross-section at null infinity; the characteristic flow propagates it to a bulk null hypersurface.

## Optical memory of the Minkowski congruence

The memory effect concerns the congruence and its light front, not the curvature of each individual Minkowski null ray. Define

$$
R=\bar r+\partial_\zeta\partial_{\bar\zeta}f_0,
\qquad
B=(\partial_\zeta^2f_0)(\partial_{\bar\zeta}^2f_0),
\qquad
\Delta=R^2-B.
$$

The transformed congruence has

$$
\widehat\sigma_{\zeta\zeta}
=\frac{\partial_\zeta^2f_0(R^2+B)}{\Delta},
\qquad
\widehat\sigma_{\zeta\bar\zeta}
=\frac{2RB}{\Delta},
\tag{9}
$$

and expansion

$$
\theta=-\frac{2R}{\Delta}.
\tag{10}
$$

At large radius, $\theta\sim-2/r$. The paper identifies the asymptotic Newman-Penrose shear with $\sigma^0=\partial_{\bar\zeta}^2f_0$. A complete comparison requires the complex-tetrad and index-conjugation dictionary because Eq. (9) displays the conjugate derivative in a different component.

## Schwarzschild flow and turning-point memory

In retarded Eddington-Finkelstein coordinates,

$$
ds^2=-A(r)du^2-2,du,dr+r^2\gamma_{ab}dx^a dx^b,
\qquad
A(r)=1-\frac{2M}{r}.
\tag{11}
$$

The eikonal equation becomes

$$
2\partial_r f
=A(r)(\partial_r f)^2
+\frac{1}{r^2}\gamma^{ab}\partial_a f\,\partial_b f.
\tag{12}
$$

Let

$$
L^2=\bar\gamma^{ab}
\partial_{\bar x^a}f_0
\partial_{\bar x^b}f_0,
\qquad
D(r)=1-\frac{L^2}{r^2}A(r).
$$

The Hamilton-Jacobi momentum on the branch that vanishes at infinity is

$$
p_r=\frac{1-\sqrt{D(r)}}{A(r)}.
$$

Equation (13) integrates the total derivative of $f$ along the characteristic, not the partial derivative $p_r$ at fixed original angle:

$$
f=f_0(\bar x^a)
+\int_{+\infty}^{r}\frac{dr'}{A(r')}
\left[
1-\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-\frac{L^2}{r'^2}A(r')}}
\right].
\tag{13}
$$

The radial and angular characteristic equations are

$$
\frac{dr}{ds}=-2\sqrt{D(r)},
\tag{14}
$$

$$
\frac{d\theta}{dr}
=-\frac{L}{r^2\sqrt{D(r)}}
\tag{15}
$$

in the axisymmetric case $L=\partial_\theta f_0$.

A turning point satisfies

$$
D(r)=0
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
r^3-L^2r+2ML^2=0.
$$

The existence of an exterior turning point is not automatic for every nonzero $L$. The cubic requires

$$
L^2\geq27M^2,
$$

with the critical orbit at $r=3M$. The invariant memory statement is conditional: a change from $L=0$ data to sufficiently large $L$ data can move the congruence between geodesic classes without and with a turning point. The supertranslation does not create a turning point absent from the Schwarzschild null-geodesic potential.

## Spherical Minkowski flow and the printed Eq. (23)

The End Matter considers

$$
ds^2=-du^2-2,du,dr+r^2\gamma_{ab}dx^a dx^b
\tag{20}
$$

and obtains

$$
2\partial_r f
=(\partial_r f)^2
+\frac{1}{r^2}\gamma^{ab}\partial_a f\,\partial_b f.
\tag{21}
$$

The Hamilton-Jacobi function is

$$
F=p_r^2-2p_r+\frac{1}{r^2}\gamma^{ab}p_ap_b=0.
\tag{22}
$$

The correct characteristic equations include

$$
\frac{dr}{ds}=2(p_r-1),
\qquad
\frac{dx^a}{ds}=\frac{2}{r^2}p^a,
\qquad
\frac{dp_r}{ds}=\frac{2}{r^3}(p_a)^2,
$$

$$
\boxed{
\frac{dp_a}{ds}
=-\frac{1}{r^2}\partial_a\gamma^{cd}p_cp_d
}.
\tag{23 corrected}
$$

The printed Eq. (23) omits $r^{-2}$ in $dp_a/ds$. With the printed equation, the next claim $d(p_a)^2/ds=0$ does not follow. Restoring $r^{-2}$ makes the metric-derivative and momentum-derivative terms cancel. One may then set $(p_a)^2=L^2$, solve

$$
p_r=1\pm\sqrt{1-\frac{L^2}{r^2}},
\tag{24}
$$

and obtain the outgoing branch

$$
f=f_0(\bar x^a)
+r\left(1-\sqrt{1-\frac{L^2}{r^2}}\right).
\tag{25}
$$

This remains an implicit solution because the general angular characteristic map $x^a(r,\bar x)$ is not printed.

## Finite null plane and the pulled-back metric

For real light-cone coordinates,

$$
ds^2=-2dx^+dx^-+dx_i dx_i,
\tag{26}
$$

the characteristic coordinate map is

$$
x^+=\bar x^+-f(\bar x^\perp)
+\frac12\bar x^-(\bar\partial_i f)^2,
$$

$$
x^-=\bar x^-,
\qquad
x_i=\bar x_i-\bar x^-\bar\partial_i f.
\tag{27}
$$

Substitution yields

$$
\begin{aligned}
ds^2={}&-2d\bar x^+d\bar x^-
+d\bar x_i d\bar x_i
-2\bar x^-\bar\partial_i\bar\partial_jf\,d\bar x_i d\bar x_j\\
&+(\bar x^-)^2
\bar\partial_k\bar\partial_i f\,
\bar\partial_k\bar\partial_j f\,
d\bar x_i d\bar x_j.
\end{aligned}
\tag{28}
$$

The mixed $d\bar x^-d\bar x^i$ and $(d\bar x^-)^2$ terms cancel exactly. The transverse induced metric is

$$
\bar q=(I-\bar x^-\operatorname{Hess}f)^T
(I-\bar x^-\operatorname{Hess}f).
$$

## Light-ray operator and its exact scope

On $\Sigma:x^+=\mathrm{const}$,

$$
P_+=\int_\Sigma dx^-d^2x^\perp,T^{+-},
\qquad
P_-=\int_\Sigma dx^-d^2x^\perp,T^{++}.
\tag{16}
$$

The shifted-surface generator is

$$
\bar P
=\int_{\bar\Sigma}
\sqrt{\bar q_{ij}},d\bar x^-d^2\bar x^\perp
,\bar T^{++}.
\tag{17}
$$

Pulling it back to $\Sigma$ under $f\to\epsilon f$ gives

$$
\bar P=P_-
+2\epsilon\int_\Sigma dx^-d^2x^\perp
\,partial_i f(x^\perp)T^{+i}
+O(\epsilon^2).
\tag{18}
$$

The End Matter also invokes

$$
Q_\xi=\int_\Sigma d\Sigma_\mu T^{\mu\nu}\xi_\nu.
\tag{29}
$$

The coefficient $2$ in Eq. (18) comes from transforming both $+$ indices of $T^{++}$. The hypersurface measure has no net first-order correction after the inverse-coordinate Jacobian is included.

Equation (18) is a fixed-background, linearized light-ray-type operator. Its claimed algebra has three limitations:

1. Two such operators commute through $O(\epsilon)$ by order counting; their mutual commutator first appears at $O(\epsilon^2)$.
2. Genuine commutators with $P_\pm$ require the equal-null-time algebra of $T^{+i}$ with $T^{++}$ and $T^{+-}$, including contact, boundary, and possible Schwinger terms. Stress-tensor conservation alone is insufficient for arbitrary $f$.
3. The source's identification with Eq. (29) specifies only $\xi^i=\epsilon\partial_i f$. The full infinitesimal vector following from Eq. (27) also has an $x^+$ shift and an $x^-$-dependent transverse part. Active/passive signs and the moving-surface contribution must be fixed before Eqs. (18) and (29) can be declared equivalent.

The source restricts the construction to perturbation theory on a fixed background and states that a fully diffeomorphism-invariant nonlinear gravitational generator remains open.

## Translation into CPS and boundary language

- $f_0$ is a boundary residual-symmetry parameter.
- The characteristic flow extends $f_0$ to a bulk eikonal solution $f$.
- $u\mapsto u+f$ is a deformation of the null foliation or embedding.
- $(\theta,\sigma)$ are optical data whose permanent change is the proposed bulk-memory observable.
- $\bar P-P_-$ is a fixed-background candidate Hamiltonian built from a light-ray stress-tensor integral.
- $Q_\xi$ is an ordinary matter or fixed-background stress-tensor generator, not an Iyer-Wald or Barnich-Brandt gravitational surface charge.

A gravitational CPS completion would still need the active field-dependent vector $\xi_f$, the presymplectic potential $\Theta[g,\delta g]$, the current $\omega[g;\delta_1g,delta_2g]$, the codimension-two charge variation, integrability and flux conditions, moving-embedding or edge-mode terms, and the adjusted bracket. None of these is constructed in the Letter.

## Verification log

### Checked

- Mathematica inverse-metric contractions reproduce Eqs. (1), (3), (12), and (21).
- Hamilton-Jacobi characteristic equations reproduce Eqs. (5)-(8), with $df/ds$ correctly treated as a total derivative.
- An explicit optical-tensor calculation reproduces all components of Eqs. (9)-(10).
- The Schwarzschild constraint and characteristic equations reproduce Eqs. (13)-(15).
- The turning-point cubic has an exterior solution only for $L^2\geq27M^2$, with critical point $r=3M$.
- The full Jacobian of Eq. (27) reproduces Eq. (28), including the cancellation of mixed and $(d\bar x^-)^2$ terms.
- The same Jacobian reproduces the coefficient $2$ in Eq. (18) and the cancellation of the first-order measure correction.
- Differentiating Eq. (22) confirms the corrected $r^{-2}$ factor in Eq. (23) and restores conservation of $(p_a)^2$.

### Blocked

- Commutators of Eq. (18) with $P_+$ and $P_-$ require the equal-null-time stress-tensor algebra and boundary/contact prescriptions.
- The equivalence of Eqs. (18) and (29) requires the full infinitesimal vector, active/passive convention, and surface-embedding variation.
- A nonlinear gravitational charge requires a quasi-local stress tensor or a CPS construction absent from the paper.

### Failed

- Printed Eq. (23) is missing $r^{-2}$ in $dp_a/ds$ and does not imply the claimed conservation of $(p_a)^2$.
- Any unqualified claim that nonzero $L$ produces a Schwarzschild turning point is false; the necessary condition is $L^2\geq27M^2$.
- The full light-ray algebra and commutation with the Hamiltonian are not proven by the linearized pullback or stress-tensor conservation alone.
