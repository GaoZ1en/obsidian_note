# Usefulness

The reusable mechanism is sharper than a general modular-theory slogan:

1. reduce the local field algebra to radiative data on a characteristic boundary;
2. straighten a proposed angle-preserving null flow independently along each generator;
3. install the universal chiral KMS kernel in that flow coordinate;
4. compare two such covariances as a direct integral over transverse generators;
5. use the angular delta function to show that any nonzero change acts like an identity on an infinite-dimensional transverse space and is therefore not Hilbert--Schmidt.

For \(D\ge3\), distinct flows consequently define inequivalent local Hilbert spaces. A Connes-cocycle argument is then used to promote this Gaussian obstruction to a uniqueness statement for states and faithful normal semifinite weights in the vacuum sector.

# Klein--Gordon algebra as a symplectic observable algebra

The on-shell polynomial algebra is generated by \(\phi[f]\) with
\[
[\phi[f_1],\phi[f_2]]
=-iE[f_1,f_2],
\qquad
\phi[(\Box-m^2)f]=0,
\]
where \(E=A-R\) is the causal propagator. The solution \(Ef\) is the Hamiltonian vector associated with the linear observable:
\[
\phi[f]
=\int_\Sigma\sqrt h\,
\left(
\phi\,n^a\nabla_aEf
-Ef\,n^a\nabla_a\phi
\right).
\]
In CPS language,
\[
\phi[f]=\Omega_\Sigma(\phi,Ef),
\qquad
E[f,g]=\Omega(Ef,Eg),
\]
up to the paper's sign convention.

A zero-mean Gaussian state is determined by its two-point function. Its antisymmetric part is fixed by \(E\), while its symmetric covariance must obey positivity:
\[
|E[f,g]|
\le
2\sqrt{\omega(\phi[f]^2)\omega(\phi[g]^2)}.
\]

# Characteristic data on the past null boundary

For the unit Minkowski diamond, the past null boundary is
\[
\mathcal C^-\cong(0,1)\times S^{D-2},
\]
with affine generator coordinate \(u\). Rescale the field and test solution by
\[
\Phi=u^{(D-2)/2}\phi,
\qquad
F=u^{(D-2)/2}Ef.
\]
The characteristic observable and commutator become
\[
\phi[f]
=-2\int_{\mathcal C^-}du\,d\Omega\,
F\,\partial_u\Phi,
\]
\[
E[f_1,f_2]
=2\int du\,d\Omega\,
(\partial_uF_1)F_2.
\]
Thus \(\partial_u\Phi\) is the radiative datum, while \(F\) is its solution-valued smearing dual.

The vacuum covariance is ultralocal between distinct generators:
\[
\omega_{\rm vac}(\phi[f_1]\phi[f_2])
=-\frac1\pi
\lim_{\epsilon\to0^+}
\int du_1du_2d\Omega\,
\frac{F_1(u_1,\Omega)F_2(u_2,\Omega)}
{(u_1-u_2-i\epsilon)^2}.
\]
This angular delta structure is the later Hilbert-space obstruction.

# Straightening a proposed null modular flow

Let the angle-preserving flow be generated by
\[
\zeta=\chi(u,\Omega)\partial_u,
\]
future directed and nowhere vanishing in the open boundary. Define a flow coordinate
\[
\rho(u,\Omega)
=\int_{1/2}^{u}\frac{du'}{\chi(u',\Omega)},
\qquad
\zeta=\partial_\rho.
\]
The paper transports the unit-temperature chiral kernel from \(\rho\)-translation:
\[
\omega_\psi(\phi[f_1]\phi[f_2])
=-\pi\lim_{\epsilon\to0^+}
\int d\rho_1d\rho_2d\Omega\,
\frac{F_1(\rho_1,\Omega)F_2(\rho_2,\Omega)}
{\sinh^2\!\left[\pi(\rho_1-\rho_2-i\epsilon)\right]}.
\]
In frequency space,
\[
\omega_\psi(\phi[f_1]\phi[f_2])
=\frac1{2\pi}
\int d\nu\,d\Omega\,
\frac{\nu e^{-\nu/2}}{\sinh(\nu/2)}
\widehat F_{\psi,1}(-\nu,\Omega)
\widehat F_{\psi,2}(\nu,\Omega).
\]
The required endpoint regularity is
\[
u^{(D-2)/2}(\rho,\Omega)
\in L^2(d\rho\,d\Omega)
\quad\text{near }\rho=-\infty.
\]
The source checks the CCR antisymmetric part, positivity through
\[
|\coth(\nu/2)|\ge1,
\]
invariance, and the KMS strip relation. KMS uniqueness then identifies \(\psi_s\) as the modular flow of the constructed state.

# Relative covariance and the transverse Hilbert--Schmidt obstruction

The symmetric covariance is
\[
\mu_\psi(f_1,f_2)
=\frac1{2\pi}
\int d\nu\,d\Omega\,
\nu\coth(\nu/2)\,
\overline{\widehat F_{\psi,1}}
\widehat F_{\psi,2}.
\]
Given another flow \(\psi'\), the coordinate change between their straightened variables defines an integral operator \(A_{\psi'|\psi}\), and hence a relative covariance \(Q_{\psi|\psi'}\) on the one-particle Hilbert space.

For zero-mean Gaussian states, normal excitability in the same representation requires, in particular,
\[
\sqrt{Q_{\psi|\psi'}-iR_\psi}
-\sqrt{1-iR_\psi}
\]
to be Hilbert--Schmidt. But the flow remains angle preserving, so \(Q-1\) is decomposable in \(\Omega\):
\[
(Q-1)(\lambda_1,\Omega_1;\lambda_2,\Omega_2)
\sim
\Lambda(\lambda_1,\lambda_2;\Omega_1)
\delta_{S^{D-2}}(\Omega_1,\Omega_2).
\]
For \(D\ge3\), a nonzero identity-like factor on
\(L^2(S^{D-2})\) is not Hilbert--Schmidt. Therefore
\[
\omega_{\psi'}\prec\omega_\psi
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\omega_{\psi'}=\omega_\psi.
\]
Here “distinct” must mean distinct on a set of nonzero angular measure; changing a flow only on a null set does not change the represented covariance.

Reeh--Schlieder closes the possibility that a globally normal vacuum-sector state might nevertheless induce a forbidden local covariance: the local vacuum GNS completion maps unitarily onto the global vacuum sector.

# From Gaussian states to vacuum-sector weights

For a faithful normal semifinite weight \(\tau_\psi\), let \(w(s)\) be its Connes cocycle relative to the vacuum:
\[
w(s)^\dagger\alpha_s^{\rm vac}(a)w(s)
=\alpha_s^{\tau_\psi}(a).
\]
The vectors
\[
|\Omega_s\rangle=w(s)|\Omega\rangle
\]
remain in the vacuum representation. If the cocycle acts geometrically on the null data, their two-point functions are vacuum covariances pulled back by
\[
\theta_s=\psi_s\circ\varphi_{-s},
\]
where \(\varphi_s\) is the vacuum modular flow. In the coordinate adapted to the conjugated flow, this again becomes one of the Gaussian \(\sinh^{-2}\) kernels. The Hilbert--Schmidt obstruction forces the pulled-back state to be the vacuum.

The final step invokes a trivial vacuum modular centralizer to infer
\[
w(s)=e^{ics}\mathbf1,
\qquad
\tau_\psi=e^c\omega_{\rm vac}.
\]
This is the least self-contained part of the proof: the required ergodicity/trivial-centralizer theorem for the double-cone algebra is asserted rather than established in the source.

# Maxwell and graviton characteristic data

For Maxwell theory, gauge-invariant observables smear \(A_a\) with compactly supported divergence-free vectors. The CPS form is
\[
\Omega_{\rm EM}[A_1,A_2]
=-\int_\Sigma\sqrt h\,n^a
\left(
A_1^bF_{2,ab}-A_2^bF_{1,ab}
\right).
\]
In \(A_v=0\) gauge with \(A_u|_{\mathcal C^-}=0\), the free data are \(A_A(u,\Omega)\) modulo residual angular gauge transformations. The canonical rescaling is stated as
\[
\mathcal A_A=u^{(D-4)/2}A_A.
\]
The proposed covariance is the scalar kernel contracted with \(q_{AB}\).

The source later defines a Maxwell test datum with \(u^{(D-2)/2}\), not \(u^{(D-4)/2}\). This exponent mismatch should be repaired before using the Maxwell extension. Residual gauge transformations and possible null-edge sectors are also not incorporated into the covariance comparison.

For linearized gravity, the radiative data are the tracefree angular perturbations
\[
s_{AB}
=
\left(
q_A{}^Cq_B{}^D
-\frac1{D-2}q_{AB}q^{CD}
\right)\gamma_{CD}.
\]
The null CPS form is
\[
\Omega_{\rm GR}
=\frac{1}{32\pi G_N}
\int_{\mathcal C^-}du\,d\Omega\,
u^{D-6}q^{AC}q^{BD}
\left(
s_{1,AB}\partial_us_{2,CD}
-s_{2,AB}\partial_us_{1,CD}
\right).
\]
Rescaling
\[
\mathcal S_{AB}=u^{(D-6)/2}s_{AB}
\]
produces the same generator-ultralocal kernel with the tracefree polarization projector. The source explicitly notes that the resulting state is not the Minkowski vacuum, because linearized gravity is not conformal.

The gravitational subsection assumes an invariantly defined diamond, an edge condition used to remove the angular trace, and a gauge quotient. It does not construct gravitational dressing, corner modes, or a uniqueness theorem parallel to the scalar one.

# Verification boundary

The technical chain above is source-derived. I independently checked only the elementary frequency-kernel algebra: the antisymmetric part of
\[
\nu e^{-\nu/2}/\sinh(\nu/2)
\]
is proportional to \(-2\nu\), as required for a state-independent commutator up to the paper's Fourier/sign convention.

Not independently verified:

- the density of the image of compact bulk smearings in the angular direct-integral one-particle space;
- the operator implication from the square-root Hilbert--Schmidt condition to \(Q-1\) in the precise domains used;
- the Reeh--Schlieder identification for this characteristic presentation;
- the triviality of the vacuum double-cone modular centralizer;
- the KMS strip-boundary uniqueness assumptions;
- Maxwell/graviton positivity, Hadamard property, gauge quotient, and global-folium claims.

The scalar Gaussian obstruction is technically compelling and directly useful. The final weight theorem and gauge-field extensions should be cited with the assumptions above.
