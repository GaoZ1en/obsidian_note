## Symmetries and Conservation Laws in Lie-Poisson Electrodynamics

- arXiv: [2607.04522v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.04522v1)
- Categories: primary `hep-th`; cross-listed `gr-qc`, `math-ph`
- Reason codes: `T1-charge`, `T1-symmetry`, `T1-symplectic`, `T2-model`
- Technical relevance: high. Author-signal relevance: none from the explicit profile watchlist.
- Detailed note: [[Symmetries and Conservation Laws in Lie-Poisson Electrodynamics (2607.04522v1)]]

Source claim: geometric Lie-Poisson electrodynamics is locally equivalent to Maxwell theory after a field-dependent diffeomorphism $y(x)$ and a Seiberg-Witten-type field redefinition $B(y(x))=K(x;A)A(x)$. This lets the author pull Maxwell symmetries and Noether currents back to deformed LPE symmetries and conserved currents.

Main technical mechanism: LPE gauge transformations close as

$$\begin{align}
[\delta_f,\delta_g]A_\mu=\delta_{\{f,g\}}A_\mu, \qquad \delta_f A_\mu=M_\mu{}^\omega\partial_{\omega}f ,
\end{align}$$

where the Poisson bracket is Lie-Poisson on spacetime and $M$ is built from the group-theoretic matrix $\gamma(A)$ plus the Poisson deformation. The deformed field strength $F^s$ is exact and, after the field-dependent coordinate map $y(x)$, becomes an ordinary Maxwell field strength

$$\begin{align}
F^t_{\mu\nu}(y)=F_{\mu\nu}(y)=\partial_{\mu}B_\nu-\partial_\nu B_\mu .
\end{align}$$

The action equivalence is

$$\begin{align}
S[A]=S_{\mathrm M}[B].
\end{align}$$

The equation-of-motion map is

$$\begin{align}
\mathcal E_A^\sigma(x) =\det J\, \mathcal E_B^\mu(y(x)) \bar J^\beta{}_\mu\bar M^\sigma{}_\beta,
\end{align}$$

with inverse

$$\begin{align}
\mathcal E_B^\mu(y(x)) =(\det J)^{-1}J^\mu{}_\beta M^\beta{}_\sigma \mathcal E_A^\sigma(x).
\end{align}$$

For any Maxwell current $j_B^\mu$, the LPE current is the density pullback

$$\begin{align}
j_A^\mu=(\det J)\bar J^\mu{}_\nu j_B^\nu(y(x)).
\end{align}$$

Using the Piola identity, this satisfies

$$\begin{align}
\partial_\mu j_A^\mu=R^A_\sigma\mathcal E_A^\sigma, \qquad R^A_\sigma=R^B_\mu(y(x))J^\mu{}_\beta M^\beta{}_\sigma .
\end{align}$$

The deformed Poincare transformation is

$$\begin{align}
\delta_\xi^{\mathrm P}A_\sigma =M^\beta{}_\sigma \tilde\xi^\alpha F^s_{\alpha\beta} +\delta_{f(x;\xi)}A_\sigma .
\end{align}$$

Likely use case: a compact testbed for transporting Noether currents, symmetry generators, and gauge orbits through nonlinear field redefinitions. It is not a covariant phase-space paper, but the off-shell current identity and the $A\leftrightarrow B$ map are directly useful for thinking about CPS under nontrivial field redefinitions and non-Abelianized $U(1)$ gauge symmetry.

Verification status: Mathematica checked the Piola identity and the current-pullback divergence identity in two dimensions for arbitrary $y^1(x^1,x^2)$ and $y^2(x^1,x^2)$. The Appendix A group-coordinate identities involving $\gamma,\rho,M$ remain source-derived.

## Lienard-Wiechert fields in AdS and flat-space antipodal matching from geodesic-centered Coulombic data

- arXiv: [2607.05395v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.05395v1)
- Categories: primary `hep-th`
- Reason codes: `T1-boundary`, `T1-symmetry`, `T2-celestial-carrollian`, `T2-model`
- Technical relevance: medium-high. Author-signal relevance: none.

Source claim: flat-space antipodal matching of Coulombic electromagnetic data can be derived by regarding a uniformly moving charge as a static Coulomb field written in coordinates centered on the charge geodesic; the same construction has a finite-radius AdS analogue using global AdS geodesics and embedding-space invariants.

Main technical mechanism: the paper starts from the Coulomb solution in global $\mathrm{AdS}_4$, recenters it on an arbitrary timelike geodesic, and reconstructs the field in global coordinates. The gauge-invariant component $F_{\rho\tau}$ obeys an exact finite-radius antipodal covariance. Taking the boundary limit near $\tau=\pm\pi/2$ and then the large-$L$ limit gives the familiar flat-space relation

$$\begin{align}
\left.\lim_{r\to\infty}r^2F_{ru}(u,\hat x)\right|_{\mathscr I^+_-} = \left.\lim_{r\to\infty}r^2F_{rv}(v,-\hat x)\right|_{\mathscr I^-_+}.
\end{align}$$

Likely use case: direct background for large-gauge charge matching, soft-photon memory, and the AdS-to-flat origin of null-infinity corner data. It is especially useful for any attempt to formulate finite-radius AdS precursors of asymptotic charge matching.

Reason it is not high-priority today: it is a strong boundary/asymptotic-structure paper, but the source is a classical Maxwell solution and matching construction rather than a new CPS charge algebra or Noether-current formalism.
