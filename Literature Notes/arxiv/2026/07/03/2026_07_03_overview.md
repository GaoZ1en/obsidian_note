## Boundary observables in string field theory

- arXiv: [2607.02454v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.02454v1)
- Categories: primary `hep-th`
- Reason codes: `T1-charge`, `T1-boundary`, `T1-symmetry`, `T1-Wald-CPS`, `T2-model`
- Technical relevance: high. Author-signal relevance: none from the explicit profile watchlist.
- Detailed note: [[Boundary observables in string field theory (2607.02454v1)]]

Source claim: free SFT with boundary admits gauge-invariant observables analogous to Brown-York charges. The SFT boundary tadpole is not a tensor but a boundary-localized state, and contracting it with a BRST-closed isometry gives a conserved number.

Main technical mechanism: the free SFT boundary operator is

$$
B_{\partial M}=[\Theta_M,Q],
$$

and a Cauchy-surface cut gives

$$
B_\Sigma=[\Theta_-,Q].
$$

For a free solution $Q\Psi_*=0$ and a BRST-closed isometry $Q\Xi=0$, the charge is

$$
q(\Xi,\Psi_*)=\omega(\Xi,B_\Sigma B_{\partial M}\Psi_*).
$$

This exactly parallels the Brown-York move where an inadmissible parameter $\lambda^a=\xi^a\Theta_-$ localizes the boundary tadpole to $\partial M\cap\Sigma$.

The open-string examples are not decorative. At the massless level the boundary-mode action reduces to Maxwell theory; the constant field-strength background gives

$$
q(\Xi,\Psi_*)=\alpha'F^{tz}\operatorname{Vol}(\Sigma\cap\partial M),
$$

while a Coulomb vacuum shift sourced by

$$
Q\Psi_{\rm v}=e\,\delta(\vec x)\alpha^t_{-1}c_0c_1|0\rangle
$$

gives $q=e$ for a compact surface enclosing the source.

In interacting SFT the paper proposes the charge one-form

$$
\eta(\Xi_\Psi,\Psi)=
\omega(\Xi_\Psi,B^\Psi_\Sigma B^\Psi_{\partial M}\delta\Psi),
\qquad
B^\Psi_{\partial M}=[\Theta_M,Q_\Psi],
$$

with $Q_\Psi$ the shifted BRST operator. Under the Hodge-Kodaira and obstruction assumptions stated in the source, $\eta$ is closed, horizontal on gauge directions, and conserved.

Likely use case: a direct SFT analogue of boundary charges and edge-mode/CPS surface-charge one-forms. The identity

$$
\eta=\iota_{\Theta_M\Xi_\Psi}\Omega
$$

is especially useful for translating the construction into the vault's CPS language.

Verification status: Mathematica checked the antisymmetric contraction in the Maxwell flux example under $F^{zt}=-F^{tz}$ and $t\neq z$. The full BRST oscillator overlaps and interacting $L_\infty$ closedness proof remain source-derived because they require a custom ghost/oscillator and homological algebra implementation.

## What's the Matter with 3D Gravity?

- arXiv: [2607.01319v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.01319v1)
- Categories: primary `hep-th`; cross-listed `gr-qc`
- Reason codes: `T2-spectral`, `T2-dS-BH-holography`, `T2-model`
- Technical relevance: medium. Author-signal relevance: none.
- Detailed note: [[What's the Matter with 3D Gravity? (2607.01319v1)]]

Source claim: scalar matter coupled to AdS3 gravity can be quantized in a Virasoro-TQFT language by rewriting the scalar determinant as a gas of worldlines and treating each worldline as a conical defect in the gravitational phase space.

Main technical mechanism: a worldline with coupling $\mu$ creates a defect angle

$$
\theta=\frac{8\pi G_N\mu}{\ell},
$$

and conformal weight

$$
h=k(1-\alpha_\theta^2)=\frac{\mu}{2}-\frac{G_N\mu^2}{\ell},
\qquad
k=\frac{\ell}{16G_N}.
$$

At fixed particle number,

$$
\mathcal M_{{\rm grav},n}
=\frac{\mathcal T_{\Sigma,n,\theta}
\times\overline{\mathcal T}_{\Sigma,n,\theta}}
{\operatorname{Map}(\Sigma,n)}.
$$

For thermal AdS3, equivariant localization over the disk with $n$ defects produces the perturbative partition function. The permutation-cycle expansion reorganizes as the Wilson spool

$$
Z=\langle\exp\mathbb W\rangle_{\rm grav},
\qquad
\mathbb W=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac1k\mathcal W^{(k)}.
$$

Likely use case: a clean bridge between AdS3 one-loop determinants, Virasoro conformal blocks, Wilson lines, and backreacted multiparticle spectral data.

Reason it is not high-priority today: it is primarily a spectral/quantization technology paper rather than a direct new boundary-charge or CPS construction.

## Quantum JT Gravity in a box as a Pöschl-Teller Scattering Problem

- arXiv: [2607.01385v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.01385v1)
- Categories: primary `hep-th`
- Reason codes: `T2-spectral`, `T2-dS-BH-holography`, `T1-boundary` nearby
- Technical relevance: medium. Author-signal relevance: none.

Source claim: finite-cutoff JT gravity with Dirichlet walls can be canonically quantized using the geodesic length $L$ between the two boundaries and its conjugate momentum $P$.

Main technical mechanism: the classical horizon dilaton is encoded by a Pöschl-Teller operator,

$$
\Phi_h^2(L,P)=P^2+\frac{\Phi_b^2}{\cosh^2(L/2)},
$$

and the energy is

$$
E(\Phi_h)=\Phi_b-\sqrt{\Phi_b^2-\Phi_h^2}.
$$

The preferred quantum ordering gives an effective potential

$$
V_{\rm eff}(L)=\frac{\nu^2}{\cosh^2(L/2)}
-\frac{1}{4\sinh^2L},
$$

with Friedrichs self-adjoint extension, wavefunctions

$$
\psi_s(L)=
\frac{\sqrt{\tanh(L/2)}}{\cosh(L/2)^{2is}}
\,{}_2F_1\!\left(\frac12+is+i\nu,\frac12+is-i\nu,1;\tanh^2(L/2)\right),
$$

and spectral density

$$
\rho(E)=
\frac{2(\nu-E)\sinh\!\left(2\pi\sqrt{E(2\nu-E)}\right)}
{\cosh\!\left(2\pi\sqrt{E(2\nu-E)}\right)+\cosh(2\pi\nu)}.
$$

Likely use case: finite-boundary and finite-cutoff spectral notes. The paper distinguishes true finite-wall quantization from a pure $T\bar T$ deformation of the asymptotic Schwarzian spectrum.

Reason it is not high-priority today: very relevant to spectral/finite-boundary work, but it is not a charge/CPS paper and would require a longer separate technical note to cover the Wilson-function correlators.

## BRST-BV approach to fields in Poincare patch of AdS

- arXiv: [2607.02495v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.02495v1)
- Categories: primary `hep-th`
- Reason codes: `T2-model`, `T3-math`
- Technical relevance: medium. Author-signal relevance: none.

Source claim: free AdS fields of arbitrary masses and symmetry type admit a universal BRST-BV Lagrangian in the Poincare patch, including symmetric massless, massive, partially-massless, and continuous-spin cases.

Main technical mechanism: all fields and antifields are packaged into a generating field $\Phi(x,z,\theta,\mathbf s)$ and the action is

$$
S=\int d^dxdz\,\mathcal L,
\qquad
\mathcal L=\int d\theta\,\Phi^{\hat\dagger}Q\Phi.
$$

The BRST operator has the universal form

$$
Q=\theta(\Box-\mathcal M^2)
+M^{\eta a}\partial^a+M^\eta
+\frac12M^{\eta\eta}\partial_\theta.
$$

The construction reduces the problem to algebraic spin operators: $M^2$ and $M^\eta$ are expressed through the radial coordinate and spin data, while the AdS mass operator $A$ is built from $B^{\hat A}$, $M^{\hat A\hat B}$, and the $so(d,2)$ Casimir.

Likely use case: a formalism reference for AdS free fields and higher-spin/continuous-spin BV bookkeeping.

Reason it is not high-priority today: it is an AdS field-formalism paper, not tied to boundary charges, finite-boundary phase space, or current Proca/Maxwell derivations.
