The central point is elementary but consequential. In the Carrollian contraction,

$$\begin{align}
[P^0,K^0]=0,
\end{align}$$

so the temporal translation chain cannot also be the chain lowered by $K^0$. Completing the representation therefore requires a second direction. The paper packages this direction into a continuous label $\kappa$, alongside continuous boost labels $\beta^a$, and solves the global Ward identities on the enlarged spacetime-plus-label space.

## Two Independent Temporal Descendant Directions

A primary obeys

$$\begin{align}
[D,\mathcal O_{\rm p}] =\Delta\mathcal O_{\rm p}, \qquad [K^\mu,\mathcal O_{\rm p}]=0. \tag{2.1}
\end{align}$$

Ordinary temporal descendants are

$$\begin{align}
(P^0)^n\mathcal O_{\rm p}.
\tag{2.2}
\end{align}$$

In an uncontracted conformal algebra, $K^0$ lowers this chain. The Carrollian algebra instead has

$$\begin{align}
[P^0,K^0]=0, \tag{2.4}
\end{align}$$

and hence

$$\begin{align}
K^0(P^0)^n\mathcal O_{\rm p}=0. \tag{2.5}
\end{align}$$

The paper introduces an independent sequence

$$\begin{align}
\mathcal O_0=\mathcal O_{\rm p}, \qquad [K^0,\mathcal O_n] =\mathcal O_{n-1}, \qquad n\ge1, \tag{2.6}
\end{align}$$

with

$$\begin{align}
[D,\mathcal O_n] =(\Delta+n)\mathcal O_n. \tag{2.7}
\end{align}$$

Every level is then closed under all translations:

$$\begin{align}
P^{\mu_1}\cdots P^{\mu_m}\mathcal O_n = \partial_{\mu_1}\cdots\partial_{\mu_m}\mathcal O_n. \tag{2.8}
\end{align}$$

Thus the module has one direction generated by $P^0$ and a separate direction lowered by $K^0$. Figure 1 makes this split explicit.

## Two-Dimensional Generating Operator

In two spacetime dimensions, the boost chain and $K^0$-chain are resummed as

$$\begin{align}
\mathcal O_\Delta(\beta,\kappa) = \sum_{n_B,n_{K^0}\ge0} (i\beta)^{n_B}(i\kappa)^{n_{K^0}} \mathcal O_\Delta(n_B,n_{K^0}). \tag{2.14}
\end{align}$$

At the origin,

$$\begin{align}
[B^1,\mathcal O] =i\beta\mathcal O, \qquad [K^0,\mathcal O] =i\kappa\mathcal O. \tag{2.16}
\end{align}$$

Algebra closure forces

$$\begin{align}
[K^1,\mathcal O_\Delta(\beta,\kappa)] = \kappa\partial_\beta \mathcal O_\Delta(\beta,\kappa), \tag{2.19}
\end{align}$$

$$\begin{align}
[D,\mathcal O_\Delta(\beta,\kappa)] = (\Delta+\kappa\partial_\kappa) \mathcal O_\Delta(\beta,\kappa). \tag{2.21}
\end{align}$$

After translation to $(u,z)$, the local representation acts on

$$\begin{align}
(u,z;\beta,\kappa).
\end{align}$$

The new terms in Eq. 2.24 are

$$\begin{align}
K^0 =-z^2\partial_u-2iz\beta+i\kappa,
\end{align}$$

$$\begin{align}
K^1 =2uz\partial_u+z^2\partial_z +2z(\kappa\partial_\kappa+\Delta) +\kappa\partial_\beta+2ui\beta.
\end{align}$$

The hypersurface $\kappa=0$ is invariant and recovers the previously used boost-charge representation. The generic $\kappa\neq0$ sector is a genuinely enlarged module, not a small deformation of that hypersurface.

## Three-Dimensional Boost Lattice

In three spacetime dimensions, the paper first uses

$$\begin{align}
B^\pm,\qquad K^\pm,
\end{align}$$

so that the spatial rotation remains diagonal. The discrete basis is

$$\begin{align}
\mathcal O_{\Delta,l}
(n_{B^+},n_{B^-},n_{K^0}),
\end{align}$$

and Figure 2 shows the resulting infinite boost lattice across rotation weights.

The generating operator is

$$\begin{align}
\mathcal O_{\Delta,l} (\xi^+,\xi^-,\kappa) = \sum (\xi^+)^{n_{B^+}} (\xi^-)^{n_{B^-}} (i\kappa)^{n_{K^0}} \mathcal O_{\Delta,l} (n_{B^+},n_{B^-},n_{K^0}). \tag{2.56}
\end{align}$$

With real labels

$$\begin{align}
\xi^\pm =\frac1{\sqrt2}(i\beta^1\pm\beta^2), \tag{2.58}
\end{align}$$

one obtains

$$\begin{align}
[B^a,\mathcal O]=i\beta^a\mathcal O,
\end{align}$$

$$\begin{align}
[K^0,\mathcal O]=i\kappa\mathcal O,
\end{align}$$

$$\begin{align}
[K^a,\mathcal O] =\kappa\partial_{\beta^a}\mathcal O. \tag{2.60}
\end{align}$$

The full local representation on

$$\begin{align}
(u,z^a;\beta^a,\kappa)
\end{align}$$

is Eq. 2.63. Its defining new pieces are $\kappa\partial_{\beta^a}$, $2ui\beta^a$, and the spatial rotation of the boost-label vector.

## Adjoint Representations and Orbit Sectors

Using anti-Hermitian generators, flat Lebesgue pairings, and discarded boundary terms, the principal lines depend on whether the pairing includes $d\kappa$.

In 2D:

$$\begin{align}
\Delta=\frac32+i\nu \quad(\kappa\neq0\text{ sector}),
\end{align}$$

$$\begin{align}
\mathcal O_\Delta^\dagger(\beta,\kappa) = \mathcal O'_{3-\Delta}(-\beta,-\kappa), \tag{2.29, 2.33}
\end{align}$$

whereas on $\kappa=0$,

$$\begin{align}
\Delta=1+i\nu,
\end{align}$$

$$\begin{align}
\mathcal O_\Delta^\dagger(\beta,0) = \mathcal O'_{2-\Delta}(-\beta,0). \tag{2.35--2.36}
\end{align}$$

In 3D:

$$\begin{align}
\Delta=2+i\nu \quad(\kappa\neq0),
\end{align}$$

$$\begin{align}
\mathcal O_{\Delta,l}^\dagger = \mathcal O'_{4-\Delta,-l} (-\beta^a,-\kappa), \tag{2.67, 2.70}
\end{align}$$

while

$$\begin{align}
\Delta=\frac32+i\nu \quad(\kappa=0),
\end{align}$$

$$\begin{align}
\mathcal O_{\Delta,l}^\dagger = \mathcal O'_{3-\Delta,-l} (-\beta^a,0). \tag{2.72--2.73}
\end{align}$$

Finite transformations split the label space into separate orbits:

- $\kappa>0$;
- $\kappa<0$;
- $\kappa=0$ with fixed $\beta$ in 2D;
- $\kappa=0$ with fixed $\vec\beta^{\,2}$ in 3D.

Conjugation exchanges the two nonzero-$\kappa$ orbits. On $\kappa=0$, a conjugate pair with nonzero boost label has vanishing two-point function in the derived vacuum pairing; the paper calls the associated state null. This is pairing-dependent nullness, not yet a constructed quotient Hilbert space.

## Casimirs and the Candidate Massive Sector

Fourier-transforming $u\leftrightarrow\rho$, the quadratic Casimirs are

$$\begin{align}
\mathcal C_2^{\rm 2D} =\rho\kappa-\beta^2, \tag{2.44}
\end{align}$$

$$\begin{align}
\mathcal C_2^{\rm 3D} =\rho\kappa-\vec\beta^{\,2}. \tag{2.80}
\end{align}$$

At $\kappa=0$, the 3D quartic Casimir reduces to

$$\begin{align}
\mathcal C_4|_{\kappa=0} =-(\Delta-2)(\Delta-1) \vec\beta^{\,2}. \tag{2.85}
\end{align}$$

For $\kappa\neq0$, higher Casimirs remain differential operators, so the corresponding orbit representations are reducible and are not decomposed further.

The paper proposes

$$\begin{align}
m^2=\mathcal C_2 =\kappa\rho-\vec\beta^{\,2},
\end{align}$$

$$\begin{align}
\rho =\frac{m^2+\vec\beta^{\,2}}{\kappa}. \tag{2.86}
\end{align}$$

A positive real $m^2$ cannot occur on $\kappa=0$, but can occur for $\kappa\neq0$. This makes the added descendant coordinate a plausible label for massive hard states in flat holography. The paper does not yet supply a bulk-boundary dictionary or demonstrate that a physical massive field realizes this module.

## Ward Identities on Spacetime and Label Space

For

$$\begin{align}
\mathcal G = \langle0| \mathcal O_1\mathcal O_2
|0\rangle, \tag{3.1}
\end{align}$$

global invariance requires

$$\begin{align}
(\mathcal D_{G,1}+\mathcal D_{G,2}) \mathcal G=0. \tag{3.3}
\end{align}$$

Because the generators differentiate $\beta^a,\kappa$ as well as spacetime coordinates, the solutions take the schematic form

$$\begin{align}
\mathcal G = f(\mathcal I_A)\, \mathcal K\, \mathcal S. \tag{3.5}
\end{align}$$

$\mathcal S$ is distributional support, $\mathcal K$ is a fixed kinematic factor, and $f$ is an arbitrary function of surviving invariants. These label-space invariants behave like two-point cross-ratios and are not fixed by global symmetry alone.

### Two Generic Nonzero-$\kappa$ Insertions in 2D

The non-contact branch is

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal G_{\rm m}
={}&
f\!\left(
\frac{z^2}{\kappa_1\kappa_2},
\frac{\kappa_1\beta_2^2+\kappa_2\beta_1^2}
{\kappa_1+\kappa_2}
\right)
e^{-iu(\beta_1+\beta_2)/z}\\
&\times
\frac{
|\kappa_1|^{1/2-\Delta_1}
|\kappa_2|^{1/2-\Delta_2}}
{|\beta_1-\beta_2|}
\delta\!\left(
z-\frac{\kappa_1+\kappa_2}
{\beta_1-\beta_2}
\right).
\end{aligned}
\tag{3.10}
$$

The independent contact branch is

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal G_{\rm e}
={}&
f\!\left(
\frac{u}{\kappa_1-\kappa_2}
\right)
e^{-iu(\beta_1^2+\beta_2^2)/
(\kappa_1-\kappa_2)}
|u|^{2-\Delta_1-\Delta_2}\\
&\times
\delta(z)
\delta(\beta_1+\beta_2)
\delta(\kappa_1+\kappa_2).
\end{aligned}
\tag{3.12}
$$

The 3D analogues have shifted-support magnetic and point-contact electric branches. A conjugate pair lies only on the contact branch.

### Two $\kappa=0$ Insertions

In 2D the magnetic branch is

$$\begin{align}
\mathcal G_{\rm m} = f(\beta_1+\beta_2) e^{-iu(\beta_1+\beta_2)/z}
|z|^{-\Delta_1-\Delta_2}
\delta(\beta_1-\beta_2)
\delta_{\Delta_1,\Delta_2}, \tag{3.15}
\end{align}$$

while the contact branch is

$$\begin{align}
\mathcal G_{\rm e} = c_{\rm e}
|u|^{1-\Delta_1-\Delta_2}
\delta(z)
\delta(\beta_1)
\delta(\beta_2).
\tag{3.16}
\end{align}$$

The $\kappa=0$ solutions must be derived separately; generic formulas are singular on these loci and do not have a uniform limit.

### Mixed Sectors

With one $\kappa\neq0$ and one $\kappa=0$ insertion, only a magnetic non-contact branch exists. A contact ansatz forces the nominally nonzero $\kappa$ to vanish and reduces to the zero-zero problem.

## Blow-Ups of Contact Support

In 3D, ordinary point support removes angular information and forces $l_1+l_2=0$. The real blow-up

$$\begin{align}
\vec z =r_z(\cos\phi_z,\sin\phi_z),
\end{align}$$

$$\begin{align}
\mathbb R^2 \longrightarrow
[0,\infty)_{r_z}\times S^1_{\phi_z}
\tag{3.29}
\end{align}$$

replaces the origin by a circle. The angular factor on the exceptional circle can carry orbital weight that compensates $l_1+l_2$. The $\kappa=0$ analysis similarly blows up the origins in $\vec z$, $\vec\beta_1-\vec\beta_2$, and $\vec\beta_1+\vec\beta_2$.

These correlators live on enlarged spaces with exceptional angular data. They are not alternate notations for ordinary $\delta^{(2)}$-supported distributions, and their physical operator interpretation remains open.

## Use for Celestial and Carrollian Work

The paper suggests the following division:

$$\begin{align}
(\vec\beta,\kappa)=(0,0) \quad\leftrightarrow\quad \text{standard massless radiative module},
\end{align}$$

$$\begin{align}
\kappa\neq0 \quad\leftrightarrow\quad \text{candidate massive hard module}.
\end{align}$$

The important technical lesson is that Ward identities must act on spacetime and representation-label variables together, and that different orbit strata define different distributional problems. This could matter when coupling massive hard insertions to soft/Carrollian Ward identities.

The limitations are substantial:

- “complete family” means closure after adjoining this chosen $K^0$ chain, not a classification of all Carrollian modules;
- the $\kappa$-series is used both formally and as a function on real orbits without a topology or convergence theorem;
- anti-Hermiticity depends on chosen flat measures and vanishing boundary terms and does not prove positivity;
- null states are not quotiented;
- nonzero-$\kappa$ sectors are not decomposed into irreducibles;
- the massive interpretation is kinematic;
- only global symmetry and two-point functions are studied, with arbitrary functions left unfixed;
- no full BMS central extension, OPE, crossing, or dynamics is included.
