The main reusable result is a division of labor: OPE data and KMS determine every nonzero Matsubara mode, while a single static bulk problem fixes the remaining spatial function. This is more useful locally than the individual coefficient table.

# Thermal OPE data and the KMS remainder

For a scalar primary \(\phi\) in a four-dimensional holographic CFT, the thermal two-point function has two large-\(C_T\) sectors:

\[
\text{multi-stress operators}:
\qquad
(\Delta,J)=(4n,0,2,\ldots,2n),
\]

\[
\text{double traces}:
\qquad
[\phi\phi]_{m,J},
\qquad
\Delta=2\Delta_\phi+2m+J.
\]

The near-boundary bulk Klein--Gordon expansion first determines the multi-stress contribution. An analytically regularized image sum then restores thermal periodicity. At nonzero spatial separation, however, KMS only implies

\[
g_\beta(\tau,x)
=g_{\rm images}(\tau,x)+c(x),
\]

where \(c(x)\) is independent of Euclidean time. Equivalently, every nonzero Matsubara coefficient is fixed, but the zero-frequency mode is not.

The source uses Hurwitz-zeta regularization for the image sum and Padé--Borel resummation for the Taylor series that encodes the double-trace sector.

# The static bulk equation

The bulk background is planar Euclidean AdS\(_5\)--Schwarzschild. For the worked example \(\Delta_\phi=3/2\), Fourier transforming the zero-frequency mode over the three boundary spatial directions gives

\[
\left[
(1-z^4)\partial_z^2
-4z^3\partial_z
-\frac94 z^2
-k^2
\right]\psi_k(z)=0.
\tag{3.7}
\]

The two boundary behaviors encode the source and response. At the other endpoint, regularity at the Euclidean horizon selects a unique solution. A convenient representative is

\[
R_k(z)
=
\operatorname{HeunG}
\left(
2,\frac{k^2}{4}+\frac9{16};
\frac34,\frac34,1,\frac12;
1-z^2
\right).
\]

Its connection data at the AdS boundary are

\[
A(k)=R_k(0),
\qquad
B(k)=R'_k(0).
\]

The static boundary Green function is therefore

\[
\boxed{
\widetilde g^{(0)}(k)
=
-4\pi\frac{R_k(0)}{R'_k(0)}.
}
\tag{3.13}
\]

This ratio is the missing input that KMS cannot supply. Its poles in complex \(k\) are also the static screening spectrum.

# Completing the image reconstruction

Let:

- \(g^{(0)}(x)\) be the inverse spatial transform of the horizon-regular ratio;
- \(g_{\rm images}^{(0)}(x)\) be the zero Matsubara component generated by the stress-sector image sum;
- \(g_{\rm images}^{[0]}(x)\) be the corresponding time-independent image contribution in position space.

The full missing term is assembled as

\[
\boxed{
g^{[0]}(x)
=
g^{(0)}(x)
+g_{\rm images}^{[0]}(x)
-g_{\rm images}^{(0)}(x).
}
\tag{3.18}
\]

Thus the complete algorithm is:

1. compute the multi-stress near-boundary solution;
2. impose KMS by the regularized image sum;
3. solve only the \(\omega_n=0\) bulk equation with horizon regularity;
4. replace the image sum’s zero mode by the exact static mode;
5. Taylor-expand and project onto spin \(J\).

For \(\Delta_\phi=3/2\), individual terms develop regulator poles and logarithms, but the combination above cancels them before the double-trace data are read off.

\[
\begin{array}{c|l}

\hline
0 & b_{0,0}=1.113079193089\\
1 & b_{1,0}=0.4892006,\quad b_{0,2}=2.3990636\\
2 & b_{2,0}=1.42104,\quad b_{1,2}=0.86546,\quad b_{0,4}=6.853229\\
3 & b_{3,0}=54.4327,\quad b_{2,2}=21.46210,\\
  & b_{1,4}=-37.7555,\quad b_{0,6}=20.38886
\end{array}
\]

The first three available comparisons agree with previous results; the remaining entries are presented as new. The large value and sign changes at the last displayed level make the resummation and projection conventions important if these numbers are reused.

# Bulk-cone singularities and their cancellation

The stress-tensor sector by itself develops complex or bouncing bulk-cone singularities at spacelike separation, with a representative onset near

\[
x_c\simeq2.622.
\]

After the double-trace completion, the source’s Borel-resummed Bessel representation and an independent bulk PDE/AAA analytic continuation agree numerically and show no corresponding singularity at the tested locations. The full Wightman correlator retains the standard light-cone singularity.

The safe statement is **strong numerical evidence for cancellation in the tested domain**, not an analytic theorem that the full correlator is globally free of every complex bulk-cone singularity.

# Relation to boundary-condition work

The horizon regularity condition functions as an infrared boundary condition that selects the missing static source/response ratio. This makes the construction conceptually close to alternative quantization and mixed-boundary problems:

\[
\text{UV OPE data}
+\text{KMS}
+\text{IR regularity}
\longrightarrow
\text{complete thermal correlator}.
\]

The paper keeps the standard quantization and a minimally coupled scalar. It does not vary the AdS boundary condition, construct a symplectic flux, or analyze the normalizability domain of alternative quantization. Those are natural extensions rather than results of the source.
