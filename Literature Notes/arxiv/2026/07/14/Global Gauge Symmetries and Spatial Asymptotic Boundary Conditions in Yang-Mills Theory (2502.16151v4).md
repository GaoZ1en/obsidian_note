The paper's reusable result is not merely the quotient

$$\begin{align}
\mathcal G_{\mathrm{phys}}=\mathcal G^I/\mathcal G_0^\infty.
\end{align}$$

It supplies a sequence of reasons for the numerator and denominator. A fixed asymptotic Dirichlet sector determines which transformations act on the instantaneous state space; the canonical phase space then exposes the Gauss-law boundary term; finally, localizability identifies the transformations that are genuinely generated by constraints. This is directly useful for separating boundary-preserving, charge-carrying symmetries from presymplectic degeneracies in the vault's CPS and finite-boundary work.

# Connections, boundary sectors, and conventions

The spacetime split is $M=\Sigma\times\mathbb R$ with $\Sigma\cong\mathbb R^3$, temporal gauge $A_0=0$, and compact gauge group $G$ with Lie algebra $\mathfrak g$. The principal bundle $P\to\Sigma$ is eventually trivialized, but the first half of the paper keeps the bundle language to distinguish intrinsic boundary data from a chosen trivialization.

The trace is an $\operatorname{Ad}(G)$-invariant inner product. The action convention is

$$\begin{align}
S[A]= -\frac12\int_M\operatorname{Tr}(F\wedge *F),
\end{align}$$

up to the paper's suppressed coupling normalization. All boundary statements are spatial and equal-time statements; the paper does not derive null-infinity soft charges.

# The instantaneous Yang--Mills theory chooses a boundary sector

The configuration space of connections is affine. Once a reference connection is chosen, a tangent vector is an adjoint-valued one-form,

$$\begin{align}
T_A\operatorname{Conn}(P)\cong\Omega^1(\Sigma,\operatorname{Ad}P).
\end{align}$$

For a spatial connection $A$ and velocity $\alpha_A$, the instantaneous Lagrangian has the schematic form

$$\begin{align}
L(A,\alpha_A) =\frac12\int_\Sigma\operatorname{Tr} \left(\alpha_A\wedge *\alpha_A-F(A)\wedge *F(A)\right).
\end{align}$$

This integral is defined only on a function space with sufficient falloff. In spherical coordinates the Euclidean norm of a one-form contains different radial and angular weights. A uniform Cartesian estimate $|\alpha_i|=O(r^{-3/2-\epsilon})$ is sufficient for square integrability; in spherical components one instead tracks the weights of $dr$, $d\theta$, and $d\phi$.

The conceptual step is to fix the boundary connection before quotienting. If the pullback of $A$ to the asymptotic boundary is fixed to $A_\infty$, then tangent vectors obey

$$\begin{align}
\delta A|_{\partial\hat\Sigma}=0.
\end{align}$$

Thus distinct $A_\infty$ are disjoint instantaneous sectors, not points connected by allowed time evolution inside one tangent bundle. An allowed gauge transformation must map the chosen sector to itself.

# Compactifying spatial infinity

At $t=0$, the conformal embedding is

$$\begin{align}
R=2\arctan r, \qquad K(r)=\frac{2}{1+r^2},
\end{align}$$

and

$$\begin{align}
f^*\left(dR^2+\sin^2R\,d\Omega_2^2\right) =K^2\left(dr^2+r^2d\Omega_2^2\right).
\end{align}$$

Spatial infinity $r\to\infty$ becomes the boundary $R=\pi$ of the compact slice $\hat\Sigma$. For a smooth one-form

$$\begin{align}
\hat\alpha=\hat\alpha_R\,dR+\hat\alpha_\theta\,d\theta+\hat\alpha_\phi\,d\phi,
\end{align}$$

the actual pullback components satisfy

$$\begin{align}
\alpha_r=K\hat\alpha_R, \qquad \alpha_\theta=\hat\alpha_\theta, \qquad \alpha_\phi=\hat\alpha_\phi.
\end{align}$$

If $\hat\alpha$ vanishes at $R=\pi$, a Taylor expansion in $\rho=\pi-R=2/r+O(r^{-3})$ gives

$$\begin{align}
\alpha_r=O(r^{-2}), \qquad \alpha_\theta=O(r^{-1}), \qquad \alpha_\phi=O(r^{-1}),
\end{align}$$

which is sufficient for square integrability. Likewise, for a smooth curvature two-form,

$$\begin{align}
F_{r\theta}=K\hat F_{R\theta}, \qquad F_{r\phi}=K\hat F_{R\phi}, \qquad F_{\theta\phi}=\hat F_{\theta\phi}.
\end{align}$$

These relations are the corrected component form; the source reverses the hatted and pulled-back radial components in two displayed prose formulas, although its $O(r^{-2})$ conclusions use the corrected direction.

# Allowed transformations and the asymptotic stabilizer

A finite transformation acts as

$$\begin{align}
A\mapsto A^g=g^{-1}Ag+g^{-1}dg.
\end{align}$$

To preserve the chosen Dirichlet sector,

$$\begin{align}
A^g|_{\partial\hat\Sigma}=A_\infty.
\end{align}$$

For an Abelian group, this requires $g$ to be constant at the boundary. For a non-Abelian group, a constant value can still conjugate $A_\infty$. The paper therefore chooses an $\operatorname{Ad}(G)$-invariant reference, represented as the zero boundary connection in the chosen trivialization. The allowed group is then

$$\begin{align}
\mathcal G^I =\left\{g:\hat\Sigma\to G\ middle|
g|_{\partial\hat\Sigma}=g_\infty\in G\right\}.
\end{align}$$

For a general boundary connection the constant value belongs only to its stabilizer,

$$\begin{align}
G_{A_\infty}=\{g_\infty\in G\mid
g_\infty^{-1}A_\infty g_\infty=A_\infty\}.
\end{align}$$

The numerator of the final quotient is therefore a property of the selected boundary source, not a universal copy of every formal gauge transformation.

# Canonical phase space and the Gauss momentum map

The phase space is the smooth dense subspace

$$\begin{align}
\mathcal P =Q_{A_\infty}\times\Omega^2_\infty(\Sigma,\mathfrak g)
\subset T^*Q_{A_\infty},
\end{align}$$

with canonical pairing (source Eq. (4.1), label `canonicalpairing`)

$$\begin{align}
\langle\alpha_A,E\rangle =\int_\Sigma\operatorname{Tr}(\alpha_A\wedge E),
\end{align}$$

and symplectic form

$$\begin{align}
\omega =\int_\Sigma\operatorname{Tr}(\mathbb dA\wedge\mathbb dE).
\end{align}$$

For $\xi\in\operatorname{Lie}(\mathcal G^I)$, the fundamental vector field is

$$\begin{align}
\mathbb X_\xi A=D_A\xi, \qquad \mathbb X_\xi E=[E,\xi].
\end{align}$$

The proposed momentum map is the Gauss law,

$$\begin{align}
\mu(A,E)=D_AE, \qquad \mathcal C=\{(A,E)\in\mathcal P\mid D_AE=0\}.
\end{align}$$

Smearing and integrating by parts gives the central identity (source label `momentummappartial`)

$$\begin{aligned}
\langle\mu,\xi\rangle &=\int_\Sigma\operatorname{Tr}(D_AE\wedge\xi)\\
&=-\int_\Sigma\operatorname{Tr}(E\wedge D_A\xi) +\int_{\partial\Sigma}\operatorname{Tr}(E\wedge\xi).
\end{aligned}$$

Varying this expression yields

$$\begin{align}
\mathbb d\langle\mu,\xi\rangle =\iota_{\mathbb X_\xi}\omega +\mathbb d\int_{\partial\Sigma}\operatorname{Tr}(E\wedge\xi).
\end{align}$$

Hence $D_AE$ is the Hamiltonian generator without an improvement only when the boundary variation vanishes. If $\xi$ approaches a nonzero constant, the remaining functional is the asymptotic electric flux,

$$\begin{align}
Q_\xi^\infty =\int_{\partial\Sigma}\operatorname{Tr}(E\wedge\xi).
\end{align}$$

This is the paper's cleanest charge statement: constant boundary transformations are allowed, but they are not generated solely by the bulk Gauss constraint when the flux can vary.

# Electric flux survives conformal compactification

Writing $E=*\mathcal E$ separates two transformation laws. The one-form $\mathcal E$ follows the component pullback rules above, whereas the Hodge star contains the metric. Consequently,

$$\begin{align}
\hat E=K^{-1}E.
\end{align}$$

The compactified electric two-form can therefore have a finite, nonzero boundary value even when the corresponding one-form $\mathcal E$ satisfies the decay required by the phase space. This resolves the apparent conflict between finite energy and nonzero flux:

$$\begin{align}
\int_\Sigma\operatorname{Tr}(D_AE) =\int_{\partial\Sigma}\operatorname{Tr}E =\int_{\partial\hat\Sigma}\operatorname{Tr}\hat E.
\end{align}$$

The result depends on treating $E$ as the cotangent two-form, not naively assigning the same component falloff to a vector, one-form, and two-form.

# Localizability identifies redundancy

The momentum-map boundary estimate alone leaves an undesirable dependence on exact decay exponents. The paper therefore uses infinitesimal localizability. A symmetry parameter is localizable when it can be multiplied by smooth bump functions supported in arbitrary subregions while remaining an allowed infinitesimal symmetry.

A parameter with nonzero constant boundary value is not localizable: multiplying it by an arbitrary cutoff changes the boundary value and leaves the allowed Lie algebra. A parameter vanishing at the boundary is localizable. Thus

$$\begin{align}
\operatorname{Lie}(\mathcal G_0^\infty) =\{\xi:\hat\Sigma\to\mathfrak g
\mid\xi|_{\partial\hat\Sigma}=0\}
\end{align}$$

is precisely the infinitesimal algebra to which Noether's second theorem and the Gauss constraint apply. Exponentiating gives the normal subgroup

$$\begin{align}
\mathcal G_0^\infty =\{g\in\mathcal G^I\mid
g|_{\partial\hat\Sigma}=e\}.
\end{align}$$

Evaluation at the boundary defines

$$\begin{align}
\operatorname{ev}_\infty:\mathcal G^I\to G, \qquad g\mapsto g_\infty,
\end{align}$$

whose kernel is $\mathcal G_0^\infty$. For the invariant boundary connection, the first isomorphism theorem yields

$$\begin{align}
\boxed{ \mathcal G_{\mathrm{phys}} =\mathcal G^I/\mathcal G_0^\infty \simeq G.}
\end{align}$$

For a general Dirichlet source, $G$ is replaced by $G_{A_\infty}$.

# Higgs boundary data

Let $\varphi$ be a Higgs field in a representation $V$ with asymptotic value $\varphi_\infty$. Its covariant derivative is

$$\begin{align}
D_A\varphi=d\varphi+A\cdot\varphi.
\end{align}$$

Finite energy requires approach to the vacuum manifold and controls $D_A\varphi$ at infinity. A boundary-preserving transformation must stabilize both sources,

$$\begin{align}
g_\infty\in \operatorname{Stab}_G(A_\infty) \cap \operatorname{Stab}_G(\varphi_\infty).
\end{align}$$

In an unbroken phase, $\varphi_\infty$ is invariant and the result reduces to the Yang--Mills stabilizer. In a broken phase, the residual physical group is the unbroken subgroup

$$\begin{align}
H=\operatorname{Stab}_G(\varphi_\infty) \cap\operatorname{Stab}_G(A_\infty),
\end{align}$$

not generically the trivial group. The source's statement that only transformations approaching the identity survive is correct only when the chosen vacuum has trivial stabilizer.

The paper does not construct the full Yang--Mills--Higgs cotangent phase space, matter contribution to the Gauss momentum map, or the corresponding surface charge. Its Higgs conclusion is therefore a boundary-stabilizer analysis rather than a complete Hamiltonian reduction with matter.

## Blocked

- The functional-analytic claims about dense cotangent subspaces, smooth group actions, and the exact Sobolev completions are source-derived. The paper works with smooth falloff spaces but does not give a full Banach-manifold completion suitable for an independent proof here.
- The theorem equating infinitesimal localizability with Noether-second-theorem redundancy is used from the cited mathematical literature; its general proof was not reconstructed.
- The non-Abelian constraint surface is singular at reducible connections. A global stratified reduction and the topology of the quotient were not computed.
- No full Yang--Mills--Higgs phase-space variation is supplied, so the matter term in the Gauss momentum map and its boundary charge cannot be independently reconstructed from this paper.
- The charge $Q_\xi^\infty$ is identified as the missing Hamiltonian boundary term, but its integrability, conservation conditions, and Poisson algebra are not analyzed in the source.

## Failed

- Section 2 writes a vertical bundle automorphism schematically as $f(p)=p\cdot s(\pi(p))$ while $s$ has already been used as a bundle section. The expression is type-inconsistent. A gauge transformation should be written with a $G$-valued map, for example $f_g(p)=p\cdot g(\pi(p))$ in a trivialization.
- Section 3.2 reverses the radial pullback components in the prose formulas. Since $dR=K\,dr$, the correct relations are $\alpha_r=K\hat\alpha_R$ and $F_{r\theta}=K\hat F_{R\theta}$, not the displayed reverse. The source's subsequent $O(r^{-2})$ conclusions require the corrected relations and were retained only in that corrected form.
- The Higgs discussion treats the broken phase as if the asymptotic stabilizer were necessarily trivial. The general residual group is $\operatorname{Stab}_G(\varphi_\infty)\cap\operatorname{Stab}_G(A_\infty)$; downstream claims were restricted to this corrected statement.
- The conclusion calls $\pi_3(G)$ a “fundamental group.” The fundamental group is $\pi_1(G)$; $\pi_3(G)$ is the third homotopy group. This terminology error does not affect the boundary quotient.
