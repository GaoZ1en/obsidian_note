# Ising model and MPS

consider the transverse field Ising model in 1+1D, where

$$\begin{align}
H & =\sum ^{L}_{j=1}-JZ_{j}Z_{j+1}-hX_{j}
\end{align}$$

with periodic boundary condition imposed. the system exhibits a global $\displaystyle{\mathbb{Z}_{2}=\left\{1,g\right\}}$ symmetry, generated by $\displaystyle{g=\prod _{j}X_{j}}$. we now represent ground state of this model using translation invariant MPS

$$\begin{align}
\ket{\Psi} & =\sum _{\left\{j\right\}}\mathrm{Tr}[\dots M_{j_{l}}\cdot M_{j_{l+1}}\dots]\ket{\dots j_{l}j_{l+1}\dots} 
\end{align}$$

where $\displaystyle{M_{j,\alpha \beta}}$ is a three leg local tensor. $\displaystyle{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}$ symmetry can be imposed on the MPS by the following local tensor constraint:

$$\begin{align}
\sum _{j'}X_{jj'}\cdot M_{j'} & =W(g)\cdot M_{j}\cdot W^{\dagger}(g)
\end{align}$$

where the gauge transformation $\displaystyle{W(g)}$ satisfies $\displaystyle{[W(g)]^{2}=\hat{1}}$.

1. for $\displaystyle{J<h}$, the system is in $\displaystyle{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}$ symmetric phase.
	1. we define the transfer matrix $\displaystyle{T_{\alpha \alpha',\beta \beta'}=\sum _{j}M_{j,\alpha \beta}M_{j,\alpha'\beta'}^{*}}$, show that it is invariant under the $\displaystyle{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}$ symmetry generated by $\displaystyle{W(g)\otimes W^{*}(g)}$. 
*sol*. we have
$$\begin{align}
T'_{\alpha \alpha',\beta \beta'} & =\sum _{j}M'_{j,\alpha \beta}\cdot M^{'*}_{j,\alpha'\beta'} \\
 & =\sum _{j}(W(g)\otimes W^{*}(g))_{\alpha \alpha',\gamma \gamma'}M_{j,\gamma \delta}\cdot M_{j,\gamma'\delta'}^{*}(W^{\dagger}(g)\cdot W^{T}(g))_{\delta \delta',\beta \beta'} \\
 & =\sum _{j}(W(g)\cdot M_{j}\cdot W^{\dagger}(g))_{\alpha \beta}\cdot (W^{*}(g)\cdot M_{j}^{*}\cdot W^{T}(g))_{\alpha' \beta'} \\
 & =\sum _{j}\sum _{j'j''} X_{jj'}M_{j',\alpha \beta}\cdot X^{*}_{jj''}M_{j'',\alpha' \beta'}^{*} \\
 & =\sum _{j'j''}\delta _{j'j''}M_{j',\alpha \beta}\cdot M_{j'',\alpha'\beta'}^{*} \\
 & =\sum _{j}M_{j,\alpha \beta}\cdot M_{j,\alpha'\beta'}^{*}=T_{\alpha \alpha',\beta \beta'}
\end{align}$$
here we use $\displaystyle{\sum _{j}X_{jj'}X^{*}_{jj''}=\delta _{j'j''}}$ since $\displaystyle{X}$ is a unitary matrix.
	2. assuming $\displaystyle{T}$ possesses a unique dominant eigenvalue (normalized to $\displaystyle{1}$), with corresponding left and right eigenvectors $\displaystyle{\bra{v_{l}}}$ and $\displaystyle{\ket{v_{r}}}$, show that $\displaystyle{\braket{ Z_{1} }=0, \braket{ Z_{1}Z_{l} }\overset{l\to \infty}{\longrightarrow}0}$.
*sol*. we have$$\begin{align}
\braket{ Z_{l} }  & = \braket{ \Psi|Z_{l}|\Psi } \\
 & =\braket{ v_{l}|O_{Z}|v_{r} } \\
\lim_{ l \to \infty } \braket{ Z_{1}Z_{l} } & = |\braket{ v_{l}|O_{Z}|v_{r} } |^{2}
\end{align}$$where $\displaystyle{O_{Z}=\sum _{jj'}Z_{jj'}M_{j,\alpha \beta}M_{j',\alpha'\beta'}^{*}}$. since $\displaystyle{gZ_{j}g=-Z_{j}}$, we have $\displaystyle{O_{Z}}$ is $\displaystyle{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}$ odd. therefore, we have
$$\begin{align}
\braket{ Z_{l} }  & =0, &  \lim_{ l \to \infty } \braket{ Z_{1}Z_{l} }  & =0
\end{align}$$
while $\displaystyle{\bra{v_{l}}}$ and $\displaystyle{\ket{v_{r}}}$ have the same $\displaystyle{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}$ quantum number.
	3. the disorder parameter $\displaystyle{\braket{ \prod ^{l}_{j=1}X_{j} }}$ is the correlator of domain wall at bonds $\displaystyle{\frac{1}{2}}$ and $\displaystyle{l+\frac{1}{2}}$. express it using transfer matrix, and show that it will approach a non-zero constant in the thermodynamic limit.
*sol*. we have
$$\begin{align}
\lim_{ l \to \infty } \braket{ \prod ^{l}_{j=1}X_{j} } & = \lim_{ l \to \infty } \frac{\braket{ \Psi|\prod ^{l}_{j=1}X_{j}|\Psi }}{\braket{ \Psi|\Psi } } \\
 & =\lim_{ l \to \infty } \frac{\mathrm{Tr}\left( (W(g)\otimes \hat{1})\cdot T^{l}\cdot(W^{\dagger}(g)\otimes \hat{1})\cdot T^{L-\ell} \right)}{\mathrm{Tr}(T^{L}) } \\
 & =\braket{ v_{l}|W(g)|v_{r} }\cdot\braket{ v_{l}|W^{\dagger}(g)|v_{r} }\neq 0
\end{align}$$
equivalently, in the double-layer notation one can write the constant as $\,\big|\braket{ v_l | (\hat{1}\otimes W^{*}) | v_r }\big|^{2}$. 
2. for $\displaystyle{J>h}$, the system is in $\displaystyle{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}$ SSB phase, and one obtains two-fold ground state degeneracy, which are cat states with distinct $\displaystyle{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}$ quantum number $\displaystyle{\pm1}$. we first express the $\displaystyle{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}$ even cat state $\displaystyle{\ket{\Psi_{e}}}$ as an translational invariant MPS. besides the physical $\displaystyle{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}$ symmetry constraint, we also impose another $\displaystyle{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}$ symmetry acting exclusively on virtual legs
   $$\begin{align}M_{j} & =J\cdot M_{j}\cdot J^{\dagger}\end{align}$$
   where $\displaystyle{J^{2}=\hat{1}}$ and $\displaystyle{\left\{W(g),J\right\}=0}$. physically, $\displaystyle{J}$ can be viewed as the "renormalized" charge.
	1. the $\displaystyle{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}$ IGG imposes additional symmetry on the transfer matrix $\displaystyle{T}$. show that spectral of $\displaystyle{T}$ is at least two-fold degeneracy due to this additional symmetry. normalize the dominant eigenvalue as $\displaystyle{1}$ with two-fold degeneracy. denote the associated two (right) eigenstates as $\displaystyle{\ket{v^{e}_{r}}}$ and $\displaystyle{\ket{v^{o}_{r}}}$, show that $\displaystyle{\ket{v^{o}_{r}}=(J\otimes \hat{1})\ket{v^{e}_{r}}}$.
*sol*. from $\displaystyle{M_{j}=J\cdot M_{j}\cdot J^{\dagger}}$ we have
$$\begin{align}
T & =\sum _{j}M_{j,\alpha \beta}M_{j,\alpha'\beta'}^{*} \\
 & =\sum _{j}(J\cdot M_{j}\cdot J^{\dagger})_{\alpha \beta}M_{j,\alpha'\beta'}^{*} \\
 & =(J\otimes \hat{1})\cdot T\cdot (J^{\dagger}\otimes \hat{1})
\end{align}$$
which means $\displaystyle{[T,J\otimes \hat{1}]=0}$, therefore $\displaystyle{T}$ and $\displaystyle{J\otimes \hat{1}}$ can be diagonalized simultaneously, gives rise to two-fold degeneracy in the spectrum of $\displaystyle{T}$. let $\displaystyle{\ket{v^{e}_{r}}}$ be one of the dominant eigenvector of $\displaystyle{T}$, then $\displaystyle{\ket{v^{o}_{r}}=(J\otimes \hat{1})\ket{v^{e}_{r}}}$ is also a dominant eigenvector of $\displaystyle{T}$ with the same eigenvalue.
	2. calculate the long-range correlator of order parameter $\displaystyle{\braket{ Z_{1}Z_{l} }}$, and show it approaches a non-zero constant in the thermodynamic limit.
*sol*. since $\displaystyle{[J\otimes\hat{1},\,O_Z]=0}$ and $\displaystyle{(W\otimes W^{*})O_{Z}(W^{\dagger}\otimes W^{T})=-O_{Z}}$, we have
$$\begin{align}
\braket{ v_{l}^{e}|O_{Z}|v^{e}_{r} } & =:m, & \braket{ v_{l}^{o}|O_{Z}|v^{o}_{r} } & =-m, & \braket{ v_{l}^{e}|O_{Z}|v^{o}_{r} } & =\braket{ v_{l}^{o}|O_{Z}|v^{e}_{r} }=0
\end{align}$$
choosing the even cat boundary condition as
$$\begin{align}
\ket{b_{r}} \propto \ket{v^{e}_{r}} +\ket{v^{o}_{r}},\quad\bra{b_{l}}\propto \bra{v^{e}_{l}} +\bra{v^{o}_{l}} 
\end{align}$$
and using the spectral decomposition $\displaystyle{T^{n}\to \ket{v^{e}_{r}}\bra{v^{e}_{l}}+\ket{v^{o}_{r}}\bra{v^{o}_{l}}}$ ($\displaystyle{n\to \infty}$), we have
$$\begin{align}
\lim_{ l \to \infty }\braket{ Z_{1}Z_{l} } & =\lim_{ l \to \infty } \frac{\braket{ b_{l}|O_{Z}T^{l-2}O_{Z}|b_{r} }}{\braket{ b_{l}|T^{l}|b_{r} } } \\
 & = \frac{m^{2}+m^{2}}{1+1}=m^{2} (>0)
\end{align}$$
	3. calculate disorder parameter $\displaystyle{\braket{ \prod ^{l}_{j=1}X_{j} }}$ in this case, show that it vanishes when $\displaystyle{l\to \infty}$
*sol*. using $\displaystyle{\sum _{j'}X_{jj'}\cdot M_{j'} =W(g)\cdot M_{j}\cdot W^{\dagger}(g)}$, we have
$$\begin{align}
\braket{ \prod ^{l}_{j=1}X_{j} }  & = \frac{\braket{ b_{l}|(W\otimes \hat{1})T^{l}(\hat{1}\otimes W^{*})|b_{r} }}{\braket{ b_{l}|T^{l}|b_{r} } }
\end{align}$$
under the SSB phase, since $\displaystyle{T}$ and $\displaystyle{J\otimes\hat{1}}$ are simultaneously diagonalized, the virtual space $\displaystyle{\mathcal{V}}$ can be decomposed into two sectors (corresponding to $\displaystyle{J=\pm 1}$) as $\displaystyle{\mathcal{V}=\mathcal{V}_{e}\oplus \mathcal{V}_{o}}$, and the tensor can be gauge transformed into block-injective form:
$$\begin{align}
T & =\begin{pmatrix}
T_{ee} & 0 \\
0 & T_{oo}
\end{pmatrix}
\end{align}$$
and the spectral radius $\displaystyle{\rho(T_{ee})=\rho(T_{oo})=1}$, while the cross-block channels $\displaystyle{T_{eo},T_{oe}}$ have spectral radius $<1$. the endpoint insertions are induced by $\displaystyle{W}$, and since $\displaystyle{\left\{ W,J \right\}=0}$, it exchanges $\displaystyle{e\leftrightarrow o}$ at the endpoints: left endpoint $\displaystyle{(W\otimes \hat{1}):\mathcal{V}_{e}\to \mathcal{V}_{o}}$, right endpoint $\displaystyle{(\hat{1}\otimes W^{*}):\mathcal{V}_{o}\to \mathcal{V}_{e}}$. therefore, propagation over length $\displaystyle{l}$ must go through the "cross-block" channels, whose dominant eigenvalue satisfies $\displaystyle{|\lambda _{X}|<1}$. thus
$$\begin{align}
\braket{ \prod ^{l}_{j=1}X_{j} } \sim C(\lambda _{X})^{l} \xrightarrow{l\to \infty} 0
\end{align}$$
	4. the additional $\displaystyle{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}$ virtual leg symmetry also helps to construct the $\displaystyle{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}$ odd ground state by inserting $\displaystyle{J}$ as $\displaystyle{\ket{\Psi_{o}}=\sum _{\left\{j\right\}}\mathrm{Tr}[\dots M_{j_{l}}\cdot J\cdot M_{j_{l+1}}]\ket{\dots j_{l}j_{l+1}\dots}}$. show $\displaystyle{\ket{\Psi_{o}}}$ is odd under $\displaystyle{g}$, and is translational invariant. 
*sol*. from $\displaystyle{\left\{W,J\right\}=0}$ and $\displaystyle{W^{2}=\hat{1}}$, we have $\displaystyle{W^{\dagger}\cdot J\cdot W=-J}$. act $\displaystyle{g}$ on $\displaystyle{\ket{\Psi_{o}}}$, we have
$$\begin{align}
g\ket{\Psi_{o}} & =\sum _{j}\mathrm{Tr}[\dots W\cdot M_{j_{l}}\cdot W^{\dagger}\cdot J\cdot W\cdot M_{j_{l+1}}\cdot W^{\dagger}\cdot\dots]\ket{\dots j_{l}j_{l+1}\dots}  \\
 & = -\sum _{j}\mathrm{Tr}[\dots M_{j_{l}}\cdot J\cdot M_{j_{l+1}}\cdot\dots]\ket{\dots j_{l}j_{l+1}\dots}=-\ket{\Psi_{o}} 
\end{align}$$
which means $\displaystyle{\ket{\Psi_{o}}}$ is $\displaystyle{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}$ odd. from $\displaystyle{M_{j}=J\cdot M_{j}\cdot J^{\dagger}}$, we have $\displaystyle{[J,M_{j}]=0}$. thus we have
$$\begin{align}
\mathrm{Tr}(\dots M_{j_{l}}JM_{j_{l+1}}\dots) & =\mathrm{Tr}(\dots M_{j_{l-1}}JM_{j_{l}}\dots)
\end{align}$$
which means $\displaystyle{\ket{\Psi_{o}}}$ is translational invariant.

# symmetric tensor

1. consider a three-leg (labeled as $\displaystyle{a,b,c}$) tensor $\displaystyle{\hat{M}=\sum _{\alpha \beta \gamma}M_{\alpha \beta \gamma}\ket{\alpha}_{a}\ket{\beta}_{b}\bra{\gamma}_{c}}$. we now impose $\displaystyle{\mathrm{SU}(2)}$ symmetry on this tensor, where each leg carries some representation of $\displaystyle{\mathrm{SU}(2)}$. write down the symmetric condition for $\displaystyle{\hat{M}}$ using spin operators.
*sol*. let $\displaystyle{\vec{S}_{a},\vec{S}_{b},\vec{S}_{c}}$ be the spin operators acting on three legs respectively. the symmetric condition for $\displaystyle{\hat{M}}$ is that
$$\begin{align}
(\vec{S}_{a}\otimes \hat{1}_{b})\hat{M}+(\hat{1}_{a}\otimes \vec{S}_{b})\hat{M}-\hat{M}\vec{S}_{c} & =0
\end{align}$$
equivalently, $\displaystyle{\forall U\in \mathrm{SU}(2)}$, we have
$$\begin{align}
(U_{a}\otimes U_{b})\hat{M}U_{c}^{\dagger}=\hat{M}
\end{align}$$
	1. let leg $\displaystyle{a}$ carry $\displaystyle{\frac{1}{2}\oplus \frac{3}{2}}$, leg $\displaystyle{b}$ carry $\displaystyle{1\oplus 2}$, and leg $\displaystyle{c}$ carries $\displaystyle{\frac{1}{2}\oplus \frac{5}{2}}$. how many free parameters in $\displaystyle{\hat{M}}$? by Wigner-Eckart theorem, express $\displaystyle{\hat{M}}$ can be expressed using CG coefficients and the free parameters
*sol*. allowed triples $\displaystyle{(j_{a},j_{b},j_{c})}$ satisfies $\displaystyle{j_{c}\in j_{a}\otimes j_{b}}$ (Clebsch-Gordan selection rule). then
$$\begin{align}
j_{a}=\frac{1}{2},j_{b}=1 & : j_{c}\in \left\{ \frac{1}{2}, \frac{3}{2} \right\} \Rightarrow j_{c}=\frac{1}{2} \\
j_{a}=\frac{3}{2},j_{b}=1 & :j_{c}\in\left\{\frac{1}{2}, \frac{3}{2}, \frac{5}{2}\right\}\implies j_{c}=\frac{1}{2}, \frac{5}{2} \\
j_{a}=\frac{1}{2},j_{b}=2 & : j_{c}\in\left\{\frac{3}{2}, \frac{5}{2}\right\}\implies j_{c}=\frac{5}{2} \\
j_{a}=\frac{3}{2},j_{b}=2 & :j_{c}\in\left\{\frac{1}{2}, \frac{3}{2}, \frac{5}{2}, \frac{7}{2}\right\}\implies j_{c}=\frac{1}{2}, \frac{5}{2}
\end{align}$$
there are $\displaystyle{6}$ alloed triples, so there are 6 free parameters. by Wigner-Eckart theorem, $\displaystyle{\hat{M}}$ can be expressed as
$$\begin{align}
M_{(j_{a},m_{a})(j_{b},m_{b})(j_{c},m_{c})} & =\sum _{(j_{a},j_{b},j_{c})\text{allowed}} \lambda ^{j_{c}}_{j_{a}j_{b}}C^{j_{c}m_{c}}_{j_{a}m_{a},j_{b}m_{b}}
\end{align}$$
where $\displaystyle{\lambda ^{j_{c}}_{j_{a}j_{b}}\in \mathbb{C}}$ are the 6 free parameters, and $\displaystyle{C}$'s are Clebsch-Gordan coefficients.
2. consider a four-leg $\displaystyle{(b,c,d,e)}$ $\displaystyle{\mathrm{SU}(2)}$ symmetric tensor $\displaystyle{\hat{N}=\sum _{\beta \gamma \delta \mu}N_{\beta \gamma \delta \mu}\bra{\beta}_{b}\ket{\gamma}_{c}\ket{\delta}_{d}\bra{\mu}_{e}}$. here, leg $\displaystyle{b}$ and $\displaystyle{c}$ are the same as in 1.1 (i.e. $\,j_b\in\{1,2\},\ j_c\in\{\tfrac12,\tfrac52\}$), leg $\displaystyle{d}$ carries $\,\tfrac12\oplus 1$, and leg $\displaystyle{e}$ carries $\,0\oplus\tfrac12\oplus 1\oplus\tfrac32$. how many free parameters?
*sol*. allowed quadruples $\displaystyle{(j_{b},j_{c},j_{d},j_{e})}$ satisfies $\displaystyle{j_{b}\otimes j_{e}\cap j_{c}\otimes j_{d}\neq \emptyset}$.
$$\begin{align}
j_{b}=1,j_{e}=0 & : j_{b}\otimes j_{e}=\left\{1\right\} \\
j_{b}=1,j_{e}=\frac{1}{2} & : j_{b}\otimes j_{e}=\left\{\frac{1}{2},\frac{3}{2}\right\} \\
j_{b}=1,j_{e}=1 & : j_{b}\otimes j_{e}=\left\{0,1,2\right\} \\
j_{b}=1,j_{e}=\frac{3}{2} & : j_{b}\otimes j_{e}=\left\{\frac{1}{2},\frac{3}{2},\frac{5}{2}\right\} \\
j_{b}=2,j_{e}=0 & : j_{b}\otimes j_{e}=\left\{2\right\} \\
j_{b}=2,j_{e}=\frac{1}{2} & : j_{b}\otimes j_{e}=\left\{\frac{3}{2},\frac{5}{2}\right\} \\
j_{b}=2,j_{e}=1 & : j_{b}\otimes j_{e}=\left\{1,2,3\right\} \\
j_{b}=2,j_{e}=\frac{3}{2} & : j_{b}\otimes j_{e}=\left\{\frac{1}{2}, \frac{3}{2}, \frac{5}{2}, \frac{7}{2}\right\}
\end{align}$$
them the number of allowed pairs $\displaystyle{(j_{b},j_{e})}$ is $\displaystyle{1\times 1+3\times 1+3\times 1+4\times 2+3\times 1+3\times1+1\times1+1\times 1=23}$, and there are $\displaystyle{23}$ free parameters in total. by Wigner-Eckart theorem,
$$\begin{align}
N_{(j_{b}m_{b})(j_{c}m_{c})(j_{d}m_{d})(j_{e}m_{e})}=\sum _{J,M}\sum ^{\text{mult}(J\in b\otimes e)}_{\alpha=1}\sum ^{\text{mult}(J\in c\otimes d)}_{\beta=1} \Lambda _{J;\alpha \beta}C^{JM}_{j_{b}m_{b},j_{e}m_{e}}C^{JM}_{j_{c}m_{c},j_{d}m_{d}}
\end{align}$$
where $\displaystyle{\Lambda _{J;\alpha \beta}\in \mathbb{C}}$ are the free parameters, and $\displaystyle{\alpha,\beta}$ label different pairing channels leading to the same total spin $\displaystyle{J}$.
3. perform tensor contraction of leg $\displaystyle{b,c}$: $\displaystyle{\mathrm{Tr}_{bc}(\hat{M}\otimes \hat{N})}$. please show that the resulting tensor is symmetric under $\displaystyle{\mathrm{SU}(2)}$
*sol*. let $\displaystyle{\hat{O}=\mathrm{Tr}_{bc}(\hat{M}\otimes \hat{N})}$, we have
$$\begin{align}
\hat{O} & =\sum _{\alpha \beta \gamma}\sum _{\beta' \gamma' \delta \mu}M_{\alpha \beta \gamma}N_{\beta' \gamma' \delta \mu}\braket{ \beta|\beta' }\braket{ \gamma|\gamma' }\ket{\alpha}_{a}\ket{\delta}_{d}\bra{\mu}_{e} \\
 & =\sum _{\alpha \delta \mu}\left( \sum _{\beta \gamma}M_{\alpha \beta \gamma}N_{\beta \gamma \delta \mu} \right)\ket{\alpha}_{a}\ket{\delta}_{d}\bra{\mu}_{e}
\end{align}$$
since $\displaystyle{a}$ carry $\displaystyle{\frac{1}{2}\oplus \frac{3}{2}}$, $\displaystyle{d}$ carry $\displaystyle{\frac{1}{2}\oplus 1}$, and $\displaystyle{e}$ carry $\displaystyle{0\oplus \frac{1}{2}\oplus 1\oplus \frac{3}{2}}$, we only need to check the allowed triples $\displaystyle{(j_{a},j_{d},j_{e})}$ that satisfies $\displaystyle{j_{e}\in j_{a}\otimes j_{d}}$

$$\begin{align}
j_{a}=\frac{1}{2},j_{d}=\frac{1}{2} & : j_{e}\in\left\{0,1\right\}\implies j_{e}=0,1 \\
j_{a}=\frac{1}{2},j_{d}=1 & : j_{e}\in\left\{\frac{1}{2},\frac{3}{2}\right\}\implies  j_{e}=\frac{1}{2},\frac{3}{2} \\
j_{a}=\frac{3}{2},j_{d}=\frac{1}{2} & : j_{e}\in\left\{1,2\right\}\implies j_{e}=1 \\
j_{a}=\frac{3}{2},j_{d}=1 & : j_{e}\in\left\{\frac{1}{2},\frac{3}{2},\frac{5}{2}\right\}\implies j_{e}=\frac{1}{2},\frac{3}{2}
\end{align}$$
thus all allowed triples $\displaystyle{(j_{a},j_{d},j_{e})}$ are satisfied, which means $\displaystyle{\hat{O}}$ is $\displaystyle{\mathrm{SU}(2)}$ symmetric.

# AKLT model and its variation

consider the spin-1 chain on $\displaystyle{N}$ sites with open or periodic boundary conditions. the AKLT Hamiltonian can be written as a sum of nearest-neighbor projectors onto total spin-2:

$$\begin{align}
H_{\mathrm{AKLT}} & =\sum _{i}P^{(2)}_{i,i+1} \\
P^{(2)}_{i,i+1} & =\frac{1}{6}(\vec{S}_{i}\cdot \vec{S}_{i+1})^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\vec{S}_{i}\cdot \vec{S}_{i+1}+\frac{1}{3}
\end{align}$$

its ground state can be represented by a translation-invariant, bond dimension $\displaystyle{D=2}$ MPS

$$\begin{align}
\ket{\Psi}  & =\sum _{m_{1},\dots,m_{N}}\mathrm{Tr}(A^{m_{1}}\dots A^{m_{N}})\ket{m_{1}\dots m_{N}} 
\end{align}$$

where $\displaystyle{m\in\left\{1,0,-1\right\}}$, and each $\displaystyle{A^{m}}$ is a $\displaystyle{2\times2}$ matrix, with

$$\begin{align}
A^{1} & =-\sqrt{ \frac{2}{3} }\sigma ^{+}, & A^{0} & =\sqrt{ \frac{1}{3} }\sigma ^{z}, & A^{-1} & =\sqrt{ \frac{2}{3} }\sigma ^{-}
\end{align}$$

1. prove the tensors $\displaystyle{A^{1},A^{0},A^{-1}}$ are invariant under spin rotation symmetry, where virtual legs carry spin-1/2.
*sol*. let the spin-1 representation be $\displaystyle{U(R)=\exp(i\theta \hat{n}\cdot \vec{S})}$, and the spin-1/2 representation be $\displaystyle{V(R)=\exp\left(i\theta \hat{n}\cdot \frac{\vec{\sigma}}{2}\right)}$. we need to verify that
$$\begin{align}
\sum _{m'\in \left\{1,0,-1\right\}} U_{m m'}(R)A^{m'} & =V(R)A^{m}V(R)^{\dagger}, & \forall R\in \mathrm{SU}(2)
\end{align}$$
or an equivalent form using generators:
$$\begin{align}
\sum _{m'}(S^{\alpha})_{m m'}A^{m'} & =\left[ \frac{\sigma ^{\alpha}}{2},A^{m} \right], & \alpha & =x,y,z
\end{align}$$
$$\begin{align}
\left[ \frac{\sigma ^{x}}{2},A^{1} \right] & = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}A^{0}, & \left[ \frac{\sigma ^{y}}{2},A^{1} \right] & =\frac{i}{\sqrt{2}}A^{0}, & \left[ \frac{\sigma ^{z}}{2},A^{1} \right] & =A^{1} \\
\left[ \frac{\sigma ^{x}}{2},A^{0} \right] & =\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(A^{1}+A^{-1}), & \left[ \frac{\sigma ^{y}}{2},A^{0} \right] & =\frac{i}{\sqrt{2}}(A^{1}-A^{-1}), & \left[ \frac{\sigma ^{z}}{2},A^{0} \right] & =0 \\
\left[ \frac{\sigma ^{x}}{2},A^{-1} \right] & =\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}A^{0}, & \left[ \frac{\sigma ^{y}}{2},A^{-1} \right] & =-\frac{i}{\sqrt{2}}A^{0}, & \left[ \frac{\sigma ^{z}}{2},A^{-1} \right] & =-A^{-1}
\end{align}$$
and
$$\begin{align}
(S^{x})_{m m'} & =\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{pmatrix}
0 & 1 & 0 \\
1 & 0 & 1 \\
0 & 1 & 0
\end{pmatrix}, & (S^{y})_{m m'} & =\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{pmatrix}
0 & -i & 0 \\
i & 0 & -i \\
0 & i & 0
\end{pmatrix}, & (S^{z})_{m m'} & =\begin{pmatrix}
1 & 0 & 0 \\
0 & 0 & 0 \\
0 & 0 & -1
\end{pmatrix}
\end{align}$$
then
$$\begin{align}
\sum _{m'}(S^{x})_{1 m'}A^{m'} & =\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}A^{0}, & \sum _{m'}(S^{y})_{1 m'}A^{m'} & =\frac{i}{\sqrt{2}}A^{0}, & \sum _{m'}(S^{z})_{1 m'}A^{m'} & =A^{1} \\
\sum _{m'}(S^{x})_{0 m'}A^{m'} & =\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(A^{1}+A^{-1}), & \sum _{m'}(S^{y})_{0 m'}A^{m'} & =\frac{i}{\sqrt{2}}(A^{1}-A^{-1}), & \sum _{m'}(S^{z})_{0 m'}A^{m'} & =0 \\
\sum _{m'}(S^{x})_{-1 m'}A^{m'} & =\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}A^{0}, & \sum _{m'}(S^{y})_{-1 m'}A^{m'} & =-\frac{i}{\sqrt{2}}A^{0}, & \sum _{m'}(S^{z})_{-1 m'}A^{m'} & =-A^{-1}
\end{align}$$
so the relations hold. therefore the local tensors are invariant under spin rotation.
2. prove that the AKLT state $\displaystyle{\ket{\Psi}}$ is invariant under spin rotation symmetry, and is annihilated by each local projector $$\begin{align}P^{(2)}_{i,i+1}\ket{\Psi}  & =0, & \forall i\end{align}$$ and hence is a ground state of $\displaystyle{H_{\mathrm{AKLT}}}$ up to an overall constant shift of the energy.
*sol*. from 3.1, act spin rotation on the MPS, we have
$$\begin{align}
\prod _{i}U_{i}(R)\ket{\Psi}  & =\sum _{m_{1},\dots,m_{N}}\mathrm{Tr}(V(R)A^{m_{1}}V^{\dagger}(R)\,V(R)A^{m_{2}}V^{\dagger}(R)\,\dots\, V(R)A^{m_{N}}V^{\dagger}(R))\ket{m_{1}\dots m_{N}}  \\
 & =\sum _{m_{1},\dots ,m_{N}}\mathrm{Tr}(A^{m_{1}}\dots A^{m_{N}})\ket{m_{1}\dots m_{N}}=\ket{\Psi} 
\end{align}$$
therefore $\displaystyle{\ket{\Psi}}$ is invariant under spin rotation symmetry.

the two-site physical space $\displaystyle{1\otimes 1=0\oplus 1\oplus 2}$ maps to virtual space components $\displaystyle{0\oplus 1\oplus 2}$, but the virtual space only contains $\displaystyle{0\oplus 1}$, so the physical spin-2 component must be mapped to zero in the virtual space. this means the physical spin-2 component has zero amplitude in the MPS state, i.e. $\displaystyle{P^{(2)}_{i,i+1}\ket{\Psi}=0}$ for all $\displaystyle{i}$. thus $\displaystyle{\ket{\Psi}}$ is a ground state of $\displaystyle{H_{\mathrm{AKLT}}}$.
3. break the full $\displaystyle{\mathrm{SO}(3)}$ symmetry down to the on-site $\displaystyle{\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times \mathbb{Z}_{2}}$ subgroup generated by $\displaystyle{\pi}$-rotations about two orthogonal axes, say $\displaystyle{x}$ and $\displaystyle{z}$:$$\begin{align}U_{x} & :=e^{i\pi S^{x}}, & U_{z} & :=e^{i\pi S^{z}}, & U_{x}^{2}=U^{2}_{z} & =\hat{1}, & U_{x}U_{z} & =U_{z}U_{x}\end{align}$$list the $\displaystyle{\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times \mathbb{Z}_{2}}$ symmetry constraint on local tensors, and count the number of free parameters of the local tensor under this reduced symmetry.
*sol*. impose on-site constraints on local tensors:
$$\sum_{m'} (U_\alpha)_{m m'} A^{m'} = V_\alpha A^{m} V_\alpha^{\dagger},\quad \alpha\in\{x,z\},\ V_\alpha^{2}=\hat 1.$$
