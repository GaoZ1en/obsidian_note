# Final Exam Ultimate Review Cheat Sheet

## 1. Group Theory (群论)

### Sylow Theorems (The "Counting" Tool)

Use this to prove a group is NOT simple (i.e., has a normal subgroup).
-   **$n_p$**: Number of Sylow $p$-subgroups.
-   **Conditions**:
    1.  $n_p \equiv 1 \pmod p$
    1.  $n_p \mid m$ (where $|G| = p^k \cdot m$)
-   **Proof Logic**:
    -   If $n_p = 1 \implies$ Unique Sylow $p$-subgroup $\implies$ **Normal**.
    -   If $n_p > 1$, define constraints (e.g., counting elements).

### Simple Group Proofs (The "Index Factorial" Trick)

-   **Problem**: Show group $G$ of order 96 is not simple.
-   **Lemma**: If $G$ has a subgroup $H$ of index $k$ ($|G:H|=k$), and $|G| \nmid k!$, then $G$ is not simple.
    -   *Why?* Action on cosets gives $\phi: G \to S_k$. Kernel is normal.

### Classification of Groups (Order $pq$)

-   If $|G| = pq$ ($p < q$) and $p \nmid (q-1)$, then $G \cong \mathbb{Z}_{pq}$ (Cyclic).
-   If $|G| = p^2$, $G$ is **Abelian**. (Isomorphic to $\mathbb{Z}_{p^2}$ or $\mathbb{Z}_p \times \mathbb{Z}_p$).

### Counting Elements

-   **Order of $g$**: Smallest $n$ such that $g^n = e$.
-   **Group of order 20**: $n_5=1$. Elements of order 5 = $(5-1) \times n_5 = 4$.

## 2. Ring Theory (环论)

### Ideals: Prime vs Maximal (The "Quotient Slogan")

To check ideal $I$, look at the quotient ring $R/I$.
-   **Prime Ideal**: $R/I$ is an **Integral Domain** (No zero divisors).
-   **Maximal Ideal**: $R/I$ is a **Field** (Every non-zero element has inverse).
-   *Example*: In $\mathbb{Z}$, ideal $(3)$ is maximal because $\mathbb{Z}/(3) \cong \mathbb{Z}_3$ is a field. Ideal $(0)$ is prime because $\mathbb{Z}$ is domain, not field.

### Factorization Hierarchy (The "Alphabet Soup")

$$\begin{align}
\text{Fields} \subset \text{Euclidean Domains (ED)} \subset \text{PIDs} \subset \text{UFDs} \subset \text{Domains}
\end{align}$$

-   **ED**: Has a "division algorithm" (e.g., $\mathbb{Z}$, $F[x]$).
-   **PID**: Every ideal is generated by one element $(a)$.
-   **UFD**: Unique factorization.
-   *Classic Counter-example*: $\mathbb{Z}[x]$ is a UFD but NOT a PID (Ideal $(2, x)$ needs two generators).
-   *Classic Counter-example*: $\mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{-5}]$ is NOT a UFD ($6 = 2 \cdot 3 = (1+\sqrt{-5})(1-\sqrt{-5})$).

### Irreducibility Criteria (Polynomials)

1.  **Degree 2 or 3**: Irreducible $\iff$ No roots in the field.
1.  **Eisenstein Criterion**: Prime $p$ divides all coeffs except leading; $p^2$ does not divide constant term.
1.  **Cyclotomic**: $x^{p-1} + \dots + 1$ is irreducible (Sub $x \mapsto x+1$ then Eisenstein).

## 3. Field Theory (域论)

### Degree of Extension

-   $[K:F] = \dim_F(K)$.
-   **Tower Law**: $[K:F] = [K:E][E:F]$.
-   *Computation*: Find minimal polynomial. Degree of poly = Degree of simple extension.
    -   $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2}, \sqrt{3})$ has degree 4. Basis: $\{1, \sqrt{2}, \sqrt{3}, \sqrt{6}\}$.

### Finite Fields (Galois Fields)

-   **Existence**: Unique field of order $p^n$ for every prime $p, n \ge 1$.
-   **Construction**: $K = \mathbb{Z}_p[x] / (f(x))$ where $f(x)$ is irreducible of degree $n$.
-   **IMPORTANT**: Are NOT isomorphic to $\mathbb{Z}_{p^n}$ (Ring integers mod $p^n$).
-   **Multiplicative Group**: $K^\times = K \setminus \{0\}$ is always **Cyclic**.
    -   Generator is called a "Primitive Element".

## 4. Special Topics (You asked about these)

### Chinese Remainder Theorem (CRT) for Rings

-   **Idea**: If ideals $I$ and $J$ are coprime ($I+J=R$), then $R/(I \cap J) \cong R/I \times R/J$.
-   **App**: $\mathbb{Q}[x] / (x^2-1) \cong \mathbb{Q}[x]/(x-1) \times \mathbb{Q}[x]/(x+1) \cong \mathbb{Q} \times \mathbb{Q}$.
-   *Translation*: Solving modulo $x^2-1$ is same as solving modulo $x-1$ AND modulo $x+1$ separately.

### Primitive Elements (Finite Fields)

-   **Concept**: In a finite field, there is an element $g$ such that every non-zero element is a power of $g$.
-   **Count**: Number of generators = $\phi(|F|-1)$.
    -   For $\mathbb{F}_{16}$, $|F^\times|=15$. Generators = $\phi(15) = \phi(3)\phi(5) = 2 \times 4 = 8$.

### Constructibility (Ruler & Compass)

-   A number $\alpha$ is constructible $\iff [\mathbb{Q}(\alpha):\mathbb{Q}]$ is a power of 2 ($2^k$).
-   $\sqrt[3]{2}$ (Delian constant) degree 3 $\implies$ Doubling cube impossible.
-   Trisection of $60^\circ$ impossible (requires root of cubic).
