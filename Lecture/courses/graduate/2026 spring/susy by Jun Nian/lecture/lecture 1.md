We choose the spacetime background to be 4d Minkowski spacetime, with signature $(-,+,+,+)$.

## $\mathcal{N}=1$ susy algebra

In 4d, the minimal supersymmetry generator is a Weyl spinor

$$\begin{align}
Q_\alpha, \qquad \bar Q_{\dot \alpha} = (Q_\alpha)^\dagger, \qquad \alpha,\dot\alpha=1,2.
\end{align}$$

The bosonic part is the Poincare algebra generated by translations $P_\mu$ and Lorentz transformations $M_{\mu\nu}$:

$$\begin{align}
[P_\mu,P_\nu] &= 0,\\
[M_{\mu\nu},P_\rho] &= i(\eta_{\nu\rho}P_\mu-\eta_{\mu\rho}P_\nu),\\
[M_{\mu\nu},M_{\rho\sigma}] &= i(\eta_{\mu\sigma}M_{\nu\rho} +\eta_{\nu\rho}M_{\mu\sigma} -\eta_{\mu\rho}M_{\nu\sigma} -\eta_{\nu\sigma}M_{\mu\rho}).
\end{align}$$

The supercharges commute with translations,

$$\begin{align}
[P_\mu,Q_\alpha]=0,\qquad [P_\mu,\bar Q_{\dot\alpha}]=0,
\end{align}$$

and transform as spinors under Lorentz transformations:

$$\begin{align}
[M_{\mu\nu},Q_\alpha] &= i(\sigma_{\mu\nu})_\alpha{}^\beta Q_\beta,\\
[M_{\mu\nu},\bar Q_{\dot\alpha}] &= i(\bar\sigma_{\mu\nu})_{\dot\alpha}{}^{\dot\beta} \bar Q_{\dot\beta}.
\end{align}$$

The fermionic anti-commutators are

$$\begin{align}
\{Q_\alpha,\bar Q_{\dot\beta}\} &=2(\sigma^\mu)_{\alpha\dot\beta}P_\mu,\\
\{Q_\alpha,Q_\beta\} &=0,\\
\{\bar Q_{\dot\alpha},\bar Q_{\dot\beta}\} &=0.
\end{align}$$

For $\mathcal{N}=1$ in four dimensions there is no Lorentz-invariant central charge in the ordinary super-Poincare algebra. Central charges become important for extended supersymmetry, $\mathcal{N}>1$.

The anti-commutator implies positivity of the Hamiltonian. Schematically,

$$\begin{align}
\sum_\alpha \left( \{Q_\alpha,Q_\alpha^\dagger\} \right) \sim H + \text{spatial momentum terms}.
\end{align}$$

In the rest frame this gives $H\geq 0$. A supersymmetric vacuum satisfies

$$\begin{align}
Q_\alpha |0\rangle = \bar Q_{\dot\alpha}|0\rangle =0,
\end{align}$$

and therefore has zero vacuum energy in an unbroken supersymmetric theory.

## irreps. of $\mathcal{N}=1$ susy algebra

The supercharges commute with $P^2$, so all states in one supermultiplet have the same mass. Since $Q_\alpha$ is fermionic, acting with a supercharge changes statistics and relates bosonic and fermionic states.

### Massless irreps

For a massless particle, choose a lightlike reference momentum. The little group is $ISO(2)$, and physical one-particle states are labeled by helicity. Positivity of the algebra removes half of the supercharge components, leaving one complex fermionic creation or annihilation operator.

Starting from a Clifford vacuum of helicity $\lambda$, one obtains

$$\begin{align}
|\lambda\rangle, \qquad Q^\dagger |\lambda\rangle \sim |\lambda+\tfrac12\rangle.
\end{align}$$

Thus an irreducible massless $\mathcal{N}=1$ multiplet contains two helicity states before imposing CPT. A CPT-invariant physical spectrum must also include the conjugate helicities.

Important examples are:

- Chiral multiplet:

$$\begin{align}
\left(-\frac12,0\right)\oplus \left(0,\frac12\right).
\end{align}$$

It contains one Weyl fermion and one complex scalar.
- Vector multiplet:

$$\begin{align}
\left(-1,-\frac12\right)\oplus \left(\frac12,1\right).
\end{align}$$

It contains one gauge boson and one gaugino.
- Gravity multiplet:

$$\begin{align}
\left(-2,-\frac32\right)\oplus \left(\frac32,2\right).
\end{align}$$

It contains the graviton and gravitino.

***here we also impose condition that the CPT symmetry holds.***

### Massive irreps

For a massive particle, go to the rest frame

$$\begin{align}
P^\mu=(m,0,0,0).
\end{align}$$

The algebra becomes a two-component fermionic oscillator algebra after rescaling

$$\begin{align}
a_\alpha = \frac{Q_\alpha}{\sqrt{2m}}, \qquad a^\dagger_\alpha = \frac{\bar Q_{\dot\alpha}}{\sqrt{2m}},
\end{align}$$

so that

$$\begin{align}
\{a_\alpha,a^\dagger_\beta\}=\delta_{\alpha\beta}, \qquad \{a_\alpha,a_\beta\}=0, \qquad \{a^\dagger_\alpha,a^\dagger_\beta\}=0.
\end{align}$$

There are two fermionic creation operators. Starting from a Clifford vacuum $|\Omega\rangle$ with spin $j$, the multiplet is generated by

$$\begin{align}
|\Omega\rangle,\qquad a^\dagger_\alpha|\Omega\rangle,\qquad a^\dagger_1a^\dagger_2|\Omega\rangle.
\end{align}$$

The two one-particle states $a^\dagger_\alpha|\Omega\rangle$ transform as spin $j\otimes \frac12$, while the two-oscillator state is again spin $j$. Hence the massive $\mathcal{N}=1$ multiplet has equal numbers of bosonic and fermionic states.

For the lowest choice $j=0$, the massive multiplet contains

$$\begin{align}
0,\qquad \frac12,\qquad 0,
\end{align}$$

which is the massive version of a chiral multiplet.

## examples

### free Wess-Zumino model

The free Wess-Zumino model is the simplest field theory realization of the chiral multiplet. The field content is

$$\begin{align}
(\phi,\psi_\alpha,F),
\end{align}$$

where $\phi$ is a complex scalar, $\psi_\alpha$ is a Weyl fermion, and $F$ is a complex auxiliary scalar. The auxiliary field has no kinetic term and is introduced so that the supersymmetry algebra closes off-shell.

With the signature $(-,+,+,+)$, the off-shell action is

$$\begin{align}
S &= \int_M \mathrm{d}^{4}x\,\mathcal{L},\\
\mathcal{L}&= -\partial_\mu \phi^* \partial^\mu \phi- i \bar\psi_{\dot\alpha}(\bar\sigma^\mu)^{\dot\alpha\beta}\partial_\mu \psi_\beta+ F^*F.
\end{align}$$

Here $\bar\psi_{\dot\alpha}=(\psi_\alpha)^\dagger$. The equation of motion for the auxiliary field is simply

$$\begin{align}
F=F^*=0.
\end{align}$$

After eliminating $F$, the on-shell free Lagrangian is

$$\begin{align}
\mathcal{L}_{\text{on-shell}}= -\partial_\mu \phi^* \partial^\mu \phi- i \bar\psi \bar\sigma^\mu \partial_\mu\psi.
\end{align}$$

The supersymmetry transformations are

$$\begin{align}
\delta_\epsilon \phi&= \sqrt{2}\,\epsilon^\alpha\psi_\alpha,\\
\delta_\epsilon \phi^*&= \sqrt{2}\,\bar\epsilon_{\dot\alpha}\bar\psi^{\dot\alpha},\\
\delta_\epsilon \psi_\alpha&= i\sqrt{2}\,(\sigma^\mu \bar\epsilon)_\alpha\,\partial_\mu\phi+\sqrt{2}\,\epsilon_\alpha F,\\
\delta_\epsilon \bar\psi_{\dot\alpha}&= -i\sqrt{2}\,(\epsilon\sigma^\mu)_{\dot\alpha}\,\partial_\mu\phi^*+\sqrt{2}\,\bar\epsilon_{\dot\alpha}F^*,\\
\delta_\epsilon F&= i\sqrt{2}\,\bar\epsilon_{\dot\alpha}(\bar\sigma^\mu)^{\dot\alpha\beta}\partial_\mu\psi_\beta,\\
\delta_\epsilon F^*&= -i\sqrt{2}\,\epsilon^\alpha(\sigma^\mu)_{\alpha\dot\beta}\partial_\mu\bar\psi^{\dot\beta}.
\end{align}$$

One can check that the Lagrangian changes by a total derivative under these transformations:

$$\begin{align}
\delta_\epsilon \mathcal{L}=\partial_\mu K^\mu.
\end{align}$$

Therefore the action is invariant for fields with suitable boundary conditions.

The commutator of two supersymmetry transformations closes onto translations:

$$\begin{align}
[\delta_\epsilon,\delta_\eta]= 2i(\epsilon\sigma^\mu\bar\eta-\eta\sigma^\mu\bar\epsilon)\partial_\mu.
\end{align}$$

With the auxiliary field $F$, this closure holds without using equations of motion. After setting $F=0$, closure on $\psi_\alpha$ requires the free Weyl equation.

The Noether current can be chosen as

$$\begin{align}
J^\mu_\alpha&= \sqrt{2}\,(\sigma^\nu\bar\sigma^\mu\psi)_\alpha\,\partial_\nu\phi^*,\\
\bar J^\mu_{\dot\alpha}&= \sqrt{2}\,(\bar\sigma^\nu\sigma^\mu\bar\psi)_{\dot\alpha}\,\partial_\nu\phi.
\end{align}$$

The corresponding conserved supercharges are

$$\begin{align}
Q_\alpha&= \int \mathrm{d}^3x\,J^0_\alpha,\\
\bar Q_{\dot\alpha}&= \int \mathrm{d}^3x\,\bar J^0_{\dot\alpha}.
\end{align}$$

The complex scalar has two real bosonic degrees of freedom, while a Weyl fermion has two on-shell fermionic degrees of freedom. Thus the free Wess-Zumino model realizes the on-shell chiral multiplet

$$\begin{align}
\left(0,\frac12\right)\oplus\left(-\frac12,0\right).
\end{align}$$

an important equality is that

$$\begin{align}
(\sigma _{\mu}\bar{\sigma}_{\nu}+\bar{\sigma}_{\mu}\sigma _{\nu})_{\alpha}^{~\gamma} & =2\eta _{\mu \nu}\delta _{\alpha}^{~\gamma}
\end{align}$$

### Superpotential deformation

The interacting Wess-Zumino model is obtained by adding a holomorphic superpotential $W(\phi)$. In components,

$$\begin{align}
\mathcal{L}= -\partial_\mu \phi^*\partial^\mu\phi-i\bar\psi\bar\sigma^\mu\partial_\mu\psi+F^*F+F W'(\phi)+F^* W'(\phi)^*-\frac12 W''(\phi)\psi\psi-\frac12 W''(\phi)^*\bar\psi\bar\psi.
\end{align}$$

The auxiliary-field equation becomes

$$\begin{align}
F^*=-W'(\phi),\qquad F=-W'(\phi)^*.
\end{align}$$

Eliminating $F$ gives the scalar potential

$$\begin{align}
V(\phi)=|W'(\phi)|^2.
\end{align}$$

For example, the massive free chiral multiplet corresponds to

$$\begin{align}
W(\phi)=\frac12 m\phi^2,
\end{align}$$

which gives equal boson and fermion masses.
