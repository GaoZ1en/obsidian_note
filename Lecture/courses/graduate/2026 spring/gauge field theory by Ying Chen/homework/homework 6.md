# problem 1

consider 3 flavors of quark $\displaystyle{q^{T}=(u,d,s)}$. under the chiral limit, the theory has chiral symmetry $\displaystyle{\mathrm{U}_{L}(3)\times \mathrm{U}_{R}(3)}$. the axial current corresponding to the axial transformation is given by

$$\begin{align}
j_{5}^{a,\mu} & =\bar{q}\gamma ^{\mu}\gamma _{5}t^{a}_{F}q,  & a=0,1,\dots,8
\end{align}$$

here $\displaystyle{t^{0}_{F}=I_{3\times3}}$, and $\displaystyle{t^{a}_{F}=\frac{\lambda ^{a}}{2}}$. for QCD, quarks belong to fundamental reps. of the color $\displaystyle{\mathrm{SU}(3)}$ group, the covariant derivative defined as $\displaystyle{D_{\mu}=\partial _{\mu}-igA_{\mu}^{a}t_{C}^{a}}$. prove that the anomalous axial currents satisfy (under the chiral limit)

$$\begin{align}
\partial _{\mu}j _{5}^{a,\mu}(x) & =\frac{g^{2}}{16\pi ^{2}} \varepsilon ^{\mu \nu \rho \sigma}F^{b}_{\mu \nu}F^{c}_{\rho \sigma}\mathrm{Tr}(t^{a}_{F}t^{b}_{C}t^{c}_{C})
\end{align}$$

and argue that there is an anomaly only for the flavor singlet current $\displaystyle{j_{5}^{0,\mu}}$, and no anomaly for flavor octet currents $\displaystyle{j_{5}^{a,\mu},a=1,\dots,8}$.

## solution

we use the Fujikawa formalism. It is convenient to work in Euclidean signature first and Wick rotate back to Minkowski at the end.

Under a local axial rotation generated by $t_{F}^{a}$,

$$\begin{align}
q(x) & \to q'(x)=e^{i\alpha ^{a}(x)t_{F}^{a}\gamma _{5}}q(x), \\
\bar{q}(x) & \to \bar{q}'(x)=\bar{q}(x)e^{i\alpha ^{a}(x)t_{F}^{a}\gamma _{5}},
\end{align}$$

the classical action varies as

$$\begin{align}
\delta S_{E} & =-\int \mathrm{d}^{4}x\,\alpha ^{a}(x)\partial _{\mu}j_{5}^{a,\mu}(x),
\end{align}$$

so the classical Ward identity is $\partial _{\mu}j_{5}^{a,\mu}=0$. The anomaly comes from the fermion measure.

Let $\left\{\varphi _{n}\right\}$ be a complete orthonormal basis of eigenfunctions of the Euclidean Dirac operator,

$$\begin{align}
iD\!\!\!/_{E}\varphi _{n}(x) & =\lambda _{n}\varphi _{n}(x),
\end{align}$$

and expand

$$\begin{align}
q(x) & =\sum _{n} a_{n}\varphi _{n}(x), \\
\bar{q}(x) & =\sum _{n}\bar{b}_{n}\varphi _{n}^{\dagger}(x).
\end{align}$$

Then the path-integral measure transforms as

$$\begin{align}
\mathcal{D}\bar{q}'\mathcal{D}q' & =J[\alpha ]\,\mathcal{D}\bar{q}\mathcal{D}q,
\end{align}$$

with

$$\begin{align}
\ln J[\alpha ] & =-2i\int \mathrm{d}^{4}x\,\alpha ^{a}(x)\sum _{n}\varphi _{n}^{\dagger}(x)t_{F}^{a}\gamma _{5}\varphi _{n}(x).
\end{align}$$

This sum is ill-defined and must be regularized. Fujikawa inserts the heat-kernel regulator

$$\begin{align}
\mathcal{A}^{a}(x) & =2\lim _{\Lambda \to \infty}\sum _{n}\varphi _{n}^{\dagger}(x)t_{F}^{a}\gamma _{5}e^{-\lambda _{n}^{2}/\Lambda ^{2}}\varphi _{n}(x) \\
 & =2\lim _{\Lambda \to \infty}\operatorname{tr}\left[t_{F}^{a}\gamma _{5}\exp\left(-\frac{(iD\!\!\!\!/_{E})^{2}}{\Lambda ^{2}}\right)\delta (x-x)\right].
\end{align}$$

After inserting plane waves into $\delta (x-x)$, the regulator becomes a local heat-kernel expansion. Because $\operatorname{tr}_{D}\gamma _{5}=0$ and $\operatorname{tr}_{D}(\gamma _{5}\sigma ^{\mu \nu})=0$, the first nonvanishing term is quadratic in $F_{\mu \nu}$:

$$\begin{align}
\mathcal{A}^{a}(x) & =2\lim _{\Lambda \to \infty}\int \frac{\mathrm{d}^{4}k}{(2\pi )^{4}}e^{-k^{2}}\operatorname{tr}\left[t_{F}^{a}\gamma _{5}\frac{1}{2!}\left(\frac{g}{2\Lambda ^{2}}\sigma ^{\mu \nu}F_{\mu \nu}^{b}t_{C}^{b}\right)\left(\frac{g}{2\Lambda ^{2}}\sigma ^{\rho \sigma}F_{\rho \sigma}^{c}t_{C}^{c}\right)\right].
\end{align}$$

The Gaussian momentum integral gives

$$\begin{align}
\int \frac{\mathrm{d}^{4}k}{(2\pi )^{4}}e^{-k^{2}} & =\frac{1}{16\pi ^{2}},
\end{align}$$

and the Dirac trace gives

$$\begin{align}
\operatorname{tr}_{D}\left(\gamma _{5}\sigma ^{\mu \nu}\sigma ^{\rho \sigma}\right) & =4\varepsilon ^{\mu \nu \rho \sigma}.
\end{align}$$

Therefore

$$\begin{align}
\mathcal{A}^{a}(x) & =\frac{g^{2}}{16\pi ^{2}}\varepsilon ^{\mu \nu \rho \sigma}F_{\mu \nu}^{b}F_{\rho \sigma}^{c}\operatorname{Tr}(t_{F}^{a}t_{C}^{b}t_{C}^{c}),
\end{align}$$

where $\operatorname{Tr}$ is the trace over the internal flavor and color spaces. Combining the Jacobian with the classical variation of the action, we obtain

$$\begin{align}
\partial _{\mu}j_{5}^{a,\mu}(x) & =\frac{g^{2}}{16\pi ^{2}}\varepsilon ^{\mu \nu \rho \sigma}F_{\mu \nu}^{b}F_{\rho \sigma}^{c}\operatorname{Tr}(t_{F}^{a}t_{C}^{b}t_{C}^{c}).
\end{align}$$

Now the flavor and color generators act on different spaces, so the trace factorizes:

$$\begin{align}
\operatorname{Tr}(t_{F}^{a}t_{C}^{b}t_{C}^{c}) & =\operatorname{Tr}_{F}(t_{F}^{a})\operatorname{Tr}_{C}(t_{C}^{b}t_{C}^{c}) \\
 & =\frac{1}{2}\delta ^{bc}\operatorname{Tr}_{F}(t_{F}^{a}).
\end{align}$$

For the octet generators $t_{F}^{a}=\lambda ^{a}/2$ with $a=1,\dots ,8$, we have

$$\begin{align}
\operatorname{Tr}_{F}(t_{F}^{a}) & =0,
\end{align}$$

so

$$\begin{align}
\partial _{\mu}j_{5}^{a,\mu}(x) & =0,\qquad a=1,\dots ,8.
\end{align}$$

For the singlet generator $t_{F}^{0}=I_{3\times 3}$,

$$\begin{align}
\operatorname{Tr}_{F}(t_{F}^{0}) & =3,
\end{align}$$

hence

$$\begin{align}
\partial _{\mu}j_{5}^{0,\mu}(x) & =\frac{3g^{2}}{32\pi ^{2}}\varepsilon ^{\mu \nu \rho \sigma}F_{\mu \nu}^{b}F_{\rho \sigma}^{b}.
\end{align}$$

Therefore only the flavor singlet axial current has an anomaly, while all flavor octet axial currents remain conserved in the chiral limit.


# problem 2

prove that the transition amplitude induced by the axial current $\displaystyle{j _{5}^{3,\mu}}$ from vacuum to two photons is

$$\begin{align}
\braket{ \gamma(p),\gamma(k)|\partial _{\mu}j _{5}^{3,\mu}|\Omega }  & =\frac{e^{2}}{2\pi ^{2}} \left(\frac{N_{c}}{3}\right) \varepsilon ^{\mu \nu \rho \sigma}(-ip_{\mu})\varepsilon _{\nu}^{*}(p)(-ik _{\rho})\varepsilon _{\sigma}^{*}(k)
\end{align}$$

where $\displaystyle{\varepsilon _{\nu}^{*}(p),\varepsilon _{\sigma}^{*}(k)}$ are polarization vectors of 2 photons with momenta $\displaystyle{p,k}$, $\displaystyle{j _{5}^{3,\mu}= \bar{u}\gamma ^{\mu}\gamma _{5}u-\bar{d}\gamma ^{\mu}\gamma _{5}d}$. notice that there are $\displaystyle{N_{c}}$ colors for quarks.

## solution

this is the same axial anomaly, but now the gauge field is the electromagnetic field. For the three light quarks,

$$\begin{align}
D_{\mu} & =\partial _{\mu}+ieQA_{\mu}, \\
Q & =\operatorname{diag}\left(\frac{2}{3},-\frac{1}{3},-\frac{1}{3}\right).
\end{align}$$

The current in the problem is

$$\begin{align}
j_{5}^{3,\mu} & =\bar{q}\gamma ^{\mu}\gamma _{5}T^{3}q, \\
T^{3} & =\operatorname{diag}(1,-1,0).
\end{align}$$

Applying the Fujikawa formula derived above with the replacement $gt_{C}^{a}A_{\mu}^{a}\to -eQA_{\mu}$, we obtain

$$\begin{align}
\partial _{\mu}j_{5}^{3,\mu}(x) & =\frac{e^{2}}{16\pi ^{2}}\varepsilon ^{\mu \nu \rho \sigma}F_{\mu \nu}F_{\rho \sigma}\operatorname{Tr}(T^{3}Q^{2}).
\end{align}$$

There are $N_{c}$ identical colors, so the full trace gives an extra factor of $N_{c}$:

$$\begin{align}
\partial _{\mu}j_{5}^{3,\mu}(x) & =\frac{N_{c}e^{2}}{16\pi ^{2}}\varepsilon ^{\mu \nu \rho \sigma}F_{\mu \nu}F_{\rho \sigma}\operatorname{Tr}_{F}(T^{3}Q^{2}).
\end{align}$$

Now

$$\begin{align}
\operatorname{Tr}_{F}(T^{3}Q^{2}) & =\left(\frac{2}{3}\right)^{2}-\left(-\frac{1}{3}\right)^{2} \\
 & =\frac{4}{9}-\frac{1}{9}=\frac{1}{3},
\end{align}$$

so

$$\begin{align}
\partial _{\mu}j_{5}^{3,\mu}(x) & =\frac{N_{c}e^{2}}{48\pi ^{2}}\varepsilon ^{\mu \nu \rho \sigma}F_{\mu \nu}F_{\rho \sigma}.
\end{align}$$

To obtain the vacuum-to-two-photon matrix element, we use

$$\begin{align}
\langle \gamma (p),\gamma (k)|F_{\mu \nu}(0)|\Omega \rangle & =0,
\end{align}$$

but the quadratic operator has a nonvanishing matrix element. For on-shell photons,

$$\begin{align}
F_{\mu \nu}^{(p)} & =-i\left(p_{\mu}\varepsilon _{\nu}^{*}(p)-p_{\nu}\varepsilon _{\mu}^{*}(p)\right), \\
F_{\rho \sigma}^{(k)} & =-i\left(k_{\rho}\varepsilon _{\sigma}^{*}(k)-k_{\sigma}\varepsilon _{\rho}^{*}(k)\right).
\end{align}$$

There are two Wick contractions, because the two identical photons can be created by the two field strengths in either order. Using the antisymmetry of $\varepsilon ^{\mu \nu \rho \sigma}$, one finds

$$\begin{align}
\langle \gamma (p),\gamma (k)|\varepsilon ^{\mu \nu \rho \sigma}F_{\mu \nu}F_{\rho \sigma}|\Omega \rangle & =8\,\varepsilon ^{\mu \nu \rho \sigma}(-ip_{\mu})\varepsilon _{\nu}^{*}(p)(-ik_{\rho})\varepsilon _{\sigma}^{*}(k).
\end{align}$$

Therefore

$$\begin{align}
\braket{ \gamma(p),\gamma(k)|\partial _{\mu}j _{5}^{3,\mu}|\Omega }  & =\frac{N_{c}e^{2}}{48\pi ^{2}}\cdot 8\,\varepsilon ^{\mu \nu \rho \sigma}(-ip_{\mu})\varepsilon _{\nu}^{*}(p)(-ik _{\rho})\varepsilon _{\sigma}^{*}(k) \\
 & =\frac{e^{2}}{2\pi ^{2}}\left(\frac{N_{c}}{3}\right)\varepsilon ^{\mu \nu \rho \sigma}(-ip_{\mu})\varepsilon _{\nu}^{*}(p)(-ik _{\rho})\varepsilon _{\sigma}^{*}(k),
\end{align}$$

which is the required result.
