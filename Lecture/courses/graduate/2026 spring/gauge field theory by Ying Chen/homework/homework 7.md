# problem 1

consider the classical action

$$\begin{align}
S[\psi,\bar{\psi},A] & =S_{A}[A]+S_{f}[A] \\
S_{f}[A] & =\int \mathrm{d}^{4}x\mathrm{d}^{4}y\bar{\psi}(x)iM(x,y;A)\psi(y)
\end{align}$$

the generating functional can be written as

$$\begin{align}
Z[\eta,\bar{\eta}] & =\int \mathcal{D}A\mathcal{D}\bar{\psi}\mathcal{D}\psi e^{iS[\psi,\bar{\psi},A]+i \int \mathrm{d}^{4}x(\bar{\eta}\psi+\bar{\psi}\eta)}
\end{align}$$

let $\displaystyle{S_{F}(x,y;A)}$ be the fermion propagator in the presence of background field $\displaystyle{A_{\mu}(x)}$\

$$\begin{align}
S_{F}(x,y;A) & = \braket{ \psi(x)\bar{\psi}(y) } _{A} \\
 & =[\det(M)]^{-1}\int \mathcal{D}\bar{\psi}\mathcal{D}\psi e^{iS_{f}[A]} \psi(x)\bar{\psi}(y)
\end{align}$$

the questions are
1. prove that $\displaystyle{S_{F}(x,y;A)=M^{-1}(x,y;A)}$
2. let $\displaystyle{\mathcal{O}_{A}(x)=\bar{\psi}(x)\Gamma _{A}\psi(x),\mathcal{O}_{B}(x)=\bar{\psi}(x)\Gamma _{B}(x)\psi(x)}$ be two local operators. present the Green function

$$\begin{align}
G_{AB}(x,y) & =\braket{ \Omega|T\mathcal{O}_{A}(x)\mathcal{O}_{B}(x)|\Omega } 
\end{align}$$

as the functional integral over gauge field $\displaystyle{A_{\mu}}$.

## solution

For a fixed background field $A_{\mu}$, introduce the fermion generating functional

$$\begin{align}
Z_{f}[\eta,\bar{\eta};A] & =\int \mathcal{D}\bar{\psi}\mathcal{D}\psi
\exp\left[-\int \mathrm{d}^{4}x\mathrm{d}^{4}y\bar{\psi}(x)M(x,y;A)\psi(y)
+i\int \mathrm{d}^{4}x(\bar{\eta}\psi+\bar{\psi}\eta)\right].
\end{align}$$

The Grassmann Gaussian integral gives

$$\begin{align}
Z_{f}[\eta,\bar{\eta};A] & =\det M[A]\,
\exp\left[-\int \mathrm{d}^{4}x\mathrm{d}^{4}y\bar{\eta}(x)M^{-1}(x,y;A)\eta(y)\right].
\end{align}$$

Therefore

$$\begin{align}
S_{F}(x,y;A) & =
\frac{1}{\det M[A]}\int \mathcal{D}\bar{\psi}\mathcal{D}\psi e^{iS_{f}[A]}\psi(x)\bar{\psi}(y) \\
 & =M^{-1}(x,y;A).
\end{align}$$

Equivalently, it is the Green function of the Dirac operator in the background field:

$$\begin{align}
\int \mathrm{d}^{4}z\,M(x,z;A)S_{F}(z,y;A) & =\delta ^{(4)}(x-y).
\end{align}$$

For the bilinear operators, the full path integral first gives

$$\begin{align}
G_{AB}(x,y) & =
\frac{1}{Z}\int \mathcal{D}A\,e^{iS_{A}[A]}\det M[A]\,
\left\langle \mathcal{O}_{A}(x)\mathcal{O}_{B}(y)\right\rangle _{A}.
\end{align}$$

Using Wick contraction in the fixed background $A_{\mu}$,

$$\begin{align}
\left\langle \mathcal{O}_{A}(x)\mathcal{O}_{B}(y)\right\rangle _{A}
& =
\operatorname{tr}\left[\Gamma _{A}S_{F}(x,x;A)\right]
\operatorname{tr}\left[\Gamma _{B}S_{F}(y,y;A)\right] \\
&\quad
-\operatorname{tr}\left[\Gamma _{A}S_{F}(x,y;A)\Gamma _{B}S_{F}(y,x;A)\right],
\end{align}$$

where the trace is over spinor and internal indices. Hence

$$\begin{align}
G_{AB}(x,y) & =
\frac{1}{Z}\int \mathcal{D}A\,e^{iS_{A}[A]}\det M[A]\,
\Big\{
\operatorname{tr}\left[\Gamma _{A}S_{F}(x,x;A)\right]
\operatorname{tr}\left[\Gamma _{B}S_{F}(y,y;A)\right] \\
&\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad
-\operatorname{tr}\left[\Gamma _{A}S_{F}(x,y;A)\Gamma _{B}S_{F}(y,x;A)\right]
\Big\}.
\end{align}$$

# problem 2

1. explain the relation among Green function, connected Green function, amputated Green function and regularized vertex function.
2. prove that connected Green function can always be expressed by full propagator and regularized vertex function.
3. effective action is the same as classical action at tree level. derive the Feynman rule of electric vertex.

## solution

Let $Z[J]$ be the generating functional and $W[J]$ be the connected generating functional,

$$\begin{align}
Z[J] & =e^{iW[J]}.
\end{align}$$

Ordinary Green functions are generated by differentiating $Z[J]$ and contain both connected and disconnected diagrams. Connected Green functions are generated by differentiating $W[J]$. For example,

$$\begin{align}
G_{c}^{(n)}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n}) & =
\frac{\delta ^{n}W}{i\delta J(x_{1})\cdots i\delta J(x_{n})}\bigg|_{J=0}.
\end{align}$$

The full propagator is the connected two-point function. An amputated Green function is obtained from a connected Green function by removing the external full propagators. In momentum space, for a scalar example,

$$\begin{align}
G_{c}^{(n)}(p_{1},\dots,p_{n})
& =
G(p_{1})\cdots G(p_{n})\,G_{\mathrm{amp}}^{(n)}(p_{1},\dots,p_{n}).
\end{align}$$

The regularized vertex function is the proper, or one-particle-irreducible, vertex generated by the effective action $\Gamma[\phi]$:

$$\begin{align}
\Gamma ^{(n)}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n}) & =
\frac{\delta ^{n}\Gamma}{\delta \phi(x_{1})\cdots \delta \phi(x_{n})}\bigg|_{\phi=0}.
\end{align}$$

The connected Green functions are obtained by connecting these proper vertices with full propagators. This follows from the Legendre transform relation between $W$ and $\Gamma$. In particular,

$$\begin{align}
\int \mathrm{d}^{4}z\,G(x,z)\Gamma ^{(2)}(z,y) & =-\delta ^{(4)}(x-y),
\end{align}$$

up to the conventional factors of $i$. Higher derivatives of this identity express every connected $n$-point function as a sum of tree graphs whose vertices are the proper vertices $\Gamma ^{(m)}$ and whose internal lines are full propagators. For example,

$$\begin{align}
G_{c}^{(3)}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})
& =
-\int \mathrm{d}^{4}y_{1}\mathrm{d}^{4}y_{2}\mathrm{d}^{4}y_{3}\,
G(x_{1},y_{1})G(x_{2},y_{2})G(x_{3},y_{3})\Gamma ^{(3)}(y_{1},y_{2},y_{3}).
\end{align}$$

At tree level, the effective action equals the classical action. For QED, with

$$\begin{align}
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{int}} & =-e\bar{\psi}\gamma ^{\mu}A_{\mu}\psi,
\end{align}$$

the three-point proper vertex is

$$\begin{align}
\frac{\delta ^{3}S_{\mathrm{int}}}{\delta A_{\mu}(x)\delta \psi(y)\delta \bar{\psi}(z)}
& =-e\gamma ^{\mu}\delta ^{(4)}(x-y)\delta ^{(4)}(x-z).
\end{align}$$

Therefore the tree-level electric vertex Feynman rule is

$$\begin{align}
\text{photon-electron vertex:}\qquad & -ie\gamma ^{\mu},
\end{align}$$

# problem 3

the generating function for QED is given by

$$\begin{align}
Z[J,\eta,\bar{\eta}] & =\int \mathcal{D}A\mathcal{D}\bar{\psi}\mathcal{D}\psi \exp\left(i \int \mathrm{d}^{4}x\left(\mathcal{L}_{\text{eff}}+A_{\mu}j ^{\mu}+\bar{\eta}\psi+\bar{\psi}\eta\right)\right) \\
\mathcal{L}_{\text{eff}} & =-\frac{1}{4}F_{\mu \nu}F^{\mu \nu}+\bar{\psi}(i\gamma ^{\mu}D_{\mu}-m)\psi-\frac{1}{2\xi}(\partial _{\mu}A^{\mu})^{2} \\
 & =\mathcal{L}_{\text{QED}}+\mathcal{L}_{\text{gf}}
\end{align}$$

1. prove that the connected Green function $\displaystyle{W}$ satisfies the following functional equation

$$\begin{align}
i \frac{1}{\xi}\partial ^{2}\partial _{\mu} \frac{\delta iW}{\delta J_{\mu}(x)}-\partial _{\mu}J^{\mu}(x)-e \bar{\eta}(x) \frac{\delta iW}{\delta \bar{\eta}(x)}-e \frac{\delta iW}{\delta \eta(x)}\eta(x)=0
\end{align}$$

2. the full propagator for photon is given by

$$\begin{align}
G^{\mu \nu}(x-y) & \equiv \braket{ \Omega|TA^{\mu}(x)A^{\nu}(y)|\Omega }  \\
 & = \frac{\delta ^{2}W}{i\delta J_{\mu}(x)i\delta J_{\nu}(y)}\bigg|_{J=\eta=\bar{\eta}=0}
\end{align}$$

prove that the transverse part of the propagator has no higher order correction.

## solution

Perform the infinitesimal gauge transformation

$$\begin{align}
A_{\mu}(x) & \to A_{\mu}(x)+\partial _{\mu}\alpha(x), \\
\psi(x) & \to e^{ie\alpha(x)}\psi(x), \\
\bar{\psi}(x) & \to \bar{\psi}(x)e^{-ie\alpha(x)}.
\end{align}$$

The QED action is invariant, while the gauge-fixing term and source terms vary as

$$\begin{align}
\delta S_{\mathrm{gf}} & =
-\int \mathrm{d}^{4}x\,\frac{1}{\xi}(\partial _{\mu}A^{\mu})\partial ^{2}\alpha, \\
\delta S_{\mathrm{source}} & =
\int \mathrm{d}^{4}x\left[
J^{\mu}\partial _{\mu}\alpha
+ie\bar{\eta}\alpha\psi
-ie\bar{\psi}\alpha\eta
\right].
\end{align}$$

After integrating the $J^{\mu}\partial _{\mu}\alpha$ term by parts and using the invariance of the functional measure, the coefficient of the arbitrary function $\alpha(x)$ must vanish inside the path integral:

$$\begin{align}
\left[
\frac{1}{\xi}\partial ^{2}\partial _{\mu}A^{\mu}(x)
-\partial _{\mu}J^{\mu}(x)
+e\bar{\eta}(x)\psi(x)
-e\bar{\psi}(x)\eta(x)
\right]Z[J,\eta,\bar{\eta}] & =0.
\end{align}$$

Using

$$\begin{align}
A^{\mu}(x)Z & =\frac{\delta Z}{i\delta J_{\mu}(x)},&
\psi(x)Z & =\frac{\delta Z}{i\delta \bar{\eta}(x)},&
\bar{\psi}(x)Z & =-\frac{\delta Z}{i\delta \eta(x)},
\end{align}$$

and $Z=e^{iW}$, this becomes

$$\begin{align}
i \frac{1}{\xi}\partial ^{2}\partial _{\mu} \frac{\delta iW}{\delta J_{\mu}(x)}
-\partial _{\mu}J^{\mu}(x)
-e \bar{\eta}(x) \frac{\delta iW}{\delta \bar{\eta}(x)}
-e \frac{\delta iW}{\delta \eta(x)}\eta(x) & =0.
\end{align}$$

Now differentiate this identity with respect to $J_{\nu}(y)$ and then set all sources to zero. The fermion-source terms vanish and we obtain

$$\begin{align}
\frac{1}{\xi}\partial _{x}^{2}\partial _{\mu}^{x}G^{\mu \nu}(x-y)
& =
\partial _{x}^{\nu}\delta ^{(4)}(x-y).
\end{align}$$

In momentum space this is

$$\begin{align}
k_{\mu}G^{\mu \nu}(k) & =
-i\xi \frac{k^{\nu}}{k^{2}+i\varepsilon}.
\end{align}$$

Writing the most general Lorentz decomposition as

$$\begin{align}
G^{\mu \nu}(k) & =
-i\left[
\frac{P_{T}^{\mu \nu}}{k^{2}-\Pi _{T}(k^{2})+i\varepsilon}
+\xi \frac{P_{L}^{\mu \nu}}{k^{2}+i\varepsilon}
\right],
\\
P_{T}^{\mu \nu} & =g^{\mu \nu}-\frac{k^{\mu}k^{\nu}}{k^{2}},
&
P_{L}^{\mu \nu} & =\frac{k^{\mu}k^{\nu}}{k^{2}},
\end{align}$$

the Ward identity fixes the longitudinal part. Thus the gauge-dependent longitudinal part has no higher-order correction. The transverse part is not fixed by this identity and in general receives the vacuum-polarization correction $\Pi _{T}(k^{2})$.

# problem 4

define the classical field in the presence of external source

$$\begin{align}
A^{\mu}(x) & = \frac{\delta iW}{i\delta J_{\mu}(x)}, & \psi(x) & =\frac{\delta iW}{i\delta \bar{\eta}(x)}, & \bar{\psi}(x) & =-\frac{\delta iW}{i\delta \eta(x)}
\end{align}$$

by Legendre transform, we define the effective action as

$$\begin{align}
\Gamma[A,\psi,\bar{\psi}] & =W[J,\eta,\bar{\eta}] -\int \mathrm{d}^{4}x\left(J_{\mu}A^{\mu}+\bar{\eta}\psi+\bar{\psi}\eta\right)
\end{align}$$

the Slavnov-Taylor identity for QED is given by

$$\begin{align}
-\frac{1}{\xi} \partial ^{2}\partial _{\mu}A^{\mu}(x)+\partial _{\mu} \frac{\delta \Gamma}{\delta A_{\mu}(x)}-ie \frac{\delta \Gamma}{\delta \psi(x)} \psi(x)-ie \bar{\psi}(x) \frac{\delta \Gamma}{\delta \bar{\psi}(x)} & =0
\end{align}$$

1. the self-energy correction of photon coming from the high order perturbation of 2-photon regularized vertex. write the effective action as $\displaystyle{\Gamma=\Gamma ^{(0)}+\Gamma'}$. here $\displaystyle{\Gamma ^{(0)}}$ is the classical approximation, i.e., the classical action $\displaystyle{S=\int \mathrm{d}^{4}x\mathcal{L}_{\text{eff}}(x)}$. then the high order correction of the 2-photon regularized vertex (photon self energy) is $\displaystyle{\Pi^{\mu \nu}(x-y)= \frac{i\delta ^{2}\Gamma'}{\delta A_{\mu}(x)\delta A_{\nu}(x)}\bigg|_{A=\psi=\bar{\psi}=0}}$. prove that the self-energy correction is transverse, i.e., we have $\displaystyle{\partial _{\mu}\Pi^{\mu \nu}(x-y)=0}$.
2. prove that the regularized vertex with $\displaystyle{n(\geqslant 3)}$ photon external lines satisfies the Ward identity

$$\begin{align}
k _{i,\mu _{i}}\Gamma ^{\mu_{1}\mu_{2},\dots \mu _{n}}(k_{1},k_{2},\dots,k _{n})=0
\end{align}$$

## solution

Set $\psi=\bar{\psi}=0$ in the Slavnov-Taylor identity. Since the classical action contains the gauge-fixing term explicitly, the higher-order part $\Gamma'$ satisfies

$$\begin{align}
\partial _{\mu}\frac{\delta \Gamma'}{\delta A_{\mu}(x)} & =0.
\end{align}$$

Differentiate this equation with respect to $A_{\nu}(y)$ and then set all fields to zero:

$$\begin{align}
\partial _{\mu}^{x}
\frac{\delta ^{2}\Gamma'}{\delta A_{\mu}(x)\delta A_{\nu}(y)}
\bigg|_{A=\psi=\bar{\psi}=0} & =0.
\end{align}$$

Using the definition

$$\begin{align}
\Pi ^{\mu \nu}(x-y)
& =
\frac{i\delta ^{2}\Gamma'}{\delta A_{\mu}(x)\delta A_{\nu}(y)}
\bigg|_{A=\psi=\bar{\psi}=0},
\end{align}$$

we get

$$\begin{align}
\partial _{\mu}^{x}\Pi ^{\mu \nu}(x-y) & =0.
\end{align}$$

In momentum space this is

$$\begin{align}
k_{\mu}\Pi ^{\mu \nu}(k) & =0,
\end{align}$$

so the photon self-energy is transverse.

For the $n$-photon proper vertex, differentiate

$$\begin{align}
\partial _{\mu}\frac{\delta \Gamma'}{\delta A_{\mu}(x)} & =0
\end{align}$$

with respect to $A_{\mu_{2}}(x_{2}),\dots,A_{\mu_{n}}(x_{n})$ and set all fields to zero. This gives

$$\begin{align}
\partial _{\mu_{1}}^{x_{1}}
\Gamma ^{\mu_{1}\mu_{2}\dots \mu_{n}}(x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{n}) & =0.
\end{align}$$

Fourier transforming,

$$\begin{align}
\Gamma ^{\mu_{1}\dots\mu_{n}}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
& =
\int \prod _{j=1}^{n}\frac{\mathrm{d}^{4}k_{j}}{(2\pi )^{4}}
e^{-i\sum _{j}k_{j}\cdot x_{j}}
(2\pi )^{4}\delta ^{(4)}\left(\sum _{j}k_{j}\right)
\Gamma ^{\mu_{1}\dots\mu_{n}}(k_{1},\dots,k_{n}),
\end{align}$$

we obtain

$$\begin{align}
k_{1,\mu _{1}}\Gamma ^{\mu_{1}\mu_{2}\dots \mu_{n}}(k_{1},k_{2},\dots,k_{n}) & =0.
\end{align}$$

Since the external photons are arbitrary, the same argument applies to any photon leg:

$$\begin{align}
k _{i,\mu _{i}}\Gamma ^{\mu_{1}\mu_{2}\dots \mu _{n}}(k_{1},k_{2},\dots,k _{n}) & =0.
\end{align}$$

# problem 5

the Lagrangian for $\displaystyle{\mathrm{SU}(3)}$ gauge theory is given by

$$\begin{align}
\mathcal{L} & =-\frac{1}{4}F_{\mu \nu}^{a}F^{a,\mu \nu}+\bar{\psi}(i\gamma ^{\mu}D_{\mu}-m)\psi
\end{align}$$

with $\displaystyle{D_{\mu}=\partial _{\mu}-igA_{\mu},A_{\mu}=A_{\mu}^{a}t^{a}}$.

1. write down the local gauge transformation of $\displaystyle{A_{\mu}(x),\psi(x),\bar{\psi}(x)}$
2. explain what gauge fixing and gauge (compensation?) mean in path integral quantization
3. the axial gauge can be expressed as $\displaystyle{F^{a}[A]=n^{\mu}A_{\mu}^{a}=0}$, where $\displaystyle{n^{\mu}}$ is an arbitrary constant vector. complete the Faddeev-Popov path integral quantization under this gauge.

## solution

Let

$$\begin{align}
U(x) & =e^{ig\alpha ^{a}(x)t^{a}}.
\end{align}$$

The local gauge transformation is

$$\begin{align}
\psi(x) & \to \psi '(x)=U(x)\psi(x), \\
\bar{\psi}(x) & \to \bar{\psi}'(x)=\bar{\psi}(x)U^{-1}(x), \\
A_{\mu}(x) & \to A'_{\mu}(x)
=U(x)A_{\mu}(x)U^{-1}(x)+\frac{i}{g}(\partial _{\mu}U(x))U^{-1}(x).
\end{align}$$

Infinitesimally,

$$\begin{align}
\delta \psi & =ig\alpha ^{a}t^{a}\psi, \\
\delta \bar{\psi} & =-ig\bar{\psi}\alpha ^{a}t^{a}, \\
\delta A_{\mu}^{a} & =
\partial _{\mu}\alpha ^{a}+gf^{abc}A_{\mu}^{b}\alpha ^{c}
\equiv (D_{\mu}\alpha)^{a}.
\end{align}$$

Gauge fixing means choosing one representative from each gauge orbit in the functional integral. Without this step, the integral over $A_{\mu}$ contains the infinite gauge-group volume. The Faddeev-Popov method inserts the identity

$$\begin{align}
1 & =
\Delta _{\mathrm{FP}}[A]\int \mathcal{D}\alpha\,
\prod _{a,x}\delta\left(F^{a}[A^{\alpha}](x)\right),
\end{align}$$

where $\Delta _{\mathrm{FP}}[A]$ compensates the Jacobian from gauge parameters to gauge-fixing functions. After factoring out the gauge-group volume, the path integral becomes finite and has a well-defined perturbation theory.

For the axial gauge

$$\begin{align}
F^{a}[A](x) & =n^{\mu}A_{\mu}^{a}(x),
\end{align}$$

the Faddeev-Popov operator is

$$\begin{align}
\frac{\delta F^{a}[A^{\alpha}](x)}{\delta \alpha ^{b}(y)}
& =
n^{\mu}D_{\mu}^{ab}(x)\delta ^{(4)}(x-y),
\end{align}$$

so

$$\begin{align}
\Delta _{\mathrm{FP}}[A] & =
\det \left(n^{\mu}D_{\mu}^{ab}\right).
\end{align}$$

The gauge-fixed generating functional is therefore

$$\begin{align}
Z & =
\int \mathcal{D}A\mathcal{D}\bar{\psi}\mathcal{D}\psi\,
\det \left(n^{\mu}D_{\mu}^{ab}\right)
\prod _{a,x}\delta\left(n^{\mu}A_{\mu}^{a}(x)\right)
\exp\left(i\int \mathrm{d}^{4}x\,\mathcal{L}\right).
\end{align}$$

Equivalently, one may introduce ghosts and a gauge parameter $\xi$:

$$\begin{align}
Z & =
\int \mathcal{D}A\mathcal{D}\bar{\psi}\mathcal{D}\psi
\mathcal{D}\bar{c}\mathcal{D}c\,
\exp\left(i\int \mathrm{d}^{4}x\,\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{axial}}\right), \\
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{axial}} & =
-\frac{1}{4}F_{\mu \nu}^{a}F^{a,\mu \nu}
+\bar{\psi}(i\gamma ^{\mu}D_{\mu}-m)\psi
-\frac{1}{2\xi}(n^{\mu}A_{\mu}^{a})^{2}
+\bar{c}^{a}n^{\mu}D_{\mu}^{ab}c^{b}.
\end{align}$$

In the strict axial gauge $n\cdot A^{a}=0$, the ghost operator reduces to $n\cdot \partial \,\delta ^{ab}$ on the gauge slice, so the ghosts decouple from the gauge field.
