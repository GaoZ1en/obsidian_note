# problem 1, 2

suppose the Polyakov action

$$\begin{align}
S & =-\frac{T}{2}\int _{\mathcal{M}} \mathrm{d}^{2}\tau \sqrt{ -g }g^{ab}G_{\mu \nu}D_{a}X^{\mu}D_{b}X^{\nu}
\end{align}$$

is defined on a worldsheet $\displaystyle{\mathcal{M}}$ sandwiched by two spacelike Cauchy surfaces $\displaystyle{\Sigma _{i}}$ and $\displaystyle{\Sigma _{f}}$, and we have introduced appropriete boundary conditions on the spatial boundary of the worldsheet so that the variation of the action is well defined. here $\displaystyle{D_{a}}$ is the covariant derivative on the worldsheet, and $\displaystyle{G_{\mu \nu}}$ is the metric of the target space. take a variation of the action with respect to $\displaystyle{X^{\mu}}$, we have

$$\begin{align}
\delta S & =T\int \mathrm{d}^{2}\tau \sqrt{ -g }G_{\mu \nu}\left(D ^{2}X^{\mu}+\Gamma ^{\mu}_{~\rho \sigma}D_{a}X^{\rho}D^{a}X^{\sigma}\right)\delta X^{\nu} \\
 & +T\int _{\Sigma _{f}-\Sigma _{i}} \mathrm{d}\tau \sqrt{ \sigma }\tau ^{a}G_{\mu \nu}D_{a}X^{\mu}\delta X^{\nu}
\end{align}$$

then we can read off the equation of motion

$$\begin{align}
D^{2}X^{\mu}+\Gamma ^{\mu}_{~\rho \sigma}D_{a}X^{\rho}D^{a}X^{\sigma} & =0
\end{align}$$

and the symplectic potential

$$\begin{align}
\theta & =T\int _{\Sigma}\mathrm{d}\tau \sqrt{ \sigma }\tau ^{a}G_{\mu \nu}D_{a}X^{\mu}\delta X^{\nu}
\end{align}$$

take a variation of the symplectic potential, we obtain the symplectic form

$$\begin{align}
\omega & =T\int _{\Sigma}\mathrm{d}\tau \sqrt{ \sigma }\tau ^{a}\nabla _{a}\delta X^{\mu}\wedge \delta X^{\nu}
\end{align}$$

where $\displaystyle{\nabla _{a}\delta X^{\mu}=D_{a}\delta X^{\mu}+\Gamma ^{\mu}_{~\rho \sigma}D_{a}X^{\rho}\delta X^{\sigma}}$ is the pullback of the covariant derivative on the target space to the worldsheet.

suppose the target space $\displaystyle{\mathcal{M}}$ has an isometry that generated by a Killing vector $\displaystyle{\xi}$, then we can write an vector of the configuration space as

$$\begin{align}
X_{\xi} & =\int \mathrm{d}^{2}\tau \sqrt{ -g }\xi ^{\mu} \frac{\delta}{\delta X^{\mu}}
\end{align}$$

act on the action with the vector $\displaystyle{X_{\xi}}$, we have

$$\begin{align}
X_{\xi}\cdot \delta S & =-\frac{T}{2}\int \mathrm{d}^{2}\tau \sqrt{ -g }\mathcal{L}_{\xi}G_{\mu \nu}D_{a}X^{\mu}D_{b}X^{\nu}g^{ab} \\
 & =0
\end{align}$$

then the corresponding Noether charge is then

$$\begin{align}
H_{\xi} & =X_{\xi}\cdot \theta \\
 & =T\int _{\Sigma}\mathrm{d}\tau \sqrt{ \sigma }\tau ^{a}\xi _{\mu}D_{a}X^{\mu} \\
 & =T\int _{\Sigma}\mathrm{d}\tau \sqrt{ \sigma }\tau ^{a}J_{a}
\end{align}$$

where $\displaystyle{J_{a}}$ is the corresponding Noether current. $\displaystyle{H_{\xi}}$ is a conserved charge, i.e., we have

$$\begin{align}
H_{\xi}|_{\Sigma _{f}}-H_{\xi}|_{\Sigma _{i}}=0
\end{align}$$

## flat target space

### translation and rotation

suppose the target space $\displaystyle{\mathcal{M}\equiv \mathbb{R}^{n}}$, and we choose $\displaystyle{\xi ^{\mu}=a^{\mu}}$, where $\displaystyle{a^{\mu}}$ is a constant vector, then the corresponding Noether current is given by

$$\begin{align}
P^{\mu}_{a} & =T\partial _{a}X^{\mu}
\end{align}$$

then

$$\begin{align}
g^{ab}\partial _{a}P_{b}^{\mu} & =T\partial ^{2}X^{\mu}=0
\end{align}$$

target space rotation generated by $\displaystyle{\xi ^{\mu}=\omega ^{\mu}_{~\nu}X^{\nu}}$, where $\displaystyle{\omega _{\mu \nu}=-\omega _{\nu \mu}}$, then the corresponding Noether current is given by

$$\begin{align}
J^{\mu \nu}_{a} & =T(X^{\mu}\partial _{a}X^{\nu}-X^{\nu}\partial _{a}X^{\mu}) \\
\implies g^{ab}\partial _{a}J_{b}^{\mu \nu} & =T(X^{\mu}\partial ^{2}X^{\nu}-X^{\nu}\partial ^{2}X^{\mu})=0
\end{align}$$

### target space Poincaré algebra

choose the conformal gauge, then the symplectic form becomes

$$\begin{align}
\omega & =T\int \mathrm{d}\sigma ^{1}\eta _{\mu \nu}\partial _{0}\delta X^{\mu}\wedge \delta X^{\nu}
\end{align}$$

the Noether charge corresponding to the translation $\displaystyle{X_{\mu}=\int \mathrm{d}^{2}\tau \frac{\delta}{\delta X^{\mu}}}$ is

$$\begin{align}
P_{\mu} & =T\int \mathrm{d}\sigma ^{1}\partial _{0}X_{\mu}
\end{align}$$

and the Noether charge corresponding to the rotation $\displaystyle{X_{\mu \nu}=\int \mathrm{d}^{2}\tau \left( X_{\mu}\frac{\delta}{\delta X^{\nu}}-X_{\nu}\frac{\delta}{\delta X^{\mu}} \right)}$ is

$$\begin{align}
J_{\mu \nu} & =T\int \mathrm{d}\sigma ^{1}(X_{\mu}\partial _{0}X_{\nu}-X_{\nu}\partial _{0}X_{\mu})
\end{align}$$

the Poisson bracket between the Noether charges can be computed by the symplectic form as

$$\begin{align}
\left\{H_{\xi},H_{\zeta}\right\} & =X_{\xi}\cdot X_{\zeta}\cdot \omega
\end{align}$$

then we obtain the Poincaré algebra

$$\begin{align}
\left\{P_{\mu},P_{\nu}\right\} & =0 \\
\left\{P_{\mu},J_{\nu \rho}\right\} & =\eta _{\mu \nu}P_{\rho}-\eta _{\mu \rho}P_{\nu} \\
\left\{J_{\mu \nu},J_{\rho \sigma}\right\} & =\eta _{\mu \rho}J_{\nu \sigma}-\eta _{\mu \sigma}J_{\nu \rho}-\eta _{\nu \rho}J_{\mu \sigma}+\eta _{\nu \sigma}J_{\mu \rho}
\end{align}$$

which is the standard Poincaré algebra.

# problem 3

under the conformal gauge, the equation of motion becomes

$$\begin{align}
\partial _{+}\partial _{-}X^{\mu} & =0
\end{align}$$

then the general solution can be written as

$$\begin{align}
X^{\mu}(\sigma) & =X^{\mu}_{L}(\sigma ^{+})+X^{\mu}_{R}(\sigma ^{-}), \quad \sigma ^{\pm}=\sigma ^{0}\pm \sigma ^{1}
\end{align}$$

consider closed string, then we have the periodic boundary condition

$$\begin{align}
X^{\mu}(\sigma ^{0},\sigma ^{1}+\ell)=X^{\mu}(\sigma ^{0},\sigma ^{1})
\end{align}$$

then we can write the general solution as Fourier expansion

$$\begin{align}
X_{L}^{\mu} & =\frac{1}{2}x^{\mu}+\frac{1}{2}\alpha ^{\prime}p^{\mu}\sigma ^{+}+i\sqrt{\frac{\alpha ^{\prime}}{2}}\sum _{n\neq 0}\frac{1}{n}\alpha _{n}^{\mu}e^{-in\sigma ^{+}} \\
X_{R}^{\mu} & =\frac{1}{2}x^{\mu}+\frac{1}{2}\alpha ^{\prime}p^{\mu}\sigma ^{-}+i\sqrt{\frac{\alpha ^{\prime}}{2}}\sum _{n\neq 0}\frac{1}{n}\tilde{\alpha} _{n}^{\mu}e^{-in\sigma ^{-}}
\end{align}$$

where $\displaystyle{\alpha ^{\prime}=\frac{1}{2\pi T}}$ is the Regge slope parameter. then the whole solution can be written as

$$\begin{align}
X^{\mu}(\sigma) & =x^{\mu}+\alpha ^{\prime}p^{\mu}\sigma ^{0}+i\sqrt{\frac{\alpha ^{\prime}}{2}}\sum _{n\neq 0}\frac{1}{n}(\alpha _{n}^{\mu}e^{-in\sigma ^{+}}+\tilde{\alpha} _{n}^{\mu}e^{-in\sigma ^{-}})
\end{align}$$

zero mode corresponds to $\displaystyle{x^{\mu}+\alpha'p^{\mu}\sigma ^{0}}$, and oscillation modes corresponds to the Fourier modes $\displaystyle{\alpha _{n}^{\mu}}$ and $\displaystyle{\tilde{\alpha} _{n}^{\mu}}$.
