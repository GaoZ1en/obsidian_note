# problem

Assume that there are three generations of neutrinos and that each neutrino is its own antiparticle. In the hot Big Bang cosmological model, after neutrino decoupling, the cosmic neutrino background (CNB) is formed in the universe.

1. Calculate the temperature at which neutrinos decouple.
1. Given that the present temperature of the cosmic background photons is $\displaystyle{2.73\mathrm{K}}$, find the present temperature of the cosmic background neutrinos.

## solution

Neutrinos decouple when the weak interaction rate becomes comparable to the Hubble expansion rate,

$$\begin{align}
\Gamma_\nu & \sim H .
\end{align}$$

For relativistic neutrinos, the weak interaction rate is estimated by

$$\begin{align}
\Gamma_\nu & \sim n\langle\sigma v\rangle \sim G_F^2T^5 .
\end{align}$$

During radiation domination,

$$\begin{align}
H & =1.66\sqrt{g_*}\frac{T^2}{M_{\mathrm{Pl}}}.
\end{align}$$

At neutrino decoupling, photons, electrons, positrons, and three active neutrino species are relativistic, so

$$\begin{align}
g_* & =2+\frac{7}{8}(4+6)=\frac{43}{4}=10.75 .
\end{align}$$

Thus

$$\begin{align}
G_F^2T_{\mathrm{dec}}^5 & \sim 1.66\sqrt{g_*}\frac{T_{\mathrm{dec}}^2}{M_{\mathrm{Pl}}}, \\
T_{\mathrm{dec}} & \sim \left(\frac{1.66\sqrt{g_*}}{G_F^2M_{\mathrm{Pl}}}\right)^{1/3}.
\end{align}$$

Using

$$\begin{align}
G_F & =1.166\times 10^{-5}\mathrm{GeV}^{-2}, & M_{\mathrm{Pl}} & =1.22\times 10^{19}\mathrm{GeV},
\end{align}$$

we obtain

$$\begin{align}
T_{\mathrm{dec}} & \sim 1.5\mathrm{MeV}.
\end{align}$$

Therefore the neutrino decoupling temperature is of order

$$\begin{align}
T_{\mathrm{dec}} & \sim 1\mathrm{MeV}.
\end{align}$$

After neutrino decoupling, electron-positron annihilation heats the photon bath but not the decoupled neutrinos. Entropy conservation in the photon-electron-positron sector gives

$$\begin{align}
\frac{T_{\nu,0}}{T_{\gamma,0}} & =\left(\frac{4}{11}\right)^{1/3}.
\end{align}$$

Hence

$$\begin{align}
T_{\nu,0} & =\left(\frac{4}{11}\right)^{1/3}T_{\gamma,0} \\
& =\left(\frac{4}{11}\right)^{1/3}(2.73\mathrm{K}) \\
& \simeq 1.95\mathrm{K}.
\end{align}$$
