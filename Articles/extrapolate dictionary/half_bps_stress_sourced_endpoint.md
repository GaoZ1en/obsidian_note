# Sourced FG tails and the Einstein covariant endpoint pairing

## Result and scope

This note closes the **tail–tail infrared endpoint calculation** left open
in [the causal source bridge](half_bps_stress_causal_source_bridge.md).
Two sourced relative FG perturbations may each grow as $u^2$. Nevertheless,
their Einstein covariant symplectic pairing, integrated over a spatial
slice before the radial limit, has an integrable $u^{-3}$ tail. The radial
flux through $u=U$ is $O(U^{-2})$ on every bounded time interval.

The result is an exact bilinear identity about the linearized response.
It also applies to the complete response of smooth compact spacetime
sources with compact radial support, because the radiative causal response then
vanishes sufficiently far down the radial direction at any fixed finite
time. It does not assert a bound uniform in the spacetime switching scale,
nor a nonlinear global Einstein phase space, nor a common domain for
interacting bulk operators.

**Verified:** direct variation of the five-dimensional Einstein potential;
all coefficients of the general spatially integrated tail pairing; an
independent Gaussian spatial example. Twelve exact symbolic residuals
vanish in [the executable checks](checks/dictionary_extensions/cps_tail_checks.py)
and [their actual output](checks/dictionary_extensions/cps_tail_checks_results.json).

**Assumptions:** the fixed Einstein covariant potential below; spatially
Schwartz tail coefficients with locally bounded smooth time derivatives;
fixed bounded time intervals; the existing ultraviolet BF prescription.

**Not verified:** estimates uniform as the radial/spacetime source supports
grow; a nonlinear solution manifold; all interacting matter contributions
to a conserved total symplectic form; the quantum operator domain.

## 1. Current derived from the action

Set $\ell=1$ temporarily and write

\[
g=u^{-2}(du^2+\eta_{ij}dX^idX^j),\qquad
\eta=\operatorname{diag}(-1,1,1,1),\qquad
\delta g_{u\mu}=0,\quad \delta g_{ij}=u^{-2}H_{ij}.
\tag{E1}
\]

Use the Einstein potential, suppressing $1/(2\kappa^2)$,

\[
\theta^\mu(g;h)=\sqrt{-g}
   (\nabla_\nu h^{\mu\nu}-\nabla^\mu h),\qquad
\omega^\mu=\delta_1\theta^\mu(g;h_2)
               -\delta_2\theta^\mu(g;h_1).
\tag{E2}
\]

This specifies the potential convention; no additional corner improvement
is made. Let $\tau_a=\eta^{ij}H_{a,ij}$ and
$D_a^i=\partial_jH_a^{ij}$, with every index on $H$ raised by $\eta$.
Direct variation gives

\[
\omega^u=\frac1{2u^3}
  \left(H_1^{ij}H'_{2,ij}-H_2^{ij}H'_{1,ij}
        -\tau_1\tau'_2+\tau_2\tau'_1\right),
\tag{E3}
\]

and

\[
\begin{split}
u^3\omega^i={}&\frac12
 [\tau_1(D_2^i-\partial^i\tau_2)
   -\tau_2(D_1^i-\partial^i\tau_1)]\\
 &+H_2^{jk}\delta\Gamma_1^i{}_{jk}
      -H_1^{jk}\delta\Gamma_2^i{}_{jk}
 +\frac12(H_1^{ij}\partial_j\tau_2-H_2^{ij}\partial_j\tau_1),
\end{split}
\tag{E4}
\]

where

\[
\delta\Gamma_a^i{}_{jk}
=\tfrac12\eta^{i\ell}
 (\partial_jH_{a,k\ell}+\partial_kH_{a,j\ell}
                       -\partial_\ell H_{a,jk}).
\tag{E5}
\]

Equations (E3)–(E5) follow by varying the inverse metrics, connection and
volume density in (E2). In particular, $\delta h_2=-h_1^{\mu\nu}h_{2,\mu\nu}$
and $\delta\sqrt{-g}=\sqrt{-g}\,h_1/2$ must be included. The executable
calculation first performs this full five-dimensional variation and
compares all five components with (E3)–(E5). Independent arbitrary
coefficients for $H$ and $H'$ suffice because the current is bilinear and
first order in derivatives. Restoring units multiplies (E3)–(E4) by
$\ell^3/(2\kappa^2)$.

## 2. General exterior response

Outside the compact radial support of the completed source, the
non-radiative FG part has the form

\[
H_{a,ij}=A_{a,ij}+u^2B_{a,ij},\qquad
A_{a,ij}=\partial_i v_{a,j}+\partial_j v_{a,i}
                         -2\sigma_a\eta_{ij},\qquad
B_{a,ij}=-\partial_i\partial_j\sigma_a.
\tag{E6}
\]

It is the relative metric generated by
$\xi_a^u=u\sigma_a$ and
$\xi_a^i=v_a^i-u^2\partial^i\sigma_a/2$.
The exterior expression is locally a gauge variation. This observation
alone does not declare that vector a null direction of a global phase
space; endpoint pairings must first be calculated.

Substitution into (E3) immediately gives

\[
\omega^u=u^{-2}\mathcal V(X),\qquad
\mathcal V=A_1:B_2-A_2:B_1
 -\operatorname{tr}A_1\operatorname{tr}B_2
 +\operatorname{tr}A_2\operatorname{tr}B_1.
\tag{E7}
\]

The $B_1:B_2$ and trace products cancel pointwise. For (E4), the decisive
stronger identity is

\[
\boxed{\int_{\mathbb R^3}\omega^0(t,\mathbf x,u)\,d^3x
            =u^{-3}\mathcal C(t).}
\tag{E8}
\]

Both apparent $u$ and $u^{-1}$ terms vanish after the spatial integration.
With $v_{a,0}$ a **lower** time component and
$\Delta=\sum_{b=1}^3\partial_b^2$, the remaining coefficient is

\[
\begin{split}
\mathcal C(t)=4\int d^3x\,[&
\sigma_1\Delta v_{2,0}-\sigma_2\Delta v_{1,0}
 +\dot\sigma_1\partial_bv_2^b
 -\dot\sigma_2\partial_bv_1^b\\
 &+3(\sigma_1\dot\sigma_2-\sigma_2\dot\sigma_1)].
\end{split}
\tag{E9}
\]

### Proof of the spatial cancellation

One may expand (E4) and integrate the spatial derivatives by parts.
The following polynomial calculation supplies a shorter exhaustive proof.
Spatial integration pairs momenta $\mathbf k$ and $-\mathbf k$.
Rotational covariance permits $\mathbf k=(k,0,0)$; time frequencies
$\omega_1,\omega_2$ remain independent. Use

\[
K_1=(-\omega_1,k,0,0),\quad K_2=(-\omega_2,-k,0,0),\quad
A_{a,ij}=i(K_{a,i}v_{a,j}+K_{a,j}v_{a,i})-2\sigma_a\eta_{ij},\quad
B_{a,ij}=K_{a,i}K_{a,j}\sigma_a.
\tag{E10}
\]

All four components of each $v_a$ are arbitrary. The coefficients of
$u^4$ and $u^2$ in $u^3\omega^0$ are identically zero. Its constant term is

\[
4[-k^2\sigma_1v_{2,0}+k^2\sigma_2v_{1,0}
-k\omega_1\sigma_1v_{2,1}-k\omega_2\sigma_2v_{1,1}
+3i(\omega_1-\omega_2)\sigma_1\sigma_2].
\tag{E11}
\]

Fourier inversion gives exactly (E9). This is a polynomial identity in
independent derivative symbols, so a temporal Fourier transform of the
actual coefficients is not needed: it holds for arbitrary smooth time
dependence. The identity at $k=0$ follows by polynomial continuity. Spatial
Schwartz regularity justifies the integration by parts or Parseval pairing.

The same expansion of (E7) yields

\[
\int d^3x\,\mathcal V=\tfrac12\dot{\mathcal C}.
\tag{E12}
\]

Consequently $\partial_t\int\omega^0+\partial_u\int\omega^u=0$,
as required for two vacuum linearized tails. This checks the relative
normalization and sign of (E3) and (E4).

## 3. Endpoint theorem

Assume $v_a,\sigma_a\in C^\infty(I;\mathcal S(\mathbb R^3))$ on a
bounded time interval $I$, and (E6) holds for $u\ge L$. Then

\[
\int_L^\infty du\int d^3x\,\omega^0
       =\frac{\mathcal C(t)}{2L^2},\qquad
\int_{t_0}^{t_1}dt\int d^3x\,\omega^u(U)
       =\frac{\mathcal C(t_1)-\mathcal C(t_0)}{2U^2}.
\tag{E13}
\]

These are exact equations in the convention (E2), with the common
$\ell^3/(2\kappa^2)$ restored if desired. They prove convergence of the
radial pairing **after** the spatial pairing and zero infrared flux.
They do not claim absolute convergence of $|\omega^0|$ over all space and
radius: the canceled coefficients need not vanish pointwise.

For smooth completed sources supported in $u\le L_s$ and in a compact
spacetime set with earliest time $t_*$, the causal radiative response is
zero when $u>L_s+t-t_*$. This is finite propagation for the radial
hyperbolic Einstein TT operator; applying a boundary retarded projector
does not enlarge its radial causal cone. The remaining response is (E6).
Its coefficients are spatially Schwartz at finite time for smooth compact
sources. Thus (E13) controls the endpoint of the **complete pair of those
linearized sourced responses**, for each fixed source support and finite
time interval. The interior pairing remains the usual finite-interval
one from the causal source bridge.

For the mass-gapped smooth free wave packets in that bridge, the mixed
free–tail pairing has the previously established rapid radial decay.
Together these results control the new tail–tail part on the corresponding
linearized test class. They do not impose a false decay condition on the
individual FG metric components.

## 4. What this does and does not finish

The earlier sourced–sourced endpoint question has a positive answer for
the specified linearized, fixed-source-support class. The response
coefficients $v,\sigma$ and hence $\mathcal C$ depend on the source support.
Equation (E13) gives no estimate uniform as $B,R\to\infty$ unless those
coefficient seminorms are controlled separately.

A sourced Einstein response is not, by itself, a tangent to the vacuum
Einstein solution space during the active interaction. In the interior,
the divergence of the Einstein current contains the source pairing.
The full coupled matter current is needed for conservation on an
interacting solution space. Higher metric variations, the endpoint
embedding and the ultraviolet counterterm current must also be included
at their relevant orders. None is supplied by calling the exterior vector
in (E6) pure gauge.

Accordingly this theorem removes an explicit linearized endpoint gap. It
does not convert the coefficient theorem into a complete interacting
Einstein CPS or a Hilbert-space operator theorem.
