here we will consider a scalar field in flat spacetime with the presence of background electric field. first we will consider the Coulomb potential generated by a point charge $\displaystyle{+Ze}$ located at the origin. the four potential is given by

$$\begin{align}
A^{\mu} & =\left(-\frac{Z\alpha}{r},0,0,0\right)
\end{align}$$

where $\displaystyle{\alpha}$ is the fine structure constant. the action of the scalar is

$$\begin{align}
S & =-\int \mathrm{d}^{4}x(D_{\mu}\phi (D^{\mu}\phi)^{*}+\mu^{2}\phi ^{*}\phi) \\
D_{\mu} & =\partial _{\mu}-iA_{\mu}
\end{align}$$

take a variation, we have

$$\begin{align}
\delta S & =\int _{\mathcal{M}}\mathrm{d}^{4}x(E_{\phi}\delta \phi ^{*}+E_{\phi ^{*}}\delta \phi)+\theta|_{\Sigma _{f}-\Sigma _{i}} \\
E_{\phi} & =D^{2}\phi-\mu^{2}\phi \\
E_{\phi ^{*}} & =(D^{2}\phi)^{*}-\mu^{2}\phi ^{*} \\
\theta & =\int _{\Sigma}\mathrm{d}^{3}x\tau ^{\mu}(D_{\mu}\phi \delta \phi ^{*}+(D_{\mu}\phi)^{*}\delta \phi)
\end{align}$$

the symplectic form is obtained by taking a variation of the symplectic potential

$$\begin{align}
\omega & =\int _{\Sigma}\mathrm{d}^{3}x\tau ^{\mu}(D_{\mu}\delta\phi \wedge \delta \phi ^{*}+(D_{\mu}\delta \phi)^{*}\wedge \delta \phi)
\end{align}$$

expand the eom for $\displaystyle{\phi}$, we have

$$\begin{align}
\left( \left( i\partial _{t}+\frac{Z\alpha}{r} \right)^{2}+\nabla ^{2}-\mu^{2} \right)\phi & =0
\end{align}$$

make the ansatz

$$\begin{align}
\phi(x) & =e^{-iEt} \frac{u(r)}{r} Y_{lm}(\theta,\phi)
\end{align}$$

the eom becomes

$$\begin{align}
u''+\left( m^{2}-E^{2}-\frac{2EZ\alpha}{r}-\frac{\ell(\ell+1)+Z^{2}\alpha ^{2}}{r^{2}} \right)u(r) & =0
\end{align}$$

when $\displaystyle{r\to \infty}$, we require the field $\displaystyle{\phi}$ converge, $\displaystyle{u\sim e^{-\beta r}}$, where $\displaystyle{\beta=\sqrt{ m^{2}-E^{2} }}$. when $\displaystyle{r\to 0}$, we require the field $\displaystyle{\phi}$ canonical, $\displaystyle{u\sim r^{\lambda+1}}$, where the effective angular quantum number $\displaystyle{\lambda=-\frac{1}{2}+\sqrt{ \left( \ell+\frac{1}{2} \right)^{2}-Z^{2}\alpha ^{2} }}$. let

$$\begin{align}
u(r) & =r^{\lambda+1}e^{-\beta r}v(r) \\
\rho & =2\beta r
\end{align}$$

the equation that $\displaystyle{v}$ satisfies is

$$\begin{align}
\rho v''(\rho)+(2(\lambda+1)-\rho) v'(\rho)+\left( \frac{EZ\alpha}{\beta}-(\lambda+1) \right)v(\rho)=0
\end{align}$$

this is the standard form of confluent hypergeometric equation with $\displaystyle{a=(\lambda+1)-\frac{EZ\alpha}{\beta},b=2(\lambda+1)}$. the general solution that satisfies boundary condition is

$$\begin{align}
v(\rho) & =F(a,b,\rho) \\
a & =-n_{r},n_{r}\in \mathbb{N}
\end{align}$$

$$\begin{align}
(n_{r}+\lambda+1)^{2}m^{2} & =E^{2}(Z^{2}\alpha ^{2}+(n_{r}+\lambda +1)^{2}) \\
\implies E & =m \left( 1+\frac{Z^{2}\alpha ^{2}}{(n_{r}+\lambda+1)^{2}} \right)^{-1/2}
\end{align}$$

and the whole solution is given by

$$\begin{align}
\phi _{n_{r}lm} & =C_{n_{r}lm}e^{-iE_{n_{r}\ell}t}r^{\lambda}e^{-\beta r}F(-n_{r},2(\lambda+1),2\beta r)Y_{\ell m}(\theta,\phi) \\
E_{n_{r}\ell} & =m \left( 1+\frac{Z^{2}\alpha ^{2}}{(n_{r}+\lambda+1)^{2}} \right)^{-1/2} \\
\beta _{n_{r}\ell} & =\frac{mZ\alpha}{\sqrt{ Z^{2}\alpha ^{2}+(n_{r}+\lambda+1)^{2} }} \\
\lambda & =-\frac{1}{2}+\sqrt{ \left( \ell+\frac{1}{2} \right)^{2}-Z^{2}\alpha ^{2} }
\end{align}$$

where the normalization constant is determined by

$$\begin{align}
\omega[\phi _{n_{r1}\ell _{1}m_{1}},\phi _{n_{r 2}\ell_{2}m_{2}}^{*}] & =-i\delta _{n_{r1}n_{r 2}}\delta _{\ell_{1}\ell_{2}}\delta _{m_{1}m_{2}} \\
\implies C_{n_{r}\ell m} & =?
\end{align}$$

the general solution can be written as

$$\begin{align}
\phi(x) & =\sum _{n_{r}=0}^{\infty}\sum _{\ell=0}^{\infty}\sum _{m=-\ell}^{\ell}(a_{n_{r}\ell m}\phi _{n_{r}\ell m}+b^{\dagger}_{n_{r}\ell m}\phi ^{*}_{n_{r}\ell m})
\end{align}$$

under such parametrization of the solution space--covariant phase space, the symplectic form becomes

$$\begin{align}
\omega & =\int _{\Sigma}\mathrm{d}^{3}x\tau ^{\mu}(D_{\mu}\delta\phi \wedge \delta \phi ^{*}+(D_{\mu}\delta \phi)^{*}\wedge \delta \phi) \\
 & = i\sum _{n_{r}=0}^{\infty}\sum _{\ell=0}^{\infty}\sum ^{\ell}_{m=-\ell}(\delta a^{\dagger}_{n_{r}\ell m}\wedge \delta a_{n_{r}\ell m}-\delta b^{\dagger}_{n_{r}\ell m}\wedge \delta b_{n_{r}\ell m})
\end{align}$$

for an observable $\displaystyle{\mathcal{O}}$, the Hamiltonian vector field $\displaystyle{X_{\mathcal{O}}}$ is defined by

$$\begin{align}
X_{\mathcal{O}} & =\sum _{n_{r}=0}^{\infty}\sum _{\ell=0}^{\infty}\sum ^{\ell}_{m=-\ell}\left( \frac{\partial \mathcal{O}}{\partial a^{\dagger}_{n_{r}\ell m}}\frac{\partial }{\partial a_{n_{r}\ell m}}-\frac{\partial \mathcal{O}}{\partial a_{n_{r}\ell m}}\frac{\partial }{\partial a^{\dagger}_{n_{r}\ell m}}+\frac{\partial \mathcal{O}}{\partial b^{\dagger}_{n_{r}\ell m}}\frac{\partial }{\partial b_{n_{r}\ell m}}-\frac{\partial \mathcal{O}}{\partial b_{n_{r}\ell m}}\frac{\partial }{\partial b^{\dagger}_{n_{r}\ell m}} \right)
\end{align}$$

then the Poisson bracket between two observables $\displaystyle{\mathcal{O}_{1},\mathcal{O}_{2}}$ is

$$\begin{align}
\left\{\mathcal{O}_{1},\mathcal{O}_{2}\right\} & =X_{\mathcal{O}_{1}}\cdot X_{\mathcal{O}_{2}}\cdot \omega
\end{align}$$

in particular, the non-vanishing Poisson brackets between the mode coefficients are

$$\begin{align}
\left\{ a_{n_{r1}\ell_{1}m_{1}},a^{\dagger}_{n_{r2}\ell_{2}m_{2}} \right\} & =-i\delta _{n_{r1}n_{r2}}\delta _{\ell_{1}\ell_{2}}\delta _{m_{1}m_{2}} \\
\left\{ b_{n_{r1}\ell_{1}m_{1}},b^{\dagger}_{n_{r2}\ell_{2}m_{2}} \right\} & =-i\delta _{n_{r1}n_{r2}}\delta _{\ell_{1}\ell_{2}}\delta _{m_{1}m_{2}}
\end{align}$$

after quantization, the Poisson brackets become commutation relations

$$\begin{align}
\left[ a_{n_{r1}\ell_{1}m_{1}},a^{\dagger}_{n_{r2}\ell_{2}m_{2}} \right] & =\delta _{n_{r1}n_{r2}}\delta _{\ell_{1}\ell_{2}}\delta _{m_{1}m_{2}} \\
\left[ b_{n_{r1}\ell_{1}m_{1}},b^{\dagger}_{n_{r2}\ell_{2}m_{2}} \right] & =\delta _{n_{r1}n_{r2}}\delta _{\ell_{1}\ell_{2}}\delta _{m_{1}m_{2}}
\end{align}$$

the vaccum state $\displaystyle{\ket{\Omega}}$ is defined by

$$\begin{align}
a_{n_{r}\ell m}\ket{\Omega}  & =\Omega \\
b_{n_{r}\ell m}\ket{\Omega}  & =\Omega, \forall n_{r},\ell,m
\end{align}$$

it should be noted that the vacuum defined here is different from the usual Minkowski vacuum due to the presence of background electric field. the excited states are constructed by acting the creation operators on the vacuum state. the Fock space is spanned by the basis states

$$\begin{align}
\ket{n_{a};n_{b}} & =\prod _{n_{r},\ell,m}\frac{(a^{\dagger}_{n_{r}\ell m})^{n_{a,n_{r}\ell m}}}{\sqrt{ n_{a,n_{r}\ell m}! }}\frac{(b^{\dagger}_{n_{r}\ell m})^{n_{b,n_{r}\ell m}}}{\sqrt{ n_{b,n_{r}\ell m}! }}\ket{0}
\end{align}$$

we should notice that the particle here is different from the usual free scalar particle, as it is influenced by the background electric field.

then we will consider a constant background electric field $\displaystyle{E}$ in the $\displaystyle{\hat{n}}$ direction. the eom for the scalar field is

$$\begin{align}
(-\partial _{t}^{2}-(i\nabla-e\vec{A})^{2}-m^{2})\phi & =0
\end{align}$$

where $\displaystyle{\vec{A}=-Et\hat{n}}$. make the ansatz

$$\begin{align}
\phi(x) & =e^{i\vec{k}\cdot \vec{x}}f(t)
\end{align}$$

then the equation for $\displaystyle{f(t)}$ gives

$$\begin{align}
\partial _{t}^{2}(t)+k _{\perp}^{2}+(k _{\parallel}+eEt)^{2}+m^{2} & =0
\end{align}$$

define $\displaystyle{\tau=\sqrt{ \frac{eE}{2} }\left( t+\frac{k_{\parallel}}{eE} \right)}$, the equation becomes

$$\begin{align}
\partial _{\tau}^{2}f+\left( \nu+\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\tau ^{2}}{4} \right)f & =0
\end{align}$$

where $\displaystyle{\nu=\frac{i(k _{\perp}^{2}+m^{2})}{2eE}}$. this is the standard form of parabolic cylinder equation, the general solution is given by

$$\begin{align}
f(\tau) & =C_{1}D_{\nu}(\tau)+C_{2}D_{\nu}(-\tau)
\end{align}$$

the asymptotic behavior of the parabolic cylinder function is

$$\begin{align}
D_{\nu}(\tau) & \sim e^{-\frac{\tau ^{2}}{4}}\tau ^{\nu},\tau \to +\infty \\
\end{align}$$

and we should keep $\displaystyle{D_{\nu}(\tau)}$ to ensure the convergence when $\displaystyle{t\to +\infty}$. the solution that satisfies the boundary condition is

$$\begin{align}
\phi _{k}(x) & =C_{k} e^{i\vec{k}\cdot \vec{x}}D_{\nu}(\tau)
\end{align}$$

the normalization constant is determined by

$$\begin{align}
\omega[\phi _{\vec{k}_{1}},\phi ^{*} _{\vec{k}_{2}}] & =-i\delta ^{3}(\vec{k}_{1}-\vec{k}_{2})
\end{align}$$

the general solution can be written as

$$\begin{align}
\phi(x) & =\int \mathrm{d}^{3}\vec{k} (a_{\vec{k}}\phi _{\vec{k}}+b^{\dagger}_{\vec{k}}\phi ^{*}_{\vec{k}})
\end{align}$$

under such parametrization of the solution space--covariant phase space, the symplectic form becomes

$$\begin{align}
\omega & =\int _{\Sigma}\mathrm{d}^{3}x\tau ^{\mu}(D_{\mu}\delta\phi \wedge \delta \phi ^{*}+(D_{\mu}\delta \phi)^{*}\wedge \delta \phi) \\
 & = i\int \mathrm{d}^{3}\vec{k}(\delta a^{\dagger}_{\vec{k}}\wedge \delta a_{\vec{k}}-\delta b^{\dagger}_{\vec{k}}\wedge \delta b_{\vec{k}})
\end{align}$$
