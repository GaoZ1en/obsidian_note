the metric of $\displaystyle{\mathrm{AdS}_{n}}$ reads

$$\tag{1.1}
\begin{align}
\mathrm{d}s^{2} & =-(1+r^{2})\mathrm{d}t^{2}+\frac{1}{1+r^{2}}\mathrm{d}r^{2}+r^{2}\mathrm{d}\Omega ^{2}_{n-2}
\end{align}
$$

where $\displaystyle{\mathrm{d}\Omega _{n-2}^{2}}$ is the metric on the unit sphere $\displaystyle{S^{n-2}}$. the whole space $\displaystyle{\mathrm{AdS}_{n}}$ can be decomposed into a space $\displaystyle{N^{2}}$ with coordinates $\displaystyle{x^{a}=(t,r)}$ and $\displaystyle{S^{n-2}}$ with coordinates $\displaystyle{x^{i}=(\theta ^{1},\dots,\theta ^{n-2})}$, and vector $\displaystyle{A_{\mu}}$ in $\displaystyle{\mathrm{AdS}_{n}}$ can be decomposed into components that are orthogonal or parallel to $\displaystyle{S^{n-2}}$,

$$\tag{1.2}
\begin{align}
A_{\mu}\mathrm{d}x^{\mu} & =A_{a}\mathrm{d}x^{a}+A_{i}\mathrm{d}x^{i}
\end{align}
$$

via Helmholtz-Hodge decomposition, we can further decompose $\displaystyle{A_{i}}$ into a divergence-free part and a curl-free part, i.e.,

$$\tag{1.3}
\begin{align}
A_{i} & =A_{i}^{(v)}+\tilde{\nabla} _{i}A^{(s)}
\end{align}
$$

with

$$\tag{1.4}
\begin{align}
\nabla _{i}A^{(v)i} & =0
\end{align}
$$

the scalar-type components of $\displaystyle{A_{\mu}}$, $\displaystyle{A_{a}}$ and $\displaystyle{A^{(s)}}$ can be expanded as

$$\tag{1.5}
\begin{align}
A_{a} & =\psi _{\ell \mathbf{m}}Y_{\ell \mathbf{m}}(x^{i}) \\
A^{(s)} & =\phi _{\ell \mathbf{m}}Y_{\ell \mathbf{m}}(x^{i})
\end{align}
$$

here $\displaystyle{Y_{\ell \mathbf{m}}(x^{i})}$ is the spherical harmonics on $\displaystyle{S^{n-2}}$, with eigenvalue equation and orthonormalization condition

$$\tag{1.6}
\begin{align}
\tilde{\Delta}_{S^{n-2}}Y_{\ell \mathbf{m}} & =-\ell(\ell+n-3)Y_{\ell \mathbf{m}} \\
\int \mathrm{d}\sigma _{S^{n-2}}
\end{align}
$$

and vector-type component of $\displaystyle{A_{\mu}}$, $\displaystyle{A_{i}^{(v)}}$ can be expanded as

$$\tag{1.6}
\begin{align}
A_{i}^{(v)} & =\chi _{\ell \mathbf{m}}Y^{(v)}_{\ell \mathbf{m},i}(x^{i})
\end{align}
$$
