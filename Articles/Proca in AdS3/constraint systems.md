# Constraint Systems: First- and Second-Class Constraints & Geometry

This note provides a compact, notation-consistent synthesis of the formalism of constrained Hamiltonian systems, focusing on: (i) classification of constraints, (ii) construction of the total / extended Hamiltonians, (iii) Dirac brackets, (iv) gauge invariance and observables, (v) geometric structure (degeneracy and reduction). No detailed proofs are included; statements follow standard treatments (Dirac; Henneaux–Teitelboim).

## 1. Setup and Notation

Configuration variables: $q^i$, momenta: $p_i = \partial L / \partial \dot q^i$, with $i=1,\dots,N$. Phase space dimension: $2N$. Poisson bracket:
$$
[F,G] = \frac{\partial F}{\partial q^i}\frac{\partial G}{\partial p_i} - \frac{\partial F}{\partial p_i}\frac{\partial G}{\partial q^i}.
$$
We write weak equality (valid on the constraint surface) as $\approx$. Primary constraints arise when the Hessian $\partial^2 L / \partial \dot q^i \partial \dot q^j$ is not invertible.

Constraints are denoted:
$$
\phi_m(q,p) = 0,\qquad m=1,\dots,M \quad (\text{primary}).
$$
All constraints (primary + secondary + higher) will be written uniformly as:
$$
\phi_j = 0,\qquad j=1,\dots,J.
$$
We assume regularity: locally independent constraints of constant rank (no bifurcations of rank on the constraint surface $\Sigma$).

## 2. Consistency Algorithm

Canonical Hamiltonian: $H_c = p_i \dot q^i - L$ (expressed in $(q,p)$). The most general primary Hamiltonian is:
$$
H_P = H_c + \lambda^m \phi_m.
$$
Preserve constraints in time:
$$
\dot \phi_m = [\phi_m,H_c] + \lambda^{m'} [\phi_m,\phi_{m'}] \approx 0.
$$
Outcomes per step: (a) fixes some multipliers $\lambda^m$; (b) yields new independent constraints (secondary, tertiary, ...); (c) produces an identity. Iterate until closure. Collect all constraints $\phi_j=0$.

Solve linear system schematically:
$$
[\phi_j,\phi_m] \lambda^m + [\phi_j,H_c] \approx 0.
$$
General solution splits into a particular part plus kernel directions:
$$
\lambda^m = U^m + v^a V_a^{~m}.
$$
Corresponding linear combinations $\phi_a = V_a^{~m} \phi_m$ have vanishing Poisson bracket with all $\phi_j$ on $\Sigma$; they are first-class. Define
$$
H' = H_c + U^m \phi_m, \qquad H_T = H' + v^a \phi_a.
$$

## 3. Classification of Constraints

Let the full set split into first- and second-class subsets:
$$
[\phi_j] = [\gamma_a] \cup [\chi_\alpha],
$$
with indices: $a=1,\dots,f$, $\alpha=1,\dots,s$, so $J = f+s$.

Definitions:
- First-class: $[\gamma_a, \phi_j] \approx f_{aj}{}^{k} \phi_k$.
- Second-class: Poisson matrix $C_{\alpha\beta} = [\chi_\alpha, \chi_\beta]$ is invertible on $\Sigma$.

Dirac conjecture (adopted here): All first-class constraints generate gauge transformations. (Known exceptions exist but are ignored for this formal development.)

## 4. Extended Hamiltonian and Gauge Generators

Extended Hamiltonian (include all first-class constraints with arbitrary multipliers):
$$
H_E = H' + u^a \gamma_a.
$$
Time evolution of any function $F$:
$$
\dot F \approx [F, H_E].
$$
Gauge variation (infinitesimal parameters $\varepsilon^a$):
$$
\delta F = \varepsilon^a [F, \gamma_a].
$$
First-class constraints are stable under Poisson brackets: $[ \gamma_a, \gamma_b ] \approx C_{ab}{}^{c} \gamma_c$.

## 5. Dirac Bracket

For the second-class subset $\chi_\alpha$, let $C_{\alpha\beta} = [\chi_\alpha, \chi_\beta]$ and $C^{\alpha\beta}$ its inverse. The Dirac bracket is defined by:
$$
[F,G]_D = [F,G] - [F,\chi_\alpha] C^{\alpha\beta} [\chi_\beta, G].
$$
Properties (on $\Sigma$):
1. $[F,\chi_\alpha]_D = 0$ (thus second-class constraints can be imposed strongly after replacing Poisson by Dirac bracket).
2. If $F,G$ are first-class (i.e. Poisson commute with $\chi_\alpha$ weakly) then $[F,G]_D \approx [F,G]$.
3. Dirac bracket satisfies antisymmetry, bilinearity and Jacobi identity.

## 6. Gauge Fixing

Choose gauge conditions $C_b(q,p) \approx 0$, $b=1,\dots,f$, equal in number to first-class constraints and such that the matrix $[C_b, \gamma_a]$ is invertible:
$$
\det [ C_b, \gamma_a ] \neq 0.
$$
Then the combined set $[C_b, \gamma_a]$ is second-class and one can use the Dirac bracket relative to this enlarged set to compute physical evolution. Global obstructions (Gribov copies) may prevent a globally unique intersection; this does not affect local formal manipulations.

## 7. Observables

An observable (gauge-invariant function) $F$ is a smooth function on $\Sigma$ satisfying
$$
[F, \gamma_a]_D \approx 0.
$$
Equivalently, its value is constant along gauge orbits generated by $\gamma_a$. Functions vanishing on $\Sigma$ form an ideal $\mathcal N$ (pointwise product). Observables form the largest subalgebra $\mathcal O \subset C^\infty(P)$ for which $\mathcal N$ is an ideal under $[\ ,\ ]_D$.

## 8. Counting Degrees of Freedom

Let $f$ be the number of first-class constraints and $s$ the number of second-class constraints. The dimension of the reduced (physical) phase space is:
$$
\dim \Gamma_{\text{phys}} = 2N - 2f - s.
$$
Hence the number of configuration-space physical degrees of freedom:
$$
N_{\text{phys}} = N - f - \tfrac{s}{2}.
$$
Interpretation: each first-class constraint removes one canonical pair direction plus one gauge direction; each second-class constraint removes half a canonical pair.

## 9. Geometry of the Constraint Surface

Let $x^\Lambda = (q^i,p_i)$, $\Lambda=1,\dots,2N$. The canonical symplectic 2-form has components $\sigma_{\Lambda\Mu} = [x^\Lambda, x^\Mu]$. Restrict to the constraint surface embedding $x^\Lambda = x^\Lambda(y^r)$, $r=1,\dots,2N-J$, to get the induced (pullback) 2-form:
$$
\sigma_{rs} = \sigma_{\Lambda\Mu} \frac{\partial x^\Lambda}{\partial y^r} \frac{\partial x^\Mu}{\partial y^s}.
$$
For a purely first-class set of $M=f$ constraints, $\sigma_{rs}$ is maximally degenerate with
$$
\operatorname{rank}\sigma_{rs} = 2N - 2f.
$$
Null directions are generated by Hamiltonian vector fields of $\gamma_a$:
$$
X_a^\Lambda = \sigma^{\Lambda\Mu} \partial_\Mu \gamma_a, \qquad X_a(F)=[F,\gamma_a].
$$
Gauge orbits are integral manifolds of the distribution spanned by $X_a$. The reduced phase space $\Gamma_{\text{red}} = \Sigma/\!\sim$ (quotient by gauge orbits) inherits a non-degenerate symplectic form.

For a purely second-class set (\(s=J\)), the restriction of the symplectic form is non-degenerate and one may equivalently enforce constraints strongly and work with the Dirac bracket.

## 10. Summary of Core Relations

| Concept | Relation |
|---------|----------|
| Total Hamiltonian | $H_T = H' + v^a \gamma_a$ |
| Extended Hamiltonian | $H_E = H' + u^a \gamma_a$ |
| First-class closure | $[\gamma_a,\gamma_b] \approx C_{ab}{}^{c}\gamma_c$ |
| Dirac bracket | $[F,G]_D = [F,G] - [F,\chi_\alpha] C^{\alpha\beta} [\chi_\beta,G]$ |
| Gauge variation | $\delta F = \varepsilon^a [F,\gamma_a]$ |
| Observables | $[F,\gamma_a]_D \approx 0$ |
| Counting | $N_{\text{phys}} = N - f - s/2$ |

## 11. Remarks and Conventions

1. Weak equality $\approx$ always means “after restricting to the full constraint surface $\Sigma$”.
2. After introducing $[\ ,\ ]_D$, second-class constraints $\chi_\alpha$ may be imposed strongly (set exactly to zero inside all expressions) without inconsistency.
3. Structure functions vs constants: coefficients $C_{ab}{}^{c}(q,p)$ may be phase-space dependent; this affects operator ordering at quantization (not pursued here).
4. Multipliers: $v^a$ (or $u^a$) parametrize gauge freedom; physical observables do not depend on their choice.
5. Global issues (Gribov problem) can obstruct existence of a single global gauge fixing; all statements here are local in phase space.
