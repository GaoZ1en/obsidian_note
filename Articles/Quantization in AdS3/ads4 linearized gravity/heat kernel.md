# Heat-kernel derivation of AdS one-loop partition functions

This note matches the positive-energy module characters in the companion notes with the thermal-AdS heat-kernel computation.  The conventions are:

- The AdS radius is set to one.
- Bulk Euclidean AdS$_d$ is $H^d=SO_0(d,1)/SO(d)$.
- The thermal quotient is generated by
  $$
  \gamma=\exp\!\left(\beta H+i\sum_{j=1}^r\alpha_jJ_j\right),
  \qquad q=e^{-\beta},\qquad y_j=e^{i\alpha_j},
  $$
  where $H$ is the compact global-energy generator after continuation back to Lorentzian AdS and $J_j$ are Cartan generators of the spatial rotation group $SO(d-1)$.
- The zero image of the heat kernel is omitted.  It is the infinite-volume, local UV contribution.  The remaining non-zero-image answer is the finite, normal-ordered thermal partition function, the object compared with the module traces.
- For a spin-$s$ totally symmetric transverse-traceless field, $s$ labels the symmetric traceless $SO(d-1)$ representation $[s]=(s,0,\ldots,0)$ appearing in the principal-series harmonic analysis.  Its character is denoted $\chi_s^{(d-1)}(\mathbf y)$.

The key result is that the non-zero-image heat kernel gives exactly the plethystic exponential of the positive-energy $SO(2,d-1)$ character.  For a long spin-$s$ module of lowest energy $\Delta$,
$$
\log Z_{s,\Delta}^{\rm long}(\beta,\mathbf\alpha)
=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac1m\,
\frac{q^{m\Delta}\chi_s^{(d-1)}(\mathbf y^m)}{D_d(q^m,\mathbf y^m)}.
$$
For a massless gauge field with $s\geq1$,
$$
\log Z_{s}^{\rm massless}(\beta,\mathbf\alpha)
=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac1m\,
\frac{q^{m(s+d-3)}\chi_s^{(d-1)}(\mathbf y^m)-q^{m(s+d-2)}\chi_{s-1}^{(d-1)}(\mathbf y^m)}{D_d(q^m,\mathbf y^m)}.
$$
These are the formulas in the two representation-theory notes, now obtained from the heat kernel.

## 1. Thermal-AdS heat kernel and Harish-Chandra character

Set
$$
n=d-1,\qquad \rho=\frac n2=\frac{d-1}{2},\qquad r=\left\lfloor \frac n2\right\rfloor.
$$
The denominator in the positive-energy $SO(2,d-1)$ character is the generating function of symmetric powers of the $SO(n)$ vector representation:
$$
\sum_{N=0}^{\infty}q^N\chi_{\mathrm{Sym}^N V}(\mathbf y)=\frac{1}{D_d(q,\mathbf y)}.
$$
Explicitly,
$$
D_d(q,\mathbf y)=
\begin{cases}
\displaystyle \prod_{j=1}^{r}(1-qy_j)(1-qy_j^{-1}), & n=2r,\quad d=2r+1,\\[1.1em]
\displaystyle (1-q)\prod_{j=1}^{r}(1-qy_j)(1-qy_j^{-1}), & n=2r+1,\quad d=2r+2.
\end{cases}
$$
It is useful to introduce the geometric image denominator
$$
\Theta_d(m;\beta,\mathbf\alpha)=q^{-m\rho}D_d(q^m,\mathbf y^m),\qquad m\geq1.
$$
Equivalently,
$$
\Theta_d(m)=
\begin{cases}
\displaystyle \prod_{j=1}^{r}
\left(2\sinh\frac{m\beta-i m\alpha_j}{2}\right)
\left(2\sinh\frac{m\beta+i m\alpha_j}{2}\right), & n=2r,\\[1.1em]
\displaystyle \left(2\sinh\frac{m\beta}{2}\right)
\prod_{j=1}^{r}
\left(2\sinh\frac{m\beta-i m\alpha_j}{2}\right)
\left(2\sinh\frac{m\beta+i m\alpha_j}{2}\right), & n=2r+1.
\end{cases}
$$
Thus $\Theta_d(m)$ is the determinant of $1-\gamma^m$ on the transverse directions, written in a form natural for the method of images.

For the $SO_0(d,1)$ principal series relevant to spin-$s$ STT fields on $H^d$, the spectral parameter is $\lambda\in\mathbb R_{\geq0}$ and the Laplacian eigenvalue is
$$
-\nabla_{(s)}^2\quad \longrightarrow\quad \lambda^2+\rho^2+s.
$$
The Harish-Chandra character evaluated on the $m$-th thermal image is
$$
\chi_{\lambda,s}^{\rm HC}(\gamma^m)
=\frac{2\cos(m\beta\lambda)\,\chi_s^{(n)}(\mathbf y^m)}{\Theta_d(m;\beta,\mathbf\alpha)}
=\frac{2q^{m\rho}\cos(m\beta\lambda)\,\chi_s^{(n)}(\mathbf y^m)}{D_d(q^m,\mathbf y^m)}.
$$
The non-zero-image traced heat kernel is therefore
$$
K_s^{(d)}(t;\beta,\mathbf\alpha)
=\frac{\beta}{2\pi}\sum_{m\in\mathbb Z\setminus\{0\}}\int_0^\infty d\lambda\,
\chi_{\lambda,s}^{\rm HC}(\gamma^m)e^{-t(\lambda^2+\rho^2+s)}.
$$
Since the terms with $m$ and $-m$ are equal for the real symmetric traceless characters, the Gaussian integral gives the closed form
$$
\boxed{
K_s^{(d)}(t;\beta,\mathbf\alpha)
=\frac{\beta}{\sqrt{\pi t}}
\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}
\frac{q^{m\rho}\chi_s^{(n)}(\mathbf y^m)}{D_d(q^m,\mathbf y^m)}
\exp\!\left[-\frac{m^2\beta^2}{4t}-t(\rho^2+s)\right].
}
$$
This is the bare STT heat kernel for the connection Laplacian.  For a second-order determinant
$$
\mathcal O_{s,\Delta}=-\nabla_{(s)}^2+M_s^2(\Delta)
$$
whose normalizable lowest energy is $\Delta$, choose the constant shift
$$
\boxed{
M_s^2(\Delta)=\Delta(\Delta-n)-s=\Delta(\Delta-d+1)-s.
}
$$
Then
$$
\rho^2+s+M_s^2(\Delta)=(\Delta-\rho)^2.
$$
The shifted heat trace entering the determinant is
$$
\boxed{
\mathbb K_{s,\Delta}^{(d)}(t;\beta,\mathbf\alpha)
=K_s^{(d)}(t;\beta,\mathbf\alpha)e^{-tM_s^2(\Delta)}
=\frac{\beta}{\sqrt{\pi t}}
\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}
\frac{q^{m\rho}\chi_s^{(n)}(\mathbf y^m)}{D_d(q^m,\mathbf y^m)}
\exp\!\left[-\frac{m^2\beta^2}{4t}-t(\Delta-\rho)^2\right].
}
$$
For the standard branch $\Delta=\rho+\nu$ with $\nu\geq0$, the bosonic one-loop determinant is
$$
Z_{s,\Delta}^{\rm long}
=\left[\det{}_{s}^{\rm TT}\mathcal O_{s,\Delta}\right]^{-1/2},
\qquad
\log Z_{s,\Delta}^{\rm long}
=\frac12\int_0^\infty\frac{dt}{t}\,\mathbb K_{s,\Delta}^{(d)}(t;\beta,\mathbf\alpha).
$$
Using
$$
\int_0^\infty dt\,t^{-3/2}
\exp\!\left[-\frac{a^2}{4t}-b^2t\right]
=\frac{2\sqrt\pi}{a}e^{-ab},\qquad a,b>0,
$$
with $a=m\beta$ and $b=\Delta-\rho$, one obtains
$$
\begin{aligned}
\log Z_{s,\Delta}^{\rm long}
&=\frac12\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}
\frac{\beta q^{m\rho}\chi_s^{(n)}(\mathbf y^m)}{\sqrt\pi\,D_d(q^m,\mathbf y^m)}
\int_0^\infty dt\,t^{-3/2}
\exp\!\left[-\frac{m^2\beta^2}{4t}-t(\Delta-\rho)^2\right]\\
&=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac1m\,
\frac{q^{m\rho}e^{-m\beta(\Delta-\rho)}\chi_s^{(n)}(\mathbf y^m)}{D_d(q^m,\mathbf y^m)}\\
&=\boxed{\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac1m\,
\frac{q^{m\Delta}\chi_s^{(n)}(\mathbf y^m)}{D_d(q^m,\mathbf y^m)}}.
\end{aligned}
$$
This is the plethystic exponential of the one-particle character.  If an alternate scalar boundary condition in the BF window is desired, the local heat kernel depends only on $\nu^2=(\Delta-\rho)^2$; the replacement $\rho+\nu\mapsto\rho-\nu$ is a boundary-condition choice rather than a change of the local image kernel.

## 4. General AdS$_d$ spin-$s$ result

The same derivation works for all $d\geq3$.  Put $n=d-1$ and $\rho=n/2$.  The non-zero-image bare STT heat kernel is
$$
\boxed{
K_s^{(d)}(t;\beta,\mathbf\alpha)
=\frac{\beta}{\sqrt{\pi t}}
\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}
\frac{q^{m\rho}\chi_s^{(n)}(\mathbf y^m)}{D_d(q^m,\mathbf y^m)}
\exp\!\left[-\frac{m^2\beta^2}{4t}-t(\rho^2+s)\right].
}
$$
The shifted kernel for a lowest-energy $\Delta$ long module is
$$
\boxed{
\mathbb K_{s,\Delta}^{(d)}(t;\beta,\mathbf\alpha)
=\frac{\beta}{\sqrt{\pi t}}
\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}
\frac{q^{m\rho}\chi_s^{(n)}(\mathbf y^m)}{D_d(q^m,\mathbf y^m)}
\exp\!\left[-\frac{m^2\beta^2}{4t}-t(\Delta-\rho)^2\right],
}
$$
with
$$
M_s^2(\Delta)=\Delta(\Delta-n)-s.
$$
The determinant is
$$
Z_{s,\Delta}^{\rm massive}
=\left[\det_s^{\rm TT}\left(-\nabla_s^2+\Delta(\Delta-n)-s\right)\right]^{-1/2},
$$
so
$$
\boxed{
\log Z_{s,\Delta}^{\rm massive}(q,\mathbf y)
=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac1m\,
\frac{q^{m\Delta}\chi_s^{(n)}(\mathbf y^m)}{D_d(q^m,\mathbf y^m)}.
}
$$
Equivalently, the one-particle character is
$$
\boxed{
Z_{1,\rm massive}^{(s)}(q,\mathbf y)=
\frac{q^\Delta\chi_s^{(n)}(\mathbf y)}{D_d(q,\mathbf y)}.
}
$$
This is the $SO(2,d-1)$ long highest-weight module $\mathcal V(\Delta,s)$.

For a massless spin-$s$ gauge field, $s\geq1$, the lowest energy is
$$
\Delta_s=s+d-3=s+n-2.
$$
The short module is
$$
\mathcal D(s+d-3,s)=\frac{\mathcal V(s+d-3,s)}{\mathcal V(s+d-2,s-1)}.
$$
The determinant ratio is
$$
\boxed{
Z_s^{\rm massless}
=\left[
\frac{\det_{s-1}^{\rm TT}\left(-\nabla_{s-1}^2+M_{s-1}^2(s+d-2)\right)}
{\det_s^{\rm TT}\left(-\nabla_s^2+M_s^2(s+d-3)\right)}
\right]^{1/2}.
}
$$
Hence the effective heat kernel is
$$
\boxed{
\mathbb K_{s,\rm massless}^{(d)}(t)
=\mathbb K_{s,s+d-3}^{(d)}(t)-\mathbb K_{s-1,s+d-2}^{(d)}(t).
}
$$
In closed form,
$$
\boxed{
\mathbb K_{s,\rm massless}^{(d)}(t)
=\frac{\beta}{\sqrt{\pi t}}
\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}
\frac{q^{m\rho}}{D_d(q^m,\mathbf y^m)}
\exp\!\left[-\frac{m^2\beta^2}{4t}\right]
\left[
\chi_s^{(n)}(\mathbf y^m)e^{-t(s+\rho-2)^2}
-
\chi_{s-1}^{(n)}(\mathbf y^m)e^{-t(s+\rho-1)^2}
\right].
}
$$
The proper-time integral gives
$$
\boxed{
\log Z_s^{\rm massless}(q,\mathbf y)
=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac1m\,
\frac{q^{m(s+d-3)}\chi_s^{(n)}(\mathbf y^m)-q^{m(s+d-2)}\chi_{s-1}^{(n)}(\mathbf y^m)}{D_d(q^m,\mathbf y^m)}.
}
$$
Thus the one-particle character is
$$
\boxed{
Z_{1,\rm massless}^{(s)}(q,\mathbf y)=
\frac{q^{s+d-3}\chi_s^{(d-1)}(\mathbf y)-q^{s+d-2}\chi_{s-1}^{(d-1)}(\mathbf y)}{D_d(q,\mathbf y)}.
}
$$
This is exactly the formula in the general $\mathfrak{so}(2,d-1)$ note.

### 4.1 General zero-angular-potential check

At $\mathbf y=1$,
$$
D_d(q,1)=(1-q)^n,
$$
and
$$
g_s^{(n)}=\chi_s^{(n)}(1)
=\binom{n+s-1}{s}-\binom{n+s-3}{s-2}
=\frac{(n+2s-2)(n+s-3)!}{s!(n-2)!}.
$$
Therefore
$$
\boxed{
Z_{1,\rm massive}^{(s)}(q,1)=\frac{g_s^{(n)}q^\Delta}{(1-q)^n}
=\frac{g_s^{(d-1)}q^\Delta}{(1-q)^{d-1}}.
}
$$
For a massless spin-$s$ field,
$$
\boxed{
Z_{1,\rm massless}^{(s)}(q,1)=
\frac{q^{s+d-3}\left(g_s^{(d-1)}-qg_{s-1}^{(d-1)}\right)}{(1-q)^{d-1}}.
}
$$
This is the unrefined version of the short-character formula.

## 5. Comparison summary

The heat-kernel derivation and the module-character derivation agree because the two non-trivial ingredients are the same object written in different languages:

1. The image denominator
   $$
   \Theta_d(m)=q^{-m\rho}D_d(q^m,\mathbf y^m)
   $$
   is the thermal quotient determinant in the heat kernel and the symmetric-descendant denominator in the positive-energy character.

2. The proper-time integral converts the spectral factor
   $$
   e^{-t(\Delta-\rho)^2}
   $$
   into
   $$
   e^{-m\beta(\Delta-\rho)},
   $$
   which combines with the geometric factor $q^{m\rho}$ to give $q^{m\Delta}$.

3. A massless gauge field is obtained by subtracting the gauge submodule heat kernel,
   $$
   \mathbb K_{s,s+d-3}^{(d)}-\mathbb K_{s-1,s+d-2}^{(d)},
   $$
   which is the heat-kernel version of the short quotient
   $$
   \mathcal V(s+d-3,s)/\mathcal V(s+d-2,s-1).
   $$

Thus the Harish-Chandra image formula on thermal Euclidean AdS$_d$ reproduces the normal-ordered Fock-space partition functions built from the $SO(2,d-1)$ highest-weight characters.
