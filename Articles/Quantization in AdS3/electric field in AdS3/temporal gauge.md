the equations of motion are

$$\begin{align}
(1+r^{2})\partial _{r}^{2}A^{t}+\frac{1}{r^{2}}\partial _{\phi}^{2}A^{t}+\frac{1+5r^{2}}{r}\partial _{r}A^{t}+4A^{t}+\frac{1}{1+r^{2}}\partial _{t}\partial _{r}A^{r}+\frac{1-r^{2}}{r(1+r^{2})^{2}}\partial _{t}A^{r}+\frac{1}{1+r^{2}}\partial _{t}\partial _{\phi}A^{\phi} & =0 \\
-(1+r^{2})\partial _{t}\partial _{r}A^{t}-2r\partial _{t}A^{t}-\frac{1}{1+r^{2}}\partial _{t}^{2}A^{r}+\frac{1}{r^{2}}\partial _{\phi}^{2}A^{r}-(1+r^{2})\partial _{r}\partial _{\phi}A^{\phi}-\frac{2(1+r^{2})}{r}\partial _{\phi}A^{\phi} & =0 \\
-\frac{1}{r^{2}}\partial _{t}\partial _{\phi}A^{t}-\frac{1}{r^{2}}\partial _{r}\partial _{\phi}A^{r}+\frac{1}{r^{3}}\partial _{\phi}A^{r}-\frac{1}{1+r^{2}}\partial _{t}^{2}A^{\phi}+(1+r^{2})\partial _{r}^{2}A^{\phi}+\frac{3+5r^{2}}{r}\partial _{r}A^{\phi}+4A^{\phi} & =0
\end{align}$$

the asymptotic behavior of $\displaystyle{A}$ is chosen to be

$$\begin{align}
A^{t} & =\mathcal{O}(r^{-2}), & A^{r} & =\mathcal{O}(r^{-1}), & A^{\phi} & =\mathcal{O}(r^{-2})
\end{align}$$

now we choose the temporal gauge $A_{t}=0$, the asymptotic behavior of $\displaystyle{A^{r}}$ and $\displaystyle{A^{\phi}}$ remains unchanged. the equations of motion become

$$\begin{align}
\frac{1}{1+r^{2}}\partial _{t}\partial _{r}A^{r}+\frac{1-r^{2}}{r(1+r^{2})^{2}}\partial _{t}A^{r}+\frac{1}{1+r^{2}}\partial _{t}\partial _{\phi}A^{\phi} & =0 \\
-\frac{1}{1+r^{2}}\partial _{t}^{2}A^{r}+\frac{1}{r^{2}}\partial _{\phi}^{2}A^{r}-(1+r^{2})\partial _{r}\partial _{\phi}A^{\phi}-\frac{2(1+r^{2})}{r}\partial _{\phi}A^{\phi} & =0 \\
-\frac{1}{r^{2}}\partial _{r}\partial _{\phi}A^{r}+\frac{1}{r^{3}}\partial _{\phi}A^{r}-\frac{1}{1+r^{2}}\partial _{t}^{2}A^{\phi}+(1+r^{2})\partial _{r}^{2}A^{\phi}+\frac{3+5r^{2}}{r}\partial _{r}A^{\phi}+4A^{\phi} & =0
\end{align}$$

perform a Fourier transformation

$$\begin{align}
A^{\mu}(t,r,\phi) & =e^{-i\omega t}e^{im\phi}f^{\mu}(r)
\end{align}$$

we have

$$\begin{align}
\partial _{r}f^{r}+\frac{1-r^{2}}{r(1+r^{2})}f^{r}+imf^{\phi} & =0 \\
\frac{\omega ^{2}}{1+r^{2}}f^{r}-\frac{m^{2}}{r^{2}}f^{r}-im(1+r^{2})\partial _{r}f^{\phi}-\frac{2im(1+r^{2})}{r}f^{\phi} & =0 \\
-\frac{im}{r^{2}}\partial _{r}f^{r}+\frac{im}{r^{3}}f^{r}+\frac{\omega ^{2}}{1+r^{2}}f^{\phi}+(1+r^{2})\partial _{r}^{2}f^{\phi}+\frac{3+5r^{2}}{r}\partial _{r}f^{\phi}+4f^{\phi} & =0
\end{align}$$

## case $\displaystyle{m=0}$

for $\displaystyle{m=0}$, the equations of motion become

$$\begin{align}
\partial _{r}f^{r}+\frac{1-r^{2}}{r(1+r^{2})}f^{r} & =0 \\
\frac{\omega ^{2}}{1+r^{2}}f^{r} & =0 \\
\frac{\omega ^{2}}{1+r^{2}}f^{\phi}+(1+r^{2})\partial _{r}^{2}f^{\phi}+\frac{3+5r^{2}}{r}\partial _{r}f^{\phi}+4f^{\phi} & =0
\end{align}$$

### case $\displaystyle{\omega=0}$

if $\displaystyle{\omega=0}$, the equations of motion become

$$\begin{align}
\partial _{r}f^{r}+\frac{1-r^{2}}{r(1+r^{2})}f^{r} & =0 \\
(1+r^{2})\partial _{r}^{2}f^{\phi}+\frac{3+5r^{2}}{r}\partial _{r}f^{\phi}+4f^{\phi} & =0
\end{align}$$

the general solution is

$$\begin{align}
f^{r} & =C_{1} \frac{1+r^{2}}{r} \\
f^{\phi} & =\frac{C_{2}}{r^{2}} +C_{3} \frac{\log(1+r^{2})}{r^{2}}
\end{align}$$

where $\displaystyle{f^{\phi}=\frac{C_{2}}{r^{2}}}$ is an allowable solution, while the other two solutions do not satisft the asymptotic boundary condition. however, this mode cannot be normalized in our present framework. this configuration is a pure gauge mode, which can be generated by the large gauge transformation

$$\begin{align}
A_{\mu} & =\partial _{\mu}\Lambda \\
\Lambda & =C_{2}\phi
\end{align}$$

- for gauge group $\displaystyle{\mathrm{U}(1)}$, the gauge parameter $\displaystyle{\Lambda}$ is periodic in $\displaystyle{\phi}$, thus we have to "quantize" $\displaystyle{C_{2}\in \mathbb{Z}}$.
- for gauge group $\displaystyle{\mathbb{R}}$, there is no such quantization condition on $\displaystyle{C_{2}}$.

since we are talking about the $\displaystyle{\mathrm{U}(1)}$ gauge theory, we will impose the "quantization" condition $\displaystyle{C_{2}\in \mathbb{Z}}$ on this zero mode. such zero modes play an role as discrete vacua in the quantum theory, which carry non-trivial topological structure -- winding number. 

### case $\displaystyle{\omega\neq 0}$

if $\displaystyle{\omega\neq 0}$, the equations of motion become

$$\begin{align}
f^{r} & =0 \\
(1+r^{2})\partial _{r}^{2}f^{\phi}+\frac{3+5r^{2}}{r}\partial _{r}f^{\phi}+\left( 4+\frac{\omega ^{2}}{1+r^{2}} \right)f^{\phi} & =0
\end{align}$$

the solution is

$$\begin{align}
f^{\phi} & =C_{1}(1+r^{2})^{-1}{}_{2}F_{1}\left(\frac{2-\omega}{2},\frac{2+\omega}{2};1; \frac{1}{1+r^{2}}\right) \\
 & +C_{2}(\text{second solution contains logarithmic term})
\end{align}$$

we neglect the second solution and impose quantization condition on $\displaystyle{\omega}$ by requiring the first solution to be normalizable, which gives

$$\begin{align}
\omega & =2n+2, \quad n\in \mathbb{N}
\end{align}$$

therefore we have the normalizable modes

$$\begin{align}
A^{t} & =0,\quad A^{r} =0 \\
A^{\phi} & =Ce^{-i(2n+2)t}(1+r^{2})^{-1}{}_{2}F_{1}\left(-n,n+2; 1; \frac{1}{1+r^{2}}\right)
\end{align}$$

## case $\displaystyle{m\neq 0}$

$$\begin{align}
\partial _{r}f^{r}+\frac{1-r^{2}}{r(1+r^{2})}f^{r}+imf^{\phi} & =0 \\
\frac{\omega ^{2}}{1+r^{2}}f^{r}-\frac{m^{2}}{r^{2}}f^{r}-im(1+r^{2})\partial _{r}f^{\phi}-\frac{2im(1+r^{2})}{r}f^{\phi} & =0 \\
-\frac{im}{r^{2}}\partial _{r}f^{r}+\frac{im}{r^{3}}f^{r}+\frac{\omega ^{2}}{1+r^{2}}f^{\phi}+(1+r^{2})\partial _{r}^{2}f^{\phi}+\frac{3+5r^{2}}{r}\partial _{r}f^{\phi}+4f^{\phi} & =0
\end{align}$$

we first turn to lower indices varibles

$$\begin{align}
(1+r^{2})\partial _{r}(rf_{r})+\frac{im}{r}f_{\phi} & =0 \\
\frac{\omega ^{2}}{1+r^{2}}f_{r}-\frac{m^{2}}{r^{2}}f_{r}-\frac{im}{r^{2}}\partial _{r}f_{\phi} & =0 \\
\dots
\end{align}$$

insert the first equation into the second and the third equation, we obtain the unique equation for $\displaystyle{f^{r}}$

$$\begin{align}
(1+r^{2})\partial _{r}^{2}f_{r}+\frac{3+5r^{2}}{r}\partial _{r}f_{r}+\left(3+\frac{\omega ^{2}}{1+r^{2}}-\frac{m^{2}-1}{r^{2}}\right)f_{r}=0
\end{align}$$

the solution is

$$\begin{align}
f_{r} & =C_{1}r^{-1+|m|}(1+r^{2})^{-(2+|m|)/2}{}_{2}F_{1}\left(\frac{2+|m|-\omega}{2},\frac{2+|m|+\omega}{2};2;\frac{1}{1+r^{2}}\right) \\
 & +C_{2}(\text{second solution contains logarithmic term})
\end{align}$$

we neglect the second solution and impose quantization condition on $\displaystyle{\omega}$ by requiring the first solution to be normalizable, which gives

$$\begin{align}
\omega & =2n+2+|m|, \quad n\in \mathbb{N}
\end{align}$$

therefore we have the normalizable modes

$$\begin{align}
A^{t} & =0 \\
A^{r} & =C_{1}r^{-1+|m|}(1+r^{2})^{-(2+|m|)/2}{}_{2}F_{1}\left(\frac{2+|m|-\omega}{2},\frac{2+|m|+\omega}{2};2;\frac{1}{1+r^{2}}\right) \\
A^{\phi} & =\frac{1}{im}\left((1+r^{2})\partial _{r}(rf_{r})\right) \\
 & =\frac{C_{1}}{im} (1+r^{2})\partial _{r}\left( r^{|m|}(1+r^{2})^{-(2+|m|)/2}{}_{2}F_{1}\left(\frac{2+|m|-\omega}{2},\frac{2+|m|+\omega}{2}; 2; \frac{1}{1+r^{2}}\right) \right)
\end{align}$$
