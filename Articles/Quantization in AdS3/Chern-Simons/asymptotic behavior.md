the asymptotic behaviors are chosen to be

$$\begin{align}
A^{t} & =\mathcal{O}(r^{-2}), & A^{r} & =\mathcal{O}(r^{-1}), & A^{\phi} & =\mathcal{O}(r^{-2}), \\
A_{t} & =\mathcal{O}(r^{0}), & A_{r} & =\mathcal{O}(r^{-3}), & A_{\phi} & =\mathcal{O}(r^{0}), \\
F^{tr} & =\mathcal{O}(r^{-1}), & F^{t\phi} & =\mathcal{O}(r^{-4}), & F^{r\phi} & =\mathcal{O}(r^{-1}) \\
F_{tr} & =\mathcal{O}(r^{-1}), & F_{t\phi} & =\mathcal{O}(r^{0}), & F_{r\phi} & =\mathcal{O}(r^{-1})
\end{align}$$

then

$$\begin{align}
\frac{k}{4\pi}\sqrt{ -g }\varepsilon ^{\mu \nu \rho}A_{\mu}\nabla _{\nu}A_{\rho} & =\mathcal{O}(r^{-1})
\end{align}$$

the action has a logarithmic divergence.

$$\begin{align}
\frac{k}{4\pi}\sqrt{ \sigma }\tau _{\mu}\varepsilon ^{\mu \nu \rho}A_{\nu}\delta A_{\rho} & =\mathcal{O}(r^{-3}) \\
-\frac{k}{4\pi} \sqrt{ -\gamma }n_{\mu}\varepsilon ^{\mu \nu \rho}A_{\nu}\delta A_{\rho} & =\mathcal{O}(r^{0})
\end{align}$$

the symplectic potential is finite. we have to impose some boundary Lagrangian to cancel terms supported on the boundary to make the variational principle well-defined.

$$\begin{align}
