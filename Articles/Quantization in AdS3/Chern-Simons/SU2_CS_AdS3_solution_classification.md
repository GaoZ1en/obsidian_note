# Classification of $SU(2)$ Chern--Simons Solutions on Lorentzian Global $AdS_3$

## 0. Setup and relation to the $U(1)$ analysis

We consider Chern--Simons theory on Lorentzian global $AdS_3$ with gauge group $SU(2)$. The spacetime is taken to be the universal cover of global $AdS_3$, with topology

$$\begin{align}
\widetilde{AdS}_3 \simeq \mathbb{R}_t \times D^2 .
\end{align}$$

Although the angular coordinate obeys

$$\begin{align}
\phi \sim \phi+2\pi,
\end{align}$$

the angular circle at fixed radius is contractible through the disk $D^2$. Therefore

$$\begin{align}
\pi_1(\widetilde{AdS}_3)=0 .
\end{align}$$

In particular, in the absence of Wilson lines, punctures, topological defects, BTZ quotients, or Euclidean thermal identifications, there are no nontrivial global holonomy sectors. Hence every flat $SU(2)$ connection is globally pure gauge.

We use the previous $U(1)$ Chern--Simons analysis as input. In that analysis, the linearized flat solutions were classified under the AdS isometry group

$$\begin{align}
SL(2,\mathbb{R})_L\times SL(2,\mathbb{R})_R
\end{align}$$

into two branches,

$$\begin{align}
(h,\bar h)=(1,0),
\qquad
(h,\bar h)=(0,1),
\end{align}$$

generated by the corresponding gauge parameters $\Lambda_{(1,0)}$, $\Lambda_{(0,1)}$, and their descendants under $\mathcal L_{\xi_{-1}}$ and $\mathcal L_{\bar\xi_{-1}}$, respectively. The $U(1)$ result also fixes the chirality selected by the sign of $k$: for $k>0$ one keeps the $(1,0)$ branch, while for $k<0$ one keeps the $(0,1)$ branch. The explicit $U(1)$ mode functions, their gauge parameters, normalization, boundary terms, and resulting $\widehat{\mathfrak u}(1)$ current algebra are taken as known input.

The goal here is to lift that classification to $SU(2)$, emphasizing the difference between the linearized classification and the full non-Abelian flat solution space.

---

## 1. $SU(2)$ Chern--Simons theory

Let

$$\begin{align}
A=A_\mu^a T_a dx^\mu
\end{align}$$

be an $\mathfrak{su}(2)$-valued connection. We take

$$\begin{align}
[T_a,T_b]=f_{ab}{}^c T_c .
\end{align}$$

When needed, we use the normalization

$$\begin{align}
\operatorname{Tr}(T_aT_b)=\frac12\delta_{ab} .
\end{align}$$

The Chern--Simons action is

$$\begin{align}
S_{\mathrm{SU}(2)} & =\frac{k}{4\pi}\int _{M}\mathrm{Tr}\left(A\wedge \mathrm{d}A+\frac{2}{3}A\wedge A\wedge A\right)
\end{align}$$

Varying the action gives

$$\begin{align}
\delta S_{SU(2)}
=
\frac{k}{2\pi}\int_M \operatorname{Tr}(\delta A\wedge F)
+
\frac{k}{4\pi}\int_{\partial M}\operatorname{Tr}(A\wedge \delta A),
\end{align}$$

where

$$\begin{align}
F=dA+A\wedge A .
\end{align}$$

Therefore the equations of motion are

$$\begin{align}
\boxed{F=0.}
\end{align}$$

In components,

$$\begin{align}
F_{\mu\nu}^a
=
\nabla_\mu A_\nu^a-
\nabla_\nu A_\mu^a
+f^a{}_{bc}A_\mu^bA_\nu^c=0 .
\end{align}$$

Thus the classical solutions are flat $SU(2)$ connections.

---

## 2. Full local and global solution space

Because $F=0$, every solution is locally pure gauge:

$$\begin{align}
A=g^{-1}dg,
\qquad
 g:M\to SU(2),
\end{align}$$

up to the convention-dependent replacement $A=-dg\,g^{-1}$.

For Lorentzian global $AdS_3$ taken as the universal cover,

$$\begin{align}
M=\widetilde{AdS}_3\simeq \mathbb R_t\times D^2,
\qquad
\pi_1(M)=0 .
\end{align}$$

Therefore there are no nontrivial flat holonomy sectors. The full smooth flat solution space can be represented as

$$\begin{align}
\boxed{
\mathcal S_{\mathrm{flat}}
=
\left\{
A=g^{-1}dg
\ \middle|\
 g:\widetilde{AdS}_3\to SU(2),\quad
 g(t,r,\phi+2\pi)=g(t,r,\phi)
\right\} .
}
\end{align}$$

The periodicity condition is imposed because $\phi$ is an angular coordinate. Since the circle is contractible, this periodicity does not generate additional holonomy sectors.

Modulo gauge equivalence, the physical moduli space of flat connections on the simply connected bulk is trivial in the absence of boundary conditions. However, with an AdS boundary and a choice of boundary variational principle, transformations that do not vanish appropriately at the boundary become physical boundary modes. These are the edge or large-gauge modes classified below.

---

## 3. Finite gauge parameter and Maurer--Cartan expansion

Write the finite gauge parameter as

$$\begin{align}
g=e^\Lambda,
\qquad
\Lambda=\Lambda^aT_a\in \mathfrak{su}(2) .
\end{align}$$

Then the corresponding flat connection is

$$\begin{align}
A=e^{-\Lambda}d e^\Lambda .
\end{align}$$

Using the Baker--Campbell--Hausdorff expansion,

$$\begin{align}
\boxed{
A
=
\frac{1-e^{-\operatorname{ad}_\Lambda}}{\operatorname{ad}_\Lambda}d\Lambda
=
d\Lambda
-\frac12[\Lambda,d\Lambda]
+\frac1{3!}[\Lambda,[\Lambda,d\Lambda]]
-\frac1{4!}[\Lambda,[\Lambda,[\Lambda,d\Lambda]]]
+\cdots .
}
\end{align}$$

At linear order,

$$\begin{align}
A=d\Lambda+O(\Lambda^2).
\end{align}$$

Thus the $U(1)$ gauge parameters directly describe the tangent space to the full $SU(2)$ flat solution space at $A=0$. The non-Abelian corrections are fixed uniquely by the Maurer--Cartan series.

This is the main difference from the Abelian theory. For $U(1)$, arbitrary linear superpositions of flat modes remain exact flat solutions. For $SU(2)$, an arbitrary $\mathfrak{su}(2)$-valued linear superposition

$$\begin{align}
A^a=\sum_n c_n^a d\Lambda_n
\end{align}$$

is generally only a linearized solution, because

$$\begin{align}
F^a
=
\frac12 f^a{}_{bc}A^b\wedge A^c
\end{align}$$

does not vanish unless the color directions commute or the full Maurer--Cartan completion is included.

---

## 4. Linearized solution space and $SL(2,\mathbb R)\times SL(2,\mathbb R)$ classification

Expand around the trivial connection:

$$\begin{align}
A=\epsilon a+O(\epsilon^2).
\end{align}$$

The flatness equation becomes

$$\begin{align}
F=dA+A\wedge A
=\epsilon da+O(\epsilon^2),
\end{align}$$

so the linearized equation is

$$\begin{align}
da=0 .
\end{align}$$

Therefore

$$\begin{align}
a=d\Lambda,
\qquad
\Lambda=\Lambda^aT_a .
\end{align}$$

The AdS isometry group

$$\begin{align}
SL(2,\mathbb R)_L\times SL(2,\mathbb R)_R
\end{align}$$

acts only on the spacetime dependence of $\Lambda^a$, not on the adjoint index $a$. Hence the $SU(2)$ linearized solution space is simply the $U(1)$ linearized solution space tensored with the adjoint representation of $SU(2)$:

$$\begin{align}
\boxed{
\mathcal S_{\mathrm{lin}}^{SU(2)}
=
\left[D(1,0)\oplus D(0,1)\right]\otimes \operatorname{Adj}_{SU(2)} .
}
\end{align}$$

Since

$$\begin{align}
\operatorname{Adj}_{SU(2)}\cong \mathbf 3,
\end{align}$$

we may also write

$$\begin{align}
\mathcal S_{\mathrm{lin}}^{SU(2)}
=
\left[D(1,0)\oplus D(0,1)\right]\otimes \mathbf 3 .
\end{align}$$

Explicitly, if the Abelian primary gauge parameters are denoted by $\Lambda_{(1,0)}$ and $\Lambda_{(0,1)}$, then the $SU(2)$ primary gauge parameters are

$$\begin{align}
\Lambda_{(1,0)}^a=T_a\Lambda_{(1,0)},
\qquad
\Lambda_{(0,1)}^a=T_a\Lambda_{(0,1)}.
\end{align}$$

Their descendants are

$$\begin{align}
\Lambda_{n}^{a,+}
=
T_a\,\mathcal L_{\xi_{-1}}^n\Lambda_{(1,0)},
\end{align}$$

and

$$\begin{align}
\Lambda_{n}^{a,-}
=
T_a\,\mathcal L_{\bar\xi_{-1}}^n\Lambda_{(0,1)}.
\end{align}$$

The corresponding linearized gauge fields are

$$\begin{align}
\psi_n^{a,+}=d\Lambda_n^{a,+},
\qquad
\psi_n^{a,-}=d\Lambda_n^{a,-}.
\end{align}$$

Thus, at the level of $SL(2,\mathbb R)_L\times SL(2,\mathbb R)_R$ representations,

$$\begin{align}
\psi_n^{a,+}\in D(1,0)\otimes \mathbf 3,
\end{align}$$

$$\begin{align}
\psi_n^{a,-}\in D(0,1)\otimes \mathbf 3.
\end{align}$$

## 5. Full non-Abelian completion of the mode expansion

The finite gauge parameter can be expanded in the same basis of linearized modes:

$$\begin{align}
\Lambda(t,r,\phi)
=
\sum_{a,n}\lambda_n^{a,+}\Lambda_n^+(t,r,\phi)T_a
+
\sum_{a,n}\lambda_n^{a,-}\Lambda_n^-(t,r,\phi)T_a .
\end{align}$$

Then the full flat connection is not simply $d\Lambda$, but

$$\begin{align}
\boxed{
A
=
e^{-\Lambda}d e^\Lambda
=
d\Lambda
-\frac12[\Lambda,d\Lambda]
+\frac1{3!}[\Lambda,[\Lambda,d\Lambda]]
-\cdots .
}
\end{align}$$

This expression gives the complete non-Abelian flat solution associated with a given finite $SU(2)$-valued gauge parameter

$$\begin{align}
g=e^\Lambda.
\end{align}$$

If $\Lambda$ takes values in a fixed Cartan direction, for example

$$\begin{align}
\Lambda=\lambda T_3,
\end{align}$$

then

$$\begin{align}
[\Lambda,d\Lambda]=0,
\end{align}$$

and the solution reduces to an Abelian embedded solution:

$$\begin{align}
A=d\lambda\,T_3.
\end{align}$$

If $\Lambda$ contains several noncommuting color components, the nonlinear commutator terms are essential.

For example, if

$$\begin{align}
\Lambda=\lambda_1T_1+\lambda_2T_2,
\end{align}$$

then

$$\begin{align}
[\Lambda,d\Lambda]
=
(\lambda_1d\lambda_2-\lambda_2d\lambda_1)[T_1,T_2].
\end{align}$$

With $[T_1,T_2]=T_3$,

$$\begin{align}
A
=
d\lambda_1T_1+d\lambda_2T_2
-\frac12(\lambda_1d\lambda_2-\lambda_2d\lambda_1)T_3
+\cdots .
\end{align}$$

Thus the non-Abelian completion generally generates color components that were absent at linear order.

## 6. Boundary variational principle and chirality selection

Introduce light-cone boundary components

$$\begin{align}
A_\pm=A_t\pm A_\phi,
\qquad
A_\pm=A_\pm^aT_a .
\end{align}$$

The boundary term in the variation of the bulk Chern--Simons action has the schematic form

$$\begin{align}
\delta S_{\mathrm{bulk}}\big|_{\Gamma}
=
-\frac{k}{4\pi}\int_\Gamma
\operatorname{Tr}\left(
A_+\delta A_- - A_-\delta A_+
\right).
\end{align}$$

The $U(1)$ analysis shows that the branch selection is tied to the sign of $k$. The same argument applies componentwise in $\mathfrak{su}(2)$, with the trace contracting the color indices.

For $k>0$, one keeps the branch satisfying

$$\begin{align}
A_-=0
\end{align}$$

on shell. The appropriate boundary term is

$$\begin{align}
S_{\mathrm{bdy}}
=
\frac{k}{4\pi}\int_\Gamma
\operatorname{Tr}(A_+A_-).
\end{align}$$

For $k<0$, one keeps the branch satisfying

$$\begin{align}
A_+=0
\end{align}$$

on shell, with boundary term

$$\begin{align}
S_{\mathrm{bdy}}
=
-\frac{k}{4\pi}\int_\Gamma
\operatorname{Tr}(A_+A_-).
\end{align}$$

Therefore the physical linearized solution space after imposing the chiral boundary condition is

$$\begin{align}
\boxed{
 k>0:
\qquad
\mathcal S_{\mathrm{phys}}^{SU(2)}
=
D(1,0)\otimes \operatorname{Adj}_{SU(2)} .
}
\end{align}$$

Similarly,

$$\begin{align}
\boxed{
 k<0:
\qquad
\mathcal S_{\mathrm{phys}}^{SU(2)}
=
D(0,1)\otimes \operatorname{Adj}_{SU(2)} .
}
\end{align}$$

Equivalently,

$$\begin{align}
 k>0:
\qquad
\Lambda
\in
D(1,0)\otimes \mathfrak{su}(2),
\end{align}$$

$$\begin{align}
 k<0:
\qquad
\Lambda
\in
D(0,1)\otimes \mathfrak{su}(2).
\end{align}$$

The full non-Abelian solution is again obtained by exponentiation:

$$\begin{align}
A=e^{-\Lambda}de^\Lambda .
\end{align}$$

## 7. Symplectic form and normalization

The covariant symplectic form of $SU(2)$ Chern--Simons theory is

$$\begin{align}
\omega
=
\frac{k}{4\pi}\int_\Sigma
\operatorname{Tr}(\delta A\wedge \delta A).
\end{align}$$

With

$$\begin{align}
\operatorname{Tr}(T_aT_b)=\frac12\delta_{ab},
\end{align}$$

this becomes

$$\begin{align}
\omega
=
\frac{k}{8\pi}\int_\Sigma
\delta A^a\wedge \delta A^a .
\end{align}$$

For linearized modes of the form

$$\begin{align}
\psi_n^{a,\pm}=T_a\psi_n^\pm,
\end{align}$$

one obtains

$$\begin{align}
\omega[\psi_m^{a,\pm},\psi_n^{b,\pm}]
=
\frac12\delta^{ab}\,
\omega_{U(1)}[\psi_m^\pm,\psi_n^\pm]
\end{align}$$

with the above trace convention. Therefore the normalization constants are inherited from the $U(1)$ analysis, up to the trace normalization of $SU(2)$. If the $U(1)$ generator is normalized as $\operatorname{Tr}(T^2)=1$, while the $SU(2)$ generators obey $\operatorname{Tr}(T_aT_b)=\frac12\delta_{ab}$, then the $SU(2)$ mode coefficient is larger by $\sqrt2$:

$$\begin{align}
C_{SU(2)}=\sqrt2\,C_{U(1)} .
\end{align}$$

Thus, using the $U(1)$ normalization quoted previously,

$$\begin{align}
 k>0:
\qquad
C_{(1,0)}^{SU(2)}=\frac{2\sqrt2}{\sqrt{k}},
\end{align}$$

and

$$\begin{align}
 k<0:
\qquad
C_{(0,1)}^{SU(2)}=\frac{2\sqrt2}{\sqrt{-k}}.
\end{align}$$

If instead one chooses

$$\begin{align}
\operatorname{Tr}(T_aT_b)=\delta_{ab},
\end{align}$$

then the $\sqrt2$ factor is absent.

## 8. Boundary charges and affine $\mathfrak{su}(2)$ algebra

For a gauge parameter

$$\begin{align}
\Lambda=\Lambda^aT_a,
\end{align}$$

the infinitesimal gauge transformation is

$$\begin{align}
\delta_\Lambda A=D\Lambda=d\Lambda+[A,\Lambda].
\end{align}$$

The associated boundary charge has the standard Chern--Simons form

$$\begin{align}
H_\Lambda
\sim
\frac{k}{2\pi}\int_{\partial\Sigma}
\operatorname{Tr}(\Lambda A)
\end{align}$$

up to convention-dependent signs and possible choices of boundary polarization. Equivalently, in the bulk expression adapted to the previous $U(1)$ computation, one may write the linearized charge as

$$\begin{align}
H_\Lambda
=
\frac{k}{2\pi}\int_\Sigma
\operatorname{Tr}(A\wedge d\Lambda)
\end{align}$$

when evaluated on the corresponding pure-gauge directions.

The Poisson bracket of charges is

$$\begin{align}
\boxed{
\{H_\Lambda,H_\Eta\}
=
H_{[\Lambda,\Eta]}
+
\frac{k}{2\pi}\int_{\partial\Sigma}
\operatorname{Tr}(\Lambda d\Eta) .
}
\end{align}$$

This is the classical affine $\mathfrak{su}(2)$ current algebra.

Expanding in modes,

$$\begin{align}
\Lambda_m^a=T^a e^{imx^+}
\end{align}$$

for the chiral branch, or using the descendant basis inherited from the $U(1)$ analysis, the charges obey

$$\begin{align}
\boxed{
\{J_m^a,J_n^b\}
=
f^{ab}{}_c J_{m+n}^c
+k m\delta^{ab}\delta_{m+n,0} .
}
\end{align}$$

Thus the Abelian boundary algebra

$$\begin{align}
\widehat{\mathfrak u}(1)_k
\end{align}$$

is enhanced to

$$\begin{align}
\boxed{
\widehat{\mathfrak{su}}(2)_k .
}
\end{align}$$

For the opposite chirality, the same statement holds with the corresponding right-moving coordinate and sign convention for the level. Physically, one obtains the opposite chiral current algebra.

---

## 9. Summary of the classification

The $SU(2)$ theory has flatness equation

$$\begin{align}
F=dA+A\wedge A=0 .
\end{align}$$

On Lorentzian global $AdS_3$, taken as the universal cover, every smooth flat connection is globally pure gauge:

$$\begin{align}
A=g^{-1}dg,
\qquad
 g:\widetilde{AdS}_3\to SU(2),
\qquad
 g(t,r,\phi+2\pi)=g(t,r,\phi).
\end{align}$$

There is no additional holonomy sector because

$$\begin{align}
\pi_1(\widetilde{AdS}_3)=0 .
\end{align}$$

Near the trivial connection,

$$\begin{align}
g=e^\Lambda,
\qquad
A=e^{-\Lambda}de^\Lambda
=d\Lambda-\frac12[\Lambda,d\Lambda]+\cdots .
\end{align}$$

At linear order, the solution space is the Abelian result tensored with the adjoint representation:

$$\begin{align}
\boxed{
\mathcal S_{\mathrm{lin}}^{SU(2)}
=
\left[D(1,0)\oplus D(0,1)\right]\otimes \mathbf 3 .
}
\end{align}$$

After choosing the boundary variational principle and imposing the chirality selected by the sign of $k$, one gets

$$\begin{align}
\boxed{
 k>0:
\qquad
\mathcal S_{\mathrm{phys}}^{SU(2)}
=
D(1,0)\otimes \mathbf 3,
}
\end{align}$$

and

$$\begin{align}
\boxed{
 k<0:
\qquad
\mathcal S_{\mathrm{phys}}^{SU(2)}
=
D(0,1)\otimes \mathbf 3.
}
\end{align}$$

The full non-Abelian solutions are obtained by exponentiating the corresponding $\mathfrak{su}(2)$-valued gauge parameter:

$$\begin{align}
\boxed{
A=e^{-\Lambda}de^\Lambda,
\qquad
\Lambda\in D(1,0)\otimes\mathfrak{su}(2)
\quad (k>0),
}
\end{align}$$

or

$$\begin{align}
\boxed{
A=e^{-\Lambda}de^\Lambda,
\qquad
\Lambda\in D(0,1)\otimes\mathfrak{su}(2)
\quad (k<0).
}
\end{align}$$

The corresponding boundary current algebra is

$$\begin{align}
\boxed{
\widehat{\mathfrak{su}}(2)_k
}
\end{align}$$

with Poisson brackets

$$\begin{align}
\boxed{
\{J_m^a,J_n^b\}
=
f^{ab}{}_cJ_{m+n}^c
+k m\delta^{ab}\delta_{m+n,0} .
}
\end{align}$$

Therefore, relative to the $U(1)$ theory, the main changes are:

1. The linearized $SL(2,\mathbb R)_L\times SL(2,\mathbb R)_R$ towers acquire an adjoint $SU(2)$ index.
2. The full finite solution is not an arbitrary linear superposition of the Abelian towers; it is the Maurer--Cartan completion $A=e^{-\Lambda}de^\Lambda$.
3. The boundary current algebra is enhanced from $\widehat{\mathfrak u}(1)_k$ to $\widehat{\mathfrak{su}}(2)_k$.
4. On Lorentzian global $AdS_3$, no additional holonomy sectors appear unless one changes the global topology or inserts defects/Wilson lines.
