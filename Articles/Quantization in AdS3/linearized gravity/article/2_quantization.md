# Quantization

Pure Einstein gravity in three dimensions has no local propagating bulk degree of freedom. The non-trivial linearized modes compatible with Brown-Henneaux boundary conditions are instead generated by diffeomorphisms that do not vanish at the AdS boundary. In this section we construct these modes and use the symplectic form of Section 1 to quantize them.

## Gauge fixing

Throughout the rest of this work we work in transverse-traceless gauge,

$$\begin{align}
h & =0, & \nabla _{\mu}^{(0)}h^{\mu \nu}=0
\end{align}$$

In this gauge the linearized equation of motion reduces to

$$\begin{align}
\nabla ^{(0)2}h_{\mu \nu}+2h_{\mu \nu} & =0
\end{align}$$

The reachability of this gauge choice, and its interpretation as a choice of representative rather than a quotient by boundary symmetries, are discussed in Appendix C.

## Operator factorization

To solve the gauge-fixed equation, we introduce the first-order differential operator $\mathcal{D}$,

$$\begin{align}
\mathcal{D}_{\mu}^{~\nu} & =\varepsilon^{(0)}{} _{\mu}^{~\rho \nu}\nabla _{\rho}^{(0)}
\end{align}$$

The second-order equation can then be written as

$$\begin{align}
(\nabla ^{(0)2}+2)h_{\mu \nu} & =(\mathcal{D}_{\mu}^{~\alpha}+\delta _{\mu}^{~\alpha})(\mathcal{D}_{\nu}^{~\beta}-\delta _{\nu}^{~\beta})h_{\alpha \beta}=0
\end{align}$$

Since the two first-order factors commute on TT perturbations, we work with the two branches satisfying

$$\begin{align}
\mathcal{D}h_{\pm,N} & =\mp h_{\pm,N}
\end{align}$$

Here the subscripts $\pm$ distinguish the two branches, and $N$ denotes an abstract label for solutions within each branch.

In the following subsections we solve these first-order equations explicitly.

## Killing symmetries

We use the Killing symmetries of global $\mathrm{AdS}_{3}$ to construct the solutions. The Casimir operators of $\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{R})_{L}\times \mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{R})_{R}$ are

$$\begin{align}
\mathcal{C} & =\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{0}}\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{0}}-\mathcal{L}_{\xi _{-1}}\mathcal{L}_{\xi _{1}}+i\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{0}} \\
\mathcal{\bar{C}} & =\mathcal{L}_{\bar{\xi}_{0}}\mathcal{L}_{\bar{\xi}_{0}}-\mathcal{L}_{\bar{\xi}_{-1}}\mathcal{L}_{\bar{\xi}_{1}}+i\mathcal{L}_{\bar{\xi}_{0}}
\end{align}$$

Acting on a metric perturbation $h_{\mu \nu}$, they give

$$\begin{align}
\mathcal{C}h_{\mu \nu} & =-\frac{1}{4}\nabla ^{(0)2}h_{\mu \nu}+\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon^{(0)}{} ^{\rho \sigma}_{~~~\mu}\nabla ^{(0)}_{\sigma}h_{\rho \nu}+\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon^{(0)}{} ^{\rho \sigma}_{~~~\nu}\nabla ^{(0)}_{\sigma}h_{\mu \rho}-\frac{3}{2}h_{\mu \nu} \\
\mathcal{\bar{C}}h_{\mu \nu} & =-\frac{1}{4}\nabla ^{(0)2}h_{\mu \nu}-\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon^{(0)}{} ^{\rho \sigma}_{~~~\mu}\nabla ^{(0)}_{\sigma}h_{\rho \nu}-\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon^{(0)}{} ^{\rho \sigma}_{~~~\nu}\nabla ^{(0)}_{\sigma}h_{\mu \rho}-\frac{3}{2}h_{\mu \nu}
\end{align}$$

Equivalently, after imposing the TT conditions, these actions can be written in terms of $\mathcal{D}$ as

$$\begin{align}
\mathcal{C}h_{\mu \nu} & =-\frac{1}{4}\mathcal{D}_{\mu}^{~\alpha}\mathcal{D}_{\alpha}^{~\beta}h_{\beta \nu}-\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{D}_{\mu}^{~\alpha}h_{\alpha \nu}-\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{D}_{\nu}^{~\alpha}h_{\mu \alpha}-\frac{3}{4}h_{\mu \nu} \\
\mathcal{\bar{C}}h_{\mu \nu} & =-\frac{1}{4}\mathcal{D}_{\mu}^{~\alpha}\mathcal{D}_{\alpha}^{~\beta}h_{\beta \nu}+\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{D}_{\mu}^{~\alpha}h_{\alpha \nu}+\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{D}_{\nu}^{~\alpha}h_{\mu \alpha}-\frac{3}{4}h_{\mu \nu}
\end{align}$$

## Solving the modes

We now use the Killing symmetries to construct the Brown-Henneaux mode representatives. These solutions organize naturally into highest-weight representations of $\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})_{L}\times \mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})_{R}$.

Using $\mathcal{D}h_{\pm,N}=\mp h_{\pm,N}$ in the Casimir formulas gives the eigenvalues in the two branches:

$$\begin{align}
\mathcal{C}h_{+,N,\mu \nu} & =0 \\
\mathcal{\bar{C}}h_{+,N,\mu \nu} & =-2h_{+,N,\mu \nu}
\end{align}$$

and

$$\begin{align}
\mathcal{C}h_{-,N,\mu \nu} & =-2h_{-,N,\mu \nu} \\
\mathcal{\bar{C}}h_{-,N,\mu \nu} & =0
\end{align}$$

For a primary state $h_{\pm,0,\mu \nu}$, the highest-weight conditions require annihilation by the raising operators $\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{1}}$ and $\mathcal{L}_{\bar{\xi}_{1}}$. We therefore impose

$$\begin{align}
\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{1}}h_{\pm,0,\mu \nu} & =0 \\
\mathcal{L}_{\bar{\xi}_{1}}h_{\pm,0,\mu \nu} & =0
\end{align}$$

Let the conformal weights of the primary be $(h_{\pm},\bar{h}_{\pm})$, namely

$$\begin{align}
\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{0}}h_{\pm,0,\mu \nu} & =-ih_{\pm}h_{\pm,0,\mu \nu} \\
\mathcal{L}_{\bar{\xi}_{0}}h_{\pm,0,\mu \nu} & =-i \bar{h}_{\pm}h_{\pm,0,\mu \nu}
\end{align}$$

Then the Casimir eigenvalues can be written in terms of these weights as

$$\begin{align}
\mathcal{C}h_{\pm,0,\mu \nu} & =(-h^{2}_{\pm}+h_{\pm})h_{\pm,0,\mu \nu} \\
\mathcal{\bar{C}}h_{\pm,0,\mu \nu} & =(-\bar{h}_{\pm}^{2}+\bar{h}_{\pm})h_{\pm,0,\mu \nu}
\end{align}$$

Solving these algebraic relations yields

$$\begin{align}
(h_{+,1},\bar{h}_{+,1}) & =(0,2) \\
(h_{+,2},\bar{h}_{+,2}) & =(1,2)
\end{align}$$

and

$$\begin{align}
(h_{-,1},\bar{h}_{-,1}) & =(2,0) \\
(h_{-,2},\bar{h}_{-,2}) & =(2,1)
\end{align}$$

Roots with negative weights are discarded by the regularity and positive-frequency conditions discussed in Appendix C. In the following discussion we keep only the $(h_{+,1},\bar{h}_{+,1})=(0,2)$ and $(h_{-,1},\bar{h}_{-,1})=(2,0)$ sectors. The mixed roots $(1,2)$ and $(2,1)$ solve the Casimir eigenvalue equations, but Appendix C explains why they are proper-gauge representatives rather than independent physical degrees of freedom.

Solving the highest-weight conditions for the $(2,0)$ primary, which we label by the subscript $-$, gives

$$\begin{align}
h^{\mu \nu}_{-,0} & =8\sqrt{ 3G } \frac{e^{-4it}}{(1+r^{2})^{2}}\bar{\xi}_{1}^{\mu}\bar{\xi}_{1}^{\nu}
\end{align}$$

Its descendants are

$$\begin{align}
h^{\mu \nu}_{-,n} & =\sqrt{ \frac{6}{n!(n+3)!} }\mathcal{L}_{\xi _{-1}}^{n}h^{\mu \nu}_{-,0}
\end{align}$$

For the $(0,2)$ primary, which we label by the subscript $+$, we have

$$\begin{align}
h^{\mu \nu}_{+,0} & =8\sqrt{ 3G } \frac{e^{-4it}}{(1+r^{2})^{2}}\xi_{1}^{\mu}\xi_{1}^{\nu}
\end{align}$$

Its descendants are

$$\begin{align}
h^{\mu \nu}_{+,\bar{n}} & =\sqrt{ \frac{6}{\bar{n}!(\bar{n}+3)!} }\mathcal{L}_{\bar{\xi}_{-1}}^{\bar{n}}h_{+,0}^{\mu \nu},\quad \bar{n}\in \mathbb{N}
\end{align}$$

The normalization constants are fixed by the condition $\Omega[h,h^{*}]=-i$. Locally, these modes are pure gauge: they can be written as

$$\begin{align}
h_{\mu \nu} & =\nabla _{\mu}\zeta _{\nu}+\nabla _{\nu}\zeta _{\mu}
\end{align}$$

where $\zeta _{\mu}$ is a vector field. For the $(2,0)$ primary and its descendants, the corresponding gauge parameters $\zeta _{-,n}^{\mu}$ are

$$\begin{align}
\zeta _{-,0}^{t} & =\frac{i\sqrt{ 3G }}{3} e^{-2i(t-\phi)} \frac{r^{4}}{(1+r^{2})^{2}} \\
\zeta _{-,0}^{r} & =-\frac{\sqrt{ 3G }}{3}e^{-2i(t-\phi)}  \frac{r(3+2r^{2})}{1+r^{2}} \\
\zeta _{-,0}^{\phi} & =-\frac{i\sqrt{ 3G }}{3}e^{-2i(t-\phi)} \frac{3+r^{2}}{1+r^{2}}
\end{align}$$

and

$$\begin{align}
\zeta ^{\mu}_{-,n} & =\sqrt{ \frac{6}{n!(n+3)!} }\mathcal{L}_{\xi _{-1}}^{n}\zeta _{-,0}^{\mu}
\end{align}$$

For the $(0,2)$ primary and its descendants, we have

$$\begin{align}
\zeta ^{t}_{+,0} & =\frac{i\sqrt{ 3G }}{3}e^{-2i(t+\phi)} \frac{r^{4}}{(1+r^{2})^{2}} \\
\zeta ^{r}_{+,0} & =-\frac{\sqrt{ 3G }}{3}e^{-2i(t+\phi)} \frac{r(3+2r^{2})}{1+r^{2}} \\
\zeta ^{\phi}_{+,0} & =\frac{i\sqrt{ 3G }}{3}e^{-2i(t+\phi)} \frac{3+r^{2}}{1+r^{2}}
\end{align}$$

and

$$\begin{align}
\zeta ^{\mu}_{+,\bar{n}} & =\sqrt{ \frac{6}{\bar{n}!(\bar{n}+3)!} }\mathcal{L}_{\bar{\xi}_{-1}}^{\bar{n}}\zeta _{+,0}^{\mu}
\end{align}$$

Although these perturbations are locally pure gauge, their gauge parameters are not proper at the AdS boundary. They therefore survive as boundary degrees of freedom rather than being quotiented out as gauge redundancies.

## Mode expansion and symplectic pairing

We now evaluate the symplectic form derived in Section 1 on the TT representatives. After gauge fixing, the symplectic form becomes

$$\begin{align}
\Omega & =-\frac{1}{16\pi G}\int _{\Sigma}\mathrm{d}^{2}x\sqrt{ \sigma ^{(0)} }\tau ^{(0)} _{\mu}\left(\frac{1}{2}\delta h^{\nu \rho}\wedge \nabla ^{(0)\mu}\delta h_{\nu \rho}-\delta h^{\nu \rho}\wedge\nabla ^{(0)}_{\rho}\delta h^{\mu}_{~\nu}\right) \\
 & =-\frac{1}{32\pi G}\int _{\Sigma}\mathrm{d}^{2}x\sqrt{ \sigma ^{(0)} }\tau ^{(0)} _{\mu}\delta h^{\nu \rho}\wedge \nabla ^{(0)\mu}\delta h_{\nu \rho}+\frac{1}{16\pi G}\int _{\partial \Sigma}\mathrm{d}x\sqrt{ h^{(0)} }\tau ^{(0)} _{\mu}n^{(0)}_{\nu}\delta h^{\mu}_{~\rho}\wedge \delta h^{\nu \rho}
\end{align}$$

Let $X_{-,n}$ and $X_{+,\bar n}$ be the tangent vectors on the linearized solution space corresponding to $h_{-,n}$ and $h_{+,\bar n}$:

$$\begin{align}
X_{-,n} & =\int \mathrm{d}^{3}xh^{\mu \nu}_{-,n} \frac{\delta}{\delta h^{\mu \nu}}, & X_{+,\bar{n}} & =\int \mathrm{d}^{3}xh^{\mu \nu}_{+,\bar{n}} \frac{\delta}{\delta h_{\mu \nu}}
\end{align}$$

With the normalization above, one finds

$$\begin{align}
\Omega[h_{-,n},h^{*}_{-,m}] & =-i\delta _{nm}, & \Omega[h_{+,\bar{n}},h^{*}_{+,\bar{m}}] & =-i\delta _{\bar{n}\bar{m}}
\end{align}$$

and all other pairings vanish. Thus a general on-shell configuration in the Brown-Henneaux sector can be expanded as

$$\begin{align}
h_{\mu \nu} & =\sum ^{\infty}_{n=0}\left(a_{-,n}h_{-,n,\mu \nu}+a^{\dagger}_{-,n}h^{*}_{-,n,\mu \nu}\right)+\sum ^{\infty}_{\bar{n}=0}(a_{+,\bar{n}}h_{+,\bar{n},\mu \nu}+a^{\dagger}_{+,\bar{n}}h^{*}_{+,\bar{n},\mu \nu})
\end{align}$$

Substituting the mode expansion into the symplectic form gives the discrete oscillator phase space

$$
\Omega=-i\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\delta a_{-,n}\wedge\delta a^{\dagger}_{-,n}-i\sum_{\bar n=0}^{\infty}\delta a_{+,\bar n}\wedge\delta a^{\dagger}_{+,\bar n}.
$$

## Quantization

We have reduced the linearized Brown-Henneaux phase space to a set of uncoupled oscillators. We now promote the classical coefficients $(a,a^{\dagger})$ to operators. Using Dirac's prescription $[\cdot,\cdot]=i\{\cdot,\cdot\}$, we obtain the non-vanishing commutators


$$\begin{align}
[a_{-,n},a^{\dagger}_{-,m}] & =\delta _{nm}, & [a_{+,\bar{n}},a^{\dagger}_{+,\bar{m}}] & =\delta _{\bar{n}\bar{m}}
\end{align}$$

and all other commutators vanish. The vacuum $\ket{0}$ is defined by

$$\begin{align}
a_{-,n}\ket{0} =a_{+,\bar{n}}\ket{0} =0, \quad n,\bar{n}\in \mathbb{N}
\end{align}$$

The linearized Hilbert space is generated by acting on $|0\rangle$ with the creation operators. A convenient Fock basis is

$$\begin{align}
\ket{\left\{N_{-,n},N_{+,\bar{n}}\right\}} & =\prod ^{\infty}_{n=0} \frac{(a^{\dagger}_{-,n})^{N_{-,n}}}{\sqrt{ N_{-,n}! }} \prod ^{\infty}_{\bar{n}=0} \frac{(a^{\dagger}_{+,\bar{n}})^{N_{+,\bar{n}}}}{\sqrt{ N_{+,\bar{n}}! }} \ket{0} 
\end{align}$$
