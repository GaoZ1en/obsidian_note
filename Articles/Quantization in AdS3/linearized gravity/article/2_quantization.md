# 2. Quantization of Brown-Henneaux edge modes

Pure Einstein gravity in three dimensions has no local propagating bulk degree of freedom. The non-trivial linearized modes compatible with Brown-Henneaux boundary conditions are instead generated by diffeomorphisms that do not vanish at the AdS boundary. In this section we construct these modes and use the symplectic form of Section 1 to quantize their coefficients.

## 2.1 Highest-weight boundary gravitons

We first solve the linearized equations by organizing the solutions into representations of the exact $\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{R})_L\oplus\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{R})_R$ isometry algebra. For a separated contravariant perturbation

$$
h^{\mu\nu}=e^{-i\omega t}e^{im\phi}f^{\mu\nu}(r),
$$

the Cartan generators act as

$$
\mathcal{L}_{\xi_0}h^{\mu\nu}=-\frac{i}{2}(\omega+m)h^{\mu\nu},\qquad
\mathcal{L}_{\bar{\xi}_0}h^{\mu\nu}=-\frac{i}{2}(\omega-m)h^{\mu\nu}.
$$

The left-moving primary is defined by the highest-weight conditions

$$
\mathcal{L}_{\xi_1}h_{L,0}^{\mu\nu}=0,\qquad
\mathcal{L}_{\xi_0}h_{L,0}^{\mu\nu}=-2ih_{L,0}^{\mu\nu},\qquad
\mathcal{L}_{\bar{\xi}_a}h_{L,0}^{\mu\nu}=0,\quad a=0,\pm1.
$$

These equations fix $\omega=m=2$. Solving the radial equations gives

$$
h_{L,0}^{\mu\nu}
=8\sqrt{3G}\,\frac{e^{-4it}}{(1+r^2)^2}\bar{\xi}_1^\mu\bar{\xi}_1^\nu.
$$

Similarly, the right-moving primary obeys

$$
\mathcal{L}_{\bar{\xi}_1}h_{R,0}^{\mu\nu}=0,\qquad
\mathcal{L}_{\bar{\xi}_0}h_{R,0}^{\mu\nu}=-2ih_{R,0}^{\mu\nu},\qquad
\mathcal{L}_{\xi_a}h_{R,0}^{\mu\nu}=0,\quad a=0,\pm1,
$$

and is

$$
h_{R,0}^{\mu\nu}
=8\sqrt{3G}\,\frac{e^{-4it}}{(1+r^2)^2}\xi_1^\mu\xi_1^\nu.
$$

Both primaries satisfy

$$
\nabla^{(0)}_\mu h^{\mu\nu}=0,\qquad g^{(0)}_{\mu\nu}h^{\mu\nu}=0,\qquad
(\nabla^{(0)2}+2)h_{\mu\nu}=0.
$$

They are also pure gauge in the local bulk sense:

$$
h_{L,0}^{\mu\nu}=\nabla^{(0)\mu}\zeta_{L,0}^{\nu}+\nabla^{(0)\nu}\zeta_{L,0}^{\mu},\qquad
h_{R,0}^{\mu\nu}=\nabla^{(0)\mu}\zeta_{R,0}^{\nu}+\nabla^{(0)\nu}\zeta_{R,0}^{\mu}.
$$

However, the vectors $\zeta_{L,0}$ and $\zeta_{R,0}$ do not vanish at the AdS boundary. Therefore these perturbations are not quotiented out by the Brown-Henneaux phase-space construction. They are the linearized boundary gravitons.

The descendants are generated by the lowering operators of the two $\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{R})$ factors:

$$
\begin{align}
h_{L,n}^{\mu\nu}
&=\sqrt{\frac{6}{n!(n+3)!}}\,
\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{-1}}^n h_{L,0}^{\mu\nu},\\
h_{R,\bar n}^{\mu\nu}
&=\sqrt{\frac{6}{\bar n!(\bar n+3)!}}\,
\mathcal{L}_{\bar{\xi}_{-1}}^{\bar n}h_{R,0}^{\mu\nu},
\end{align}
$$

with $n,\bar n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq0}$. The mode $h_{L,n}$ has left excitation level $n+2$, while $h_{R,\bar n}$ has right excitation level $\bar n+2$. The absence of levels $0$ and $1$ reflects the fact that the exact $\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{R})$ isometries are not physical boundary oscillators.

The explicit gauge parameters and the normalization calculation are collected in Appendix C.

## 2.2 Mode expansion and symplectic pairing

We now use the symplectic form derived in Section 1. Let $X_{L,n}$ and $X_{R,\bar n}$ be the tangent vectors on the linearized solution space corresponding to $h_{L,n}$ and $h_{R,\bar n}$:

$$
X_{L,n}=\int\mathrm{d}^3x\,h_{L,n}^{\mu\nu}\frac{\delta}{\delta h^{\mu\nu}},\qquad
X_{R,\bar n}=\int\mathrm{d}^3x\,h_{R,\bar n}^{\mu\nu}\frac{\delta}{\delta h^{\mu\nu}}.
$$

The bilinear symplectic pairing is defined by

$$
\Omega[h_1,h_2]=X_{h_2}\cdot X_{h_1}\cdot\Omega.
$$

With the normalization above, one finds

$$
\Omega[h_{L,n},h^*_{L,m}]=-i\delta_{nm},\qquad
\Omega[h_{R,\bar n},h^*_{R,\bar m}]=-i\delta_{\bar n\bar m},\qquad
\Omega[h_{L,n},h_{R,\bar m}]=0.
$$

Thus a real linearized boundary-graviton configuration can be expanded as

$$
h_{\mu\nu}
=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left(a_{L,n}h_{L,n,\mu\nu}
+a_{L,n}^*h^*_{L,n,\mu\nu}\right)
+\sum_{\bar n=0}^{\infty}\left(a_{R,\bar n}h_{R,\bar n,\mu\nu}
+a_{R,\bar n}^*h^*_{R,\bar n,\mu\nu}\right).
$$

Substituting the mode expansion into the symplectic form gives the discrete oscillator phase space

$$
\Omega
=-i\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\delta a_{L,n}\wedge\delta a^*_{L,n}
-i\sum_{\bar n=0}^{\infty}\delta a_{R,\bar n}\wedge\delta a^*_{R,\bar n}.
$$

Equivalently, after the reindexing $k=n+2$ and $\bar k=\bar n+2$, the independent positive-frequency edge oscillators are labelled by $k,\bar k\geq2$.

## 2.3 Quantization

The corresponding classical Poisson brackets are

$$
\{a_{L,n},a^*_{L,m}\}=-i\delta_{nm},\qquad
\{a_{R,\bar n},a^*_{R,\bar m}\}=-i\delta_{\bar n\bar m},\qquad
\{a_{L,n},a^*_{R,\bar m}\}=0.
$$

Canonical quantization promotes the coefficients to operators satisfying

$$
[\hat a_{L,n},\hat a_{L,m}^{\dagger}]=\delta_{nm},\qquad
[\hat a_{R,\bar n},\hat a_{R,\bar m}^{\dagger}]=\delta_{\bar n\bar m},\qquad
[\hat a_{L,n},\hat a_{R,\bar m}^{\dagger}]=0.
$$

The vacuum is defined by

$$
\hat a_{L,n}|0\rangle=0,\qquad
\hat a_{R,\bar n}|0\rangle=0,\qquad n,\bar n\geq0.
$$

The linearized Hilbert space is generated by acting on $|0\rangle$ with the creation operators. Since $h_{L,n}$ and $h_{R,\bar n}$ are large-diffeomorphism modes, this is the perturbative Brown-Henneaux boundary-graviton Hilbert space rather than a Fock space of local bulk gravitons.
