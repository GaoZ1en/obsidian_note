# Quantization

Pure Einstein gravity in three dimensions has no local propagating bulk degree of freedom. The non-trivial linearized modes compatible with Brown-Henneaux boundary conditions are instead generated by diffeomorphisms that do not vanish at the AdS boundary. In this section we construct these modes and use the symplectic form of Section 1 to quantize them.

## gauge fixing

through out the rest of this work, we will impose the TT gauge, i.e., we have

$$\begin{align}
h & :=g^{(0)}_{\mu \nu}h^{\mu \nu}=0, & \nabla _{\mu}^{(0)}h^{\mu \nu}=0
\end{align}$$

therefore the equation of motion is simplified to

$$\begin{align}
\nabla ^{(0)2}h_{\mu \nu}+2h_{\mu \nu} & =0
\end{align}$$

we will discuss the reachability of this gauge choice in Appendix ?

## operator factorization

to solve the gauge fixed equation of motion, we introduce the first order differential operators $\displaystyle{\mathcal{D}}$

$$\begin{align}
\mathcal{D}_{\mu}^{~\nu} & =\varepsilon _{\mu}^{~\nu \rho}\nabla _{\rho}^{(0)}
\end{align}$$

the second order equation becomes

$$\begin{align}
(\nabla ^{(0)2}+2)h_{\mu \nu} & =(\mathcal{D}_{\mu}^{~\alpha}+\delta _{\mu}^{~\alpha})(\mathcal{D}_{\nu}^{~\beta}-\delta _{\nu}^{~\beta})h_{\alpha \beta}=0
\end{align}$$

since $\displaystyle{[\mathcal{D}+I,\mathcal{D}-I]=0}$, the general solution to the second order equation can be expressed as a linear combination of two independent branches satisfying the respective first order equations

$$\begin{align}
\mathcal{D}h_{\pm,N} & =\mp h_{\pm,N}
\end{align}$$

here, subscripts $\displaystyle{\pm}$ are introduced to distinguish the two branches, and $\displaystyle{N}$ stands for abstract index of solutions in each branch.

in the following subsections, we will explicitly solve these two first order equations.

## the Killing symmetries

we exploit the Killing symmetries of global $\displaystyle{\mathrm{AdS}_{3}}$ to contruct the solutions. we introduce the Casimir operators of the Killing symmetry algebra $\displaystyle{\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{R})_{L}\times \mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{R})_{R}}$ as

$$\begin{align}
\mathcal{C} & =\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{0}}\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{0}}-\mathcal{L}_{\xi _{-1}}\mathcal{L}_{\xi _{1}}+i\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{0}} \\
\mathcal{\bar{C}} & =\mathcal{L}_{\bar{\xi}_{0}}\mathcal{L}_{\bar{\xi}_{0}}-\mathcal{L}_{\bar{\xi}_{-1}}\mathcal{L}_{\bar{\xi}_{1}}+i\mathcal{L}_{\bar{\xi}_{0}}
\end{align}$$

act on the metric perturbation $\displaystyle{h_{\mu \nu}}$, we have

$$\begin{align}
\mathcal{C}h_{\mu \nu} & =-\frac{1}{4}\nabla ^{(0)2}h_{\mu \nu}+\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon ^{\rho \sigma}_{~~~\mu}\nabla ^{(0)}_{\sigma}h_{\rho \nu}+\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon ^{\rho \sigma}_{~~~\nu}\nabla ^{(0)}_{\sigma}h_{\mu \rho}-\frac{3}{2}h_{\mu \nu} \\
\mathcal{\bar{C}}h_{\mu \nu} & =-\frac{1}{4}\nabla ^{(0)2}h_{\mu \nu}-\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon ^{\rho \sigma}_{~~~\mu}\nabla ^{(0)}_{\sigma}h_{\rho \nu}-\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon ^{\rho \sigma}_{~~~\nu}\nabla ^{(0)}_{\sigma}h_{\mu \rho}-\frac{3}{2}h_{\mu \nu}
\end{align}$$

equivalently, by imposing the gauge conditions, these actions can be written in terms of the operator $\displaystyle{\mathcal{D}}$

$$\begin{align}
\mathcal{C}h_{\mu \nu} & =-\frac{1}{4}\mathcal{D}_{\mu}^{~\alpha}\mathcal{D}_{\alpha}^{~\beta}h_{\beta \nu}-\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{D}_{\mu}^{~\alpha}h_{\alpha \nu}-\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{D}_{\nu}^{~\alpha}h_{\mu \alpha}-\frac{3}{4}h_{\mu \nu} \\
\mathcal{\bar{C}}h_{\mu \nu} & =-\frac{1}{4}\mathcal{D}_{\mu}^{~\alpha}\mathcal{D}_{\alpha}^{~\beta}h_{\beta \nu}+\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{D}_{\mu}^{~\alpha}h_{\alpha \nu}+\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{D}_{\nu}^{~\alpha}h_{\mu \alpha}-\frac{3}{4}h_{\mu \nu}
\end{align}$$

## solving the modes

we now use the Killing symmetries to construct a complete set of normalizable modes. these solutions organize naturally into highest-weight representations of $\displaystyle{\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})_{L}\times \mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})_{R}}$.

applying $\displaystyle{\mathcal{D}h_{\pm,N}=\mp h_{\pm,N}}$ to the Casimir operators gives their eigenvalues in each branches

$$\begin{align}
\mathcal{C}h_{+,N,\mu \nu} & =0 \\
\mathcal{\bar{C}}h_{+,N,\mu \nu} & =-2h_{+,N,\mu \nu}
\end{align}$$

and

$$\begin{align}
\mathcal{C}h_{-,N,\mu \nu} & =-2h_{-,N,\mu \nu} \\
\mathcal{\bar{C}}h_{-,N,\mu \nu} & =0
\end{align}$$

for a primary state $\displaystyle{h_{\pm,0,\mu \nu}}$, the highest-weight conditions require annihilation by the raising operators $\displaystyle{\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{1}}}$ and $\displaystyle{\mathcal{L}_{\bar{\xi}_{1}}}$. we therefore impose

$$\begin{align}
\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{1}}h_{\pm,0,\mu \nu} & =0 \\
\mathcal{L}_{\bar{\xi}_{1}}h_{\pm,0,\mu \nu} & =0
\end{align}$$

suppose the conformal dimensions of the primary are $\displaystyle{(h_{\pm},\bar{h}_{\pm})}$, namely

$$\begin{align}
\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{0}}h_{\pm,0,\mu \nu} & =-ih_{\pm}h_{\pm,0,\mu \nu} \\
\mathcal{L}_{\bar{\xi}_{0}}h_{\pm,0,\mu \nu} & =-i \bar{h}_{\pm}h_{\pm,0,\mu \nu}
\end{align}$$

then the Casimir eigenvalues can be written in terms of these conformal dimensions as

$$\begin{align}
\mathcal{C}h_{\pm,0,\mu \nu} & =(-h^{2}_{\pm}+h_{\pm})h_{\pm,0,\mu \nu} \\
\mathcal{\bar{C}}h_{\pm,0,\mu \nu} & =(-\bar{h}_{\pm}^{2}+\bar{h}_{\pm})h_{\pm,0,\mu \nu}
\end{align}$$

solving these relations yields

$$\begin{align}
(h_{+,1},\bar{h}_{+,1}) & =(0,2) \\
(h_{+,2},\bar{h}_{+,2}) & =(1,2)
\end{align}$$

and

$$\begin{align}
(h_{-,1},\bar{h}_{-,1}) & =(2,0) \\
(h_{-,2},\bar{h}_{-,2}) & =(2,1)
\end{align}$$

The roots with negative weights are discarded by the regularity and positive-frequency conditions explains in Appendix E. In the following discussion, we will only keep the $\displaystyle{(h_{+,1},\bar{h}_{+,1})=(0,2)}$ and $\displaystyle{(h_{-,1},\bar{h}_{-,1})=(2,0)}$ sectors. The mixed roots $\displaystyle{(1,2)}$ and $\displaystyle{(2,1)}$ solve the Casimir eigenvalue equations, but Appendix E explains why they are proper gauge representatives rather than independent physical degree of freedoms.

to solve the primaries explicitly, we write down the highest weight conditions satisfied by the $\displaystyle{(0,2)}$ primary


$$\begin{align}
\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{0}}h^{\mu \nu}_{-,0} & =-2ih_{-,0}^{\mu \nu} \\
\mathcal{L}_{\xi _{1}}h^{\mu \nu}_{-,0} & =0 \\
\mathcal{L}_{\bar{\xi}_{0}}h^{\mu \nu}_{-,0} & =0 \\
\mathcal{L}_{\bar{\xi}_{1}}h^{\mu \nu}_{-,0} & =0
\end{align}$$

which gives

$$\begin{align}
h^{\mu \nu}_{-,0} & =8\sqrt{ 3G } \frac{e^{-4it}}{(1+r^{2})^{2}}\bar{\xi}_{1}^{\mu}\bar{\xi}_{1}^{\nu}
\end{align}$$

and its descendants

$$\begin{align}
h^{\mu \nu}_{-,n} & =\sqrt{ \frac{6}{n!(n+3)!} }\mathcal{L}_{\xi _{-1}}^{n}h^{\mu \nu}_{-,0}
\end{align}$$

and for the $\displaystyle{(0,2)}$ primary, the highest weight conditions are

$$\begin{align}
\mathcal{L}_{\xi _{0}}h_{+,0}^{\mu \nu} & =0 \\
\mathcal{L}_{\xi _{1}}h_{+,0}^{\mu \nu} & =0 \\
\mathcal{L}_{\bar{\xi}_{0}}h_{+,0}^{\mu \nu} & =-2ih_{+,0}^{\mu \nu} \\
\mathcal{L}_{\bar{\xi}_{1}}h_{+,0}^{\mu \nu} & =0
\end{align}$$

which gives

$$\begin{align}
h^{\mu \nu}_{+,0} & =8\sqrt{ 3G } \frac{e^{-4it}}{(1+r^{2})^{2}}\xi_{1}^{\mu}\xi_{1}^{\nu}
\end{align}$$

and its descendants

$$\begin{align}
h^{\mu \nu}_{+,\bar{n}} & =\sqrt{ \frac{6}{\bar{n}!(\bar{n}+3)!} }\mathcal{L}_{\bar{\xi}_{-1}}^{\bar{n}}h_{+,0}^{\mu \nu},\quad \bar{n}\in \mathbb{N}
\end{align}$$

the normalization constants are fixed by the confition $\displaystyle{\Omega[h,h^{*}]=-i}$. these modes are actually pure gauge, i.e., they can be written as the form

$$\begin{align}
h_{\mu \nu} & =\nabla _{\mu}\zeta _{\nu}+\nabla _{\nu}\zeta _{\mu}
\end{align}$$

where $\displaystyle{\zeta _{\mu}}$ is a vector field. for the $\displaystyle{(2,0)}$ primary and its desendants, the corresponding gauge parameter $\displaystyle{\zeta _{-,n}^{\mu}}$ are given by

$$\begin{align}
\zeta _{-,0}^{t} & =\frac{i\sqrt{ 3G }}{3} e^{-2i(t-\phi)} \frac{r^{4}}{(1+r^{2})^{2}} \\
\zeta _{-,0}^{r} & =-\frac{\sqrt{ 3G }}{3}e^{-2i(t-\phi)}  \frac{r(3+2r^{2})}{1+r^{2}} \\
\zeta _{-,0}^{\phi} & =-\frac{i\sqrt{ 3G }}{3}e^{-2i(t-\phi)} \frac{3+r^{2}}{1+r^{2}}
\end{align}$$

and

$$\begin{align}
\zeta ^{\mu}_{-,n} & =\sqrt{ \frac{6}{n!(n+3)!} }\mathcal{L}_{\xi _{-1}}^{n}\zeta _{-,0}^{\mu}
\end{align}$$

for the $\displaystyle{(0,2)}$ primary and its descendants, we have

$$\begin{align}
\zeta ^{t}_{+,0} & =\frac{i\sqrt{ 3G }}{3}e^{-2i(t+\phi)} \frac{r^{4}}{(1+r^{2})^{2}} \\
\zeta ^{r}_{+,0} & =-\frac{\sqrt{ 3G }}{3}e^{-2i(t+\phi)} \frac{r(3+2r^{2})}{1+r^{2}} \\
\zeta ^{\phi}_{+,0} & =\frac{i\sqrt{ 3G }}{3}e^{-2i(t+\phi)} \frac{3+r^{2}}{1+r^{2}}
\end{align}$$

and

$$\begin{align}
\zeta ^{\mu}_{+,\bar{n}} & =\sqrt{ \frac{6}{\bar{n}(\bar{n}+3)!} }\zeta _{+,0}^{\mu}
\end{align}$$

## Mode expansion and symplectic pairing

We now evaluate the symplectic form derived in Section 1. after gauge fixing, the symplectic form becomes

$$\begin{align}
\Omega & =-\frac{1}{16\pi G}\int _{\Sigma}\mathrm{d}^{2}x\sqrt{ \sigma ^{(0)} }\tau ^{(0)} _{\mu}\left(\frac{1}{2}\delta h^{\nu \rho}\wedge \nabla ^{(0)\mu}\delta h_{\nu \rho}-\delta h^{\nu \rho}\wedge\nabla ^{(0)}_{\rho}\delta h^{\mu}_{~\nu}\right) \\
 & =-\frac{1}{32\pi G}\int _{\Sigma}\mathrm{d}^{2}x\sqrt{ \sigma ^{(0)} }\tau ^{(0)} _{\mu}\delta h^{\nu \rho}\wedge \nabla ^{(0)\mu}\delta h_{\nu \rho}+\frac{1}{16\pi G}\int _{\partial \Sigma}\mathrm{d}x\sqrt{ h^{(0)} }\tau ^{(0)} _{\mu}n^{(0)}_{\nu}\delta h^{\mu}_{~\rho}\wedge \delta h^{\nu \rho}
\end{align}$$

Let $X_{-,n}$ and $X_{+,\bar n}$ be the tangent vectors on the linearized solution space corresponding to $h_{-,n}$ and $h_{+,\bar n}$:

$$\begin{align}
X_{-,n} & =\int \mathrm{d}^{3}xh^{\mu \nu}_{-,n} \frac{\delta}{\delta h^{\mu \nu}}, & X_{+,\bar{n}} & =\int \mathrm{d}^{3}xh^{\mu \nu}_{+,\bar{n}} \frac{\delta}{\delta h_{\mu \nu}}
\end{align}$$

With the normalization above, one finds

$$\begin{align}
\Omega[h_{-,n},h^{*}_{-,m}] & =-i\delta _{nm}, & \Omega[h_{+,\bar{n}},h^{*}_{+,\bar{m}}] & =-i\delta _{\bar{n}\bar{m}}
\end{align}$$

and all other pairs vanish. Thus a general onshell configuration can be expanded as

$$\begin{align}
h_{\mu \nu} & =\sum ^{\infty}_{n=0}\left(a_{-,n}h_{-,n,\mu \nu}+a^{\dagger}_{-,n}h^{*}_{-,n,\mu \nu}\right)+\sum ^{\infty}_{\bar{n}=0}(a_{+,\bar{n}}h_{+,\bar{n},\mu \nu}+a^{\dagger}_{+,\bar{n}}h^{*}_{+,\bar{n},\mu \nu})
\end{align}$$

Substituting the mode expansion into the symplectic form gives the discrete oscillator phase space

$$
\Omega=-i\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\delta a_{-,n}\wedge\delta a^{\dagger}_{-,n}-i\sum_{\bar n=0}^{\infty}\delta a_{+,\bar n}\wedge\delta a^{\dagger}_{+,\bar n}.
$$

## Quantization

so far, we have reduced the theory to a set of uncoupled oscillators. we now perform canonical quantization. we promote the classical coefficients $\displaystyle{(a,a^{\dagger})}$ to operators and using Dirac's prescription $\displaystyle{[\cdot,\cdot]=i\left\{\cdot,\cdot\right\}}$, we obtain the non-vanishing commutators


$$\begin{align}
[a_{-,n},a^{\dagger}_{-,m}] & =\delta _{nm}, & [a_{+,\bar{n}},a^{\dagger}_{+,\bar{m}}] & =\delta _{\bar{n}\bar{m}}
\end{align}$$

and all other commutators vanish. The vacuum $\displaystyle{\ket{0}}$ is defined by

$$\begin{align}
a_{-,n}\ket{0} =a_{+,\bar{n}}\ket{0} =0, \quad n,\bar{n}\in \mathbb{N}
\end{align}$$

The linearized Hilbert space is generated by acting on $|0\rangle$ with the creation operators, a convenient Fock basis are expanded by

$$\begin{align}
\ket{\left\{N_{-,n},N_{+,\bar{n}}\right\}} & =\prod ^{\infty}_{n=0} \frac{(a^{\dagger}_{-,n})^{N_{-,n}}}{\sqrt{ N_{-,n}! }} \prod ^{\infty}_{\bar{n}=0} \frac{(a^{\dagger}_{+,\bar{n}})^{N_{+,\bar{n}}}}{\sqrt{ N_{+,\bar{n}}! }} \ket{0} 
\end{align}$$
