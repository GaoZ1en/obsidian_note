# Appendix B: Noether charge and surface charge variation

This appendix records the charge formulas used in Section 3. All currents are written with the background measure $\sqrt{-g^{(0)}}$.

## B.1 Noether potential

For a diffeomorphism generated by $\xi^\mu$, the gravitational Noether potential can be written as

$$
Q_\xi^{\mu\nu}[g]
=-\frac{1}{16\pi G}\frac{\sqrt{-g}}{\sqrt{-g^{(0)}}}
\left(\nabla_g^\mu\xi^\nu-\nabla_g^\nu\xi^\mu\right).
$$

Expanding $g_{\mu\nu}=g^{(0)}_{\mu\nu}+h_{\mu\nu}$ gives

$$
Q_\xi^{\mu\nu}=Q_\xi^{(1)\mu\nu}+Q_\xi^{(2)\mu\nu}+\cdots,
$$

after dropping the background constant contribution. Define

$$
\begin{align}
A_\xi^{(0)\mu\nu}
&=\nabla^{(0)\mu}\xi^\nu-\nabla^{(0)\nu}\xi^\mu,\\
A_\xi^{(1)\mu\nu}
&=-h^{\mu\rho}\nabla^{(0)}_\rho\xi^\nu
+h^{\nu\rho}\nabla^{(0)}_\rho\xi^\mu
+\xi^\rho\left(\nabla^{(0)\mu}h^\nu{}_\rho-\nabla^{(0)\nu}h^\mu{}_\rho\right),\\
D^{(2)}
&=\frac{1}{8}h^2-\frac{1}{4}h_{\rho\sigma}h^{\rho\sigma}.
\end{align}
$$

The first two non-trivial terms are

$$
\begin{align}
Q_\xi^{(1)\mu\nu}
&=-\frac{1}{16\pi G}
\left(A_\xi^{(1)\mu\nu}+\frac{1}{2}hA_\xi^{(0)\mu\nu}\right),\\
Q_\xi^{(2)\mu\nu}
&=-\frac{1}{16\pi G}
\left(A_\xi^{(2)\mu\nu}+\frac{1}{2}hA_\xi^{(1)\mu\nu}
+D^{(2)}A_\xi^{(0)\mu\nu}\right),
\end{align}
$$

where

$$
\begin{align}
A_\xi^{(2)\mu\nu}
&=h^\mu{}_\rho h^{\rho\sigma}\nabla^{(0)}_\sigma\xi^\nu
-h^\nu{}_\rho h^{\rho\sigma}\nabla^{(0)}_\sigma\xi^\mu\\
&\quad+\xi^\lambda\left[
h^{\mu\rho}\left(\nabla^{(0)\nu}h_{\rho\lambda}
-\nabla^{(0)}_\rho h^\nu{}_\lambda\right)
-h^{\nu\rho}\left(\nabla^{(0)\mu}h_{\rho\lambda}
-\nabla^{(0)}_\rho h^\mu{}_\lambda\right)
\right].
\end{align}
$$

## B.2 Surface charge variation

The surface charge variation is

$$
\delta H_\xi
=\int_{\partial\Sigma}\mathrm{d}x\sqrt{h^{(0)}}\,
\tau_\mu n_\nu\,k_\xi^{\nu\mu}.
$$

The covariant phase space two-form entering this expression is

$$
k_\xi^{\mu\nu}
=\delta Q_\xi^{\mu\nu}-2\xi^{[\mu}\theta^{\nu]}(\delta h;h).
$$

For the leading charge variation, one may use the linearized expression

$$
\begin{align}
k_\xi^{(0)\mu\nu}[\delta h]
&=\frac{1}{16\pi G}\Big[
-\xi^\rho\left(\nabla^{(0)\mu}\delta h^\nu{}_\rho
-\nabla^{(0)\nu}\delta h^\mu{}_\rho\right)
-\frac{1}{2}\delta h
\left(\nabla^{(0)\mu}\xi^\nu-\nabla^{(0)\nu}\xi^\mu\right)\\
&\quad-\xi^\nu\left(\nabla^{(0)\mu}\delta h
-\nabla^{(0)}_\rho\delta h^{\mu\rho}\right)
+\xi^\mu\left(\nabla^{(0)\nu}\delta h
-\nabla^{(0)}_\rho\delta h^{\nu\rho}\right)\\
&\quad-\delta h^{\nu\rho}\nabla^{(0)}_\rho\xi^\mu
+\delta h^{\mu\rho}\nabla^{(0)}_\rho\xi^\nu
\Big].
\end{align}
$$

This is the expression used to evaluate the Brown-Henneaux central extension.

## B.3 Brown-Henneaux central term

The asymptotic Killing vectors obey

$$
[\xi_n,\xi_m]=-i(n-m)\xi_{n+m}+\mathcal{O}(r^{-4}),
$$

and similarly for the barred sector. The Poisson bracket of charges is computed from

$$
\{H_\xi,H_\zeta\}\simeq X_\zeta\cdot\delta H_\xi.
$$

Evaluating the surface term gives

$$
\begin{align}
\{H_n,H_m\}
&=-i(n-m)H_{n+m}
-\frac{i}{8G}n(n^2-1)\delta_{n+m,0},\\
\{\bar H_n,\bar H_m\}
&=-i(n-m)\bar H_{n+m}
-\frac{i}{8G}n(n^2-1)\delta_{n+m,0},\\
\{H_n,\bar H_m\}&=0.
\end{align}
$$

Thus the central charge in the standard Virasoro convention is

$$
c=\frac{3}{2G}.
$$
