# Appendix B: Normalization of the highest-weight modes

In this appendix, we determine the normalization constants of the highest-weight modes $f_{+,n,\bar{n}}$ and $f_{-,n,\bar{n}}$ by imposing

$$\begin{align}
\Omega[f_{+,n,\bar{n}},f_{+,n,\bar{n}}^{*}] & =-i, \\
\Omega[f_{-,n,\bar{n}},f_{-,n,\bar{n}}^{*}] & =-i.
\end{align}$$

For convenience, we first recall the primary modes obtained in the main text:

$$\begin{align}
f_{+,0,0}^{\mu} & =C_{+} \frac{e^{-i(\mu _{+}+2)t}}{(1+r^{2})^{(\mu _{+}+2)/2}}\xi _{1}^{\mu}, \\
f_{-,0,0}^{\mu} & =C_{-} \frac{e^{-i(\mu _{-}+2)t}}{(1+r^{2})^{(\mu _{-}+2)/2}}\bar{\xi}_{1}^{\mu}.
\end{align}$$

The descendant modes are generated by repeated actions of $\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{-1}}$ and $\mathcal{L}_{\bar{\xi}_{-1}}$ on these primaries.

We begin with the normalization of the primary states. The symplectic form can be written as

$$\begin{align}
\Omega[\delta _{1}A,\delta _{2}A] & =\int _{\Sigma} \mathrm{d}^{2}x\sqrt{ \sigma }\tau _{\mu}\varepsilon ^{\mu \nu \rho}\left(\mathcal{D}_{\nu}^{\sigma}+\frac{k}{2\pi}\delta _{\nu}^{\sigma}\right)\delta _{1} A_{\sigma}\wedge \delta _{2} A_{\rho}.
\end{align}$$

Evaluating this expression on the primary modes gives

$$\begin{align}
\Omega[f_{+,0,0},f_{+,0,0}^{*}] & =-\frac{i\pi\bar{\mu}}{1+\mu _{+}}|C_{+}|^{2}=-i, \\
\Omega[f_{-,0,0},f_{-,0,0}^{*}] & =-\frac{i\pi\bar{\mu}}{1+\mu _{-}}|C_{-}|^{2}=-i.
\end{align}$$

Therefore the normalization constants are fixed to be

$$\begin{align}
C_{+} & =\sqrt{ \frac{\mu _{+}+1}{\pi \bar{\mu}} }, \\
C_{-} & =\sqrt{ \frac{\mu _{-}+1}{\pi \bar{\mu}} }.
\end{align}$$

We now turn to the descendant modes. Since the mode towers are generated by Killing symmetries, the key input is the invariance of the symplectic form under the Lie derivative:

$$\begin{align}
\mathcal{L}_{\xi}\Omega[A_{1},A_{2}] & =0 \\
\implies \Omega[\mathcal{L}_{\xi}A_{1},A_{2}]+\Omega[A_{1},\mathcal{L}_{\xi}A_{2}] & =0.
\end{align}$$

Motivated by this property, we define the normalized descendants by

$$\begin{align}
f_{+,n,\bar{n}}^{\mu} & =\sqrt{ \frac{1}{n!\bar{n}!(\mu _{+})_{n}(\mu _{+}+2)_{\bar{n}}} }\mathcal{L}_{\xi _{-1}}^{n}\mathcal{L}_{\bar{\xi}_{-1}}^{\bar{n}}f_{+,0,0}^{\mu}, \\
f_{-,n,\bar{n}}^{\mu} & =\sqrt{ \frac{1}{n!\bar{n}!(\mu _{-}+2)_{n}(\mu _{-})_{\bar{n}}} }\mathcal{L}_{\xi _{-1}}^{n}\mathcal{L}_{\bar{\xi}_{-1}}^{\bar{n}}f_{-,0,0}^{\mu}.
\end{align}$$

These definitions immediately imply the following recursion relations. For the $+$ branch,

$$ \begin{align}
\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{0}}f_{+,n,\bar{n}}^{\mu} & =-i\left(\frac{\mu _{+}}{2}+n\right)f_{+,n,\bar{n}}^{\mu}, \\
\mathcal{L}_{\xi _{-1}}f_{+,n,\bar{n}}^{\mu} & =\sqrt{ (n+1)(\mu _{+}+n) }f_{+,n+1,\bar{n}}^{\mu}, \\
\mathcal{L}_{\xi _{1}}f_{+,n,\bar{n}}^{\mu} & =-\sqrt{ n(\mu _{+}+n-1) }f_{+,n-1,\bar{n}}^{\mu}, \\
\mathcal{L}_{\bar{\xi}_{0}}f_{+,n,\bar{n}}^{\mu} & =-i\left(\frac{\mu _{+}}{2}+1+\bar{n}\right)f_{+,n,\bar{n}}^{\mu}, \\
\mathcal{L}_{\bar{\xi}_{-1}}f_{+,n,\bar{n}}^{\mu} & =\sqrt{ (\bar{n}+1)(\mu _{+}+2+\bar{n}) }f_{+,n,\bar{n}+1}^{\mu}, \\
\mathcal{L}_{\bar{\xi}_{1}}f_{+,n,\bar{n}}^{\mu} & =-\sqrt{ \bar{n}(\mu _{+}+1+\bar{n}) }f_{+,n,\bar{n}-1}^{\mu}.
\end{align} $$

For the $-$ branch, we similarly obtain

$$ \begin{align}
\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{0}}f_{-,n,\bar{n}}^{\mu} & =-i\left(\frac{\mu _{-}}{2}+1+n\right)f_{-,n,\bar{n}}^{\mu}, \\
\mathcal{L}_{\xi _{-1}}f_{-,n,\bar{n}}^{\mu} & =\sqrt{ (n+1)(\mu _{-}+2+n) }f_{-,n+1,\bar{n}}^{\mu}, \\
\mathcal{L}_{\xi _{1}}f_{-,n,\bar{n}}^{\mu} & =-\sqrt{ n(\mu _{-}+1+n) }f_{-,n-1,\bar{n}}^{\mu}, \\
\mathcal{L}_{\bar{\xi}_{0}}f_{-,n,\bar{n}}^{\mu} & =-i\left(\frac{\mu _{-}}{2}+\bar{n}\right)f_{-,n,\bar{n}}^{\mu}, \\
\mathcal{L}_{\bar{\xi}_{-1}}f_{-,n,\bar{n}}^{\mu} & =\sqrt{ (\bar{n}+1)(\mu _{-}+\bar{n}) }f_{-,n,\bar{n}+1}^{\mu}, \\
\mathcal{L}_{\bar{\xi}_{1}}f_{-,n,\bar{n}}^{\mu} & =-\sqrt{ \bar{n}(\mu _{-}-1+\bar{n}) }f_{-,n,\bar{n}-1}^{\mu}.
\end{align} $$

We now verify that these definitions indeed guarantee the desired normalization for all descendants. Since the proof for the $-$ branch is completely analogous, it is sufficient to consider the $+$ branch.

The base case, namely $\Omega[f_{+,0,0},f_{+,0,0}^{*}] = -i$, has already been established. Assume that for some fixed pair $(n,\bar{n})$ we have

$$\begin{align}
\Omega[f_{+,n,\bar{n}},f_{+,n,\bar{n}}^{*}] & = -i.
\end{align}$$

We first consider the step $(n,\bar{n})\to (n+1,\bar{n})$. Using the recursion relation, we find

$$\begin{align}
\Omega[f_{+,n+1,\bar{n}}, f_{+,n+1,\bar{n}}^{*}] & = \frac{1}{\sqrt{ (n+1)(\mu _{+}+n) }} \, \Omega[\mathcal{L}_{\xi _{-1}}f_{+,n,\bar{n}}, f_{+,n+1,\bar{n}}^{*}] \\
& = -\frac{1}{\sqrt{ (n+1)(\mu _{+}+n) }} \, \Omega[f_{+,n,\bar{n}}, \mathcal{L}_{\xi _{-1}}f_{+,n+1,\bar{n}}^{*}],
\end{align}$$

where the second equality follows from the Killing invariance of $\Omega$. Next, we take the complex conjugate of

$$\begin{align}
\mathcal{L}_{\xi _{1}}f_{+,n+1,\bar{n}} & = -\sqrt{ (n+1)(\mu _{+}+n) } \, f_{+,n,\bar{n}},
\end{align}$$

and use $\xi_{-1}^{*}=\xi_{1}$ to obtain

$$\begin{align}
\mathcal{L}_{\xi _{-1}}f_{+,n+1,\bar{n}}^{*} & = -\sqrt{ (n+1)(\mu _{+}+n) } \, f_{+,n,\bar{n}}^{*}.
\end{align}$$

Substituting this relation back into the symplectic product gives

$$\begin{align}
\Omega[f_{+,n+1,\bar{n}}, f_{+,n+1,\bar{n}}^{*}] & = -\frac{1}{\sqrt{ (n+1)(\mu _{+}+n) }} \, \Omega\left[f_{+,n,\bar{n}}, -\sqrt{ (n+1)(\mu _{+}+n) } \, f_{+,n,\bar{n}}^{*}\right] \\
& = \Omega[f_{+,n,\bar{n}}, f_{+,n,\bar{n}}^{*}] \\
& = -i.
\end{align}$$

The step $(n,\bar{n})\to (n,\bar{n}+1)$ is treated in exactly the same way. We start from

$$\begin{align}
\Omega[f_{+,n,\bar{n}+1}, f_{+,n,\bar{n}+1}^{*}] & = \frac{1}{\sqrt{ (\bar{n}+1)(\mu _{+}+2+\bar{n}) }} \, \Omega[\mathcal{L}_{\bar{\xi} _{-1}}f_{+,n,\bar{n}}, f_{+,n,\bar{n}+1}^{*}] \\
& = -\frac{1}{\sqrt{ (\bar{n}+1)(\mu _{+}+2+\bar{n}) }} \, \Omega[f_{+,n,\bar{n}}, \mathcal{L}_{\bar{\xi} _{-1}}f_{+,n,\bar{n}+1}^{*}].
\end{align}$$

We then take the complex conjugate of

$$\begin{align}
\mathcal{L}_{\bar{\xi} _{1}}f_{+,n,\bar{n}+1} & = -\sqrt{ (\bar{n}+1)(\mu _{+}+2+\bar{n}) } \, f_{+,n,\bar{n}},
\end{align}$$

and use $\bar{\xi}_{-1}^{*}=\bar{\xi}_{1}$ to obtain

$$\begin{align}
\mathcal{L}_{\bar{\xi} _{-1}}f_{+,n,\bar{n}+1}^{*} & = -\sqrt{ (\bar{n}+1)(\mu _{+}+2+\bar{n}) } \, f_{+,n,\bar{n}}^{*}.
\end{align}$$

Substituting this expression back yields

$$\begin{align}
\Omega[f_{+,n,\bar{n}+1}, f_{+,n,\bar{n}+1}^{*}] & = -\frac{1}{\sqrt{ (\bar{n}+1)(\mu _{+}+2+\bar{n}) }} \, \Omega\left[f_{+,n,\bar{n}}, -\sqrt{ (\bar{n}+1)(\mu _{+}+2+\bar{n}) } \, f_{+,n,\bar{n}}^{*}\right] \\
& = \Omega[f_{+,n,\bar{n}}, f_{+,n,\bar{n}}^{*}] \\
& = -i.
\end{align}$$

By induction, the normalization conditions hold for all highest-weight descendants:

$$\begin{align}
\Omega[f_{+,n,\bar{n}},f_{+,n,\bar{n}}^{*}] & =-i, \\
\Omega[f_{-,n,\bar{n}},f_{-,n,\bar{n}}^{*}] & =-i.
\end{align}$$
