# Conventions

All three descriptions use modes with time and angular dependence

$$\begin{align}
A^{\mu} & \sim e^{-i\omega t+im\phi}.
\end{align}$$

With

$$\begin{align}
\xi _{0} & =\frac{1}{2}(\partial _{t}-\partial _{\phi}), &
\bar{\xi}_{0} & =\frac{1}{2}(\partial _{t}+\partial _{\phi}),
\end{align}$$

the conformal weights are

$$\begin{align}
h & =\frac{\omega+m}{2}, &
\bar{h} & =\frac{\omega-m}{2}.
\end{align}$$

Thus

$$\begin{align}
E & =\omega=h+\bar{h}, &
J & =m=h-\bar{h}.
\end{align}$$

In the following comparison $n=0,1,2,\ldots$ denotes the radial quantum number of the propagating normalizable modes.

# Temporal gauge

In temporal gauge one sets $A^{t}=0$. After imposing the normalizability condition, the propagating modes have

$$\begin{align}
\omega _{n,m}^{\mathrm{temp}} & =2n+2+|m|, &
J & =m.
\end{align}$$

For $m=0$, the normalizable mode has only an angular component,

$$\begin{align}
A^{\phi}_{n,0} & \propto e^{-i(2n+2)t}(1+r^{2})^{-1}
P_{n}^{(0,1)}\left(\frac{r^{2}-1}{r^{2}+1}\right).
\end{align}$$

For $m\neq 0$, the normalizable mode has $A^{r}$ and $A^{\phi}$ components, with the same spectrum

$$\begin{align}
\omega _{n,m}^{\mathrm{temp}} & =2n+2+|m|.
\end{align}$$

The residual gauge transformations that preserve temporal gauge must satisfy

$$\begin{align}
\delta A_{t}=\partial _{t}\Lambda=0 .
\end{align}$$

Thus the residual gauge parameter in this gauge slice is time-independent,

$$\begin{align}
\Lambda=\Lambda(r,\phi).
\end{align}$$

In particular, temporal gauge can contain static large-gauge transformations, such as the pure-gauge winding configuration generated by $\Lambda=C\phi$, but it does not contain the chiral time-dependent large-gauge representatives that appear naturally in Lorenz gauge and in the highest-weight construction. This residual sector should be treated separately from the propagating oscillator spectrum.

# Lorenz gauge

In Lorenz gauge the transverse variables

$$\begin{align}
\Phi _{\pm} & =\frac{i}{2}f^{r}\pm \frac{1}{2}rf^{\phi}
\end{align}$$

give two families before quotienting by residual gauge transformations:

$$\begin{align}
\omega _{+,n,m} & =1+|m-1|+2n, \\
\omega _{-,n,m} & =1+|m+1|+2n.
\end{align}$$

The two families contain redundant representatives of the same physical states. The gauge-equivalent pairs are

$$\begin{align}
h^{\mu}_{+,m=0,n} & \sim h^{\mu}_{-,m=0,n}, \\
h^{\mu}_{+,m\geqslant 1,n+1} & \sim h^{\mu}_{-,m\geqslant 1,n}, \\
h^{\mu}_{+,m\leqslant -1,n} & \sim h^{\mu}_{-,m\leqslant -1,n+1}.
\end{align}$$

The null direction in these pairs is a proper gauge transformation. If

$$\begin{align}
p&=|m|, & N&\geq 1, & \omega&=p+2N ,
\end{align}$$

then a representative gauge parameter is

$$\begin{align}
\Lambda _{m,N}
&\propto e^{-i(p+2N)t+im\phi}
\frac{r^{p}}{(1+r^{2})^{p/2+1}}
{}_{2}F_{1}\left(1-N,N+p+1;2;\frac{1}{1+r^{2}}\right),
\end{align}$$

with

$$\begin{align}
\Lambda _{m,N}&=\mathcal{O}(r^{-2}), & r&\to\infty .
\end{align}$$

Thus these null directions vanish at the boundary and should be quotiented as ordinary gauge redundancy. After quotienting by this residual gauge redundancy and keeping the separate large-gauge edge representatives out of the propagating spectrum, the Lorenz-gauge spectrum is

$$\begin{align}
\omega _{n,m}^{\mathrm{Lorenz}} & =2n+2+|m|, &
J & =m.
\end{align}$$

The physical representatives may be chosen as follows:

| range of $m$ | Lorenz-gauge representative | conformal weights | energy and angular momentum |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $m\geqslant 1$ | $h^{\mu}_{-,m,n}\sim h^{\mu}_{+,m,n+1}$ | $(h,\bar{h})=(n+m+1,n+1)$ | $E=2+2n+m,\ J=m$ |
| $m=0$ | $g^{\mu}_{0,n}=\frac{1}{2}(h^{\mu}_{+,0,n}-h^{\mu}_{-,0,n})$ | $(h,\bar{h})=(n+1,n+1)$ | $E=2+2n,\ J=0$ |
| $m\leqslant -1$ | $h^{\mu}_{+,m,n}\sim h^{\mu}_{-,m,n+1}$ | $(h,\bar{h})=(n+1,n+1-m)$ | $E=2+2n-m,\ J=m$ |

Here $2+2n-m=2+2n+|m|$ in the last row.

# Highest-weight construction

The highest-weight construction organizes the modes into

$$\begin{align}
(0,0),\qquad (1,0),\qquad (0,1),\qquad (1,1)
\end{align}$$

representations of $\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})\times \mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})$.

The propagating normalizable modes are the descendants of the $(1,1)$ primary:

$$\begin{align}
\psi ^{\mu}_{(k,\bar{k}),(1,1)}
& =
\mathcal{L}_{\xi _{-1}}^{k}
\mathcal{L}_{\bar{\xi}_{-1}}^{\bar{k}}
\psi _{(1,1)}^{\mu},
\qquad k,\bar{k}=0,1,2,\ldots .
\end{align}$$

Their conformal weights are

$$\begin{align}
(h,\bar{h}) & =(1+k,1+\bar{k}),
\end{align}$$

so

$$\begin{align}
E & =2+k+\bar{k}, &
J & =k-\bar{k}.
\end{align}$$

This agrees with temporal and Lorenz gauge after the identification

$$\begin{align}
m & =k-\bar{k}, &
n & =\min(k,\bar{k}).
\end{align}$$

Equivalently,

$$\begin{align}
k & =n+\max(m,0), &
\bar{k} & =n+\max(-m,0).
\end{align}$$

Substituting this into the highest-weight result gives

$$\begin{align}
E & =2+2n+|m|, &
J & =m.
\end{align}$$

# Direct dictionary

The propagating sectors of the three descriptions match as follows:

| temporal-gauge label | Lorenz-gauge physical class | highest-weight descendant | $(E,J)$ |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $m\geqslant 1,\ n$ | $h^{\mu}_{-,m,n}\sim h^{\mu}_{+,m,n+1}$ | $\psi ^{\mu}_{(n+m,n),(1,1)}$ | $(2+2n+m,\ m)$ |
| $m=0,\ n$ | $g^{\mu}_{0,n}$ | $\psi ^{\mu}_{(n,n),(1,1)}$ | $(2+2n,\ 0)$ |
| $m\leqslant -1,\ n$ | $h^{\mu}_{+,m,n}\sim h^{\mu}_{-,m,n+1}$ | $\psi ^{\mu}_{(n,n-m),(1,1)}$ | $(2+2n-m,\ m)$ |

Thus all three methods give the same normalizable propagating spectrum:

$$\begin{align}
E_{n,m} & =\omega _{n,m}=2n+2+|m|, \\
J_{n,m} & =m.
\end{align}$$

# Explicit gauge transformation between temporal and covariant representatives

The agreement above can be made mode-by-mode at the level of the vector potential. Choose the phase convention for the covariant-gauge physical representative as

$$\begin{align}
A_{\mathrm{cov},n,m}^{\mu}
&=
\begin{cases}
-h_{-,m,n}^{\mu}, & m\geq 1, \\
g_{0,n}^{\mu}, & m=0, \\
h_{+,m,n}^{\mu}, & m\leq -1,
\end{cases}
\end{align}$$

so that its field strength agrees with the temporal-gauge representative,

$$\begin{align}
F[A_{\mathrm{cov},n,m}]_{\mu\nu}
&=
F[A_{\mathrm{temp},n,m}]_{\mu\nu}.
\end{align}$$

Then

$$\begin{align}
A_{\mathrm{cov},n,m}^{\mu}
&=
A_{\mathrm{temp},n,m}^{\mu}
+\nabla ^{\mu}\Lambda_{n,m}.
\end{align}$$

For $m=0$, the two representatives are already identical in this normalization:

$$\begin{align}
\Lambda_{n,0}&=0, &
g_{0,n}^{\mu}&=A_{\mathrm{temp},n,0}^{\mu}.
\end{align}$$

For $m\neq0$, set

$$\begin{align}
q&=|m|, &
\omega_{n,m}&=2n+2+q, &
y&=\frac{r^{2}-1}{r^{2}+1},
\end{align}$$

and define

$$\begin{align}
N_{n,q}
&=
\sqrt{\frac{1}{8\pi(n+1)(n+q+1)}} ,
\end{align}$$

$$\begin{align}
B_{n,q}(r)
&=
\left[-q r^{2}-2(q+1)\right]
r^{q}(1+r^{2})^{-(q+2)/2}
P_{n}^{(0,q+1)}(y) \\
&\quad
-4r^{q+2}(1+r^{2})^{-(q+4)/2}
\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}y}P_{n}^{(0,q+1)}(y).
\end{align}$$

The gauge parameter can be chosen as

$$\begin{align}
\Lambda_{n,m}
&=
i\,\mathrm{sgn}(m)\,
\frac{N_{n,|m|}}{\omega_{n,m}}\,
e^{-i\omega_{n,m}t+im\phi}
B_{n,|m|}(r).
\end{align}$$

Equivalently, since $A_{\mathrm{temp}}^{t}=0$, this parameter is read directly from the time component as

$$\begin{align}
\Lambda_{n,m}
&=
\frac{1+r^{2}}{i\omega_{n,m}}
A_{\mathrm{cov},n,m}^{t}.
\end{align}$$

A direct component check gives

$$\begin{align}
\nabla ^{t}\Lambda_{n,m}
&=
A_{\mathrm{cov},n,m}^{t}, \\
\nabla ^{r}\Lambda_{n,m}
&=
A_{\mathrm{cov},n,m}^{r}
-A_{\mathrm{temp},n,m}^{r}, \\
\nabla ^{\phi}\Lambda_{n,m}
&=
A_{\mathrm{cov},n,m}^{\phi}
-A_{\mathrm{temp},n,m}^{\phi}.
\end{align}$$

Thus the covariant-gauge and temporal-gauge representatives differ only by the proper gauge transformation generated by $\Lambda_{n,m}$. The expression contains only Jacobi polynomials and rational powers of $r$ and $1+r^{2}$; no $\log r$ term is required.

# Edge and large-gauge sectors

The comparison above only concerns normalizable propagating modes. The remaining sectors are gauge or edge data and are treated differently in the three files.

The important distinction is between proper gauge transformations and large gauge transformations:

| sector | gauge parameter near $r\to\infty$ | interpretation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Lorenz null directions with $N\geq1$ | $\Lambda=\mathcal{O}(r^{-2})$ | proper gauge redundancy |
| Lorenz $n=0$ edge modes | $\Lambda=\mathcal{O}(r^{0})$ with nonzero boundary value | large gauge transformations |
| highest-weight $(1,0)$ and $(0,1)$ descendants | $\Lambda=\mathcal{O}(r^{0})$ with chiral boundary dependence | large gauge transformations |
| temporal-gauge residual transformations | $\partial_t\Lambda=0$ | static residual gauge transformations |

In Lorenz gauge, the non-normalizable edge representatives are

$$\begin{align}
h_{+,m\geqslant 1,n=0}^{\mu},\qquad
h_{-,m\leqslant -1,n=0}^{\mu}.
\end{align}$$

They have vanishing field strength and are pure gauge in the bulk. Their gauge parameters are

$$\begin{align}
\Lambda _{+,m}
&=\frac{1}{im}e^{-im(t-\phi)}
\left(\frac{r}{\sqrt{1+r^{2}}}\right)^{m},
&m&\geq1,\\
\Lambda _{-,m}
&=\frac{1}{i(-m)}e^{im(t+\phi)}
\left(\frac{r}{\sqrt{1+r^{2}}}\right)^{-m},
&m&\leq-1 .
\end{align}$$

At the boundary,

$$\begin{align}
\Lambda _{+,m}
&\to \frac{1}{im}e^{-im(t-\phi)}, &
\Lambda _{-,m}
&\to \frac{1}{i(-m)}e^{im(t+\phi)} .
\end{align}$$

These are precisely the chiral large-gauge representatives that match the highest-weight $(1,0)$ and $(0,1)$ towers. They carry

$$\begin{align}
(h,\bar{h})&=(m,0), & m&\geq1,\\
(h,\bar{h})&=(0,-m), & m&\leq-1 .
\end{align}$$

They are not part of the normalizable propagating oscillator tower.

Temporal gauge does not show the same chiral representatives as residual transformations because preserving $A_{t}=0$ requires $\partial_t\Lambda=0$. Therefore the temporal-gauge residual large transformations are static boundary transformations, such as $\Lambda=C\phi$ or more generally time-independent boundary functions. To recover the chiral representatives one must either leave the temporal-gauge slice or accompany an isometry action by a compensating gauge transformation.

With these sectors kept separate, there is no contradiction between the three descriptions.
