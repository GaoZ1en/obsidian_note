# Diffeomorphism Invariance of a Particle Action with Gravitational and Electromagnetic Fields

I'll derive how the given expression appears in the variation of the action under an infinitesimal diffeomorphism by direct calculation.

## Setting Up the Calculation

We start with the relativistic point particle action in background gravitational and electromagnetic fields:

$$S_{p} = -m\int \mathrm{d}\tau \sqrt{-g_{\mu \nu} \frac{\mathrm{d}x^{\mu}}{\mathrm{d}\tau} \frac{\mathrm{d}x^{\nu}}{\mathrm{d}\tau}} + q\int \mathrm{d}\tau A_{\mu} \frac{\mathrm{d}x^{\mu}}{\mathrm{d}\tau}$$

with $U^{\mu} = \frac{\mathrm{d}x^{\mu}}{\mathrm{d}\tau}$ being the four-velocity.

## Transformation Rules Under Diffeomorphisms

Under an infinitesimal diffeomorphism generated by the vector field $\xi^{\mu}$, the various quantities transform as:

1. **Coordinates**: $\delta_{\xi} x^{\mu} = \xi^{\mu}(x)$
2. **Metric**: $\delta_{\xi} g_{\mu\nu} = \mathcal{L}_{\xi}g_{\mu\nu} = \nabla_{\mu}\xi_{\nu} + \nabla_{\nu}\xi_{\mu}$
3. **Vector potential**: $\delta_{\xi} A_{\mu} = \mathcal{L}_{\xi}A_{\mu} = \xi^{\nu}\nabla_{\nu}A_{\mu} + A_{\nu}\nabla_{\mu}\xi^{\nu}$
4. **Four-velocity**: $\delta_{\xi} U^{\mu} = \frac{d}{d\tau}\delta_{\xi}x^{\mu} + \delta_{\xi}\Gamma^{\mu}_{\nu\lambda}x^{\nu}x^{\lambda}$

## Direct Calculation

### Step 1: Variation of the First Term

Let's start with the variation of the first term in the action:

$$\delta_{\xi}\left(-m\int \mathrm{d}\tau \sqrt{-g_{\mu \nu}U^{\mu}U^{\nu}}\right)$$

Defining proper time normalization where $g_{\mu\nu}U^{\mu}U^{\nu} = -1$, this becomes:

$$-m\int \mathrm{d}\tau \delta_{\xi}\left(\sqrt{-g_{\mu \nu}U^{\mu}U^{\nu}}\right) = -\frac{m}{2}\int \mathrm{d}\tau \frac{\delta_{\xi}(g_{\mu \nu}U^{\mu}U^{\nu})}{\sqrt{-g_{\mu \nu}U^{\mu}U^{\nu}}}$$

Substituting the variation of the metric:

$$\delta_{\xi}(g_{\mu \nu}U^{\mu}U^{\nu}) = (\nabla_{\mu}\xi_{\nu} + \nabla_{\nu}\xi_{\mu})U^{\mu}U^{\nu} + g_{\mu \nu}\delta_{\xi}(U^{\mu}U^{\nu})$$

### Step 2: Variation of the Second Term

For the electromagnetic coupling term:

$$\delta_{\xi}\left(q\int \mathrm{d}\tau A_{\mu}U^{\mu}\right) = q\int \mathrm{d}\tau \left[(\delta_{\xi}A_{\mu})U^{\mu} + A_{\mu}\delta_{\xi}U^{\mu}\right]$$

Using the Lie derivative of $A_{\mu}$:

$$\delta_{\xi}A_{\mu} = \xi^{\nu}\nabla_{\nu}A_{\mu} + A_{\nu}\nabla_{\mu}\xi^{\nu} = \xi^{\nu}F_{\nu\mu} + \nabla_{\mu}(A_{\nu}\xi^{\nu})$$

where $F_{\nu\mu} = \nabla_{\nu}A_{\mu} - \nabla_{\mu}A_{\nu}$ is the electromagnetic field tensor.

### Step 3: Combining Terms and Integration by Parts

After substituting these variations into the action and performing several integration by parts, we can isolate terms that involve the covariant divergence:

$$\int \mathrm{d}^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\nabla_{\mu}[T^{\mu \nu}\xi_{\nu}+j^{\mu}A^{\nu}\xi_{\nu}]$$

where $T^{\mu\nu}$ and $j^{\mu}$ are precisely the stress-energy tensor and current density given in (1.3).

### Step 4: Boundary Terms from Integration by Parts

The integration by parts also produces boundary terms of the form:

$$\int \mathrm{d}\tau \frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\tau}\left((mU_{\nu}+qA_{\nu})\xi^{\nu}\right)$$

This represents the total derivative term arising from the worldline integral.

## Key Connection

To make the connection explicit, let's use the definitions of $T^{\mu\nu}$ and $j^{\mu}$ from (1.3):

$$T^{\mu \nu} = m\int \mathrm{d}\tau U^{\mu}U^{\nu} \frac{\delta^{4}(x-x(\tau))}{\sqrt{-g}}$$
$$j^{\mu} = q\int \mathrm{d}\tau U^{\mu} \frac{\delta^{4}(x-x(\tau))}{\sqrt{-g}}$$

When we integrate:

$$\int \mathrm{d}^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\nabla_{\mu}[T^{\mu \nu}\xi_{\nu}+j^{\mu}A^{\nu}\xi_{\nu}]$$

The delta functions in $T^{\mu\nu}$ and $j^{\mu}$ effectively convert the spacetime integral into a worldline integral. After careful manipulation, we can show this equals:

$$\int \mathrm{d}\tau \frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\tau}\left((mU_{\nu}+qA_{\nu})\xi^{\nu}\right) + (\text{bulk terms})$$

The bulk terms contain the equations of motion, which vanish on-shell. Thus, the equality in equation (1.2) is established by direct calculation.

This result is a manifestation of Noether's theorem applied to diffeomorphism invariance, where the conserved current corresponds to energy-momentum conservation modified by electromagnetic interactions.
