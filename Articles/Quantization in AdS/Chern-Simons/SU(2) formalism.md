here we choose the anti-Hermitian basis of $\mathfrak{su}(2)$ as $\displaystyle{T_{a}}$ with $a=1,2,3$, which satisfy the normalization

$$\begin{align}
\mathrm{Tr}(T_{a}T_{b}) & =-\frac{1}{2}\delta _{ab}
\end{align}$$

and commutation relation

$$\begin{align}
[T_{a},T_{b}] & =\varepsilon _{abc}T_{c}
\end{align}$$

define the bracket as $\displaystyle{\braket{T_{a}T_{b}}=-\mathrm{Tr}(T_{a}T_{b})}$. the action for Chern-Simons theory with gauge group $\displaystyle{\mathrm{SU}(2)}$ is given by

$$\begin{align}
S_{\text{bulk}} & =\frac{k}{4\pi}\int _{M} \braket{ A\wedge \mathrm{d}A+\frac{2}{3}A\wedge A\wedge A }
\end{align}$$

take a variation of the action, we have

$$\begin{align}
\delta S_{\text{bulk}} & =\int _{M}\braket{ \delta A\wedge E } +\theta|_{\Sigma _{f}}-\theta|_{\Sigma _{i}}+\alpha |_{\Gamma} \\
E & =\frac{k}{2\pi} F \\
\theta & =\frac{k}{4\pi}\int _{\Sigma}\braket{ A\wedge \delta A }  \\
\alpha & =\frac{k}{4\pi}\int _{\Gamma}\braket{ A\wedge \delta A }  \\
\implies \omega & =\frac{k}{4\pi}\int _{\Sigma}\braket{ \delta A\wedge \delta A }
\end{align}$$

where $\displaystyle{F=\mathrm{d}A+A\wedge A}$ is the curvature of the gauge field $A$.

## linear approximation

in the linear approximation, the eom is given by

$$\begin{align}
\mathrm{d}A & =0 \\
\implies \mathcal{D}_{\mu}^{~\nu}A_{\nu}^{a} & =0, a=1,2,3
\end{align}$$

where $\displaystyle{\mathcal{D}_{\mu}^{~\nu}=\varepsilon _{\mu}^{~\alpha \nu}\partial _{\alpha}}$ is the linearized eom operator. similar to the $\mathrm{U}(1)$ case, the solutions of the eom can be classified into three branches, which are generated by the gauge parameters

1. $\displaystyle{(0,0)}$ branch: $\displaystyle{\Lambda _{(0,0)}=0}$, which gives the trivial vacuum solution $\displaystyle{\psi _{(0,0)}^{a}=0}$.
1. $\displaystyle{(1,0)}$ branch: $\displaystyle{\Lambda _{(1,0)}= -i\sqrt{ \frac{2}{k} }\frac{e^{-i(t-\phi)}r}{(1+r^{2})^{1/2}}}$, which gives the non-trivial vacuum solution $\displaystyle{\psi _{(1,0)}^{a}=\mathrm{d}\Lambda _{(1,0)}T_{a}}$ and its descendants $\displaystyle{\mathcal{L}_{\xi _{-1}}^{n}\psi _{(1,0)}^{a}}$. only for $\displaystyle{k> 0}$
1. $\displaystyle{(0,1)}$ branch: $\displaystyle{\Lambda _{(0,1)}=-i\sqrt{ \frac{2}{-k} }\frac{e^{-i(t+\phi)}r}{(1+r^{2})^{1/2}}}$, which gives the non-trivial vacuum solution $\displaystyle{\psi _{(0,1)}^{a}=\mathrm{d}\Lambda _{(0,1)}^{a}T_{a}}$ and its descendants $\displaystyle{\mathcal{L}_{\bar{\xi} _{-1}}^{n}\psi _{(0,1)}^{a}}$. only for $\displaystyle{k< 0}$

the normalization constants are fixed by the symplectic form, which gives

$$\begin{align}
\omega[A,A^{*}] & =X_{A^{*}}\cdot X_{A}\cdot \omega \\
 & =-i
\end{align}$$

where $\displaystyle{X_{A}}$ is defined as

$$\begin{align}
X_{A} & =\int \mathrm{d}^{3}x\sqrt{ -g } A_{\mu}^{a}\frac{\delta}{\delta A_{\mu}^{a}}
\end{align}$$

the extra $\displaystyle{\sqrt{ 2 }}$ factor in the normalization constants compared to the $\mathrm{U}(1)$ case is due to the normalization of the $\mathfrak{su}(2)$ generators

## nonlinear theory

for simplicity, we only consider the case $\displaystyle{k> 0}$ in the following discussion. we denote the gauge parameters as $\displaystyle{\Lambda_{0}=0}$, $\displaystyle{\Lambda_{n}=\mathcal{L}_{\xi _{-1}}^{n-1}\Lambda_{(1,0)}}$ and $\displaystyle{\Lambda_{-n}=\Lambda ^{*}_{n}}$ for $\displaystyle{n\geqslant 1}$.

in the nonlinear theory, the modes are generated by the gauge parameters

$$\begin{align}
A & =g^{-1}\mathrm{d}g
\end{align}$$

take the gauge parameter as

$$\begin{align}
g & =e^{\Lambda}
\end{align}$$

then we have

$$\begin{align}
A & =g^{-1}\mathrm{d}g \\
 & =e^{-\Lambda}\mathrm{d}e^{\Lambda} \\
 & =\mathrm{d}\Lambda -\frac{1}{2}[\Lambda ,\mathrm{d}\Lambda ]+\cdots
\end{align}$$

so we can construct a general solution as

$$\begin{align}
\Lambda & =\sum _{a=1}^{3}\sum _{n=0}^{\infty} (a^{a}_{n}\Lambda ^{a}_{n}+a^{a\dagger}_{n}\Lambda ^{a}_{-n})T_{a} \\
A & =e^{-\Lambda}\mathrm{d}e^{\Lambda}
\end{align}$$

the symplectic form is given by

$$\begin{align}
\omega & =\frac{k}{4\pi}\int _{\Sigma}\braket{ \delta A\wedge \delta A }  \\
 & =\frac{k}{4\pi}\int _{\Sigma}\braket{ \delta (e^{-\Lambda}\mathrm{d}e^{\Lambda})\wedge \delta (e^{-\Lambda}\mathrm{d}e^{\Lambda}) }  \\
 & =\dots
\end{align}$$

## boundary Lagrangian and boundary conditions

similar as the $\displaystyle{U(1)}$ case, for $\displaystyle{k> 0}$, we have to cancel the term $\displaystyle{A^{+}\delta A^{-}}$ by adding a boundary Lagrangian

$$\begin{align}
S_{\text{bdy}} & =-\frac{k}{4\pi}\int _{\Gamma}\mathrm{d}^{2}x\braket{ A_{+}A_{-} }
\end{align}$$

in the following discussion, we will only consider terms supported on the bulk and the final and initial Cauchy surfaces, and ignore the terms supported on the boundary $\Gamma$, since they are fixed by the boundary Lagrangian.

## Noether charge

for a gauge parameter

$$\begin{align}
\Lambda & =\Lambda ^{a}T_{a}
\end{align}$$

the corresponding gauge transformation is given by

$$\begin{align}
X_{\Lambda} & =\int \mathrm{d}^{3}x\sqrt{ -g }(\mathrm{d}\Lambda+[A,\Lambda])\frac{\delta}{\delta A}
\end{align}$$

act on the action, we have

$$\begin{align}
X_{\Lambda}\cdot \delta S & \approx \alpha _{\Lambda}|_{\Sigma _{f}}-\alpha _{\Lambda}|_{\Sigma _{i}}\\
\alpha _{\Lambda} & = \frac{k}{4\pi} \int _{\Sigma} \braket{ D\Lambda \wedge A }
\end{align}$$

then the corresponding Noether charge is given by

$$\begin{align}
H_{\Lambda} & =X_{\Lambda}\cdot \theta-\alpha _{\Lambda} \\
 & =\frac{k}{2\pi}\int _{\Sigma}\braket{ A\wedge D\Lambda }
\end{align}$$

by rescaling the gauge parameters $\displaystyle{\Lambda _{n}^{a}\to \tilde{\Lambda}^{a}_{n}=\sqrt{ kn }\Lambda _{n}^{a}, n\geqslant 0}$ and $\displaystyle{\tilde{\Lambda} _{-n}^{a}=\tilde{\Lambda}_{n}^{*a}}$, we can recover the $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ Kac-Moody algebra (by some algebraic manipulation)

$$\begin{align}
\left\{\tilde{H}^{a}_{m},\tilde{H}^{b}_{n}\right\} & =f^{abc}\tilde{H}^{c}_{m+n}+km\delta _{m+n,0}\delta ^{ab}
\end{align}$$
