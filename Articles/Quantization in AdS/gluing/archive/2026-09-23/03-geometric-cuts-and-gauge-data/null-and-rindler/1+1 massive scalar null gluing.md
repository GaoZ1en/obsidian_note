The goal is to define the regional observable algebras for null cuts, release their common characteristic histories, and compare the resulting Peierls bracket with the independently computed global bracket. The first construction uses one wall-to-wall cut. The two-cut construction below uses intersecting null lines: the left and right wedges supply the two incoming histories that reconstruct the future region $F$ and its observable algebra.

The spacetime setup is

$$\begin{align}
M & =[t_{i},t_{f}]\times[0,L]
\end{align}$$

put

$$\begin{align}
u & =t-x, & v & =t+x \\
M_{-} & =\left\{u<0\right\}\cap M, & M_{+} & =\left\{u>0\right\}\cap M \\
N & =\left\{u=0,0\leqslant v\leqslant 2L\right\}
\end{align}$$

consider the regional action

$$\begin{align}
S[\phi] & =\dfrac{1}{2}\int _{M}\left(\dot{\phi}^{2}-\phi'^{2}-m^{2}\phi ^{2}\right)\mathrm{d}t\mathrm{d}x \\
 & =S_{+}[\phi _{+}]+S_{-}[\phi _{-}] \\
S_{\pm}[\phi _{\pm}] & =\int _{M_{\pm}}\left(\partial _{u}\phi _{\pm}\partial _{v}\phi _{\pm}-\dfrac{m^{2}}{4}\phi ^{2}_{\pm}\right)\mathrm{d}u\mathrm{d}v
\end{align}$$

with Dirichlet boundary condition at physical boundary

$$\begin{align}
\phi(t,0) & =\phi(t,L)=0
\end{align}$$

## Phase Space and Observable Algebra

Denote

$$\begin{align}
q_{\pm}(v) & =\phi _{\pm}(0,v)
\end{align}$$

take a variation of regional actions

$$\begin{align}
\delta S_{-} & =-\dfrac{1}{2}\int _{M_{-}}\mathrm{d}u\mathrm{d}v(4\partial _{u}\partial _{v}\phi _{-}+m^{2}\phi _{-})\delta \phi _{-}+\int _{N}\mathrm{d}v\partial _{v}q_{-}\delta q_{-}-\theta _{-,i} \\
\delta S_{+} & =-\dfrac{1}{2}\int _{M_{+}}\mathrm{d}u\mathrm{d}v\left(4\partial _{u}\partial _{v}\phi _{+}+m^{2}\phi _{+}\right)\delta \phi _{+}-\int _{N}\mathrm{d}v\partial _{v}q_{+}\delta q_{+}\mathrm{d}v+\theta _{+,f} \\
\implies \Omega _{\pm} & =\int _{N}\mathrm{d}v\partial _{v}\delta q_{\pm}\wedge\delta q_{\pm}
\end{align}$$

denote

$$\begin{align}
\Pi_{-} & =\partial _{v}q_{-}, & \Pi_{+} & =-\partial _{v}q_{+}
\end{align}$$

define the regional solution spaces by

$$\begin{align}
\mathcal{P}_{\pm} & =\left\{\phi _{\pm}: 4\partial _{u}\partial _{v}\phi _{\pm}+m^{2}\phi _{\pm}=0,(\text{Dirichlet BC})\right\}
\end{align}$$

and the observable algebra

$$\begin{align}
\mathcal{H}_{\pm} & =\left\{F\in C^{\infty}(\mathcal{P}_{\pm},\mathbb{R}):\exists X_{F}\in \Gamma(T\mathcal{P}_{\pm}), X_{F}\cdot \Omega _{\pm}=-\delta F\right\}
\end{align}$$

## Algebra Gluing

Define the characteristic data space on the cut

$$\begin{align}
\mathcal{B}_{N} & =\left\{q\right\}, & \Omega _{N}=\int _{N}\mathrm{d}v\partial _{v}\delta q\wedge \delta q
\end{align}$$

we have the bijective characteristic trace maps

$$\begin{align}
\gamma _{\pm}: & \mathcal{P}_{\pm}\to \mathcal{B}_{N}, & \phi _{\pm} & \mapsto q_{\pm} \\
\gamma _{\pm}^{*}\Omega _{\pm} & =\Omega _{N} \\
\implies \mathcal{P}_{-} & \simeq\mathcal{B}_{N}\simeq \mathcal{P}_{+}
\end{align}$$

impose the gluing condition

$$\begin{align}
 & q_{+}=q_{-}=q \\
\xRightarrow{\text{varaitional principle}} & \Pi_{+}+\Pi_{-}=0
\end{align}$$

define the matched phase space

$$\begin{align}
\mathcal{C} & =\left\{(\phi _{-},\phi _{+})\in \mathcal{P}_{-}\times \mathcal{P}_{+}:\gamma _{-}\phi _{-}=\gamma _{+}\phi _{+}\right\}
\end{align}$$

with the symplectic form

$$\begin{align}
\Omega _{\mathcal{C}}=\mathrm{pr}^{*}_{\pm}\Omega _{\pm}
\end{align}$$

here $\displaystyle{\mathrm{pr}_{\pm}}$ are the projections onto the regional factors

$$\begin{align}
\mathrm{pr}_{\pm}: & \mathcal{C}\to \mathcal{P}_{\pm}, & \mathrm{pr}_{\pm}(\phi _{-},\phi _{+}) & =\phi _{\pm}
\end{align}$$

we define the glued algebra as

$$\begin{align}
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{glue}} & =\mathcal{H}(\mathcal{C},\Omega _{\mathcal{C}})
\end{align}$$

consider the assembly map

$$\begin{align}
A: & \mathcal{C}\to \mathcal{P} \\
A(\phi _{-},\phi _{+}) & =\begin{cases}
\phi _{-}, & u< 0 \\
\phi _{+}, & u> 0
\end{cases}
\end{align}$$

well-posedness of CIBVP and matching imply

$$\begin{align}
A: & \mathcal{C}\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathcal{P},\quad A^{*}\Omega=\Omega _{\mathcal{C}} \\
\implies & (\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{glue}},\left\{\cdot,\cdot\right\}_{\mathrm{glue}})=(\mathcal{H},\left\{\cdot,\cdot\right\})
\end{align}$$

## Realization of the Poisson Bracket -- Peierls Bracket

Again we use Peierls bracket to realize $\displaystyle{\left\{\cdot,\cdot\right\}_{\mathrm{glue}}}$. Consider the Hamiltonian flow generated by an observable $\displaystyle{G[\phi _{-},\phi _{+}]\in \mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{glue}}}$...

$$\begin{align}
G & =\dfrac{\delta G}{\delta \phi _{-}}\delta \phi _{-}+\dfrac{\delta G}{\delta \phi _{+}}\delta \phi _{+}
\end{align}$$

...

The final result is the same as spacelike cut

$$\begin{align}
\left\{F,G\right\}_{\mathrm{glue}} & =\begin{pmatrix}
\dfrac{\delta F}{\delta \phi _{-}} & \dfrac{\delta F}{\delta \phi _{+}}
\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}
E_{-} & H_{-}T^{A}_{+} \\
-H_{+}T^{R}_{-} & E_{+}
\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}
 \dfrac{\delta G}{\delta \phi _{-}} \\
 \dfrac{\delta G}{\delta \phi _{+}}
\end{pmatrix}
\end{align}$$

#### Characteristic Propagation Kernel

The local Riemann function is

$$\begin{align}
R_m(a,b)&=J_0(m\sqrt{ab}), & (4\partial_a\partial_b+m^2)R_m&=0, & R_m(a,0)&=R_m(0,b)=1.
\end{align}$$

For $a,b\geq0$ it gives the retarded line kernel

$$\begin{align}
g_m^R(\tau,d)&=\frac12\theta(\tau-|d|)J_0\!\left(m\sqrt{\tau^2-d^2}\right).
\end{align}$$

The factor $1/2$ gives $Pg_m^R=\delta(t-t')\delta(x-x')=2\delta(u-u')\delta(v-v')$. The Bessel factor is used only inside its supported cone. The two characteristic boundary values fix the wavefront normalization.

Let $s(v)=(v/2,v/2)$. For $z\in M_-$ and $p\in M_+$, solve the regional reflected characteristic problems and define

$$\begin{align}
Q_z(v)&=(T_-^R)(v,z), & B_p(v)&=\lim_{z'\to s(v),\ z'\in M_+}G_+^R(p,z').
\end{align}$$

Odd reflection at the physical walls gives the explicit formulas

$$\begin{align}
Q_z(v)&=\sum_{n\in\mathbb Z}\left[ g_m^R(v/2-t_z,v/2-x_z+2nL) -g_m^R(v/2-t_z,v/2+x_z+2nL)\right],\\
B_p(v)&=\sum_{n\in\mathbb Z}\left[ g_m^R(t_p-v/2,x_p-v/2+2nL) -g_m^R(t_p-v/2,x_p+v/2+2nL)\right].
\end{align}$$

These sums are locally finite by causal support. They implement only the physical walls. For each causal path contributing to $Q_z$ or $B_p$, monotonicity of $u$ keeps the path in its corresponding region until it reaches $N$.

The Green identity in null coordinates is

$$\begin{align}
\frac12(A PB-B PA) &=\partial_u(A\partial_vB-B\partial_vA) +\partial_v(A\partial_uB-B\partial_uA).
\end{align}$$

Apply it in $M_+$ to its regional Green function and the incoming homogeneous solution. The physical-wall terms vanish, and causal support removes the temporal-cap terms. The lower $u$ boundary gives

$$\begin{align}
(H_+^Rq)(p)&=\int_0^{2L}\left[B_p(v)q'(v)-B_p'(v)q(v)\right]\,\mathrm dv,\\
\mathcal G_{+-}^R(p,z)&=\int_0^{2L}\left[B_p(v)Q_z'(v)-B_p'(v)Q_z(v)\right]\,\mathrm dv.
\end{align}$$

All derivatives include the delta functions from the step functions. The endpoints have the physical Dirichlet compatibility. The formula is first understood on smooth bulk sources; point-source kernels below are evaluated away from intersecting wavefronts.

#### Direct Propagation Across the Cut

Before a physical-wall reflection arrives, put

$$\begin{align}
a&=-u_z>0, & b&=u_p>0, & V&=v_p-v_z>0.
\end{align}$$

Then $Q_z(v)=\tfrac12\theta(v-v_z)R_m(a,v-v_z)$ and $B_p(v)=\tfrac12\theta(v_p-v)R_m(b,v_p-v)$. Substitution gives

$$\begin{align}
\mathcal G_{+-}^R(p,z) &=\frac14\left[R_m(a,V)+R_m(b,V)\right]\\
&\quad+\frac14\int_0^V\left[ R_m(b,V-w)\partial_wR_m(a,w) +(\partial_sR_m(b,s))|_{s=V-w}R_m(a,w)\right]\,\mathrm dw\\
&=\frac12R_m(a+b,V).
\end{align}$$

The first line contains the two wavefront contact contributions. To verify the last equality independently, take the Laplace transform in $V$:

$$\begin{align}
\widetilde R_m(a,p)&=p^{-1}\exp\!\left(-\frac{m^2a}{4p}\right), & p&>0,\\
\widetilde{\mathcal G}_{+-}^R&=\frac p2\widetilde R_m(a,p)\widetilde R_m(b,p) =\frac1{2p}\exp\!\left(-\frac{m^2(a+b)}{4p}\right).
\end{align}$$

Thus the propagation across the null cut reconstructs the full massive tail, including its normalization. This is an exact convolution identity, not a small-mass approximation.

#### Independent Global Comparison

Independently solve the full interval source problem, without an artificial boundary at $t=x$. Odd reflection gives

$$\begin{align}
G_D^R(p,z)&=\sum_{n\in\mathbb Z}\left[ g_m^R(t_p-t_z,x_p-x_z+2nL) -g_m^R(t_p-t_z,x_p+x_z+2nL)\right],\\
G_D^A(p,z)&=G_D^R(z,p), & E_D&=G_D^A-G_D^R.
\end{align}$$

This kernel vanishes at both physical walls, has the unit bulk source, and has retarded support. The assembled regional response has these same properties: its characteristic traces agree, the interface source vanishes, and the response has zero past Cauchy data. Uniqueness of the Dirichlet Cauchy problem therefore gives

$$\begin{align}
\mathcal G_{ij}^{R/A}(p,z)&=G_D^{R/A}(p,z), & \mathcal E_{ij}(p,z)&=E_D(p,z),\qquad p\in M_i,\ z\in M_j.
\end{align}$$

For the diagonal blocks this also follows because a causal path between two points on the same side cannot leave that side and return. Unlike a timelike fixed-Dirichlet construction, the intrinsic regional kernel has introduced no artificial reflected wave to remove.

The independent spectral expression fixes the canonical sign:

$$\begin{align}
E_D(t,x;t',z)&=-\frac2L\sum_{n\geq1}\sin\frac{n\pi x}{L}\sin\frac{n\pi z}{L} \frac{\sin(\omega_n(t-t'))}{\omega_n}, & \omega_n^2&=m^2+\frac{n^2\pi^2}{L^2},\\
E_D(t,x;t,z)&=0, & \left.\partial_{t'}E_D(t,x;t',z)\right|_{t'=t}&=\delta(x-z).
\end{align}$$

Here $P$ is the positive wave operator, $\delta S/\delta\phi=-P\phi$, and $E_D=G_D^A-G_D^R$. This gives $\{\phi,\pi\}=\delta$ with $\Omega=\int\delta\pi\wedge\delta\phi$.

#### Kernel and Image on the Polynomial Algebra

The preceding construction gives a constant antisymmetric form $\mathcal E$ on $V_-\oplus V_+$. Give $\operatorname{Sym}(V_-\oplus V_+)$ this bracket, including its crossing blocks. Let

$$\begin{align}
K&=\{(f_-,f_+):\mathcal E f=0\}, & \mathcal A_{\mathrm{glue}}&=\operatorname{Sym}(V_-\oplus V_+)/\langle K\rangle.
\end{align}$$

There is a concrete regional test for $K$. At a full early slice $t=t_*<0$, compute the field and time derivative of the assembled homogeneous response from the regional blocks. Both must vanish. Homogeneous Cauchy uniqueness then makes the response vanish everywhere. Thus the kernel is the full cancellation of these two Cauchy data, rather than just equality of formal source labels at the cut.

Independently define $V_D=\mathscr D_D/\ker E_D$ and $\mathcal A_D=\operatorname{Sym}(V_D)$ for the global Dirichlet problem, with the same smooth compatible source class. Extend regional source labels by zero across $N$. The map

$$\begin{align}
(f_-,f_+)&\longmapsto[f_-+f_+]\in V_D
\end{align}$$

preserves the bracket by the independently verified kernel identity. It is onto. Indeed, for a global source $h$, let $\psi=E_Dh$ and choose a smooth time cutoff $\chi$, increasing from zero to one in a compact interval entirely before $t=0$. Then

$$\begin{align}
k&=-[P,\chi]\psi, & \operatorname{supp}k&\subset M_-, & E_Dk&=\psi.
\end{align}$$

To check the sign, the retarded and advanced solutions for $[P,\chi]\psi$ are $\chi\psi$ and $(\chi-1)\psi$, respectively. The source $k$ has the smooth wall compatibility of $\psi$. Hence every global linear observable has a regional time-slice representative. The kernel is exactly $K$, and taking the symmetric algebra gives

$$\begin{align}
\mathcal A_{\mathrm{glue}}&\simeq\mathcal A_D.
\end{align}$$

This is an isomorphism of the stated finite polynomial Poisson algebras. The ordinary independent-product bracket, whose crossing blocks are zero, does not descend to this result. Both null regions contain a full time slice, so there are substantial relations between their presentations of the same observables.

For several ordered cuts $u=c_j$, apply the same incoming-history propagation successively. On each intervening region the propagated solution is uniquely fixed by its incoming data and source. Propagating through two successive regions or their union therefore gives the same outgoing data. This proves order consistency for successive parallel null cuts with the same wall and corner compatibility; no arbitrary network of intersecting null cuts is included.

### Explicit Sources on the Null History

An independent source $Q_\beta=\int_N\beta(v)q(v)\,\mathrm dv$ needs a separate domain check. If one insists that the perturbed field remain continuous across $N$, the released interface equation would be

$$\begin{align}
\partial_vq-\partial_vq+\varepsilon\beta&=0.
\end{align}$$

A nonzero $\beta$ cannot be accommodated in that smooth common-history response problem. Equivalently, its spacetime source is $2\beta(v)\delta(u)$; a distributional solution generally requires a jump of the field, with $2\partial_v[\delta\phi]=\beta$. It lies outside the smooth transmission class used above.

The null symplectic form also makes the domain question explicit. The actual Hamiltonian condition is

$$\begin{align}
\int_0^{2L}\left[2\partial_v(X_{Q_\beta}q)+\beta\right]\delta q\,\mathrm dv&=0, &\qquad \delta q&\in T_q\mathcal B,
\end{align}$$

where $\mathcal B$ is the compatible common trace image. A formal local inversion, before resolving this restricted variation space, would impose

$$\begin{align}
2\partial_v(X_{Q_\beta}q)&=-\beta, & X_{Q_\beta}q(0)&=X_{Q_\beta}q(2L)=0.
\end{align}$$

This formal inversion already requires $\int_0^{2L}\beta\,\mathrm dv=0$ to meet the two endpoint values. Its solution must still belong to $T_q\mathcal B$, including the massive corner conditions; the formal differential equation is not a construction of that inverse on the actual trace image. Arbitrary smooth smearing along a null cut therefore cannot simply be assumed to give a smooth Hamiltonian observable. We do not append a $qq$ block by substituting $t=x$ into the timelike formula. The present result includes smooth bulk observables and their induced characteristic responses; a larger algebra with independent null-supported sources would require its own response and trace prescription.

## Two Intersecting Cuts: $L,R\longrightarrow F$

Keep the same cavity $0\leq x\leq L$ and choose $O=(0,a)$, with $0<a<L$. In this section reset the null coordinates to

$$\begin{align}
u&=t-(x-a), & v&=t+(x-a), & P&=4\partial_u\partial_v+m^2.
\end{align}$$

Cut along both $u=0$ and $v=0$. Denote the four wedges, intersected with the physical cavity, by

$$\begin{align}
W_L&=\{u>0,\ v<0\}, & W_R&=\{u<0,\ v>0\},\\
F&=\{u>0,\ v>0\}, & W_P&=\{u<0,\ v<0\}.
\end{align}$$

Here the subscripts $L,R$ name the wedges; the unsubscripted $L$ remains the cavity length. Their arrangement is

```text
                    t
                    ^
              N_L \ F / N_R
                   \ /
                L   O   R   -> x
                   / \
                  / P \
```

The incoming boundary of $F$ consists of two finite rays,

$$\begin{align}
N_L&=\{v=0,\ 0\leq u\leq2a\}, & s_L(u)&=(u/2,a-u/2),\\
N_R&=\{u=0,\ 0\leq v\leq2(L-a)\}, & s_R(v)&=(v/2,a+v/2),\\
q_L(u)&=\phi_L(s_L(u)), & q_R(v)&=\phi_R(s_R(v)).
\end{align}$$

The left wedge supplies $q_L$, the right wedge supplies $q_R$, and both contain the common corner value $c=q_L(0)=q_R(0)$. The past wedge introduces no additional datum for evolution into $F$.

The result will be a symplectic assembly map from compatible pairs of wedge solutions to solutions in $F$, and an isomorphism of their finite polynomial Poisson algebras. The compatibility domain and the regional observable class are specified below; they are part of the result.

### Regional Actions and Release of Both Histories

For the future evolution, restrict the wedge actions to $W_i^+=W_i\cap\{t\geq0\}$ and truncate $F$ at $t=T>\max(a,L-a)$. These three regions partition the slab $0\leq t\leq T$. Put

$$\begin{align}
I_L&=[0,a], & I_R&=[a,L], & Q_i(x)&=\phi_i(0,x), & P_i(x)&=\dot\phi_i(0,x),\\
S_i^+&=\int_{W_i^+}\left(\phi_{i,u}\phi_{i,v}-\frac{m^2}{4}\phi_i^2\right)\,\mathrm du\mathrm dv.
\end{align}$$

Initially prescribe each characteristic history. The on-shell action variations are

$$\begin{align}
\delta S_L^+&=\int_0^{2a}q_L'\delta q_L\,\mathrm du-\int_{I_L}P_L\delta Q_L\,\mathrm dx,\\
\delta S_R^+&=\int_0^{2(L-a)}q_R'\delta q_R\,\mathrm dv-\int_{I_R}P_R\delta Q_R\,\mathrm dx,\\
\delta S_F&=\theta_T-\int_0^{2a}(q_L^F)'\delta q_L^F\,\mathrm du -\int_0^{2(L-a)}(q_R^F)'\delta q_R^F\,\mathrm dv.
\end{align}$$

The off-shell bulk term in each region is $-\tfrac12\int(P\phi)\delta\phi\,\mathrm du\mathrm dv$. Physical-wall terms vanish on Dirichlet variations. Impose $q_i^F=q_i$ and release the common histories in $S_L^++S_R^++S_F$. Each oriented null coefficient cancels separately. Thus the action requires common characteristic values and gives the same automatic tangential-flux matching as the single cut. It does not supply two independent normal momenta or an inverse of a sum of null Dirichlet-to-Neumann operators.

The variations at $O$ obey $\delta q_L(0)=\delta q_R(0)=\delta c$. For this scalar action the displayed potential requires no additional point term at $O$. The common value will nevertheless enter the reconstruction explicitly.

### Independent Wedge Spaces and Algebras

Define $\mathcal P_i$ as solutions intrinsic to $W_i$, smooth up to its horizons and physical wall. They are determined by smooth Cauchy data $(Q_i,P_i)$ on the closed half-interval $I_i$, with the physical-wall compatibility at $0$ or $L$. There is no reflecting wall at $x=a$. Concretely, each datum has a smooth odd extension through its physical endpoint; its derivatives at $a$ are initially unrestricted. Regional Cauchy evolution and the physical wall determine the actual trace map

$$\begin{align}
T_i:\mathcal P_i&\longrightarrow\mathcal B_i, & (Q_i,P_i)&\longmapsto q_i, & \mathcal B_i&=T_i\mathcal P_i.
\end{align}$$

This defines the regional history space without assuming arbitrary prescribed profiles are realizable. The CPS forms are

$$\begin{align}
\Omega_i&=\int_{I_i}\delta P_i\wedge\delta Q_i\,\mathrm dx,\\
\Omega_L&=\int_0^{2a}\delta q_L'\wedge\delta q_L\,\mathrm du, & \Omega_R&=\int_0^{2(L-a)}\delta q_R'\wedge\delta q_R\,\mathrm dv.
\end{align}$$

The second line follows by conserving the symplectic current through each triangle $W_i^+$. The energy identity gives a useful injectivity check:

$$\begin{align}
\frac12\int_{I_i}\left(P_i^2+(Q_i')^2+m^2Q_i^2\right)\,\mathrm dx &=\int_{N_i}\left((q_i')^2+\frac{m^2}{4}q_i^2\right)\,\mathrm ds.
\end{align}$$

Here $s=u$ on $N_L$ and $s=v$ on $N_R$. Hence $T_i$ has zero kernel. Each ray, together with its physical wall and actual trace conditions, carries the data of its own wedge. Neither wedge contains a complete cavity Cauchy slice.

For a precise algebra, let $\mathscr C_i$ be the real smooth functions on $I_i$ with this odd physical-wall compatibility. Define independently

$$\begin{align}
V_i&=\mathscr C_i\oplus\mathscr C_i, & \mathcal A_i&=\operatorname{Sym}_{\mathbb R}(V_i),\\
\ell_i[\alpha_i,\beta_i]&=\int_{I_i}(\alpha_iQ_i+\beta_iP_i)\,\mathrm dx,\\
\{\ell_i[\alpha_i,\beta_i],\ell_i[\widetilde\alpha_i,\widetilde\beta_i]\}_i &=\int_{I_i}(\alpha_i\widetilde\beta_i-\beta_i\widetilde\alpha_i)\,\mathrm dx.
\end{align}$$

These are finite polynomials in smooth linear Hamiltonian observables. Indeed, $\iota_{X_{\ell_i}}\Omega_i=-\delta\ell_i$ gives $X_{\ell_i}(Q_i,P_i)=(\beta_i,-\alpha_i)$, which has the required wall compatibility. The smearings may reach $a$ from their respective sides. This information is retained when the two algebras are sewn.

### Compatible Pairs and the Solution in $F$

For smooth sewing define the relation in the product of the independently constructed wedge spaces by

$$\begin{align}
\mathcal C=\big\{(\phi_L,\phi_R)\in\mathcal P_L\times\mathcal P_R:
\partial_x^kQ_L(a)&=\partial_x^kQ_R(a),\\
\partial_x^kP_L(a)&=\partial_x^kP_R(a)\quad\text{for every }k\geq0\big\}.
\end{align}$$

All derivatives use the same increasing $x$ coordinate. These conditions say precisely that the half-interval data assemble into smooth full-interval data. They are regularity conditions for the chosen target, beyond the tangential interface equation from the action. In characteristic variables they include

$$\begin{align}
q_L(0)&=q_R(0)=c,\\
q_L'(0)&=\tfrac12(P_L-Q_L')(a)=\tfrac12(P_R-Q_R')(a),\\
q_R'(0)&=\tfrac12(P_R+Q_R')(a)=\tfrac12(P_L+Q_L')(a).
\end{align}$$

The higher mixed jets follow from

$$\begin{align}
\partial_u^{j+1}\partial_v^{k+1}\phi(O) &=-\frac{m^2}{4}\partial_u^j\partial_v^k\phi(O).
\end{align}$$

Merely equating the two values $c$ does not make arbitrary wedge solutions smooth across both seams. On an isolated characteristic rectangle, however, any two smooth profiles with equal $c$ do determine a smooth solution in that rectangle. The distinction is between this local problem and smooth continuation of the given wedge fields with the physical cavity walls.

The reconstruction inside $0\leq u\leq2a$, $0\leq v\leq2(L-a)$ is explicit:

$$\begin{align}
\phi_F(u,v)&=q_L(u)+q_R(v)-c -\frac{m^2}{4}\int_0^u\mathrm da'\int_0^v\mathrm db'\,\phi_F(a',b'),\\
&=cR_m(u,v)+\int_0^uR_m(u-s,v)q_L'(s)\,\mathrm ds +\int_0^vR_m(u,v-s)q_R'(s)\,\mathrm ds,\\
R_m(u,v)&=J_0(m\sqrt{uv}).
\end{align}$$

Both boundary values are recovered exactly. The massive kernel propagates each profile into the interior. At $m=0$ this reduces to $q_L(u)+q_R(v)-c$, so the common corner is counted once. For $P\phi=f$, add $\tfrac14\int_0^u\mathrm da'\int_0^v\mathrm db'\,R_m(u-a',v-b')f(a',b')$ to the second line.

This is an exact evolution formula. On every compact rectangle, the $n$th iterated homogeneous Volterra operator has norm bounded by $(m^2UV/4)^n/(n!)^2$. Its series converges and gives uniqueness; smooth data allow differentiation on compact subrectangles. Beyond the displayed rectangle, continue with the same physical Dirichlet walls. On $\mathcal C$ this continuation exists: evolve the assembled smooth Cauchy data, and local characteristic uniqueness identifies its restriction with the displayed formula. This also makes reconstruction independent of the order in which characteristic patches are traversed in this two-cut geometry.

Independently define $\mathcal P_F$ from the scalar action on $F$, physical Dirichlet walls, and arbitrary smooth compatible Cauchy data on a full slice $t=T>\max(a,L-a)$. Its CPS form is $\Omega_F=\int_0^L\delta\dot\phi_F(T,x)\wedge\delta\phi_F(T,x)\,\mathrm dx$. The assembly map is

$$\begin{align}
U:\mathcal C&\longrightarrow\mathcal P_F, & (\phi_L,\phi_R)&\longmapsto H_F(T_L\phi_L,T_R\phi_R).
\end{align}$$

It is one-to-one and onto. Forward cavity evolution gives existence. Conversely, evolve the independently specified late Cauchy data backward and restrict to the two wedges. Cauchy uniqueness gives the inverse. Equivalently, a vanishing assembled solution has vanishing two traces, so the two regional energy identities give a zero pair. Conservation of the symplectic current gives

$$\begin{align}
U^*\Omega_F=\Omega_{\mathcal C} &=\left.(\Omega_L+\Omega_R)\right|_{\mathcal C}\\
&=\int_{N_L}\delta q_L'\wedge\delta q_L\,\mathrm du +\int_{N_R}\delta q_R'\wedge\delta q_R\,\mathrm dv.
\end{align}$$

Here the forms are added: the two wedges carry complementary halves of one Cauchy slice. This is different from adding two complete copies of the single-cut history form.

### The Algebra Map and Its Exact Domain

Define $\mathcal A_F$ independently as finite polynomials in smooth wall-compatible linear Cauchy smearings on the late slice $T$, with the bracket induced by $\Omega_F$. To obtain its regional presentation, take

$$\begin{align}
V_{\mathrm{match}}=\big\{(\alpha_L,\beta_L;\alpha_R,\beta_R)\in V_L\oplus V_R:
\partial_x^k\alpha_L(a)&=\partial_x^k\alpha_R(a),\\
\partial_x^k\beta_L(a)&=\partial_x^k\beta_R(a)\quad\text{for every }k\geq0\big\}.
\end{align}$$

The Hamiltonian vector of $\ell_L+\ell_R$ is tangent to $\mathcal C$ exactly when its label lies in this space. Define

$$\begin{align}
\mathcal A_{\mathrm{glue}}&=\operatorname{Sym}_{\mathbb R}(V_{\mathrm{match}})
\subset\mathcal A_L\otimes\mathcal A_R,\\
\{\ell_L+\ell_R,\widetilde\ell_L+\widetilde\ell_R\}_{\mathrm{glue}} &=\sum_{i=L,R}\int_{I_i}(\alpha_i\widetilde\beta_i-\beta_i\widetilde\alpha_i)\,\mathrm dx.
\end{align}$$

The bracket is constant on linear generators and extends by Leibniz, so this is a Poisson subalgebra. Restrict its functions to $\mathcal C$ and transport them with $U$. This gives

$$\begin{align}
\mathfrak G:\mathcal A_{\mathrm{glue}}&\xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }\mathcal A_F, & \mathfrak G(\ell_L+\ell_R)[\phi_F] &=(\ell_L+\ell_R)[U^{-1}\phi_F].
\end{align}$$

Its kernel is zero: matching labels are ordinary smooth full-interval Cauchy coefficients, and a linear functional vanishing on all such data has zero coefficients. Finite polynomial functionals remain faithful. Its image is all of $\mathcal A_F$: backward symplectic Cauchy evolution takes each late linear observable to smooth full-interval coefficients on $t=0$; restrict those coefficients to $I_L,I_R$. The bracket is preserved by $U^*\Omega_F=\Omega_{\mathcal C}$. This proves the stated polynomial algebra isomorphism.

The whole independent tensor product cannot be restricted as a Poisson algebra in this smooth target: a one-sided Hamiltonian vector with unmatched corner derivatives leaves $\mathcal C$. The construction retains precisely the matched collective generators. Conversely, if one kept only bulk tests compactly supported strictly inside the open wedges, every finite sum of their Cauchy responses would vanish in a neighborhood of $a$, by finite propagation. A generic smooth observable in $F$ has a response nonzero there. Such a smaller algebra would not be onto algebraically. The smooth Cauchy smearings reaching the corner, already included in $\mathcal A_L,\mathcal A_R$, are therefore substantive regional data in this result.

### Direct Peierls Comparison in $F$

We can compute the bracket without choosing modes. Let $G_F^R$ be the intrinsic retarded inverse on $F$ with the physical walls and causal support, and set

$$\begin{align}
B_{i,p}(s)&=\lim_{z\to s_i(s),\ z\in F}G_F^R(p,z), & i&=L,R.
\end{align}$$

The regional Green identity gives the two-history reconstruction for a homogeneous field,

$$\begin{align}
\phi_F(p)&=\sum_{i=L,R}\int_{N_i} \left[B_{i,p}(s)q_i'(s)-B_{i,p}'(s)q_i(s)\right]\,\mathrm ds.
\end{align}$$

The physical-wall and temporal terms vanish by the boundary conditions and causal support. In the region before wall reflections, $B_{L,p}(s)=\tfrac12\theta(u_p-s)R_m(u_p-s,v_p)$ and $B_{R,p}(s)=\tfrac12\theta(v_p-s)R_m(u_p,v_p-s)$. Integrating by parts on both rays gives the explicit Goursat formula above: each lower endpoint contributes $-\tfrac12cR_m(u_p,v_p)$, and the two wavefront terms supply the upper-end values. Thus no independent corner source is being silently dropped.

For a smooth bulk observable $\Phi_f=\int_F f\phi_F\,\mathrm dV$, smear $B_{i,p}$ in $p$ to obtain $B_{i,f}$. From the displayed functional and $\Omega_{\mathcal C}$, its Hamiltonian trace is $X_{\Phi_f}q_i=B_{i,f}$. On this smooth bulk domain the traces are admissible and matched. The resulting distribution kernel of the glued bracket is

$$\begin{align}
E_{\mathrm{glue}}(p,z) &=\sum_{i=L,R}\int_{N_i}\left[B_{i,p}B_{i,z}'-B_{i,p}'B_{i,z}\right]\,\mathrm ds.
\end{align}$$

In particular, both boundary contributions are needed for a bracket between two observables in $F$. Point fields here denote kernels; the algebra statement uses smooth smearings.

To evaluate the regional boundary propagators, solve the retarded equation in $F$ by odd reflection at the physical walls:

$$\begin{align}
B_{i,p}(s)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb Z}\Big[ g_m^R(t_p-t_i(s),x_p-x_i(s)+2nL) -g_m^R(t_p-t_i(s),x_p+x_i(s)+2nL)\Big].
\end{align}$$

The sums are locally finite. Since future causal paths have nondecreasing $u,v$, paths from the incoming boundary to $p\in F$, including physical reflections, stay in $F$. These are regional propagators with no imposed reflecting condition on $N_L,N_R$.

Independently compute $G_D^{R/A}$ on the whole cavity by the full source problem described above. Its restriction to $F$ satisfies the same intrinsic causal problem, hence $E_F=E_D|_{F\times F}$. Applying the conserved Green current to the two causal solutions between $N_L\cup N_R$ and a full Cauchy slice yields

$$\begin{align}
E_{\mathrm{glue}}(p,z)&=E_D(p,z)=E_F(p,z),\\
\{\Phi_f,\Phi_h\}_{\mathrm{glue}} &=\int_{F\times F}f(p)E_F(p,z)h(z)\,\mathrm dV_p\mathrm dV_z.
\end{align}$$

The sign is $E=G^A-G^R$, as throughout this note. The two-sheet integral supplies a direct check of this comparison, including the massive tails and every wavefront contact term.
