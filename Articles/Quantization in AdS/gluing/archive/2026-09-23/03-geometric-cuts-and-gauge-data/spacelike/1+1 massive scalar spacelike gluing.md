The goal is to put a spacelike cut into the same action-first construction as the timelike and null examples: prescribe the regional interface data, release their common value, derive the transmission equation, and compute the glued observable bracket from regional responses.

The spacetime setup is given by

$$\begin{align}
M & =\mathbb{R}\times[0,L], & M_{-} & =[t_{i},0]\times[0,L], & M_{+} & =[0,t_{f}]\times[0,L]
\end{align}$$

the action of a massive free scalar is given by

$$\begin{align}
S[\phi] & =S_{+}[\phi _{+}]+S_{-}[\phi _{-}] \\
 & =\dfrac{1}{2} \int _{t_{i}}^{0}\mathrm{d}t \int _{0}^{L}\mathrm{d}x\left(\dot{\phi}_{-}^{2}-\phi _{-}'^{2}-m^{2}\phi _{-}^{2}\right) \\
 & +\dfrac{1}{2}\int _{0}^{t_{f}}\mathrm{d}t\int _{0}^{L}\mathrm{d}x\left(\dot{\phi}_{+}^{2}-\phi _{+}'^{2}-m^{2}\phi _{+}^{2}\right)
\end{align}$$

with Dirichlet boundary conditions at physical boundary $\displaystyle{x=0}$ and $\displaystyle{x=L}$

$$\begin{align}
\phi(t,0)=\phi(t,L)=0
\end{align}$$

## Phase Space and Observable Algebra

Put $\displaystyle{q_{\pm}(x)=\phi _{\pm}(0,x)}$ and $\displaystyle{p_{\pm}=\dot{\phi}_{\pm}(0,x)}$. Variational principle gives

$$\begin{align}
\delta S_{-} & =\int _{M_{+}}\mathrm{d}t\mathrm{d}x(-\ddot{\phi}_{-}+\phi _{-}''-m^{2}\phi _{-})\delta \phi _{-}+\int _{0}^{L}\mathrm{d}xp_{-}\delta q_{-}-\theta _{i} \\
\delta S_{+} & =\int _{M_{-}}\mathrm{d}t\mathrm{d}x\left(-\ddot{\phi}_{+}+\phi''_{+}-m^{2}\phi _{+}\right)\delta \phi _{+}-\int _{0}^{L}\mathrm{d}xp_{+}\delta q_{+}+\theta _{f}
\end{align}$$

define the regional solution spaces by

$$\begin{align}
\mathcal{P}_{\pm} & =\left\{\phi _{\pm}: -\ddot{\phi}_{\pm}+\phi''_{\pm}-m^{2}\phi _{\pm}=0,(\text{Dirichlet BC})\right\}
\end{align}$$

regional symplectic form is

$$\begin{align}
\Omega _{\pm}  & =\int _{0}^{L}\mathrm{d}x \delta \dot{\phi}\wedge \delta \phi \\
 & =\int _{0}^{L}\mathrm{d}x\delta p_\pm\wedge\delta q_\pm
\end{align}$$

the regional algebra $\displaystyle{\mathcal{H}_{\pm}}$ is defined as

$$\begin{align}
\mathcal{H}_{\pm} & =\left\{F\in C^{\infty}(\mathcal{P}_{\pm},\mathbb{R}): \exists X_{F}\in \Gamma(T\mathcal{P}_{\pm}), X_{F}\cdot \Omega _{\pm}=-\delta F\right\}
\end{align}$$

the corresponding Poisson bracket $\displaystyle{\left\{F,G\right\}_{\pm}, \forall F,G\in \mathcal{H}_{\pm}}$ is defined as

$$\begin{align}
\left\{F,G\right\}_{\pm} & =X_{F}\cdot X_{G}\cdot \Omega _{\pm}
\end{align}$$

## Gluing

Define the Cauchy data space on the cut

$$\begin{align}
\mathcal{C}_{\Sigma} & =\left\{(q,p)\right\}, & \Omega _{\Sigma}=\int _{\Sigma}\mathrm{d}x\delta p\wedge \delta q
\end{align}$$

we have two bijective symplectic maps

$$\begin{align}
r_{\pm} & :\mathcal{P}_{\pm}\to \mathcal{C}_{\Sigma}, & \phi _{\pm}\mapsto(q_{\pm},p_{\pm}) \\
r^{*}_{\pm}\Omega _{\pm} & =\Omega _{\Sigma} \\
\implies \mathcal{P}_{-} & \simeq \mathcal{C}_{\Sigma}\simeq \mathcal{P}_{+}
\end{align}$$

Impose the gluing condition

$$\begin{align}
q_{+} & =q_{-}=q \\
\xRightarrow{\text{variational principle}}p_{+} & =p_{-}=p
\end{align}$$

the matched phase space is the fiber product

$$\begin{align}
\mathcal{C} & := \mathcal{P}_{-}\times _{\mathcal{C}_{\Sigma}}\mathcal{P}_{+}=\left\{(\phi _{-},\phi _{+}):r_{-}\phi _{-}=r_{+}\phi _{+}\right\}
\end{align}$$

with the symplectic form

$$\begin{align}
\Omega _{\mathcal{C}} & =\mathrm{pr}_{\pm}^{*}\Omega _{\pm}
\end{align}$$

here $\displaystyle{\mathrm{pr}_{\pm}}$ are the projections onto the regional factors

$$\begin{align}
\mathrm{pr}_{\pm}: & \mathcal{C}\to \mathcal{P}_{\pm}, & \mathrm{pr}_{\pm}(\phi_{-},\phi_{+})=\phi_{\pm}
\end{align}$$

we define the glued algebra as

$$\begin{align}
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{glue}} & =\mathcal{H}(\mathcal{C},\Omega _{\mathcal{C}})
\end{align}$$

consider the assembly map

$$\begin{align}
A: & \mathcal{C}\to \mathcal{P} \\
A(\phi _{-},\phi _{+})(t) & =\begin{cases}
\phi _{-}(t), & t<0 \\
\phi _{+}(t), & t>0
\end{cases}
\end{align}$$

Cauchy uniqueness and matching imply

$$\begin{align}
A: & \mathcal{C}\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathcal{P},\quad A^{*}\Omega=\Omega _{\mathcal{C}} \\
\implies &  (\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{glue}},\left\{\cdot,\cdot\right\}_{\mathrm{glue}})=(\mathcal{H},\left\{\cdot,\cdot\right\})
\end{align}$$

## Realization of the Poisson Bracket -- Peierls Bracket

Again we use Peierls bracket to realize $\displaystyle{\left\{\cdot,\cdot\right\}_{\mathrm{glue}}}$. Consider the Hamiltonian flow generated by an observable $\displaystyle{G[\phi _{-},\phi _{+}]\in \mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{glue}}}$...

$$\begin{align}
G & = \dfrac{\delta G}{\delta \phi _{-}}\delta \phi _{-}+ \dfrac{\delta G}{\delta \phi _{+}}\delta \phi _{+}
\end{align}$$

the response

$$\begin{align}
\delta ^{R}_{G}\phi _{+} & =G^{R}_{+} \dfrac{\delta G}{\delta \phi _{+}}+C\eta ^{R}_{G}+S\zeta ^{R}_{G} \\
\delta ^{A}_{G}\phi _{+} & =G^{A}_{+} \dfrac{\delta G}{\delta \phi _{+}} \\
\delta ^{R}_{G}\phi _{-} & =G^{R}_{-} \dfrac{\delta G}{\delta \phi _{-}} \\
\delta ^{A}_{G}\phi _{-} & =G^{A}_{-} \dfrac{\delta G}{\delta \phi _{-}}+C\eta ^{A}_{G}+S\zeta ^{A}_{G}
\end{align}$$

here

$$\begin{align}
\eta ^{R}_{G} & =\delta ^{R}_{G}q=S \dfrac{\delta G}{\delta \phi _{-}}, & \eta ^{A}_{G} & =\delta ^{A}_{G}q=-S \dfrac{\delta G}{\delta \phi _{+}} \\
\zeta ^{R}_{G} & =\delta ^{R}_{G}p=C \dfrac{\delta G}{\delta \phi _{-}}, & \zeta ^{A}_{G} & =\delta ^{A}_{G}p=-C \dfrac{\delta G}{\delta \phi _{+}}
\end{align}$$

therfore

$$\begin{align}
\left\{F,G\right\}_{\mathrm{glue}} & =\delta ^{A}_{G}F-\delta ^{R}_{G}F \\
 & =(\delta ^{A}_{G}\phi _{-}-\delta ^{R}_{G}\phi _{-}) \dfrac{\delta F}{\delta \phi _{-}}+(\delta ^{A}_{G}\phi _{+}-\delta ^{R}_{G}\phi _{+}) \dfrac{\delta F}{\delta \phi _{+}} \\
 & = \dfrac{\delta F}{\delta \phi _{-}}E_{-} \dfrac{\delta G}{\delta \phi _{-}}+\dfrac{\delta F}{\delta \phi _{+}}E_{+} \dfrac{\delta G}{\delta \phi _{+}} \\
 &  +\dfrac{\delta F}{\delta \phi _{-}}(C\eta ^{A}_{G}+S\zeta ^{A}_{G})-\dfrac{\delta F}{\delta \phi _{+}}(C\eta ^{R}_{G}+S\zeta ^{R}_{G})
\end{align}$$

we write

$$\begin{align}
T^{R}_{-} \dfrac{\delta G}{\delta \phi _{-}} & =(\eta ^{R}_{G},\zeta ^{R}_{G}), & T^{A}_{+} \dfrac{\delta G}{\delta \phi _{+}} & =(\eta ^{A}_{G},\zeta ^{A}_{G}) \\
H_{\pm}(\eta,\zeta) & =C\eta+S\zeta
\end{align}$$

then

$$\begin{align}
\left\{F,G\right\}_{\mathrm{glue}} & =\begin{pmatrix}
\dfrac{\delta F}{\delta \phi _{-}} & \dfrac{\delta F}{\delta \phi _{+}}
\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}
E_{-} & H_{-}T^{A}_{+} \\
-H_{+}T^{R}_{-} & E_{+}
\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}
 \dfrac{\delta G}{\delta \phi _{-}} \\
 \dfrac{\delta G}{\delta \phi _{+}}
\end{pmatrix}
\end{align}$$

and for this example, we can show that

$$\begin{align}
H_{-}T^{A}_{+}=H_{+}T^{R}_{-}=E_{\pm}
\end{align}$$

which means the spacelike cut means the composition of time evolution.

#### Independent Global Comparison

Independently define the global theory by its action, physical walls, and Cauchy data on $t=0$. Solving its causal source problem gives

$$\begin{align}
G_D^R(t,s)&=\theta(t-s)S(t-s), & G_D^A(t,s)&=-\theta(s-t)S(t-s),\\
E_D(t,s)&=G_D^A(t,s)-G_D^R(t,s)=-S(t-s).
\end{align}$$

The regional crossing response agrees with $G_D^R$ by the identity above. The diagonal blocks agree by regional Cauchy uniqueness, and the advanced blocks follow by transpose. Consequently

$$\begin{align}
\mathcal E_{ij}(t,x;s,z)&=E_D(t,x;s,z), & t&\in\mathbb R_i,\quad s\in\mathbb R_j.
\end{align}$$

Directly from the glued Cauchy algebra, the same bracket is

$$\begin{align}
\{\phi(t),\phi(s)\}_{\mathrm{glue}} &=C(t)S(s)-S(t)C(s)=-S(t-s).
\end{align}$$

In spatial modes the independent global expression is

$$\begin{align}
e_n(x)&=\sqrt{\frac2L}\sin\frac{n\pi x}{L}, & \omega_n&=\sqrt{m^2+\frac{n^2\pi^2}{L^2}},\\
E_D(t,x;s,z)&=-\sum_{n\geq1}e_n(x)e_n(z)\frac{\sin(\omega_n(t-s))}{\omega_n}.
\end{align}$$

The sign agrees with $\delta S/\delta\phi=-P\phi$, $X_F\mathbin{\cdot}\Omega=-\delta F$, and $\{q(x),p(z)\}=\delta(x-z)$.

#### Sources and Brackets at the Cut

For a configuration source $Q_\alpha=\int_0^L\alpha(x)q(x)\,\mathrm dx$, the released equation becomes

$$\begin{align}
\delta p_--\delta p_++\alpha&=0, & [\delta p]&=\alpha.
\end{align}$$

Its spacetime source is $\alpha(x)\delta(t)$. The retarded and advanced responses are

$$\begin{align}
\delta_{Q_\alpha}^R\phi(t)&=\theta(t)S(t)\alpha, & \delta_{Q_\alpha}^A\phi(t)&=-\theta(-t)S(t)\alpha,\\
X_{Q_\alpha}\phi(t)&=-S(t)\alpha, & X_{Q_\alpha}q&=0, & X_{Q_\alpha}p&=-\alpha.
\end{align}$$

Each causal response has the required momentum jump; their difference is smooth. In particular, vanishing $qq$ bracket does not mean that a configuration source has no effect. It changes the conjugate momentum. On $\alpha,\beta\in\mathscr D$, the complete Cauchy brackets are

$$\begin{align}
Q_\alpha&=\langle\alpha,q\rangle, & P_\beta&=\langle\beta,p\rangle,\\
\{Q_\alpha,Q_\beta\}&=0, & \{P_\alpha,P_\beta\}&=0, & \{Q_\alpha,P_\beta\}&=\langle\alpha,\beta\rangle,\\
\{\phi(t),Q_\alpha\}&=-S(t)\alpha, & \{\phi(t),P_\beta\}&=C(t)\beta.
\end{align}$$

The momentum observables here are defined through the Cauchy algebra. No product of singular action sources is needed. Unlike the null-supported source problem, smooth spatial smearing on this spacelike Cauchy surface gives smooth Hamiltonian solutions in the declared domain.

### Kernel, Image and Composition

Let $V=\mathscr D\oplus\mathscr D$ denote the linear Cauchy labels, and $\sigma((a,b),(c,d))=\langle a,d\rangle-\langle b,c\rangle$. Before sewing, the two copies have independent labels $v_-,v_+\in V$. On their polynomial presentation, the bracket obtained from the released action is

$$\begin{align}
\sigma_{\mathrm{glue}}((v_-,v_+),(w_-,w_+)) &=\sigma(v_-+v_+,w_-+w_+).
\end{align}$$

It includes the crossing brackets computed above and preserves each regional bracket. The radical and full polynomial kernel are

$$\begin{align}
K&=\{(v_- ,v_+):v_-+v_+=0\}, & I&=\langle Q^-_\alpha-Q^+_\alpha,\ P^-_\beta-P^+_\beta:\alpha,\beta\in\mathscr D\rangle.
\end{align}$$

The matching generators are central for $\sigma_{\mathrm{glue}}$, so $I$ is a Poisson ideal. Independently define $\mathcal A_D=\operatorname{Sym}(V)$ using the global Cauchy data and their CPS form. The map

$$\begin{align}
\operatorname{Sym}(V\oplus V)/I&\longrightarrow\mathcal A_D, & [(v_-,v_+)]&\longmapsto v_-+v_+
\end{align}$$

is a Poisson isomorphism. It is onto with section $v\mapsto(v,0)$, and the displayed ideal is its complete kernel because the symmetric algebra of a linear quotient is the quotient by its linear kernel. This proves exactness on the stated finite polynomial class.

The ordinary independent-product bracket cannot simply be quotiented by the same relations: it gives $\{Q^-_\alpha-Q^+_\alpha,P^-_\beta-P^+_\beta\}_{\mathrm{ind}}=2\langle\alpha,\beta\rangle$. The released bracket is essential.

For cuts at several times, use the regional transfer operator

$$\begin{align}
U(t)&=\begin{pmatrix}C(t)&S(t)\\
-A^2S(t)&C(t)\end{pmatrix}, &
U(a)U(b)&=U(a+b).
\end{align}$$

It preserves the Cauchy symplectic form. Identify matching data at each cut and propagate through the intervening slab; eliminating two adjacent cuts in either order gives the same transfer and the same bracket. This establishes composition for ordered constant-time cuts with the fixed physical walls.

### The Common Logic for the Three Cuts

In all three cases the action supplies an oriented interface potential $\int\Pi_i\delta q_i$. Prescribe the regional data, identify their common configuration, and release it in the summed action. The remaining step is to solve the appropriate regional response problem:

| Interface | Data and release equation | Response used for composition |
|---|---|---|
| Timelike | $q$ is a boundary history; oriented normal fluxes must cancel. | In the interval example, the specified causal Dirichlet-to-Neumann response yields $(D_1+D_2)\eta=\text{source mismatch}$. |
| Spacelike | $q$ is a configuration on a Cauchy surface; release gives $p_-=p_+$. | Propagate the pair $(\eta,\zeta)$ of field and momentum responses with $C,S$. |
| Null | $q$ is characteristic data; the two fluxes are $\pm\partial_vq$, so their sum vanishes on common histories. | Propagate compatible characteristic traces with their wall and corner data. |

The common construction is action variation followed by regional response and transmission. It does not require the same scalar inverse $(D_1+D_2)^{-1}$ for every interface. In the spacelike case, prescribing $q$ alone does not determine $p$, so it does not define a causal Dirichlet-to-Neumann map. Fixing additional temporal endpoint data can instead define a two-time boundary-value problem away from resonances, but its inverse is not the retarded or advanced Green operator used in the Peierls bracket.
