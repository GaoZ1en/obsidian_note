We study how separately quantized regional free-scalar theories can be glued by a quadratic interface interaction. The construction is a penalty realization of gluing, not a general or intrinsic definition of QFT gluing. Regional covariant phase spaces are first reduced to canonical oscillators; after truncation, their boundary traces define a finite coupled Hamiltonian. Because the omitted modes carry part of the boundary response, the bare interface coupling must depend on the cutoff. Matching this response at zero spatial energy reconstructs the low-energy joined spectrum more rapidly than a direct hard projection. On two Neumann half intervals, the continuum finite-coupling spectrum is analytic, the joined-endpoint running coupling has an exact trigamma form, and the same response-matched Hamiltonian reconstructs selected smeared equal-time vacuum two-point functions across and adjacent to the interface. Four-region and global $\mathrm{AdS}_2$ benchmarks test edgewise composition and curvature dependence. The results are restricted to free Gaussian theories, fixed spectral windows, and the displayed observables; they do not establish arbitrary state or correlator convergence, continuum product-Fock equivalence, or interacting-QFT gluing.
