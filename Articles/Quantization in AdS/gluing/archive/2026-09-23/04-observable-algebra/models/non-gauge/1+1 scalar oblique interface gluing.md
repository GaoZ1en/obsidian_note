The goal is to test observable-algebra gluing across a cut which moves in the original coordinates. The cut is $x=vt$, with $|v|<1$. Its flux mixes a time derivative with a spatial derivative. We will retain that term, release the common history, and compare the glued Poisson bracket with the uncut answer.

## 1+1 Example

Take a real scalar with mass $m\geq0$ in flat spacetime, $ds^2=-dt^2+dx^2$. Introduce

$$\begin{align}
\tau&=\gamma(t-vx),&\xi&=\gamma(x-vt),&\gamma&=(1-v^2)^{-1/2}.
\end{align}$$

Then $ds^2=-d\tau^2+d\xi^2$. Choose the regions

$$\begin{align}
\Sigma_1&=[-L,0]_\xi,&\Sigma_2&=[0,L]_\xi,
\end{align}$$

and impose Dirichlet conditions at $\xi=\pm L$. In the original coordinates the cut and the physical walls are

$$\begin{align}
x_{\mathrm{cut}}(t)&=vt,&x_\pm(t)&=vt\pm L/\gamma.
\end{align}$$

Thus the entire cavity moves at constant velocity. This choice gives an exact comparison at every frequency. It is a test of an oblique timelike cut, with comoving physical walls; it does not describe a moving cut between stationary walls or an accelerating cavity.

The regional action is

$$\begin{align}
S_i[\phi_i]&=\frac12\int_{M_i}dt\,dx\left[(\partial_t\phi_i)^2-(\partial_x\phi_i)^2-m^2\phi_i^2\right]\\
&=\frac12\int d\tau\,d\xi\left[(\partial_\tau\phi_i)^2-(\partial_\xi\phi_i)^2-m^2\phi_i^2\right].
\end{align}$$

At the cut, $\tau=t/\gamma$. Let $q_i(\tau)=\phi_i(\gamma\tau,\gamma v\tau)$. The outward normal derivatives towards the cut are

$$\begin{align}
\Pi_1&=\partial_\xi\phi_1=\gamma(v\partial_t+\partial_x)\phi_1,\\
\Pi_2&=-\partial_\xi\phi_2=-\gamma(v\partial_t+\partial_x)\phi_2.
\end{align}$$

The cut term in the action variation is $-\int d\tau\,\Pi_i\delta q_i$. Written using the original time, the region-1 term is

$$\begin{align}
-\int dt\,[(v\partial_t+\partial_x)\phi_1]_{x=vt}\,\delta q_{1,\mathrm{lab}}(t),&\qquad q_{1,\mathrm{lab}}(t)=q_1(t/\gamma).
\end{align}$$

This is where the oblique cut enters the action. Replacing its flux by $\partial_x\phi$ alone would miss the moving-boundary contribution.

### Regional Observable Algebra

At fixed $q_i$, let $\mathcal P_i[q_i]$ be the regional solutions with this history and the physical Dirichlet wall. Define

$$\begin{align}
\Omega_i&=\int_{\Sigma_i}d\xi\,\delta(\partial_\tau\phi_i)\wedge\delta\phi_i,\\
\mathcal H_i[q_i]&=\{F:\exists X_{F,i},\ X_{F,i}\mathbin{\cdot}\Omega_i=-\delta F\},\\
\{F,G\}_{i,q_i}&=X_{F,i}\mathbin{\cdot}X_{G,i}\mathbin{\cdot}\Omega_i.
\end{align}$$

Collect the history-dependent families as in the stationary scalar example, retaining their response to changes of $q_i$. The fixed-history bracket acts only on the regional solution; a pure function of the prescribed history has zero bracket. The independent product has zero cross-region bracket.

We use smooth smeared fields and histories and their finite sums and products. Point-field brackets below mean their smearing kernels. Causal responses start from zero past or zero future data, and source insertions are away from the temporal ends.

### Algebra Gluing

Set $q_1=q_2=q$ and release $q$ in the summed action. The resulting condition is

$$\begin{align}
\Pi_1+\Pi_2&=0,\\
[(v\partial_t+\partial_x)\phi_1]_{x=vt}&=[(v\partial_t+\partial_x)\phi_2]_{x=vt}.
\end{align}$$

Equality of the histories also gives equality of their tangential derivatives:

$$\begin{align}
[(\partial_t+v\partial_x)\phi_1]_{x=vt}&=[(\partial_t+v\partial_x)\phi_2]_{x=vt}.
\end{align}$$

Since $1-v^2\neq0$, these two equations give continuity of both $\partial_t\phi$ and $\partial_x\phi$. Joining the two fields therefore gives the uncut solution, and cutting that solution recovers the matched pair.

On a common $\tau$ slice the symplectic forms simply add. The same form on a laboratory-time slice is

$$\begin{align}
\Omega_{\mathrm{glue}}&=\int_{vt-L/\gamma}^{vt+L/\gamma}dx\,\delta(\partial_t\phi)\wedge\delta\phi.
\end{align}$$

The symplectic current has no flux through a physical Dirichlet wall, and the two cut fluxes cancel after matching. Thus the two slice expressions agree when evaluated on the same variations. The oblique choice has not introduced another degree of freedom.

As before, identify common-history expressions which agree on matched solutions, and calculate their bracket with the history released. The response calculation below constructs that bracket before using the global comparison.

If $\mathcal H_{\mathrm{com}}$ is the algebra generated by regional smeared fields and common histories, write

$$\begin{align}
\mathcal I&=\{F\in\mathcal H_{\mathrm{com}}:F|_{\mathcal P_{\mathrm{glue}}}=0\},& \mathcal H_{\mathrm{glue}}&=\mathcal H_{\mathrm{com}}/\mathcal I.
\end{align}$$

The global algebra is independently generated by the same smeared observables evaluated on the uncut moving cavity, including field values along its interior curve $x=vt$. Its bracket is obtained from the uncut action.

### Regional Response and the Glued Bracket

Write the variation of an observable as

$$\begin{align}
\delta G&=\sum_i\int d\tau\,d\xi\,f_i\delta\phi_i+\int d\tau\,g\delta q.
\end{align}$$

At either causal prescription, define

$$\begin{align}
\delta_G\phi_i&=G_i f_i+H_i\eta_G,\\
\delta_G\Pi_i&=\rho_i f_i+D_i\eta_G,&\eta_G&=\delta_Gq.
\end{align}$$

The released equation is $\delta_G\Pi_1+\delta_G\Pi_2=g$. Therefore

$$\begin{align}
K&=(D_1+D_2)^{-1},&\eta_G&=K\left(g-\sum_j\rho_jf_j\right),\\
\mathcal G_{ij}&=\delta_{ij}G_i-H_iK\rho_j,&\mathcal G_{iq}&=H_iK,\\
\mathcal G_{qj}&=-K\rho_j,&\mathcal G_{qq}&=K.
\end{align}$$

Set $Z=(\phi_1,\phi_2,q)$. With $\mathcal E=\mathcal G^A-\mathcal G^R$, the glued bracket is

$$\begin{align}
\{F,G\}_{\mathrm{glue}}&=\sum_{a,b}\int\frac{\delta F}{\delta Z_a}\mathcal E_{ab}\frac{\delta G}{\delta Z_b}.
\end{align}$$

The structure is unchanged by the tilt. Its geometric content sits in the action-derived flux and the history time variable.

### Explicit Verification

Use inward regional coordinates $y_1=\xi+L$, $y_2=L-\xi$, both in $[0,L]$. Fourier transform with respect to $\tau$, not $t$, and put $\kappa^2=m^2-\zeta^2$, $\zeta_{R/A}=\omega\pm i0$. The regional response is

$$\begin{align}
\widehat G_i(y,y')&=\frac{\sinh(\kappa y_<)\sinh(\kappa(L-y_>))}{\kappa\sinh(\kappa L)},\\
\widehat H_i(y)&=\frac{\sinh(\kappa y)}{\sinh(\kappa L)},&\widehat\rho_i(y')&=-\frac{\sinh(\kappa y')}{\sinh(\kappa L)},\\
\widehat D_i&=\kappa\coth(\kappa L),&\widehat K&=\frac{\sinh^2(\kappa L)}{\kappa\sinh(2\kappa L)}.
\end{align}$$

These follow by solving $(-\partial_y^2+\kappa^2)G_i=1$, with zero endpoint values, and the homogeneous equation for $H_i$, with $H_i(0)=0$, $H_i(L)=1$. Products are formed before the causal limits. In particular,

$$\begin{align}
\widehat{\mathcal G}_{12}(y,y')&=\widehat H_1(y)\widehat K\widehat H_2(y') =\frac{\sinh(\kappa y)\sinh(\kappa y')}{\kappa\sinh(2\kappa L)}.
\end{align}$$

Independently solve the uncut interval source equation in $\xi\in[-L,L]$. Its answer is

$$\begin{align}
\widehat G_{\mathrm{global}}(\xi,\xi')&=\frac{\sinh(\kappa(\xi_<+L))\sinh(\kappa(L-\xi_>))}{\kappa\sinh(2\kappa L)}.
\end{align}$$

The cross block above equals this expression at $\xi=-L+y$, $\xi'=L-y'$. The same-region block equals it by

$$\begin{align}
\widehat G_i+\widehat H_i\widehat K\widehat H_i^{\mathsf T}&=\widehat G_{\mathrm{global}}\big|_{\Sigma_i\times\Sigma_i}.
\end{align}$$

Likewise $H_iK$, $KH_j^{\mathsf T}$ and $K$ are the global response with one or both points at $\xi=0$. Thus all released blocks agree with the uncut ones, separately for $R$ and $A$.

The transformation from $(t,x)$ to $(\tau,\xi)$ has determinant one and preserves the scalar action. Consequently the global laboratory kernel, obtained from the uncut moving cavity, is

$$\begin{align}
E_{\mathrm{lab}}(t,x;t',x')&=E_{\mathrm{global}}\left(\gamma(t-vx),\gamma(x-vt);\gamma(t'-vx'),\gamma(x'-vt')\right).
\end{align}$$

The regional calculation gives exactly this same kernel. There is no additional boost factor on the bulk field bracket. Equality of the kernels gives equality of the generated smeared observable algebras and their Poisson brackets.

Thus assembly obeys

$$\begin{align}
\mathrm{Ass}^*:\mathcal H_{\mathrm{global}}&\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathcal H_{\mathrm{glue}},& \mathrm{Ass}^*\{F,G\}_{\mathrm{global}}&=\{\mathrm{Ass}^*F,\mathrm{Ass}^*G\}_{\mathrm{glue}}.
\end{align}$$

A global integral splits into two regional integrals; conversely every regional expression can be evaluated on the assembled field. The source terms cancel in the advanced-minus-retarded response, which therefore preserves matching. Expressions vanishing on all matched solutions have zero bracket there. This makes the bracket on the quotient independent of the representative.

There is a time-measure factor when writing history responses in laboratory time. If $\delta G=\int dt\,g_{\mathrm{lab}}\delta q_{\mathrm{lab}}$, then

$$\begin{align}
g(\tau)&=\gamma g_{\mathrm{lab}}(\gamma\tau),\\
\delta_Gq_{\mathrm{lab}}(t)&=\int dt'\,K((t-t')/\gamma)g_{\mathrm{lab}}(t')\qquad(f_i=0),\\
\{q_{\mathrm{lab}}(t),q_{\mathrm{lab}}(t')\}_{\mathrm{glue}}&=(K^A-K^R)((t-t')/\gamma).
\end{align}$$

The source rescaling and $d\tau'=dt'/\gamma$ cancel. Keeping both is necessary to get the history bracket right.

### A Nonzero Oblique Crossing Bracket

Before a reflected signal arrives, the massive line kernel gives

$$\begin{align}
\{\phi(t,x),\phi(t',x')\}_{\mathrm{glue}} &=-\frac12\operatorname{sgn}(\Delta\tau) J_0\!\left(m\sqrt{\Delta\tau^2-\Delta\xi^2}\right),\\
|\Delta\xi|<|\Delta\tau|&<\min(2L+\xi+\xi',2L-\xi-\xi').
\end{align}$$

Choose $v=3/5$, so $\gamma=5/4$, and two events with rest coordinates

$$\begin{align}
(\tau,\xi)&=(3L/4,-L/4),&(\tau',\xi')&=(0,L/4).
\end{align}$$

Their laboratory coordinates are

$$\begin{align}
(t,x)&=(3L/4,L/4),&(t',x')&=(3L/16,5L/16).
\end{align}$$

The first event lies to the left of the moving cut and the second lies to its right. They obey

$$\begin{align}
\Delta t&=9L/16,&\Delta x&=-L/16,&\Delta t^2-\Delta x^2&=5L^2/16.
\end{align}$$

Therefore

$$\begin{align}
\{\phi_1(3L/4,L/4),\phi_2(3L/16,5L/16)\}_{\mathrm{glue}} &=-\frac12J_0(m\sqrt5L/4),\\
\{\phi_1(3L/4,L/4),\phi_2(3L/16,5L/16)\}_{\mathrm{ind}}&=0.
\end{align}$$

For $m=0$, the first value is exactly $-1/2$, also for normalized smooth smearings in sufficiently small neighborhoods of these events. The kernel is constant there. A source at the second event induces a nonzero cut history, and the $H_1KH_2^{\mathsf T}$ response carries it to the first event.

The cut can therefore be tilted without changing the gluing mechanism. What changes is the physical meaning of its flux: it is the derivative normal to the moving cut, including its time-derivative contribution.
