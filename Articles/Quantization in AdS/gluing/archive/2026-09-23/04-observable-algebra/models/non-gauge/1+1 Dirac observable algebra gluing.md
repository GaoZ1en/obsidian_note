The goal is to glue the classical observable algebras of two massive Dirac intervals. Since the equation is first order, the cut history contains only half of the spinor components. The complementary components are the response. Releasing the history will reconnect them and restore the global graded Poisson bracket.

## 1+1 Example

Take $\Sigma_1=[-L,0]$, $\Sigma_2=[0,L]$, $L>0$, with $ds^2=-dt^2+dx^2$ and $m>0$. We keep the same increasing $x$ coordinate and spinor basis in both regions. Let $\psi=(u,v)^{\mathsf T}$ be a Grassmann-odd field, with action

$$\begin{align}
S_i&=\int dt\,dx\left[\frac{i}{2}\psi_i^\dagger\overleftrightarrow{\partial_t}\psi_i
+\frac{i}{2}\psi_i^\dagger\sigma_3\overleftrightarrow{\partial_x}\psi_i
-m\psi_i^\dagger\sigma_1\psi_i\right]+B_i.
\end{align}$$

Here $a\overleftrightarrow\partial b=a\partial b-(\partial a)b$. The bulk equation is

$$\begin{align}
i\partial_t\psi_i&=(-i\sigma_3\partial_x+m\sigma_1)\psi_i.
\end{align}$$

The components $u,v$ propagate in opposite directions and the mass couples them. It is convenient to use

$$\begin{align}
a&=\frac{u+v}{\sqrt2},&b&=\frac{u-v}{\sqrt2},&\Psi&=\begin{pmatrix}a\\b\end{pmatrix},\\
\mathscr D&=i\partial_t+i\sigma_1\partial_x-m\sigma_3,&\mathscr D\Psi&=0.
\end{align}$$

At the physical walls impose $b=0$, or $u=v$. This is reflecting: the normal current is proportional to $a^\dagger b+b^\dagger a$, which vanishes there.

At the cut let $n_1=+1$ and $n_2=-1$ be the outward spatial normals. Choose

$$\begin{align}
B_i&=\frac{i n_i}{2}\int dt\,(a_i^\dagger b_i-b_i^\dagger a_i)_{x=0}.
\end{align}$$

The cut term in the variation becomes

$$\begin{align}
\delta S_i\big|_{\mathrm{cut}}&=i n_i\int dt\,(a_i^\dagger\delta b_i-\delta b_i^\dagger a_i).
\end{align}$$

Thus we prescribe $b_i=q_i$ and its adjoint. The complementary component $a_i$ is free to respond. Fixing both $a_i$ and $b_i$ would remove this response and would not be the regional problem used here.

### Regional Observable Algebra

For fixed $q_i,q_i^\dagger$, define $\mathcal P_i[q_i]$ to be the solutions with the physical wall condition and this cut history. The symplectic form is

$$\begin{align}
\Omega_i&=i\int_{\Sigma_i}dx\,\delta\Psi_i^\dagger\wedge_{\mathrm{gr}}\delta\Psi_i.
\end{align}$$

The subscript reminds us that the fields are odd. With $X_F\mathbin{\cdot}\Omega_i=-\delta F$, the canonical bracket is

$$\begin{align}
\{\Psi_{i\alpha}(t,x),\Psi_{i\beta}^\dagger(t,x')\}_i&=-i\delta_{\alpha\beta}\delta(x-x'),\\
\{F,G\}_i&=-(-1)^{|F||G|}\{G,F\}_i.
\end{align}$$

In particular, the bracket of two odd observables is symmetric under exchanging the observables, including their arguments. We use the graded product rule throughout.

Define $\mathcal H_i[q_i]$ as the Hamiltonian observables on $\mathcal P_i[q_i]$, and collect them into history-dependent families. At fixed history, the bracket of pure history functions is zero, as is the bracket between different regions. Retain the regional response along with these brackets.

The explicit algebra below is generated by smooth smearings of $\Psi,\Psi^\dagger,q,q^\dagger$ and their finite sums and products. Point-field expressions specify the kernels for these smearings. Sources are switched off near temporal ends. Retarded and advanced responses start with zero past and zero future data, respectively.

### Algebra Gluing

Set $q_1=q_2=q$ and release $q,q^\dagger$. The boundary variation of $S_1+S_2$ gives

$$\begin{align}
a_1=a_2,&\qquad a_1^\dagger=a_2^\dagger.
\end{align}$$

Together with $b_1=b_2=q$, this is continuity of the full spinor. No normal derivative matching is added: the first-order equation already determines how the spinor continues. The two $B_i$ terms cancel on the matched fields, leaving precisely the uncut action.

Let $\mathcal P_{\mathrm{glue}}$ be the matched solutions. Assembly and cutting are inverse, and

$$\begin{align}
\Omega_{\mathrm{glue}}&=\Omega_1+\Omega_2=\Omega_{\mathrm{global}}.
\end{align}$$

Two expressions in the common-history algebra represent the same glued observable if they agree on these solutions. To find their bracket we must allow $q$ to respond. Keeping the old fixed-history bracket would still leave the two intervals dynamically disconnected.

Writing $\mathcal H_{\mathrm{com}}$ for the algebra generated by regional smeared spinors and common histories, this identification is

$$\begin{align}
\mathcal I&=\{F\in\mathcal H_{\mathrm{com}}:F|_{\mathcal P_{\mathrm{glue}}}=0\},&
\mathcal H_{\mathrm{glue}}&=\mathcal H_{\mathrm{com}}/\mathcal I.
\end{align}$$

The quotient uses the usual multiplication with the fermionic signs; its new graded bracket comes from the released response. Define the global algebra independently on the uncut interval, using the same smearings interpreted on the global field, including $b(t,0)$ and its adjoint.

### Regional Response and the Released Bracket

Let $G_i^{R/A}$ solve $\mathscr D G_i^{R/A}=1$, with $b=0$ at both ends of the regional interval. Let $H_i^{R/A}\eta$ solve the homogeneous bulk equation with $b=\eta$ at the cut and $b=0$ at the physical wall. Write

$$\begin{align}
a_i\big|_{\mathrm{cut}}&=r_i\eta\qquad\text{for the response }H_i\eta,\\
\rho_i f_i&=n_i\,[G_i f_i]_a\big|_{\mathrm{cut}},&K&=(r_1-r_2)^{-1}.
\end{align}$$

For an even source perturbation

$$\begin{align}
G&=\sum_i\int(\Psi_i^\dagger f_i+f_i^\dagger\Psi_i)+\int(q^\dagger g+g^\dagger q),
\end{align}$$

the source spinors $f_i,g$ are odd and the displayed order is fixed. Vary $\Psi^\dagger,q^\dagger$ from the left. The perturbed bulk and cut equations are

$$\begin{align}
\mathscr D\delta_G\Psi_i&=-f_i,&\delta_G(a_1-a_2)&=-ig.
\end{align}$$

Hence

$$\begin{align}
\delta_G\Psi_i&=-G_i f_i+H_i\eta_G,\\
\eta_G&=K\left(\sum_j\rho_jf_j-ig\right).
\end{align}$$

Define the full source-response matrix by $\delta_G Z=-\mathcal G(f_1,f_2,g)^{\mathsf T}$, where $Z=(\Psi_1,\Psi_2,q)$. Its blocks are

$$\begin{align}
\mathcal G_{ij}&=\delta_{ij}G_i-H_iK\rho_j,&\mathcal G_{iq}&=iH_iK,\\
\mathcal G_{qj}&=-K\rho_j,&\mathcal G_{qq}&=iK.
\end{align}$$

All equations hold separately for $R$ and $A$. Since the action perturbation produces $-\mathcal G f$, its advanced-minus-retarded change is

$$\begin{align}
\mathcal E&=\mathcal G^R-\mathcal G^A,&\{Z_\alpha,Z_\beta^\dagger\}_{\mathrm{glue}}&=\mathcal E_{\alpha\beta},\\
\{Z_\alpha,Z_\beta\}_{\mathrm{glue}}&=0,&\{Z_\alpha^\dagger,Z_\beta^\dagger\}_{\mathrm{glue}}&=0.
\end{align}$$

These generator brackets and the graded product rule define the bracket of the polynomial observables. The $R-A$ here follows from $\mathscr D$ being the action operator; the scalar convention uses a source operator with the opposite sign. In either case the Hamiltonian flow is the advanced-minus-retarded response to the action perturbation.

### Explicit Verification

Fourier transform in time with $e^{-i\omega t}$ and set

$$\begin{align}
\kappa^2&=m^2-\zeta^2,&d&=\frac{i(\zeta-m)}{\kappa},&\zeta_{R/A}&=\omega\pm i0.
\end{align}$$

The homogeneous equation is an elementary transfer equation:

$$\begin{align}
\partial_x\widehat\Psi&=\begin{pmatrix}0&i(\zeta+m)\\i(\zeta-m)&0\end{pmatrix}\widehat\Psi.
\end{align}$$

The square of this matrix is $\kappa^2 1$. For any interval $[A,B]$, define the two column solutions

$$\begin{align}
w_L(x)&=\begin{pmatrix}\cosh\kappa(x-A)\\d\sinh\kappa(x-A)\end{pmatrix},&
w_R(x)&=\begin{pmatrix}\cosh\kappa(B-x)\\-d\sinh\kappa(B-x)\end{pmatrix}.
\end{align}$$

They obey $b(A)=0$ and $b(B)=0$, respectively. The Green matrix has the form

$$\begin{align}
\widehat G^{[A,B]}(x,x')&=\begin{cases}w_L(x)c_L(x'),&x<x',\\w_R(x)c_R(x'),&x>x',\end{cases}\\
\begin{pmatrix}c_L(x')\\c_R(x')\end{pmatrix}
&=\begin{pmatrix}-w_L(x')&w_R(x')\end{pmatrix}^{-1}(-i\sigma_1).
\end{align}$$

Here $c_L,c_R$ are rows, so the products above are two-by-two matrices. The determinant of the matrix being inverted is $d\sinh\kappa(B-A)$. This formula explicitly solves the source jump

$$\begin{align}
i\sigma_1[\widehat G(x'+0,x')-\widehat G(x'-0,x')]&=1.
\end{align}$$

Use $[A,B]=[-L,0]$ for $G_1$ and $[0,L]$ for $G_2$. The history responses are

$$\begin{align}
\widehat H_1(x)&=\frac1{d\sinh\kappa L}\begin{pmatrix}\cosh\kappa(x+L)\\d\sinh\kappa(x+L)\end{pmatrix},\\
\widehat H_2(x)&=\frac1{-d\sinh\kappa L}\begin{pmatrix}\cosh\kappa(L-x)\\-d\sinh\kappa(L-x)\end{pmatrix},\\
\widehat r_1&=\frac{\coth\kappa L}{d},&\widehat r_2&=-\frac{\coth\kappa L}{d},&\widehat K&=\frac d2\tanh\kappa L.
\end{align}$$

Together with $\widehat\rho_i=n_i[\widehat G_i(0,x')]_a$, this specifies every regional response. Products are formed at complex frequency before the causal limits; removable expressions are evaluated by their limits.

Now independently solve the uncut problem using exactly the source-jump construction with $[A,B]=[-L,L]$. Call the answer $G_{\mathrm{global}}$. Substituting the regional formulas gives

$$\begin{align}
\delta_{ij}\widehat G_i-\widehat H_i\widehat K\widehat\rho_j
&=\widehat G_{\mathrm{global}}\big|_{\Sigma_i\times\Sigma_j},\\
i\widehat H_i\widehat K&=\widehat G_{\mathrm{global}}(x,0)e_b,\\
-\widehat K\widehat\rho_j&=e_b^{\mathsf T}\widehat G_{\mathrm{global}}(0,x'),\\
i\widehat K&=e_b^{\mathsf T}\widehat G_{\mathrm{global}}(0,0)e_b,&e_b&=\begin{pmatrix}0\\1\end{pmatrix}.
\end{align}$$

The last value is unambiguous: a source in the $b$ equation jumps $a$, while $b$ remains continuous. These identities follow by multiplying the displayed two-by-two matrices and applying the hyperbolic addition formulas.

Thus all bulk, mixed and history brackets coincide with the independently calculated global brackets. Assembly identifies the generated graded observable algebras. In particular, the history $q$ is the global $b$ field at the cut, not an additional fermion.

For the assembly map this reads

$$\begin{align}
\mathrm{Ass}^*:\mathcal H_{\mathrm{global}}&\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathcal H_{\mathrm{glue}},&
\mathrm{Ass}^*\{F,G\}_{\mathrm{global}}&=\{\mathrm{Ass}^*F,\mathrm{Ass}^*G\}_{\mathrm{glue}}.
\end{align}$$

Bulk integrals split into the two regional integrals. In the advanced-minus-retarded response the source terms cancel, leaving a variation of the matched spinor. It therefore annihilates expressions that vanish on all matched solutions. Consequently the bracket is independent of the chosen representative. Its agreement on the field generators extends to their finite products by the graded product rule.

### A Nonzero Crossing Bracket

Let $U(\tau;x,x')$ propagate the uncut spinor by a time $\tau$. The causal matrices obey

$$\begin{align}
G^R(\tau)&=-i\theta(\tau)U(\tau),&G^A(\tau)&=i\theta(-\tau)U(\tau),\\
\{\Psi(t,x),\Psi^\dagger(t',x')\}_{\mathrm{glue}}&=-iU(t-t';x,x').
\end{align}$$

At equal times this returns $-i1\delta(x-x')$, fixing the sign and normalization. Before either physical wall can reflect a signal, the global answer is the line answer. Put $\tau=t-t'>0$, $s=x-x'$ and

$$\begin{align}
S_m(\tau,s)&=\frac12\theta(\tau-|s|)J_0\!\left(m\sqrt{\tau^2-s^2}\right),\\
U(\tau,s)&=(\partial_\tau-\sigma_1\partial_s-im\sigma_3)S_m(\tau,s).
\end{align}$$

The derivatives include the wavefront pulses. Strictly inside the direct lightcone, let $r=\sqrt{\tau^2-s^2}$. Then

$$\begin{align}
\{a(t,x),a^\dagger(t',x')\}_{\mathrm{glue}}
&=-\frac m2J_0(mr)+\frac{im\tau}{2r}J_1(mr),\\
|s|<\tau&<\min(2L+x+x',2L-x-x').
\end{align}$$

For $x=-L/4$, $x'=L/4$, $\tau=3L/4$ and $m=1/L$, this gives

$$\begin{align}
\{a(t,-L/4),a^\dagger(t',L/4)\}_{\mathrm{glue}}
&=\frac1L\left[-\frac12J_0(\sqrt5/4)+\frac{3i}{2\sqrt5}J_1(\sqrt5/4)\right]\\
&\simeq\frac{-0.461694+0.180271i}{L},\\
\{a(t,-L/4),a^\dagger(t',L/4)\}_{\mathrm{ind}}&=0.
\end{align}$$

Smooth smearings in small neighborhoods give the corresponding nonzero averaged bracket. The result comes entirely from $-H_1K\rho_2$ in the released Green matrix. The same perturbation gives $\eta_G=K\rho_2f_2$, even with no explicit history source.

This is the first-order version of the scalar mechanism: one half of the cut spinor is prescribed, the other half responds, and releasing the first half reconnects propagation across the cut.
