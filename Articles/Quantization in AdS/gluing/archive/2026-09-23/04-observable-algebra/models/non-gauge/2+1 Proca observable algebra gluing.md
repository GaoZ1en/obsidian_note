The goal is to glue the classical observable algebras of two massive Proca regions. The new ingredient is a constraint: the temporal component is determined by the electric field, but there is no gauge freedom to quotient. We will keep the interface history, release it, and compute the resulting Poisson bracket directly.

## 2+1 Example

Take

$$\begin{align}
\Sigma_1&=[-L,0]\times S^1_R,&\Sigma_2&=[0,L]\times S^1_R,\\
ds^2&=-dt^2+dx^2+dz^2,&z&\sim z+2\pi R,
\end{align}$$

with $L,R>0$ and $m>0$. Both regions use coordinates pointing from the physical wall towards the cut:

$$\begin{align}
x_1(y)&=-L+y,&x_2(y)&=L-y,&0\leq y\leq L.
\end{align}$$

Write $A_y=A_x$ in region 1 and $A_y=-A_x$ in region 2. The regional action is

$$\begin{align}
S_i[A_i]&=\int dt\,dy\,dz\left(-\frac14F_{i\mu\nu}F_i^{\mu\nu}-\frac{m^2}{2}A_{i\mu}A_i^\mu\right),\\
F_{i\mu\nu}&=\partial_\mu A_{i\nu}-\partial_\nu A_{i\mu}.
\end{align}$$

At the physical wall impose $A_t=A_z=0$. At the cut prescribe the two tangential components

$$\begin{align}
q_i&=\begin{pmatrix}A_{it}\\A_{iz}\end{pmatrix}_{y=L},&
\Pi_i&=\begin{pmatrix}F_i^{yt}\\F_i^{yz}\end{pmatrix}_{y=L}.
\end{align}$$

The cut term in the variation is $-\int dt\,dz\,\Pi_i^{\mathsf T}\delta q_i$. There is no independent term multiplying $\delta A_y$: its absence is part of the Proca dynamics, not a discarded boundary condition.

The equations and their divergence give

$$\begin{align}
\partial_\mu F_i^{\mu\nu}-m^2A_i^\nu&=0,&\partial_\mu A_i^\mu&=0.
\end{align}$$

For example, with $E_a=F_{ta}$, $a=y,z$, the temporal equation gives

$$\begin{align}
A_t&=-\frac{\partial_yE_y+\partial_zE_z}{m^2},&
\Omega_i&=\int dy\,dz\,\delta E_a\wedge\delta A_a.
\end{align}$$

Thus $A_t$ is not another canonical coordinate. The two spatial components carry the two physical polarizations.

### Regional Observable Algebra

For prescribed $q_i$, let $\mathcal P_i[q_i]$ be the regional solutions with this cut history and the physical wall condition. Define

$$\begin{align}
\mathcal H_i[q_i]&=\{F:\exists X_{F,i},\ X_{F,i}\mathbin{\cdot}\Omega_i=-\delta F\},\\
\{F,G\}_i&=X_{F,i}\mathbin{\cdot}X_{G,i}\mathbin{\cdot}\Omega_i.
\end{align}$$

Collect these into families $F[A_i,q_i]$, with the bracket evaluated at fixed $q_i$. Pure history functions have zero bracket at this stage. To glue these families we retain the regional response as well as this bracket: it tells us how changing $q_i$ changes the bulk field and the flux.

We use smeared fields, their finite sums and products, and smeared histories. A field written at a point below denotes the kernel to be integrated against smooth smearing functions. Retarded responses have zero past data and advanced responses have zero future data; sources are switched off near the chosen temporal ends. Start with bulk sources away from the walls and cut when taking boundary derivatives.

### Algebra Gluing

First set $q_1=q_2=q$. Releasing this common history in $S_1+S_2$ gives

$$\begin{align}
\Pi_1+\Pi_2&=0.
\end{align}$$

The fields then have the same tangential components and oriented flux at the cut. The normal Proca equation also gives the same global $A_x$: on either side it determines $m^2A_y$ from tangential derivatives of $\Pi$. There is no additional interface vector field.

Let $\mathcal P_{\mathrm{glue}}$ be these matched solutions. Joining their fields produces a solution on $[-L,L]\times S^1_R$, and cutting such a global solution reverses the construction. On a common time slice,

$$\begin{align}
\Omega_{\mathrm{glue}}&=\Omega_1+\Omega_2=\Omega_{\mathrm{global}}.
\end{align}$$

For observables, identify two common-history expressions when they agree on all matched solutions. Their glued bracket is computed after release. It is not the old fixed-history bracket passed to these equivalence classes. We now calculate the new bracket using regional data only, then compare it with the independently defined global algebra.

In formulas, let $\mathcal H_{\mathrm{com}}$ denote the algebra generated by the regional smeared fields and common histories. Set

$$\begin{align}
\iota_{\mathrm{glue}}^*F&=F|_{\mathcal P_{\mathrm{glue}}},&
\mathcal I&=\{F\in\mathcal H_{\mathrm{com}}:\iota_{\mathrm{glue}}^*F=0\},&
\mathcal H_{\mathrm{glue}}&=\mathcal H_{\mathrm{com}}/\mathcal I.
\end{align}$$

This quotient identifies expressions for the same observable. The response below supplies its Poisson bracket. The global algebra is defined independently by smeared fields on the uncut cylinder, with its own action and bracket.

### Realization of the Poisson Bracket

Perturb the action by $\varepsilon G$, writing

$$\begin{align}
\delta G&=\sum_i\int f_i^\mu\delta A_{i\mu}+\int g^{\mathsf T}\delta q.
\end{align}$$

The sources $f_i$ need not be conserved. Indeed the perturbed equation implies $m^2\partial_\mu\delta A_i^\mu=\partial_\mu f_i^\mu$. This source term must be retained when calculating the longitudinal response.

Define the four regional responses, at either retarded or advanced prescription, by

$$\begin{align}
\delta_G A_i&=G_i f_i+H_i\eta_G,\\
\delta_G\Pi_i&=\rho_i f_i+D_i\eta_G,&\eta_G&=\delta_Gq.
\end{align}$$

Varying the released action gives $\delta_G\Pi_1+\delta_G\Pi_2=g$, hence

$$\begin{align}
K&=(D_1+D_2)^{-1},&\eta_G&=K\left(g-\sum_j\rho_jf_j\right),\\
\mathcal G_{ij}&=\delta_{ij}G_i-H_iK\rho_j,&\mathcal G_{iq}&=H_iK,\\
\mathcal G_{qj}&=-K\rho_j,&\mathcal G_{qq}&=K.
\end{align}$$

Each of these equations holds separately for $R$ and $A$. With

$$\begin{align}
\mathcal E&=\mathcal G^A-\mathcal G^R,&
\{F,G\}_{\mathrm{glue}}&=\sum_{a,b}\int\frac{\delta F}{\delta Z_a}\mathcal E_{ab}\frac{\delta G}{\delta Z_b},&Z&=(A_1,A_2,q),
\end{align}$$

the response of the common history supplies both cross-region and history brackets. The formula has the same form as for the scalar, but $q$ now has two components and $G_i$ includes the Proca constraint.

### Explicit Regional Response

Use $e^{-i\omega t+ikz}$, with $k=n/R$, $n\in\mathbb Z$, and put

$$\begin{align}
\kappa^2&=m^2+k^2-\zeta^2,&\zeta_R&=\omega+i0,&\zeta_A&=\omega-i0.
\end{align}$$

Frequency formulas are evaluated at complex $\zeta$ before taking either causal limit. Apparent removable singularities are interpreted by their limits. For an interval of length $a$, define

$$\begin{align}
g_D^{[a]}(y,y')&=\frac{\sinh(\kappa y_<)\sinh(\kappa(a-y_>))}{\kappa\sinh(\kappa a)},\\
g_N^{[a]}(y,y')&=\frac{\cosh(\kappa y_<)\cosh(\kappa(a-y_>))}{\kappa\sinh(\kappa a)}.
\end{align}$$

The first vanishes at the ends; the second has zero normal derivative there. Both solve the scalar source equation with mass squared $m^2+k^2$. In component order $(t,y,z)$, the fixed-history Proca response is

$$\begin{align}
\widehat G_{i\mu\nu}&=\operatorname{diag}(-g_D^{[L]},g_N^{[L]},g_D^{[L]})_{\mu\nu}
+\frac1{m^2}\partial_\mu\partial'_{\nu}g_D^{[L]}.
\end{align}$$

Here $\partial_t=-i\zeta$, $\partial'_t=i\zeta$, $\partial_z=ik$, $\partial'_z=-ik$, and the remaining derivatives act on $y,y'$. The second index pairs with $f_i^\nu$. In particular, the double normal derivative includes its local source term:

$$\begin{align}
\partial_y\partial_{y'}g_D^{[L]}&=\delta(y-y')-\kappa^2g_N^{[L]}.
\end{align}$$

Keeping this term makes the sourced constraint correct. Its identical local contribution to $G^A$ and $G^R$ cancels from their difference.

Set $h(y)=\sinh(\kappa y)/\sinh(\kappa L)$ and $c(y)=\cosh(\kappa y)/\sinh(\kappa L)$. Solving for a prescribed tangential history gives

$$\begin{align}
\widehat H_i(y)&=\begin{pmatrix}
h(y)&0\\
-i\zeta c(y)/\kappa&-ikc(y)/\kappa\\
0&h(y)
\end{pmatrix}.
\end{align}$$

The normal row follows directly from $i\zeta A_t+\partial_yA_y+ikA_z=0$. Its derivative vanishes at the physical wall. Taking $F^{yt}$ and $F^{yz}$ at the cut gives

$$\begin{align}
\widehat D_i&=\frac{\coth(\kappa L)}{\kappa}
\begin{pmatrix}-(m^2+k^2)&-\zeta k\\-\zeta k&m^2-\zeta^2\end{pmatrix},\\
\widehat\rho_i&=-\widehat H_i^{\vee},&
\widehat H_i^{\vee}(y)&=\widehat H_i(-\zeta,-k;y)^{\mathsf T}.
\end{align}$$

The symbol $\vee$ here only reverses the tangential Fourier variables and exchanges component slots in the displayed analytic expression; it does not exchange $R$ and $A$. The normal row changes sign under this reversal.

The inverse response is particularly simple:

$$\begin{align}
T(\zeta,k)&=\frac1{m^2}\begin{pmatrix}\zeta^2-m^2&-\zeta k\\-\zeta k&m^2+k^2\end{pmatrix},\\
\widehat K&=T\frac{\sinh^2(\kappa L)}{\kappa\sinh(2\kappa L)}.
\end{align}$$

Multiplication gives $(\widehat D_1+\widehat D_2)\widehat K=1$. Thus all released kernels are explicit:

$$\begin{align}
\widehat{\mathcal G}_{ij}&=\delta_{ij}\widehat G_i+\widehat H_i\widehat K\widehat H_j^{\vee},&
\widehat{\mathcal G}_{iq}&=\widehat H_i\widehat K,\\
\widehat{\mathcal G}_{qj}&=\widehat K\widehat H_j^{\vee},&
\widehat{\mathcal G}_{qq}&=\widehat K.
\end{align}$$

### Comparison with the Global Algebra

Independently impose $A_t=A_z=0$ at $x=\pm L$ on the uncut cylinder. Put $X=x+L\in[0,2L]$. Solving the global source equation gives exactly the displayed formula for $\widehat G_i$, with $L$ replaced by $2L$ and $(y,y')$ by $(X,X')$. Call this result $\widehat G_{\mathrm{global}}$.

Let $O_1=1$ and $O_2=\operatorname{diag}(1,-1,1)$ convert global components to regional components. Direct substitution and the hyperbolic addition formulas give

$$\begin{align}
\widehat{\mathcal G}_{ij}(y,y')&=O_i\widehat G_{\mathrm{global}}(x_i(y),x_j(y'))O_j^{\mathsf T}.
\end{align}$$

For example, its tangential block reduces to the scalar identity

$$\begin{align}
g_D^{[L]}(y,y')+h(y)\frac{\sinh^2(\kappa L)}{\kappa\sinh(2\kappa L)}h(y')&=g_D^{[2L]}(y,y'),
\end{align}$$

multiplied by $T$. Differentiating this identity and using the displayed $g_N$ relation supplies the normal and mixed components. The $iq,qi,qq$ blocks are the corresponding tangential values of the global response at $x=0$.

The equality holds for each circle momentum and each causal prescription. Taking $A-R$ and summing $e^{ik(z-z')}/(2\pi R)$ proves equality of all the glued and global Poisson kernels. Therefore assembly identifies the algebras generated by these smeared fields and histories, with their products and brackets. The same source formula computes any further Hamiltonian observable whose variation has the stated form.

Explicitly, if $\mathrm{Ass}$ joins the regional fields, the comparison is

$$\begin{align}
\mathrm{Ass}^*:\mathcal H_{\mathrm{global}}&\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathcal H_{\mathrm{glue}},&
\mathrm{Ass}^*\{F,G\}_{\mathrm{global}}&=\{\mathrm{Ass}^*F,\mathrm{Ass}^*G\}_{\mathrm{glue}}.
\end{align}$$

Here the global generators allow the same regional smearings, interpreted on the uncut field, and tangential cut smearings. A global bulk integral splits into the two regional integrals. The response identities show that any expression vanishing on all matched fields has zero bracket there, so the released bracket does not depend on the representative.

### A Nonzero Crossing Bracket

Take a real normalized circle mode

$$\begin{align}
\chi_n(z)&=\frac{\cos(nz/R)}{\sqrt{\pi R}},&
B_n(t,x)&=\int_0^{2\pi R}\chi_n(z)A_z(t,x,z)\,dz,&n&\geq1.
\end{align}$$

Its bracket is the interval scalar bracket of mass $M_n=\sqrt{m^2+n^2/R^2}$, multiplied by $1+n^2/(m^2R^2)$. Before a physical-wall reflection arrives, for $x<0<x'$ and $\tau=t-t'>0$,

$$\begin{align}
|x-x'|<\tau&<\min(2L+x+x',2L-x-x'),\\
\{B_n(t,x),B_n(t',x')\}_{\mathrm{glue}}
&=-\frac12\left(1+\frac{n^2}{m^2R^2}\right)J_0\!\left(M_n\sqrt{\tau^2-(x-x')^2}\right).
\end{align}$$

For $R=L$, $m=1/L$, $n=1$, $x=-L/4$, $x'=L/4$ and $\tau=3L/4$, this is

$$\begin{align}
\{B_1(t,-L/4),B_1(t',L/4)\}_{\mathrm{glue}}
&=-J_0(\sqrt{10}/4)\simeq-0.849749,\\
\{B_1(t,-L/4),B_1(t',L/4)\}_{\mathrm{ind}}&=0.
\end{align}$$

The first value equals the independently computed global bracket. Small smooth smearings around these events give its averaged, still nonzero value. Here the circle momentum is nonzero: the example tests a genuinely tangential variation, not just the spatially constant circle mode.

The mechanism is the same as in the scalar example. A bulk source changes the cut flux, the released tangential history responds, and this response reaches the other region. The constraint fixes the accompanying normal component throughout the calculation.
