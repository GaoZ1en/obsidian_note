The goal is to construct free scalar quantum sewing from independent regional Wick algebras. We first define the regional algebras and their response to prescribed histories, then determine a quantum multiplication compatible with transmission, and only then impose the interface relations. The resulting polynomial algebra is compared with an independently defined global algebra.

## Independent Regional Algebras

Take $\Sigma_i=[0,\ell_i]$, $\ell_i>0$, with inward coordinate $y_i$ and time $t\in\mathbb R$. The regional quantum field obeys

$$
\mathcal L_i\widehat\phi_i=0,\qquad
\mathcal L_i=\partial_t^2-\partial_{y_i}^2+m^2,\qquad m\geq0,
$$

with a physical Dirichlet wall at $y_i=0$. At the artificial endpoint prescribe a real history $q_i(t)$ and define the inward derivative history $\Pi_i$:

$$
\widehat\phi_i(t,\ell_i)=q_i(t)\mathbf1,\qquad
\widehat\Pi_i(t)=\partial_{y_i}\widehat\phi_i(t,\ell_i).
$$

Admissible histories are those supplied by smooth regional solutions with the physical wall condition. No relation with another region has been imposed. For fixed $q_i$, impose the canonical equal-time relations in the regional interior,

$$
[\widehat\phi_i(t,y),\partial_t\widehat\phi_i(t,y')]
=i\hbar\delta(y-y')\mathbf1,\qquad
[\widehat\phi_i(t,y),\widehat\phi_i(t,y')]=0,
$$

and the corresponding zero momentum-momentum commutator. Equivalently, at different times,

$$
[\widehat\phi_i(X),\widehat\phi_i(Y)]
=i\hbar E_i(X,Y)\mathbf1,\qquad E_i=G_i^A-G_i^R.
$$

The kernel is independent of the prescribed mean history because the field equation is linear. Choose regional normal-ordering coordinates using

$$
\widetilde W_i(\omega)=i\theta(\omega)
\left(\widetilde G_i^A(\omega)-\widetilde G_i^R(\omega)\right),
\qquad W_i-W_i^T=iE_i.
$$

The Fourier convention is $F(t)=\int\frac{\mathrm d\omega}{2\pi}e^{-i\omega t}\widetilde F(\omega)$; a contraction is $\hbar W_i$. This is one common rule for writing the algebras, not a prescription for sewing physical states.

Define $\mathfrak W_i[q_i]$ as the unital polynomial $*$-algebra generated by the smeared fields and the local Wick monomials

$$
\int\mathrm dt\,\mathrm dy_i\,
f(t,y_i):D^{\alpha_1}\widehat\phi_i\cdots
D^{\alpha_n}\widehat\phi_i:_{W_i},\qquad n<\infty,
$$

together with time-smeared linear field and derivative histories at interior points and the endpoint histories $q_i(h),\widehat\Pi_i(h)$. Here $D^\alpha$ is any finite string of time or spatial derivatives. Take finite sums and finite products, with no bound on the finite polynomial degree. Smearing is smooth and compact in time; spatial weights may be piecewise smooth on finitely many subintervals, may reach the artificial endpoint, and vanish near the physical wall. Endpoint histories are always time-smeared. The common positive-frequency boundary-value prescription defines the contractions. At the artificial endpoint use regional $W_i$ normal ordering; the flat local subtraction appropriate to an interior point will be made after sewing.

In normal symbols, namely polynomial expressions before making contractions, the regional product is

$$
F\star_i G
=\sum_{r\geq0}\frac{\hbar^r}{r!}
\left\langle W_i^{\otimes r},F^{(r)}\otimes G^{(r)}\right\rangle.
$$

To specify the rule, write $W$ for the chosen contraction. A normal symbol such as $F[\phi]=\int f(X)\phi(X)^2\,\mathrm dX$ represents the already normal-ordered observable $\mathcal N_W(F)=\int f(X):\widehat\phi(X)^2:_W\,\mathrm dX$, not the unrenormalized square. More generally $\mathcal N_W$ replaces each ordinary polynomial field expression by its joint $W$-normal ordering. The star product is defined to encode the actual operator product:

$$
\mathcal N_W(F\star_W G)=\mathcal N_W(F)\mathcal N_W(G).
$$

The derivative kernels have no hidden factorial:

$$
\left.\frac{\partial^r}{\partial s_1\cdots\partial s_r}
F\!\left[\phi+\sum_{\nu=1}^r s_\nu h_\nu\right]\right|_{s=0}
=\int\prod_{\nu=1}^r\mathrm dX_\nu\,
F^{(r)}[\phi](X_1,\ldots,X_r)
\prod_{\nu=1}^r h_\nu(X_\nu).
$$

Differentiate the polynomial recipe before using the field equations. Each derivative selects a field factor from that recipe. For bulk insertions the pairing in the product means explicitly

$$
\left\langle W^{\otimes r},F^{(r)}\otimes G^{(r)}\right\rangle
=\int\prod_{\nu=1}^r(\mathrm dX_\nu\,\mathrm dY_\nu)\,
F^{(r)}[\phi](X_1,\ldots,X_r)
\prod_{\nu=1}^r W(X_\nu,Y_\nu)
G^{(r)}[\phi](Y_1,\ldots,Y_r).
$$

Thus the rule contracts $r$ field factors from $F$ with $r$ from $G$, never two factors inside one of them. The factor $1/r!$ removes the repeated ordering of these contractions. For fields with derivatives, differentiate the corresponding slots of $W$. For labelled regions, also sum over those labels; a history slot uses the appropriate boundary value or derivative of $W$, without counting the same trace as an additional independent field.

For example, let $F=\int f(X)\phi(X)^2\,\mathrm dX$ and $G=\int g(Y)\phi(Y)^2\,\mathrm dY$. Then

$$
F^{(1)}(X)=2f(X)\phi(X),\qquad
F^{(2)}(X_1,X_2)=2f(X_1)\delta(X_1-X_2),
$$

and all higher derivatives vanish. Substitution gives

$$\begin{aligned}
F\star_W G
&=FG+4\hbar\int\mathrm dX\,\mathrm dY\,
f(X)g(Y)W(X,Y)\phi(X)\phi(Y)\\
&\quad+2\hbar^2\int\mathrm dX\,\mathrm dY\,
f(X)g(Y)W(X,Y)^2.
\end{aligned}$$

The remaining fields in this expression are still normal symbols; applying $\mathcal N_W$ normal orders them together. Powers such as $W(X,Y)^2$ use the common positive-frequency prescription before smearing in the two insertion variables. The formula does not insert a coincident self-contraction $W(X,X)$.

Use $W=W_i$ for the regional product. The sum terminates at $r=\min(\deg F,\deg G)$. Regional equations and fixed-history identities hold in this algebra because $W_i$ solves the homogeneous field equation and has zero field value at the prescribed endpoint in either slot.

Now retain the same finite polynomial recipes for every admissible $q_i$:

$$
\mathfrak W_i=
\left\{q_i\longmapsto F_i[q_i]\in\mathfrak W_i[q_i]\right\},
\qquad
(F_i\star_i G_i)[q_i]=F_i[q_i]\star_i G_i[q_i].
$$

The family is generated by the regional field recipes and finite polynomials in smeared histories; it is not an arbitrary independent choice of an element at every $q_i$. Histories are central in this fixed-history family. Its multiplication alone does not specify how to transmit a quantum disturbance into a second region.

## Regional Quantum Response

The required additional data come from the response of this regional quantum field. Couple a bulk source through the Hamiltonian term $-\int\mathrm dy_i\,j_i\widehat\phi_i$. With $q_i$ fixed, the linear response is

$$
G_i^R(X,Y)\mathbf1
=\frac{i}{\hbar}\theta(t-t')
[\widehat\phi_i(X),\widehat\phi_i(Y)].
$$

Thus $\mathcal L_iG_i^R=1$ with zero endpoint field response; the advanced response is defined with the opposite time condition. A change of prescribed history has response $H_i$. Taking the endpoint derivative defines $\rho_i,D_i$:

$$
\delta\widehat\phi_i=G_i\,j_i+H_i\,\delta q_i,\qquad
\delta\widehat\Pi_i=\rho_i\,j_i+D_i\,\delta q_i.
$$

These are linear operator response identities; the displayed kernels are scalar. For the free field they are exactly the same response kernels used in the classical Peierls calculation: the linear source equation is the same and the factor of $\hbar$ in the field commutator cancels in the Kubo formula. The quantum construction adds the contraction product, rather than a new correction to these free response kernels. For complex frequency $\zeta$, set $\kappa^2=m^2-\zeta^2$. Solving the regional equation gives

$$\begin{aligned}
G_i(y,y')&=
\frac{\sinh(\kappa y_<)\sinh(\kappa(\ell_i-y_>))}
{\kappa\sinh(\kappa\ell_i)},&
H_i(y)&=\frac{\sinh(\kappa y)}{\sinh(\kappa\ell_i)},\\
\rho_i&=-H_i^T,&D_i&=\kappa\coth(\kappa\ell_i).
\end{aligned}$$

The transpose exchanges source and observation slots. Retarded and advanced limits mean $\zeta=\omega+i0$ and $\zeta=\omega-i0$. All products below are formed at complex frequency before taking these limits.

The regional object to be composed is therefore

$$
\boxed{\quad
\mathbf A_i=(\mathfrak W_i;\,q_i,\Pi_i;\,G_i^{R/A},H_i^{R/A},
\rho_i^{R/A},D_i^{R/A}).
\quad}
$$

Everything in $\mathbf A_i$ has been determined within region $i$. At independent prescribed histories the product of the two objects has the block-diagonal contraction $W_1\oplus W_2$, and the two regional fields commute.

## Quantum Sewing and the Interface Relations

Define transparent sewing to mean that the piecewise quantum field propagates by the same free operator through the artificial cut, with no operator source or interaction supported there. This specifies the interface dynamics at the operator level.

Put $L=\ell_1+\ell_2$, $a=\ell_1$, and use $x=y_1$ on the left and $x=L-y_2$ on the right. Before identifying either field value, form the piecewise operator field

$$
\widehat\Phi(t,x)=
\begin{cases}
\widehat\phi_1(t,x),&x<a,\\
\widehat\phi_2(t,L-x),&x>a.
\end{cases}
$$

Denote its two mismatch histories by

$$
c=q_1-q_2,\qquad p=\Pi_1+\Pi_2.
$$

Applying the free field operator to this piecewise field gives, after the two regional bulk equations,

$$
\boxed{\quad
(\partial_t^2-\partial_x^2+m^2)\widehat\Phi
=\widehat p(t)\,\delta(x-a)+\widehat c(t)\,\delta'(x-a).
\quad}
$$

For example, smearing in $x$ with a function $f$ supported away from the physical walls leaves $f(a)\widehat p-f'(a)\widehat c$. The values of $f(a)$ and $f'(a)$ can be chosen independently. The quantum equation without a cut source therefore requires

$$
\boxed{\qquad
\widehat q_1=\widehat q_2,\qquad
\widehat\Pi_1+\widehat\Pi_2=0.
\qquad}
$$

These relations follow from the operator field equation, without varying an action. In the free theory the equation is linear, so no composite-field subtraction enters this derivation. We have identified the relations to impose; we must still construct a quantum multiplication in which imposing them is consistent.

## Multiplication Before Imposing Matching

First hold the mismatch histories $c,p$ prescribed and allow the common field history to respond. A bulk source changes neither prescribed mismatch. The regional response identities then give

$$
\delta q_1=\delta q_2=\delta q,\qquad
\rho_1j_1+\rho_2j_2+(D_1+D_2)\delta q=0.
$$

Consequently

$$\begin{aligned}
K&=(D_1+D_2)^{-1}
=\frac{\sinh(\kappa\ell_1)\sinh(\kappa\ell_2)}
{\kappa\sinh(\kappa L)},\\
\delta q&=-K(\rho_1j_1+\rho_2j_2),\\
\mathcal G_{ij}&=\delta_{ij}G_i-H_iK\rho_j
=\delta_{ij}G_i+H_iKH_j^T.
\end{aligned}$$

These formulas describe fluctuations with the mismatches fixed, including when their prescribed values are nonzero. They do not yet set the field operators $c,p$ to zero. The response to a common interface source and the resulting history response are

$$
\mathcal G_{iq}=H_iK,\qquad
\mathcal G_{qj}=KH_j^T,\qquad
\mathcal G_{qq}=K.
$$

Define the new contraction entirely from this assembled quantum response:

$$
\boxed{\quad
\widetilde W_{\mathrm{sew},ab}(\omega)
=i\theta(\omega)
\left(\widetilde{\mathcal G}_{ab}^A(\omega)
-\widetilde{\mathcal G}_{ab}^R(\omega)\right).
\quad}
$$

Taking positive-frequency parts of $H_i$ and $K$ separately and multiplying them would not give this prescription. The new contraction replaces both the cross-region contraction and the contraction between two insertions in the same region.

Promote $q_i$ from a prescribed parameter to the endpoint field symbol. Its old centrality is not retained when changing the product: the common history now has contractions $W_{qj}$ with the bulk. Only the mismatches remain prescribed. Let $\mathfrak W_{\mathrm{pre}}$ be the polynomial normal symbols in the regional fields and histories, with regional equations retained but matching not yet imposed, equipped with

$$
F\star_{\mathrm{sew}}G
=\sum_{r\geq0}\frac{\hbar^r}{r!}
\left\langle W_{\mathrm{sew}}^{\otimes r},
F^{(r)}\otimes G^{(r)}\right\rangle.
$$

This uses the explicit derivative and contraction rule above with $W=W_{\mathrm{sew}}$, including the sums over region labels. The precise mismatch class and its polynomial extension are specified below. For three factors this formula contracts every pair of distinct factors independently of the order of multiplication; hence it is associative. Complex conjugation reverses the product because $\overline{W(X,Y)}=W(Y,X)$.

Write $B\phi=(c,p)$. The transmission response implies

$$
B_XW_{\mathrm{sew}}(X,Y)=0,\qquad
B_YW_{\mathrm{sew}}(X,Y)=0.
$$

For a bulk source, the two traces of $\mathcal G$ agree and their inward derivatives sum to zero. For a history source their derivative sum is the unit contact term $(D_1+D_2)K=1$ in both the retarded and advanced responses; these contacts cancel in their difference. Reciprocity gives the identities in the other slot. Thus neither mismatch can be contracted with any field in this new product. They remain freely prescribed central quantities until the next step.

This construction changes the quantum multiplication before imposing matching. It is not a quotient of the old product with contraction $W_1\oplus W_2$.

## Imposing Matching and Determining the Kernel

Use independent regional solutions whose mismatch histories $c,p$ are smooth and vanish outside a finite time interval. This allows a disturbance to the cut during a finite experiment and supplies a definite retarded response. Each individual history $q_i$ can continue for all time. Every exactly matched free solution is included, since its mismatch is zero.

There is an explicit way to remove an arbitrary allowed mismatch. Define the retarded field response

$$
\mathcal R\binom{c}{p}
=
\binom{H_1K(D_2c+p)}{H_2K(-D_1c+p)},
$$

with the products taken before the retarded limit. Its two endpoint values differ by $c$, while its two inward derivatives sum to $p$:

$$
\begin{pmatrix}1&-1\\D_1&D_2\end{pmatrix}
\begin{pmatrix}KD_2&K\\-KD_1&K\end{pmatrix}
=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&1\end{pmatrix},
\qquad B\mathcal R=1.
$$

The compact-time mismatches are the actual inputs of this response. No inverse acting on arbitrary infinite-past histories is assumed. Subtracting the field radiated by the mismatch gives

$$
P\phi=\phi-\mathcal R B\phi,\qquad
BP=0,\qquad P^2=P.
$$

Thus $P$ fixes each matched field and sends every allowed unmatched field to a matched one.

For the polynomial presentation, also allow the finite expressions obtained by inserting $\mathcal R B\phi$ in slots of the original regional polynomial recipes, and by making their Wick contractions. These additional expressions are specified by the regional response above. They introduce no new polynomial degree limit and vanish after matching whenever a mismatch slot remains.

Define $\mathcal I_{\mathrm{match}}$ to consist of the finite sums of these smeared polynomial expressions containing at least one $c$ or $p$ slot. A mismatch may occur inside a local integral, not just as a separately smeared factor. Since both slots of $W_{\mathrm{sew}}$ annihilate $B$, a contraction cannot remove this mismatch. Therefore

$$
\mathfrak W_{\mathrm{pre}}\star\mathcal I_{\mathrm{match}}
\subseteq\mathcal I_{\mathrm{match}},\qquad
\mathcal I_{\mathrm{match}}\star\mathfrak W_{\mathrm{pre}}
\subseteq\mathcal I_{\mathrm{match}},\qquad
\mathcal I_{\mathrm{match}}^*=\mathcal I_{\mathrm{match}}.
$$

We can now impose the quantum interface relations:

$$
\boxed{\quad
\operatorname{Sew}(\mathbf A_1,\mathbf A_2)
=\mathfrak W_{\mathrm{sew}}
:=\mathfrak W_{\mathrm{pre}}/\mathcal I_{\mathrm{match}}.
\quad}
$$

The complete kernel of restriction to matched fields is exactly this ideal. To see this, expand any finite polynomial $F$ after writing $\phi=P\phi+\mathcal R B\phi$. Every term in

$$
F(\phi)-F(P\phi)
$$

contains a mismatch slot. If $F$ vanishes on every matched field, then $F(P\phi)=0$, so $F$ belongs to $\mathcal I_{\mathrm{match}}$. The reverse inclusion is immediate. The expansion also applies inside each local smearing integral and coefficient by coefficient in $\hbar$; it does not replace a local integral by a finite sum of products of separately smeared fields.

## Independent Global Algebra and the Isomorphism

Independently define a free scalar on $x\in[0,L]$, with the canonical equal-time algebra and physical Dirichlet walls at $0,L$. Define $\mathfrak W_L$ by the same finite Wick polynomial rule, using its own response and normal ordering. Global observables may be integrated over any finite spatial subinterval: their spatial weights may be piecewise smooth, with smooth compact time smearing and support away from the physical walls. Time-smeared linear field and derivative histories at interior points are also allowed. No artificial cut is selected in this definition.

The independently solved global response is

$$
G_L(\zeta;x,x')
=\frac{\sinh(\kappa x_<)\sinh(\kappa(L-x_>))}
{\kappa\sinh(\kappa L)}.
$$

Set $X_1(y)=y$, $X_2(y)=L-y$. Direct substitution of the regional expressions gives

$$
\mathcal G_{ij}(\zeta;y,y')
=G_L(\zeta;X_i(y),X_j(y')),\qquad
K(\zeta)=G_L(\zeta;a,a).
$$

The mixed history identities follow by setting one point at $a$. Applying the same normal-ordering prescription therefore gives the full contraction identity

$$
\boxed{\quad
W_{\mathrm{sew},ij}(t-t';y,y')
=W_L(t-t';X_i(y),X_j(y')).
\quad}
$$

Define the map on the algebra before imposing matching by substitution,

$$
\mathcal S:\mathfrak W_{\mathrm{pre}}\longrightarrow\mathfrak W_L,
\qquad
\widehat\phi_i(t,y)\longmapsto\widehat\phi(t,X_i(y)),
\qquad
q_i(t)\longmapsto\widehat\phi(t,a).
$$

Spatial derivatives have the orientations $\partial_x=\partial_{y_1}=-\partial_{y_2}$. Every contraction in $\mathcal S(F\star_{\mathrm{sew}}G)$ equals the corresponding global contraction, so $\mathcal S$ preserves multiplication and the involution.

Every global local integral splits into its two regional integrals, including integrals crossing $a$; global interior linear histories have their regional representatives as well. Hence the map is onto. A pair satisfying the two matching relations assembles into a global solution: continuity of higher spatial derivatives follows successively from the field equation and the matching of the field and first derivative. Conversely, every global solution restricts to such a pair. The kernel calculation above therefore applies to $\mathcal S$ and gives

$$
\ker\mathcal S=\mathcal I_{\mathrm{match}},\qquad
\operatorname{im}\mathcal S=\mathfrak W_L,
$$

and consequently

$$
\boxed{\quad
\overline{\mathcal S}:
\operatorname{Sew}(\mathbf A_1,\mathbf A_2)
\xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }\mathfrak W_L.
\quad}
$$

This is a statement about every finite Wick polynomial and every finite product in the declared class. The local square and stress tensor below test the actual contraction coefficients and the local subtraction.

For finitely many cuts in the same static interval model, the sewing order is immaterial. At complex frequency all common histories satisfy one linear system built from the regional endpoint responses. Successive sewing eliminates different unknowns from this same system, giving the same final response before either causal limit is taken. It therefore gives the same contraction and polynomial product. The final quotient imposes the field-value and flux relations at every removed cut, independently of how the intervals were grouped.

For a direct evaluation of the independent global kernel one may now use its modes:

$$\begin{aligned}
W_L(\tau;x,x')&=\lim_{\epsilon\downarrow0}
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{e_n(x)e_n(x')}{2\omega_n}
e^{-i\omega_n(\tau-i\epsilon)},\\
e_n(x)&=\sqrt{\frac2L}\sin\frac{n\pi x}{L},\qquad
\omega_n=\sqrt{m^2+\frac{n^2\pi^2}{L^2}}.
\end{aligned}$$

There is no zero mode, including at $m=0$. A choice of physical state remains downstream of the algebra comparison.

## The Local Square

Write $\phi_X=\widehat\phi(X)$ and

$$
V_W(X)=:\phi_X^2:_W,\qquad
B_W(X,Y)=:\phi_X\phi_Y:_W.
$$

There are four ways to make one contraction and two ways to make two contractions. Consequently

$$
\boxed{\quad
V_W(X)V_W(Y)
=:\phi_X^2\phi_Y^2:_W
+4\hbar W(X,Y)B_W(X,Y)
+2\hbar^2W(X,Y)^2\mathbf1.
\quad}
$$

Subtract the reversed product. The normal bilocal field is symmetric, while $W(X,Y)-W(Y,X)=iE(X,Y)$, so

$$\begin{aligned}
[V_W(X),V_W(Y)]
&=4i\hbar E(X,Y)B_W(X,Y)\\
&\quad+2\hbar^2\left(W(X,Y)^2-W(Y,X)^2\right)\mathbf1\\
&=4i\hbar E(X,Y)B_W(X,Y)\\
&\quad+2i\hbar^2E(X,Y)\left(W(X,Y)+W(Y,X)\right)\mathbf1.
\end{aligned}$$

The $\hbar^2$ term is where the symmetric part of the contraction becomes visible. Its separation from the bilocal term depends on the normal-ordering convention; it is not an ordering-independent anomaly. The entire product and commutator agree under sewing.

## Stress Tensor Products

Use the global spatial orientation and define

$$\begin{aligned}
\psi_a&=(\phi,\dot\phi,\partial_x\phi),&
s_a&=(m^2,1,1),& a&=0,1,2,\\
D_a&=(1,\partial_t,\partial_x),&
W_{ab}(X,Y)&=D_a^X D_b^Y W(X,Y).
\end{aligned}$$

The normal-ordered stress tensor is

$$\begin{aligned}
\mathcal T_W=T_{00,W}&=\frac12\sum_a s_a:\psi_a^2:_W,\\
T_{01,W}&=:\dot\phi\,\partial_x\phi:_W,\\
T_{11,W}&=\frac12:\dot\phi^2+(\partial_x\phi)^2-m^2\phi^2:_W.
\end{aligned}$$

Applying the same contractions gives

$$\begin{aligned}
V_W(X)\mathcal T_W(Y)
&=:V_W(X)\mathcal T_W(Y):_W\\
&\quad+2\hbar\sum_b s_bW_{0b}(X,Y):\phi_X\psi_b(Y):_W
+\hbar^2\sum_b s_bW_{0b}(X,Y)^2\mathbf1,\\[2pt]
\mathcal T_W(X)\mathcal T_W(Y)
&=:\mathcal T_W(X)\mathcal T_W(Y):_W\\
&\quad+\hbar\sum_{a,b}s_as_bW_{ab}(X,Y):\psi_a(X)\psi_b(Y):_W\\
&\quad+\frac{\hbar^2}{2}\sum_{a,b}s_as_bW_{ab}(X,Y)^2\mathbf1.
\end{aligned}$$

The first term in each expression means normal ordering all four underlying fields together. These are exact formulas, including every contraction. In particular, with $\widetilde W_{ab}(X,Y)=D_a^X D_b^Y W(Y,X)$ and $E_{ab}=D_a^X D_b^Y E(X,Y)$,

$$\begin{aligned}
[\mathcal T_W(X),\mathcal T_W(Y)]
&=i\hbar\sum_{a,b}s_as_bE_{ab}(X,Y):\psi_a(X)\psi_b(Y):_W\\
&\quad+\frac{\hbar^2}{2}\sum_{a,b}s_as_b
\left(W_{ab}(X,Y)^2-\widetilde W_{ab}(X,Y)^2\right)\mathbf1.
\end{aligned}$$

Other stress components follow by contracting their displayed quadratic expressions. Since the released and global $W$ agree, their derivatives and all these products agree as well. The field equation gives

$$
\partial_tT_{00,W}-\partial_xT_{01,W}=0,\qquad
\partial_tT_{01,W}-\partial_xT_{11,W}=0.
$$

At the cut, $\dot\phi_1=\dot\phi_2$ and $\partial_{y_1}\phi_1=-\partial_{y_2}\phi_2$. The energy flux therefore continues through the cut with the correct orientation.

## A Common Local Subtraction

Normal ordering with $W$ is convenient for multiplication, but a local composite should also be expressible using a common local subtraction $C$. In point-split notation its square is

$$
V_C(X)=\lim_{Y\to X}
\left(\widehat\phi(X)\widehat\phi(Y)-\hbar C(X,Y)\mathbf1\right),
$$

with the local insertion understood after smearing and with the same short-distance prescription in the two terms. This subtraction is Wick renormalization of the composite field. Choosing $C$ also fixes its finite normalization. A $W$-normal symbol has already made such a subtraction; the star formula multiplies the resulting observables and does not define bare coincident powers.

After defining $V_C$, its ordinary product at two insertion points is still interpreted with those points separately smeared. Setting $Y=X$ in $V_C(X)V_C(Y)$ is an additional coincidence limit and is not supplied by the star formula. The local field $:\widehat\phi^4:_C$ is instead defined by its own Wick subtraction. Ordinary finite Wick products considered here require no further choice of local counterterms for their cross-contractions; time-ordered coincident products are a separate construction.

For $m>0$ choose the flat-space contraction

$$
C_m(X,Y)=\lim_{\epsilon\downarrow0}\int_{\mathbb R}
\frac{\mathrm dk}{4\pi\sqrt{k^2+m^2}}
e^{-i\sqrt{k^2+m^2}(t-t'-i\epsilon)+ik(x-x')}.
$$

For $m=0$ use, with one fixed inverse-length scale $\mu>0$,

$$
C_\mu(X,Y)=-\frac1{4\pi}
\log\!\left[\mu^2\left((x-x')^2-(t-t'-i\epsilon)^2\right)\right].
$$

The massless expression is a local subtraction; an infinite-line massless vacuum is not being assumed. Use the same $C$ on both sides and in the uncut problem. At an interior point,

$$\begin{aligned}
V_C(X)&=V_W(X)+\hbar v_W(X)\mathbf1,&
v_W(X)&=\lim_{Y\to X}(W(X,Y)-C(X,Y)),\\
T_{00,C}(X)&=T_{00,W}(X)+\hbar t_{00,W}(X)\mathbf1,&
t_{00,W}(X)&=\frac12\lim_{Y\to X}
\left(\partial_t\partial_{t'}+\partial_x\partial_{x'}+m^2\right)(W-C).
\end{aligned}$$

The other components use their corresponding point-split quadratic expressions. These flat-space subtractions fix a conserved stress tensor in the interior. Equality of the full sewn and global contraction gives equality of these finite shifts. Thus the sewing map also identifies $V_C$, $T_{\mu\nu,C}$, and all their products.

For two normal-ordering choices applied to the same released field,

$$
V_{W_{\mathrm{sew}}}
=V_{W_i}-\hbar\lim_{Y\to X}(W_{\mathrm{sew},ii}-W_i)\mathbf1
$$

holds wherever this local difference has a finite limit. This formula changes the subtraction used for one field. It does not identify the old reflecting dynamics with the released dynamics. Near the artificial wall the old subtraction becomes singular, so the full contraction must be assembled before taking the coincidence limit there.

## An Explicit Crossing Calculation

Set $m=0$ and introduce $u=t-x$, $v=t+x$. The global mode sum can be evaluated without a mode cutoff:

$$\begin{aligned}
c_L(s)&=-\frac1{4\pi}\log\left(1-e^{-i\pi(s-i\epsilon)/L}\right),\\
W_L(X,Y)&=c_L(u-u')+c_L(v-v')-c_L(u-v')-c_L(v-u').
\end{aligned}$$

Keep the same $\epsilon$ and logarithm branch in all four terms. The first two terms propagate directly; the last two enforce reflection at the physical walls. There is no reflection at the former cut.

Cut at $x=L/2$ and choose

$$
x=\frac{3L}{8},\qquad x'=\frac{5L}{8},\qquad
t-t'=\frac{3L}{8},\qquad
R=\frac{\sin(5\pi/16)}{\sin(\pi/16)}.
$$

The points lie on opposite sides, inside the direct light cone and before a reflected signal arrives. Substitution gives

$$
W_L(X,Y)=\frac{\log R}{4\pi}-\frac i4,
\qquad E_L(X,Y)=-\frac12.
$$

The square commutator is therefore

$$
\boxed{\quad
[V_W(X),V_W(Y)]
=-2i\hbar B_W(X,Y)
-\frac{i\hbar^2}{2\pi}\log R\,\mathbf1.
\quad}
$$

The scalar coefficient is approximately $-0.230732031653\,i\hbar^2$. Both terms are recovered from the regional responses. In the tensor product of the two fixed-history algebras the cross commutator would have been zero.

The same pair also gives a nontrivial stress-tensor product. Its double-contraction contribution is

$$
\boxed{\quad
\left.\mathcal T_W(X)\mathcal T_W(Y)\right|_{\text{double contraction}}
=\frac{\hbar^2\pi^2}{128L^4}
\left[3\csc^4\frac{5\pi}{16}+\csc^4\frac{\pi}{16}\right]\mathbf1.
\quad}
$$

Its coefficient is approximately $53.7128021343\,\hbar^2/L^4$. At this non-null separated pair the massless stress commutator itself vanishes: its propagation is on the direct and reflected light fronts. The central part of that commutator is tested next. Smearing in small neighborhoods gives the corresponding integrated kernels, with the same sewn/global equality.

## The Stress Central Term

For the massless field, write $j(u)=\partial_u\phi$ and $T_{uu,W}(u)=:j(u)^2:_W$. The chiral field is periodic with period $2L$ after reflection. Its contraction is

$$\begin{aligned}
J_L(s)&=\partial_u\partial_{u'}W_L(X,Y)
=-\frac{\pi}{16L^2}\csc^2\frac{\pi(s-i0)}{2L},\qquad s=u-u',\\
J_L(s)&=-\frac1{4\pi(s-i0)^2}-\frac{\pi}{48L^2}+O(s^2).
\end{aligned}$$

The two-contraction term in the stress product has expansion

$$
2\hbar^2J_L(s)^2
=\frac{\hbar^2}{8\pi^2(s-i0)^4}
+\frac{\hbar^2}{48L^2(s-i0)^2}+O(1).
$$

Subtract the reversed product, using

$$\begin{aligned}
(s-i0)^{-2}-(s+i0)^{-2}&=-2i\pi\delta'(s),\\
(s-i0)^{-4}-(s+i0)^{-4}&=-\frac{i\pi}{3}\delta'''(s).
\end{aligned}$$

Let $\delta_{2L}(s)=\sum_{r\in\mathbb Z}\delta(s-2rL)$, with primes differentiating $s$. The single-contraction term uses $J_L(s)-J_L(-s)=\frac i2\delta'_{2L}(s)$. Combining both terms gives

$$\begin{aligned}
[T_{uu,W}(u),T_{uu,W}(u')]
&=i\hbar\left(T_{uu,W}(u)+T_{uu,W}(u')\right)\delta'_{2L}(s)\\
&\quad-\frac{i\hbar^2}{24\pi}
\left(\delta'''_{2L}(s)+\frac{\pi^2}{L^2}\delta'_{2L}(s)\right)\mathbf1.
\end{aligned}$$

There is also a direct mode check of the central coefficient. With $a=\pi/L$,

$$
J_L(s)=\frac{a^2}{4\pi}\sum_{n\geq1}n e^{-ina(s-i\epsilon)},\qquad
\sum_{n=1}^{N-1}n(N-n)=\frac{N(N^2-1)}6.
$$

Squaring the first series produces exactly the cubic-minus-linear coefficient in the displayed central term.

For the common local subtraction,

$$
T_{uu,C}=T_{uu,W}-\frac{\hbar\pi}{48L^2}\mathbf1.
$$

Substituting this shift cancels the scalar $\delta'$ term and gives

$$
\boxed{\quad
[T_{uu,C}(u),T_{uu,C}(u')]
=i\hbar\left(T_{uu,C}(u)+T_{uu,C}(u')\right)\delta'_{2L}(s)
-\frac{i\hbar^2}{24\pi}\delta'''_{2L}(s)\mathbf1.
\quad}
$$

This is the $c=1$ stress central term in these conventions. The released contraction reproduces it, including its normalization. No central contribution supported at the artificial cut is introduced.

## Finite Local Terms at the Cut

The same massless contraction and local subtraction give

$$\begin{aligned}
v_L(x)&=\frac1{2\pi}\log\left[\frac{2\mu L}{\pi}\sin\frac{\pi x}{L}\right],\\
T_{00,C}&=T_{00,W}-\frac{\hbar\pi}{24L^2}\mathbf1,\\
T_{11,C}&=T_{11,W}-\frac{\hbar\pi}{24L^2}\mathbf1,\qquad
T_{01,C}=T_{01,W}.
\end{aligned}$$

For the energy density, $\frac12(\partial_t\partial_{t'}+\partial_x\partial_{x'})$ cancels the two reflected logarithms. The two direct chiral terms each leave $-\pi/(48L^2)$ after subtraction, giving the displayed constant shift. The square shift is finite at the cut $x=\ell_1$, and the stress shift is constant through it. As a downstream diagnostic, the Gaussian reference state associated with $W_L$ assigns zero to the $W_L$-normal fields. It therefore gives the usual interval Casimir energy,

$$
\langle T_{00,C}\rangle=-\frac{\hbar\pi}{24L^2},\qquad
\langle H_C\rangle=-\frac{\hbar\pi}{24L}.
$$

For an isolated region the corresponding shifts contain $\ell_i$ instead of $L$. In particular,

$$
v_L(X_i(y))-v_{\ell_i}(y)
=\frac1{2\pi}\log
\frac{L\sin(\pi X_i(y)/L)}{\ell_i\sin(\pi y/\ell_i)}.
$$

This difference diverges as $y\to\ell_i$: the isolated region still sees a reflecting wall there. In the released algebra that wall has disappeared, and the finite value is $v_L(\ell_1)$. The change of contraction removes the artificial-wall contribution before local subtraction. Summing the isolated regional vacuum energies would describe a different boundary problem.

The construction above covers ordinary finite Wick products for the free field, with a common local subtraction and the specified physical walls. The response and contraction comparison holds for $m\geq0$; the logarithms, central-term calculation and explicit finite shifts were evaluated at $m=0$. Physical-wall-supported composites, time-ordered coincident products and interactions require additional prescriptions and are not included.

The calculation record and reproducible checks are in [Scalar Wick sewing verification](<../../../verification/numerics/scalar Wick sewing verification.md>). For the general role of a common local prescription and its finite normalization freedom, see [Hollands and Wald, Local Wick Polynomials and Time Ordered Products](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/0103074). Quantum scalar theories with actual point defects form a different interface choice; examples are constructed by [Mintchev and Ragoucy](https://arxiv.org/abs/0705.1322). The transparent-interface derivation and interval calculations here are explicit.
