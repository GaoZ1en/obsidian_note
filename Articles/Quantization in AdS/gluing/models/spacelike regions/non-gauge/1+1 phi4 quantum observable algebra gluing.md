In particular it includes the finite image terms, the local correction to spatial stress, the interface current and the identification of total stress energy with the time generator. ## The Model and the Regional Objects

Take two intervals $y_i\in[0,\ell_i]$, with time $t\in\mathbb R$, a physical Dirichlet wall at $y_i=0$ and an artificial endpoint at $y_i=\ell_i$. Write $\ell=\ell_1+\ell_2$ and $a=\ell_1$. The equal-half example is $\ell_1=\ell_2=L$, translated to $[-L,0]\cup[0,L]$. Use $m>0$ and a real, time-independent function $h$. It is one near the cut and zero near the physical walls. Its two regional expressions agree with all their derivatives in the common spatial coordinate.

The reference physical potential is

$$
U_*(\phi,x)=\frac{m^2}{2}[\phi^2]_*+
\frac{\lambda h(x)}{4!}[\phi^4]_*+\epsilon_*\mathbf1.
$$

The star on square brackets labels a fixed local subtraction and finite normalization convention; it is not multiplication. The interaction is present at every time. A finite $t_0$ used to compare evolutions is not a switching time. All results are statements about formal coefficients in $\lambda,\hbar$; each coefficient has finite field degree, which may grow with perturbative order.

At a prescribed history $q_i$, start with the regional free Wick algebra and response in [the free quantum example](<1+1 scalar Wick observable algebra gluing.md>):

$$
\phi_i^0(t,\ell_i)=q_i(t)\mathbf1,
\qquad \Pi_i^0=\partial_{y_i}\phi_i^0|_{\ell_i},
\qquad E_i=G_i^A-G_i^R,
\qquad W_i-W_i^T=iE_i.
$$

Retain the response of the field and its endpoint derivative to both bulk sources and changes of $q_i$:

$$
\delta\phi_i^0=G_i j_i+H_i\delta q_i,
\qquad
\delta\Pi_i^0=\rho_i j_i+D_i\delta q_i.
$$

The interaction-picture regional potential is

$$
V_{i,*}(s)=\frac1{4!}\int_0^{\ell_i}\!dy_i\,
h_i(y_i)[\phi_i^0(s,y_i)^4]_*.
$$

Here the expression $[\phi_i^4]_*$ is a local subtraction recipe. It is not declared equal to regional vacuum normal ordering. Near an isolated reflecting endpoint the change between these expressions has image terms. These terms must be retained when writing the regional potential, and recomputed when the reflecting endpoint is released.

Choose a regional prescription $R_i$ for the local composite fields and time-ordered products. Before evaluating a time-ordered graph, retain the field labels without imposing the bulk field equation. Also retain its local contact terms. The regional observable algebra $\mathfrak A_{i,\lambda}^{R_i}[q_i]$ is generated by the resulting interacting polynomial insertions and their finite ordered products, together with ordinary products of the time-smeared linear histories and the energy generator. Complete in formal $\lambda,\hbar$ coefficients. Include the polynomial kernels produced by contractions: a term such as $\int f(X)g(Y)W_F(X,Y)\phi(X)^3\phi(Y)^3$ is a polynomial observable even when its kernel does not split into finitely many products.

The same recipes are used as $q_i$ varies. Admissible initial data and histories must satisfy the regional interacting equation at their common time corner, order by order. Choosing a finite $t_0$ does not authorize arbitrary incompatible history jets there.

The enhanced regional object retains

$$
\mathbf A_{i,\lambda}^{R_i}
=\bigl(\mathfrak A_{i,\lambda}^{R_i}[q_i]\text{ as a family};
q_i,\Pi_i;G_i,H_i,\rho_i,D_i;V_i;R_i;\text{insertion recipes}\bigr).
$$

All these data belong to region $i$. The recipe is essential: sewing will change the contractions, including contractions within a single region. An abstract algebra from which the history response and insertion recipes have been discarded is not this object.

## The Two-Loop Endpoint

The chosen scope keeps total coupling degree $n\le2$ and loop number $\mathcal L\le2$, including the tree contributions and all counterterm insertions at those orders. Coupling factors already present in a stress insertion count toward $n$. The physical coupling remains $\lambda h(x)$ at every time.

Coupling degree and loop number are distinct. For a connected graph with $I$ internal lines and $V$ vertices, including composite insertion vertices when present,

$$
\mathcal L=I-V+1.
$$

For ordinary connected scalar graphs built from quartic interaction vertices, some examples are

| Contribution | Coupling degree | Loops | In the chosen scope? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Four-point tree vertex | $\lambda$ | 0 | Yes |
| Four-point bubble | $\lambda^2$ | 1 | Yes |
| Two-point sunset | $\lambda^2$ | 2 | Yes |
| Four-point two-loop graph with three quartic vertices | $\lambda^3$ | 2 | No |
| Connected vacuum graph with two quartic vertices and four joining lines | $\lambda^2$ | 3 | No |

Thus this endpoint is a two-loop check through $\lambda^2$, not a two-loop result at every coupling order. It includes the lower-order mass, vacuum and composite-field counterterms that contribute to the retained graphs. A counterterm carries its inherited loop order even when drawn as a single local vertex. Removing a local tadpole by the reference subtraction does not authorize dropping finite image terms.

The power of $\hbar$ also depends on the external insertion normalization. A connected graph with $v$ interaction vertices and $s$ ordinary composite insertions has the raw factor $\hbar^{I-v}=\hbar^{\mathcal L+s-1}$ before counterterm weights. For one interacting insertion this is $\hbar^{\mathcal L}$. For products and commutators, count the actual connected graphs and retain their free Wick factors; do not discard every raw $\hbar^3$ term by calling it three-loop. For example, the free fully contracted product of two quadratic stress insertions has one loop and a factor $\hbar^2$.

The stopping condition is agreement, at the retained orders, of the regional and independent global constructions for:

1. the interacting field and polynomial multiplication, including crossing commutators;
2. the specified stress insertions and interface current, with their finite local terms and independent-convention conversion;
3. the regional energy exchange and identification of the sum of the regional stress integrals with the time generator.

The calculation below passes these tests at the chosen perturbative accuracy. An all-order stress theorem, higher loops, independent multiple-stress sources and operator-limit questions are not further acceptance requirements. Loop selection is a rule for comparing connected coefficients, not a new quotient of the ordinary algebra obtained by erasing every high power of $\hbar$.

## What Independent Renormalization Means

Independent conventions are allowed for the same local bulk theory, subject to the same reality, locality, time-translation and classical normalization requirements. They may change finite mass and vacuum-energy terms, the definition of composite fields and the associated contact terms. Adding an independent physical boundary potential is different data and is not included in this comparison.

Write the interaction-action label as $\mathcal V=-\lambda\int dt\,V_*(t)$. A compact auxiliary time test may be used in the following source identities. It does not define the physical time dependence of the coupling. Let $S_*$ be the reference time-ordered formal series. After putting Wick coordinates in a common convention, an allowed regional rule has the form

$$
S_i(\mathcal V)=S_*\bigl(Z_i(\mathcal V)\bigr),
\qquad Z_i=\mathrm{id}+O(\hbar).
$$

This equation is between prescriptions on the same regional propagation problem. It does not identify an isolated regional propagator with the transparent one. The local finite map $Z_i$ is part of the input; its inverse exists order by order. The [finite-renormalization theorem](https://arxiv.org/html/0901.2038#S4.SS1) supplies this description for prescriptions satisfying its hypotheses. Its application to a full reflecting-wall history theory requires the corresponding boundary extension, not just the interior theorem.

To represent the chosen physical interaction $\mathcal V_*$ in convention $i$, set

$$
\mathcal V_i=Z_i^{-1}(\mathcal V_*).
$$

For one observable insertion $F_*$, use

$$
F_i=\bigl(DZ_i(\mathcal V_i)\bigr)^{-1}F_*.
$$

Indeed, differentiating the relative source series gives

$$
\widehat{\mathcal R}_{\mathcal V}(F)
=\mathcal R_{Z(\mathcal V)}\bigl(DZ(\mathcal V)F\bigr).
$$

Thus an interaction and its observables must be transported together. The numerical coefficients written beside $\phi^2$ and $\phi^4$ need not stay the same in two conventions for the same theory.

For several insertions keep the whole source identity:

$$
S_i(\mathcal V_i)^{-1}\star S_i(\mathcal V_i+J_i)
=S_*(\mathcal V_*)^{-1}\star
S_*\bigl(\mathcal V_*+Z_i(\mathcal V_i+J_i)-Z_i(\mathcal V_i)\bigr).
$$

The transformed source is

$$
J_*=DZ_i(\mathcal V_i)J_i
+\frac12D^2Z_i(\mathcal V_i)[J_i,J_i]+\cdots.
$$

If $J_i=sF_i+tG_i$, its $st$ term is $D^2Z_i(\mathcal V_i)[F_i,G_i]$. This is a local contact insertion. Higher source derivatives give the corresponding higher contact insertions. Ordinary $\star$-products are transported by an algebra homomorphism; these additional terms occur when comparing the time-ordered, multiple-source products. For the stress tensor, retain the energy and momentum-flow labels when applying $Z_i$. The single derivative transports an ordinary stress observable. If multiple-source time-ordered responses are also compared, their higher derivatives are required as well.

## An Explicit Change of Convention

Suppose the only change is a constant finite Wick subtraction, with convention

$$
[\phi^2]_B=[\phi^2]_A+\hbar c\mathbf1.
$$

Wick's rule gives

$$
[\phi^4]_B=[\phi^4]_A+6\hbar c[\phi^2]_A
+3\hbar^2c^2\mathbf1.
$$

Write the same physical potential in both conventions:

$$
\frac{m_A^2}{2}[\phi^2]_A+\frac{\lambda h}{24}[\phi^4]_A+\epsilon_A
=\frac{m_B^2}{2}[\phi^2]_B+\frac{\lambda h}{24}[\phi^4]_B+\epsilon_B.
$$

Comparing coefficients gives

$$
\boxed{\quad
m_B^2=m_A^2-\frac{\lambda h\hbar c}{2},\qquad
\epsilon_B=\epsilon_A-\frac{m_A^2\hbar c}{2}
+\frac{\lambda h\hbar^2c^2}{8}.
\quad}
$$

With a spatially varying $h$, $m_B^2$ denotes the local quadratic coefficient in this expression, not a new physical mass profile. The transformed potential is exactly equal to the original one. This check includes the constant term: matching only the force would not fix the energy convention. General finite renormalization also allows composite-field mixing and contact terms, so this example is not its full parameterization.

## Releasing the Interface Before Imposing Matching

Use the free regional responses to construct the reference contraction for the new multiplication. At complex frequency $\zeta$, with $\kappa^2=m^2-\zeta^2$,

$$
D_i=\kappa\coth(\kappa\ell_i),\qquad
K=(D_1+D_2)^{-1},\qquad
\mathcal G_{ij}=\delta_{ij}G_i-H_iK\rho_j.
$$

The common-history entries are $\mathcal G_{iq}=H_iK$, $\mathcal G_{qi}=KH_i^T$ and $\mathcal G_{qq}=K$. Take the retarded and advanced limits of the complete expressions, and set

$$
\widetilde W_s(\omega)=i\theta(\omega)
\bigl(\widetilde{\mathcal G}^A-\widetilde{\mathcal G}^R\bigr).
$$

Define $c=q_1-q_2$, $p=\Pi_1+\Pi_2$ and $B\phi=(c,p)$. Before setting either mismatch to zero, the new free multiplication obeys

$$
B_XW_s=B_YW_s=0.
$$

Thus $c,p$ are central in this multiplication, while the released common history is not. The relevant free polynomial algebra before imposing matching is $\mathfrak W_{\mathrm{pre}}$, with the explicit contraction rule and mismatch section from the free example. In particular,

$$
\mathcal R\binom cp=
\binom{H_1K(D_2c+p)}{H_2K(-D_1c+p)},
\qquad B\mathcal R=1,\qquad P=1-\mathcal RB.
$$

Compact-time mismatch histories are allowed here; the physical coupling remains constant. The restriction on a temporary mismatch is not a restriction on the time support of the interaction.

Re-expand every regional interaction and insertion using $W_s$ and the reference local subtraction. Retain vertices from both regions and all mixed contractions. For derivative/contact graphs, assign the local extension on a smooth neighborhood crossing the cut before writing it in regional pieces. Products of a contact delta with an unspecified step at the cut are not a definition. The needed common-neighborhood labels and their division into regional pieces belong to the sewing data.

This prescription uses regional responses, insertion labels and the interface identification. It does not import the uncut interacting algebra. The independently defined uncut algebra will be used only for comparison.

There is a concrete contact term to keep at this stage. Let $B_p$ take the summed endpoint derivative and let the other slot be a common-history source. The regional response gives

$$
B_p\mathcal G^R_{\cdot q}=B_p\mathcal G^A_{\cdot q}
=(D_1+D_2)K=1,
\qquad B_pW_{s,\cdot q}=0.
$$

The unit on the right is $\delta(t-t')$ in time. Consequently the off-shell Feynman prescription $W_F=W_s-i\mathcal G^A$ gives

$$
B_pW_{F,\cdot q}=-i\delta(t-t').
$$

For time tests $f,g$, its contraction contributes $-i\hbar\int f(t)g(t)dt$. The ordinary-product relation $p=0$ cannot simply be inserted before this off-shell time-ordered calculation. In particular, the zero contraction with $W_s$ does not imply zero contraction with $W_F$. The subsequent descent argument concerns the evaluated polynomial coefficients; it does not declare every off-shell time-ordered insertion of a matching relation to vanish.

## Constant Coupling and Finite-Time Evolution

Let $V_s(t)=V_{1,*}(t)+V_{2,*}(t)$ after this change of multiplication and local subtraction. The finite-time interaction-picture series is specified recursively by

$$
U_s^{(0)}(t,t_0)=1,\qquad
U_s^{(n)}(t,t_0)=-\frac{i}{\hbar}
\int_{t_0}^{t}ds\,V_s(s)\star_s U_s^{(n-1)}(s,t_0),
\qquad
U_s=\sum_{n\ge0}\lambda^n U_s^{(n)}.
$$

All coincident vertex integrals use the canonical prescription in the next section. The displayed endpoints label a finite evolution interval. Time-labelled derivative observables are used after smooth time smearing, with the endpoint commutators of that prescription retained. No claim about an arbitrary product of sharp-time stress insertions is made.

For a free insertion $A_0(t)$, define

$$
A_\lambda(t)=U_s(t,t_0)^{-1}\star_s A_0(t)\star_s U_s(t,t_0).
$$

For $t\ge t_0$, its first two corrections are

$$\begin{aligned}
A_\lambda(t)&=A_0(t)+\frac{i\lambda}{\hbar}\int_{t_0}^{t}ds\,
[V_s(s),A_0(t)]_{\star_s}\\
&\quad+\left(\frac{i\lambda}{\hbar}\right)^2
\int_{t_0}^{t}ds\int_{t_0}^{s}du\,
[V_s(u),[V_s(s),A_0(t)]_{\star_s}]_{\star_s}
+O(\lambda^3).
\end{aligned}$$

The earlier vertex is the outer commutator. At order $n$ every surviving nested commutator has at least $n$ contractions, cancelling the apparent $\hbar^{-n}$. A quartic vertex increases the field degree by at most two in a commutator. Thus an insertion of degree $d$ gives finite polynomial coefficients of degree at most $d+2n$ in this reference interaction. The intermediate series $U_s$ can have negative powers of $\hbar$; it is an auxiliary expression, not an additional observable required in the nonnegative formal algebra.

Use the algebra of polynomial coefficients generated by these operations, their finite products and the permitted derivative insertions. Also retain the expressions obtained by replacing field slots with $P\phi$ or $\mathcal RB\phi$. This specifies the closure required in the next theorem. It is larger than the finite span of products of local integrals, but every coefficient is still a finite-degree polynomial. No inverse quantum Møller map on an unspecified class is assumed.

## The Canonical Single-Insertion Prescription

We now specify the reference prescription used in the energy calculation. At a resolved separation let $c_\epsilon$ be the local massive free contraction with its factor of $\hbar$ removed. Define the Wick polynomials by

$$
Q_{n,\epsilon}(\phi)=
\sum_{r=0}^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}
\frac{n!(-\hbar c_\epsilon)^r}
{2^r r!(n-2r)!}\phi^{n-2r}.
$$

In particular,

$$
Q_{2,\epsilon}=\phi^2-\hbar c_\epsilon,\qquad
Q_{4,\epsilon}=\phi^4-6\hbar c_\epsilon\phi^2
+3\hbar^2c_\epsilon^2.
$$

The local subtraction and its finite scale are common comparison data. It is not the full contraction of an isolated interval: the finite image terms of each region remain in its coefficients. This is the usual normal-ordered Hamiltonian definition of the two-dimensional quartic interaction; see [Rychkov--Vitale, Section 2.1](https://arxiv.org/html/1412.3460#S2.SS1). Their periodic finite-volume spectrum is not used to define our interval sewing.

Use the same resolved canonical fields for the free quadratic energy and current, subtracting the corresponding differentiated contraction. Retain the common finite energy normalization at the physical walls. On matched fields, the free energy is an actual field expression,

$$
H_0=\int_0^\ell dx\,e_0(t,x),\qquad
e_0=\frac12[\pi^2+(\partial_x\phi)^2+m^2\phi^2]_*+\epsilon_*\mathbf1,
\qquad \pi=\partial_t\phi.
$$

As throughout this note, a time-labelled density is used after smooth time smearing. Equivalently choose $\int dt\,\sigma(t)=1$ and write
$H_0=\int dt\,dx\,\sigma(t)e_0(t,x)$.
The free conservation equation and the reflecting physical walls make this expression independent of $\sigma$. The canonical commutators give

$$
[H_0,\phi]=-i\hbar\pi,\qquad
[H_0,\pi]=-i\hbar(\partial_x^2\phi-m^2\phi).
$$

There is no additional independent energy variable in this realization. Before matching, prescribed boundary histories can do work on each region; that work is calculated below.

The physical-wall subtraction can be checked locally. At distance $d$ from a Dirichlet wall the reflected massive contraction contributes

$$
e_{0,\mathrm{image}}(d)
=-\frac{\hbar m^2}{2\pi}K_0(2md)
$$

to the energy's scalar coefficient. The inverse-square terms in the separate kinetic pieces cancel; the remaining logarithm is integrable at $d=0$. The interaction vanishes near the physical walls. We keep their common finite energy constant, rather than assigning separate isolated-interval vacuum zeros and adding them after release.

For the perturbative insertions, use the canonical time ordering of these resolved fields. When a time derivative is moved through it, retain

$$
\partial_t T_{\mathrm{can}}\bigl(A(t)F(s)\bigr)
=T_{\mathrm{can}}\bigl(\dot A(t)F(s)\bigr)
+\delta(t-s)[A(t),F(s)].
$$

Use this rule recursively for one insertion $F$ and any number of quartic vertices. Wick-contract the complete expression, including these contacts, then perform the local subtractions on its complete graphs. Do not subtract each factor independently and subsequently multiply the answers. In a notation where time derivatives act on Feynman kernels, the displayed contacts must be added back. In particular the previously computed $B_pW_{F,\cdot q}$ contact is retained.

This prescription fixes the energy insertion by the same Hamiltonian which evolves the fields. It is not the instruction to take a classical coincident expression and set every contact to zero. The quartic double-contraction contact calculated later is part of converting its local stress labels to another prescription.

Only the one-insertion families are needed for the present algebra. Free stress contractions specify their zeroth order. The single-stress/single-quartic contractions and the displayed equal-time rule specify the first collision. The short-distance count below shows that, with two or more undifferentiated bulk vertices, there is no further contact supported only at the full collision once its smaller collisions have been treated. This supplies the formal recursion used here; it does not prove arbitrary multiple-stress-source identities.

An interface current is defined by the limit of this bulk current with smooth time smearing. It is not introduced as another vertex of the time-ordered interaction. Regional energies use the spatial integral of the same density, with smooth time smearing; their sharp spatial edge is taken after the complete density has been formed. These prescriptions specify the observables and their graph recipes. All continuum identities below are identities in this renormalized formal calculus; no claim of operator-norm convergence is used.

The stationary theory itself is defined by the canonical Hamiltonian

$$
H_\lambda=H_0+\lambda V_*(t_0),\qquad
\frac d{dt}A_\lambda(t)=\frac i\hbar[H_\lambda,A_\lambda(t)].
$$

Here $V_*(t_0)$ is the spatially smeared normal quartic of the canonical reference fields. It has no temporal switching factor. The finite-time interaction-picture recursion is the expansion of this equation around $H_0$. It applies in either time direction, with oriented integrals for $t<t_0$. Changing $t_0$ composes the same interacting evolution. It does not attach a free past to the model or select a vacuum state.

## The All-Order Descent Argument

This is an algebraic implication for evaluated recipes with compatible local subtractions and history limits. The explicit calculation below establishes the required compatibility for physical regional stress integrals and interface currents through the stated two-loop endpoint. The implication at higher orders remains separate from that finite-order result.

Define the algebra before matching using the actual free section already constructed from regional responses. Write

$$
\phi=\psi+b,\qquad \psi=P\phi,\qquad
b=\mathcal R\binom cp,\qquad B\psi=0,\quad Bb=\binom cp.
$$

Here $b$ is a prescribed smooth external field in each open region. Its two expressions may have the prescribed jumps. The compatibility conditions at a reference-time corner are retained. It has no quantum contractions; the quantum field $\psi$ has the released contraction $W_s$. The common history of $\psi$ is still an operator. Setting $b=0$ will impose matching, not set this common history to zero.

Expand each interaction and insertion in $\psi+b$ before evaluating its canonical graphs. Contract only quantum slots, using the previously constructed regional response and the common local subtraction. Differentiate the external field wherever the insertion requires it. This gives polynomial dependence on the external histories and their derivatives at every formal order. Counterterm insertions are expanded by the same rule. For example the normal quartic obeys the ordinary binomial formula in $b$, with Wick powers of $\psi$ as its coefficients.

The completed coefficient algebra generated by these recipes, their finite ordinary products and their external-field replacements is $\mathfrak A_{\mathrm{pre},\lambda}$. Relations between its coefficients are the free Wick relations and the evaluated polynomial identities, not freely named graphs. The single-insertion prescription specifies the stress coefficients; its energy identity is derived below. Thus the following argument concerns these formal polynomial recipes, not every possible choice of a continuum history algebra.

An external $p$ in this expansion must be distinguished from an off-shell insertion $B_p\phi$ inside a time-ordered graph. The latter still has the contact $-i\delta$ computed above. Time ordering is evaluated with those contacts before passing to ordinary observable coefficients. No identity $T(P\phi)=P\,T(\phi)$ is asserted.

Since the external slots have no contractions, their polynomial ideal is preserved by ordinary multiplication and by the commutator evolution:

$$
[V_s,c]=[V_s,p]=0,
\qquad
[V_s,cF]=c[V_s,F],\qquad
[V_s,pF]=p[V_s,F].
$$

The same argument applies to a mismatch slot inside a smearing integral and to each nested commutator. External time derivatives are retained as external slots, so differentiation also preserves the ideal. At a given time the common $U_s$ acts on both sides of the cut; its conjugation leaves the central $c,p$ unchanged. Spatial differentiation does not differentiate $U_s(t,t_0)$. Consequently the evolved fields retain these prescribed jumps. Take the formal closure of the mismatch-slot ideal, denoted $\mathcal I_\lambda$.

For any evaluated coefficient $F$, its external-field expansion gives

$$
F(\psi,b)-F(\psi,0)\in\mathcal I_\lambda.
$$

Evaluation at $b=0$ commutes with Wick multiplication because the contracted slots are precisely the $\psi$ slots. If $F$ vanishes there, its external degree-zero coefficient is zero in the free Wick algebra; every surviving term has an external slot. Hence the complete restriction kernel is $\mathcal I_\lambda$ coefficient by coefficient. This includes mismatch slots inside integrals, not merely finite products of separately smeared linear constraints. The actual identity $B\mathcal R=1$ supplies the section; no unspecified extension of matched data is needed.

Only now impose matching:

$$
\mathfrak A_{s,\lambda}=
\mathfrak A_{\mathrm{pre},\lambda}/\mathcal I_\lambda.
$$

Independently construct the scalar on $[0,\ell]$ with the same physical walls, physical potential, polynomial operations and reference prescription. Its free response is

$$
G_\ell(\zeta;x,x')=
\frac{\sinh(\kappa x_<)\sinh(\kappa(\ell-x_>))}
{\kappa\sinh(\kappa\ell)}.
$$

For $x=y_1$ and $x=\ell-y_2$, substitution gives $\mathcal G_{ij}=G_\ell|_{ij}$, including the history entries, and hence $W_s=W_\ell|_{ij}$. Let $\mathcal S_0$ be the resulting free sewing map. Equality of the reference local recipes gives $\mathcal S_0(V_s)=V_\ell$. Applying $\mathcal S_0$ to the recursion for $U_s^{(n)}$ proves by induction

$$
\mathcal S_0(U_s^{(n)})=U_\ell^{(n)},\qquad
\mathcal S_0(A_{s,\lambda})=A_{\ell,\lambda}.
$$

For the induction to include physical stress coefficients, their smaller collision subtractions and finite contact terms must agree before evaluating the next graph. The time-smeared bulk-current limit and regional energy integrals require the corresponding explicit comparison as well. Around the artificial cut the subtraction must be performed in one smooth neighborhood before splitting its labels; an unassigned product of a contact delta with a spatial step cannot supply that comparison.

For a class of evaluated recipes satisfying these compatibility conditions, every global generating recipe has a regional representative, including its mixed slots near the cut. The operations generating its polynomial closure have regional representatives as well. The map is onto, and the external-field expansion gives its full kernel. Consequently, on that class,

$$
\boxed{\quad
\mathfrak A_{\mathrm{pre},\lambda}/\mathcal I_\lambda
\simeq\mathfrak A_{\ell,\lambda}
\quad}
$$

The same recursion intertwines the declared time evolution. Changing the finite reference time uses composition of those evolution maps; it does not require a physical free past. For finitely many cuts, the free response is independent of elimination order. Keeping all vertices, remaining ports and mixed labels then gives the same induction at each perturbative order.

## The Operator Equation and Energy at the Cut

The following canonical identities describe the energy relations. The two-vertex calculation below supplies their finite contact and interface coefficients, including the distinction between energy and spatial stress.

Use the canonical reference prescription, with constant $\lambda$. Choose a reference time $t_0$ at which to label the canonical fields; the interaction is present before and after this time. At fixed prescribed history the free regional fields already contain the driving by $q_i$. Their interaction-picture evolution uses $V_{i,*}(t)$ with no temporal switching factor. The canonical relations give

$$
\dot\Phi_i=\pi_i,\qquad
\dot\pi_i=\partial_{y_i}^2\Phi_i-m^2\Phi_i
-\frac{\lambda h_i}{6}[\Phi_i^3]_*.
$$

Indeed, the potential commutes with $\Phi_i$, while its commutator with $\pi_i$ differentiates the Wick polynomial. The free quadratic energy supplies the spatial derivative and mass terms. Consequently the regional operator equation is

$$
(\partial_t^2-\partial_{y_i}^2+m^2)\Phi_i
+\frac{\lambda h_i}{6}[\Phi_i^3]_*=0.
$$

Form the piecewise field in the common coordinate. Since the potential contains no spatial derivatives, it creates no delta derivative at a jump. The distributional field equation is

$$
(\partial_t^2-\partial_x^2+m^2)\Phi
+\frac{\lambda h}{6}[\Phi^3]_*
=p_\lambda(t)\delta(x-a)+c_\lambda(t)\delta'(x-a).
$$

An interface without an additional operator source therefore requires $c_\lambda=p_\lambda=0$. This is the quantum field-equation derivation of continuity and flux matching. The product $[\Phi^3]_*$ has its prescribed definition before the equation is used. In a different convention the equation is the transported equation, including the transformed quadratic coefficient and any allowed improvement of the flux.

Write the reference energy density and energy current as

$$\begin{aligned}
e_i&=\frac12[(\partial_t\Phi_i)^2+(\partial_{y_i}\Phi_i)^2+m^2\Phi_i^2]_*
+\frac{\lambda h_i}{24}[\Phi_i^4]_*+\epsilon_*\mathbf1,\\
j_i&=-\frac12[\partial_t\Phi_i\,\partial_{y_i}\Phi_i
+\partial_{y_i}\Phi_i\,\partial_t\Phi_i]_*.
\end{aligned}$$

The spatial stress is also included. Its classical label is $\tfrac12(\pi_i^2+(\partial_{y_i}\Phi_i)^2)-U_*(\Phi_i,y_i)$; the canonical current/vertex contact calculated below adds $-\lambda\hbar h_i[\Phi_i^2]_*/(8\pi)$ to this label. This term belongs to the spatial stress, not the Hamiltonian density. Local stress labels in the logarithmic convention used later must be converted together with their contacts. We do not impose an unchanged classical trace identity.

These expressions use the canonical single-insertion prescription. To derive their energy identity, do the calculation with the common resolved Wick polynomials before taking their finite coefficients. Their subtraction constants have no time dependence. For any such polynomial $U$ the canonical relation gives the exact symmetrized chain rule

$$
\frac d{dt}U(\Phi)
=\frac12\bigl(U'(\Phi)\pi+\pi U'(\Phi)\bigr).
$$

It follows by differentiating each field factor and using the central equal-time commutator. The same ordering occurs in the derivative of the quadratic kinetic energy. Thus, suppressing the regional label and writing $\{A,B\}_+=AB+BA$,

$$\begin{aligned}
\partial_t e
&=\frac12[\{\pi,\Phi''-U'(\Phi)\}_+
+\{\Phi',\pi'\}_++\{U'(\Phi),\pi\}_+]_*\\
&=\partial_y\frac12[\{\pi,\Phi'\}_+]_*.
\end{aligned}$$

The potential terms cancel as ordered polynomials. The differentiated subtraction and equal-time contact terms must be evaluated together with this expression. The renormalized prescription must reproduce the resulting energy identity,

$$
\partial_t e_i+\partial_{y_i}j_i=0.
$$

Define $E_i(t)=\int_0^{\ell_i}e_i(t,y_i)\,dy_i$ as a time-smeared observable, and define the endpoint current by the same bulk-current prescription. For a prescribed smooth $q_i$, the boundary time derivative is $\dot q_i$. Integrating the identity, with a reflecting physical wall, gives

$$
\frac{dE_i}{dt}=-j_i(\ell_i)
=\frac12[\dot q_i\Pi_i+\Pi_i\dot q_i]_*.
$$

After matching, the two endpoint terms cancel. If
$J_{1\to2}=-[\dot q\Pi_1+\Pi_1\dot q]_*/2$, then

$$
\dot E_1=-J_{1\to2},\qquad
\dot E_2=J_{1\to2},\qquad
\dot H_\lambda=0,\qquad H_\lambda=E_1+E_2.
$$

Both sides use the same bilinear subtraction: the common time derivative has the same orientation and the two outward spatial derivatives have opposite orientations. Their contraction terms therefore cancel as well as their uncontracted field terms. There is no independently chosen boundary-current source prescription in this argument.

At the reference time, the physical total Hamiltonian is the field expression

$$
H_\lambda=H_0+\lambda V_*(t_0)
=\int dt\,\sigma(t)\bigl(E_1(t)+E_2(t)\bigr),\qquad
\int dt\,\sigma(t)=1.
$$

Conservation makes the total independent of the normalized smooth time test $\sigma$. This avoids assigning a separate sharp-time meaning to either regional integral. Setting a reference time for the canonical algebra does not set the interaction to zero on either side of it.

For smooth $f,g$ supported in the interior, the canonical commutators give

$$\begin{aligned}
[e(f),\Phi(g)]&=-i\hbar\int dx\,fg\,\partial_t\Phi,\\
[e(f),\partial_t\Phi(g)]&=i\hbar\int dx\,g\left[
-\partial_x(f\partial_x\Phi)+f\left(m^2\Phi+
\frac{\lambda h}{6}[\Phi^3]_*\right)\right].
\end{aligned}$$

The corresponding total-energy relations are
$[H_\lambda,\Phi]=-i\hbar\partial_t\Phi$ and
$[H_\lambda,\partial_t\Phi]=-i\hbar\partial_t^2\Phi$.
These identities test that the sewn energy generates the same dynamics. Spatial translation is broken where $h'(x)\ne0$. With the convention $j=-[\pi\Phi'+\Phi'\pi]_*/2$, the corrected stress obeys $\partial_t j+\partial_x T^{11}=-\lambda h'[\Phi^4]_*/24$ through the orders computed below. No spatial momentum conservation is claimed where this force is present.

The energy contact with a potential vertex can be written explicitly. Let $v(y)=h(y)[\Phi(y)^4]_*/24$, with the coupling factor outside this label. At equal time, only the quadratic momentum term of $e$ fails to commute with $v$. Hence

$$
[e(t,x),v(t,y)]
=-\frac{i\hbar}{2}\delta(x-y)
[\pi(t,y)\,\partial_\Phi v(t,y)
+\partial_\Phi v(t,y)\,\pi(t,y)]_*
=-i\hbar\delta(x-y)\,\partial_t v(t,y).
$$

This is the exact ordered-polynomial contact, including the quadratic subtraction in the quartic. Moving the derivative through canonical time ordering therefore fixes the required family with one energy insertion and any number of potential vertices:

$$\begin{aligned}
&\partial_t T_{\mathrm{can}}\!\left(e(X)\prod_{k=1}^n v(Y_k)\right)
+\partial_x T_{\mathrm{can}}\!\left(j(X)\prod_{k=1}^n v(Y_k)\right)\\
&\qquad=-i\hbar\sum_{k=1}^n\delta^{(2)}(X-Y_k)
T_{\mathrm{can}}\!\left(\partial_{s_k}v(Y_k)
\prod_{r\ne k}v(Y_r)\right),\qquad Y_k=(s_k,y_k).
\end{aligned}$$

Evaluate the contacts and their smaller Wick collisions together, as specified above. This displays the initial contact used in the one-stress recursion; conservation has not simply been imposed inside a time-ordering symbol. Integrating the same bulk identity over a region defines its interface contact with these vertices. An additional, independently time-ordered boundary-current source is not needed for that operation. Free central stress commutators remain present in ordinary products of stress observables.

The energy identities above concern this canonical single-insertion prescription. They impose no unchanged classical trace identity and no Ward identities for arbitrary multiple stress sources. The dimension-two qualification in [Hollands--Wald](https://arxiv.org/html/gr-qc/0404074#S6) is therefore not being bypassed by claiming their stronger curved-space theorem. The following contact calculation records what must still be retained when the local stress is expressed in another convention.

## Which Collisions Need New Contact Data

There is a useful simplification specific to two dimensions. A scalar contraction is logarithmic at short separation. Each derivative can add one inverse power of separation. A kinetic stress insertion has at most two derivatives; an undifferentiated polynomial insertion has none. Mass terms and reflected image logarithms do not increase this count.

Consider a group of $s$ bulk kinetic stress insertions and $n$ undifferentiated bulk insertions approaching the same interior point. The latter include interaction vertices and external polynomial probes; the potential part of a stress insertion belongs in this count as well. There are $2(s+n-1)$ relative coordinates, while the contraction coefficient has at most $2s$ inverse powers. Shrinking all separations by $\varepsilon$ therefore leaves the margin

$$
2(s+n-1)-2s=2(n-1).
$$

A contact delta at the full collision costs $2(s+n-1)$ inverse powers; a derivative of it costs still more. Consequently, for $n\ge2$, no new local term supported only on this full collision is allowed by this bound. Once every smaller collision has been treated, the value at the full collision is fixed by the separated-point coefficient and the same bound. Logarithmic factors do not change a strictly positive margin.

For a collision at a straight boundary, also include $b$ linear history insertions, each with its own time label. Here $q$, $\dot q$ and $\Pi$ cost at most one derivative per insertion. The bulk vertices contribute two coordinates each, the boundary vertices one each, and the common boundary time removes one. The corresponding margin is

$$
\bigl(2(s+n)+b-1\bigr)-(2s+b)=2n-1.
$$

Thus an interaction-containing boundary collision, $n\ge1$, needs no new subtraction at the full collision after the smaller ones have been fixed. This assertion concerns the stated derivative bound and individually labelled linear histories.

Stress conservation applies one more derivative. The margins available for a new local defect in that identity are therefore

$$
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
 & \text{product} & \text{one stress divergence}\\ \hline
\text{bulk collision} & 2(n-1) & 2n-3\\
\text{boundary collision} & 2n-1 & 2n-2
\end{array}
$$

For $n\ge2$ both divergence margins are positive. At $n=1$ the boundary divergence margin is zero: a delta contact can remain even though the product itself required no new subtraction. Local integrability alone does not prove the boundary conservation identity.

This gives a recursive reduction. Fix the free contact families and the families containing one undifferentiated bulk vertex, including their stress identities. At higher $n$, subtract the already fixed smaller collisions and impose the same short-distance bound. There is no additional freedom, or new defect supported only at the full collision, in the table's sector. The step must be applied to every subgroup of vertices, including overlapping subgroups. It reduces the required normalization data; it does not itself construct compatible choices for those initial families. They still allow arbitrarily many stress insertions. These statements use the bound in the reference prescription; an independent finite redefinition is first converted to that prescription.

One local boundary calculation makes the distinction concrete. Use Euclidean time difference $\tau$ and inward distances $r,s>0$ only to display the short-distance coefficient at a reflecting wall:

$$
G_D(\tau;r,s)
=-\frac1{4\pi}\log
\frac{\tau^2+(r-s)^2}{\tau^2+(r+s)^2},
\qquad
\left.\partial_rG_D\right|_{r=0}
=\frac{s}{\pi(\tau^2+s^2)}.
$$

The last kernel has unit integral over $\tau$. Its integral over the half disk $\tau^2+s^2<\varepsilon^2$, $s>0$, is $2\varepsilon/\pi$. It is locally integrable, yet tends to a delta in $\tau$ when $s$ goes to zero. Taking one more derivative or a boundary limit must retain that contact. At the artificial endpoint our $\partial_y$ is minus the inward derivative used in this example.

The calculation uses only the local logarithmic coefficient. It does not change the massive Lorentzian interval theory or select a new state. Released transparent kernels have the same derivative bound, although their coefficients differ from the reflecting ones.

There are two scope restrictions. A nonlinear boundary current such as $[\dot q\Pi](t)$, treated as an independent time-ordered source, has two derivatives but only one time coordinate. It cannot be counted as two independently labelled linear histories. Also, a probe with more than two derivatives requires its actual derivative count. Independent time ordering of these boundary sources, or probes with more than two derivatives, requires a further construction. The ordinary external-field replacement used in the descent argument does not assert such a time-ordering identity.

## A Stress Contact with One Quartic Vertex

We can determine an interaction contact directly by Wick contraction. In local Euclidean coordinates use the logarithmic contraction, now including $\hbar$,

$$
C(z,w)=-\frac{\hbar}{4\pi}
\log\!\bigl(\mu^2(z-w)(\bar z-\bar w)\bigr),
\qquad
\mathscr T(z)=-\frac{2\pi}{\hbar}:[\partial_z\phi(z)]^2:.
$$

The colons in this calculation use this local logarithmic subtraction. $\mathscr T$ is a convenient normalized chiral stress label for computing contact coefficients, not the full massive stress tensor. The leading double-pole coefficient is fixed by the logarithm. Finite state or image contributions must be converted to this local convention along with the composite fields.

For any finite polynomial $F(\phi)$, there are only single and double contractions with $\mathscr T$. Since
$\partial_z C=-\hbar/[4\pi(z-w)]$, they give the pole terms

$$
\boxed{\quad
\mathscr T(z)[F(\phi(w))]
\sim \frac{\partial_w[F(\phi(w))]}{z-w}
-\frac{\hbar}{8\pi}
\frac{[F''(\phi(w))]}{(z-w)^2}.
\quad}
$$

The prime on $F$ means a derivative with respect to $\phi$. The single contraction has two choices of the stress field. The double contraction has $F''$ choices and uses both stress fields. For the quartic polynomial the double pole is

$$
-\frac{3\hbar}{2\pi}\frac{[\phi^2(w)]}{(z-w)^2}.
$$

The same pairing rule gives $1/[2(z-w)^4]$ for the fully contracted term in $\mathscr T(z)\mathscr T(w)$: the free scalar central coefficient is retained.

To turn these poles into contacts, use

$$
\bar\partial_z\frac1{z-w}=\pi\delta^{(2)}(z-w),
\qquad
\bar\partial_z\frac1{(z-w)^2}
=-\pi\partial_z\delta^{(2)}(z-w).
$$

The normalization can be checked without a contour prescription. Replace $1/z$ by $\bar z/(z\bar z+\varepsilon^2)$. Its $\bar\partial$ derivative is $\varepsilon^2/(|z|^2+\varepsilon^2)^2$, whose integral is $\pi$. Applying $-\partial_z$ gives the second sign.

Insert one Euclidean interaction vertex with weight $-\int d^2w\,g(w)[F(\phi(w))]/\hbar$. The pole contribution to its stress divergence is

$$
\bigl(\bar\partial\mathscr T\bigr)_{\text{one-vertex contact}}
=-\frac{\pi}{\hbar}\left(
g\,\partial[F]+\frac{\hbar}{8\pi}\partial\bigl(g[F'']\bigr)
\right).
$$

Define the local companion for this contact by

$$
\Theta_{\mathrm{loc}}=\frac{\pi}{\hbar}\,
g\left([F]+\frac{\hbar}{8\pi}[F'']\right).
$$

Then the contact part of
$\bar\partial\mathscr T+\partial\Theta_{\mathrm{loc}}$
is $(\pi/\hbar)(\partial g)[F]$. For constant $g$ it vanishes; for varying $g$ the source-gradient term remains. This is a calculation of the local pole contact. The massive separated-point terms and other stress insertions have not been replaced by this equation.

For $F=\lambda\phi^4/24$ the additional polynomial in the companion is

$$
\boxed{\qquad
\Delta U_{\mathrm{loc}}=\frac{\hbar}{8\pi}[F'']
=\frac{\lambda\hbar}{16\pi}[\phi^2].
\qquad}
$$

This is an operator term, not merely a choice of constant energy zero. Its coefficient agrees with the lightcone short-distance computation in [the two-dimensional quartic model, Section 3.2](https://arxiv.org/html/1704.04500#S3.SS2), at $\hbar=1$. That computation also distinguishes the local stress component from the Hamiltonian integrand in its lightcone convention. We therefore do not add this term by hand to the reference potential $U_*$. The local stress, its source contacts and the energy identification must be transported together to the reference convention.

This particular contact can be checked under sewing. In the common smooth neighborhood of the cut, split the vertex test as $g_1+g_2=h$. This is bookkeeping for the released vertex recipe, not an alteration of the two isolated regional interactions. For example, $g_i=\xi_i h$ with smooth $\xi_1+\xi_2=1$ avoids multiplying a delta derivative by a discontinuous step. The contact calculation is linear in the vertex test, so

$$
\Theta_{\mathrm{loc}}[g_1]+\Theta_{\mathrm{loc}}[g_2]
=\Theta_{\mathrm{loc}}[h],
\qquad
\frac{\pi}{\hbar}\bigl(\partial g_1+\partial g_2\bigr)[F]
=\frac{\pi}{\hbar}(\partial h)[F].
$$

Every derivative of the artificial partition cancels, including the contribution from $[F'']$. Since $h=1$ near the cut, this single-stress, single-quartic bulk contact leaves no artificial source there. The independent global calculation gives precisely the same local term. This checks sewing of this contact; it does not determine a time-ordered product with a nonlinear boundary current.

There is a useful consistency check on this polynomial contact. Write its action on polynomial labels as

$$
L_\epsilon=\epsilon(z)\partial_z
-\frac{\hbar}{8\pi}\epsilon'(z)\partial_\phi^2.
$$

Here $\partial_z$ acts on the coordinate dependence of the label, and commutes with $\partial_\phi^2$. Direct multiplication gives

$$
[L_\epsilon,L_\eta]
=L_{\epsilon\eta'-\eta\epsilon'}.
$$

Thus the single-stress polynomial rule has a consistent commutator at arbitrary polynomial degree. A constant Wick change
$\exp[(\hbar c/2)\partial_\phi^2]$ commutes with every $L_\epsilon$. This verifies compatibility of this contact rule with the explicit finite subtraction used earlier. It is not the multiple-stress Ward construction: the stress central term and the marginal boundary contact still have to be included there.

## Transporting the Entire Sewing Construction

Suppose the reference construction has the stated operations and sewing map $\mathcal S_*$. Let $\beta_i$ convert region $i$ from its own convention to the reference convention using $Z_i$, its insertion derivatives and the full source map. Let $\beta_{\mathrm{pre}}$ apply these conversions to the regional recipes and their interface data. It is defined before reference sewing is evaluated. Let $\beta_\ell$ similarly convert an independently chosen global convention to the reference one.

Define the released product in the independent regional conventions by

$$
F\star_{\mathrm{pre}}^{\mathrm{ind}}G
=\beta_{\mathrm{pre}}^{-1}
\bigl(\beta_{\mathrm{pre}}F\star_{\mathrm{pre},*}\beta_{\mathrm{pre}}G\bigr),
$$

and define

$$
\mathcal I^{\mathrm{ind}}=\beta_{\mathrm{pre}}^{-1}\mathcal I_*,
\qquad
\mathcal S^{\mathrm{ind}}=
\beta_\ell^{-1}\mathcal S_*\beta_{\mathrm{pre}}.
$$

These definitions do not use the global algebra to construct the regional product: that product uses $\beta_{\mathrm{pre}}$ and the already regional reference construction. The global $\beta_\ell$ enters only its comparison map.

**Finite-renormalization transport theorem.** If the reference map is onto with kernel $\mathcal I_*$, then

$$
\ker\mathcal S^{\mathrm{ind}}=\mathcal I^{\mathrm{ind}},
\qquad
\operatorname{im}\mathcal S^{\mathrm{ind}}=\mathfrak A_\ell^{\mathrm{ind}},
\qquad
\mathfrak A_{\mathrm{pre}}^{\mathrm{ind}}/\mathcal I^{\mathrm{ind}}
\simeq\mathfrak A_\ell^{\mathrm{ind}}.
$$

**Proof.** Invertibility of $\beta_\ell$ makes vanishing equivalent to
$\mathcal S_*\beta_{\mathrm{pre}}F=0$, which is precisely the displayed preimage ideal. For a target element $G$, lift $\beta_\ell G$ through $\mathcal S_*$ and apply $\beta_{\mathrm{pre}}^{-1}$. The product definition makes both maps algebra homomorphisms and preserves the involution. Transporting the time-evolution and source identities gives their intertwining, including every stress contact insertion. Formal inversion and the source chain rule hold at every order. $\square$

For successive convention changes, the first source derivative obeys the ordinary chain rule. The second obeys

$$
D^2(Z\circ Y)[F,G]
=D^2Z(Y)[DYF,DYG]+DZ(Y)D^2Y[F,G].
$$

Keeping both terms makes successive stress/contact conversions agree with a single conversion. Higher derivatives work in the same way. Consequently independent convention choices add no new sewing obstruction once the reference construction and these admissible maps exist. They also do not repair a missing reference stress/history construction. The common reference is a comparison convention; it does not constrain each region to present its algebra in that convention.

## Explicit Interaction Checks

For the reference normally ordered quartic vertices, write $W=W_s$ before taking the required time-ordering prescription. Cross contractions give

$$\begin{aligned}
[\phi_X^4]_W\star[\phi_Y^4]_W
&=[\phi_X^4\phi_Y^4]_W
+16\hbar W_{XY}[\phi_X^3\phi_Y^3]_W\\
&\quad+72\hbar^2W_{XY}^2[\phi_X^2\phi_Y^2]_W
+96\hbar^3W_{XY}^3[\phi_X\phi_Y]_W
+24\hbar^4W_{XY}^4\mathbf1.
\end{aligned}$$

The coefficient of $r$ contractions is $\binom4r^2r!$. All four possibilities occur when the vertices lie on opposite sides of the cut. Their contribution to a time-ordered two-vertex expression uses $W_F$ and the prescribed local extension; it is not obtained by retaining only the single-contraction tree term. The normally ordered two-dimensional bulk kernels have logarithmic singularities, so powers without derivative insertions are locally integrable. This observation does not justify derivative stress graphs or all history limits.

For the field itself, the first correction at $t\ge t_0$ is

$$
\Phi_\lambda(X)=\phi^0(X)-\frac\lambda6
\int_{t_0}^{t_X}\!dZ\,G_s^R(X,Z)h(Z)[\phi^0(Z)^3]_*+O(\lambda^2).
$$

Here the notation includes spatial integration. Set $G_{s,t_0}^R(X,Z)=G_s^R(X,Z)$ for $t_Z\ge t_0$ and zero otherwise. The resulting commutator is

$$\begin{aligned}
[\Phi_\lambda(X),\Phi_\lambda(Y)]
&=i\hbar E_s(X,Y)\\
&\quad-\frac{i\hbar\lambda}{2}\int dZ\,h(Z)
\bigl(G_{s,t_0}^R(X,Z)E_s(Z,Y)
+G_{s,t_0}^R(Y,Z)E_s(X,Z)\bigr)
[\phi^0(Z)^2]_*+O(\lambda^2).
\end{aligned}$$

It is already operator-valued at first order. Splitting the vertex integral into the two regions, and using the explicit regional/global response identity in every factor, reproduces the independent global expression, including the crossed terms. Its tree symbol gives the corresponding interacting Poisson bracket. The displayed finite-time formula presupposes the compatible initial and history limits specified above; it is not an arbitrary-history existence proof.

The coefficient of $\lambda^2$ tests more than the tree response. In common $W$-normal coordinates, put $Q_n(X)=[\phi^0(X)^n]_W$. With $Y=(s,y)$, $Z=(u,z)$, it is

$$
\Phi^{(2)}(X)=\left(\frac i\hbar\right)^2
\int_{t_0}^{t_X}ds\int_{t_0}^sdu\int dy\,dz\,
\frac{h(y)h(z)}{(4!)^2}
\bigl[Q_4(Z),[Q_4(Y),\phi^0(X)]\bigr].
$$

The inner commutator is $4i\hbar E(Y,X)Q_3(Y)$. The remaining one is exactly

$$\begin{aligned}
[Q_4(Z),Q_3(Y)]
&=\sum_{r=1}^3\binom4r\binom3r r!\,\hbar^r
\bigl(W_{ZY}^{\,r}-W_{YZ}^{\,r}\bigr)
[\phi_Z^{4-r}\phi_Y^{3-r}]_W,\\
\binom4r\binom3r r!&=12,36,24\qquad(r=1,2,3).
\end{aligned}$$

Thus the second-order field contains degrees $5,3,1$, with explicit powers $\hbar^0,\hbar^1,\hbar^2$, respectively. The three-contraction term includes the two-loop contribution. If the chosen local subtraction differs from $W$-normal ordering, first expand its quartic into these Wick coordinates; the resulting quadratic and constant terms are transported too.

Split both vertex integrals into their left and right parts. Every factor in each of the four regional assignments is the restriction of its independently global counterpart. This checks the quantum terms as well as the tree term. To obtain the commutator to order $\lambda^2$, retain

$$
[\Phi^{(2)}(X),\phi^0(Y)]
+[\phi^0(X),\Phi^{(2)}(Y)]
+[\Phi^{(1)}(X),\Phi^{(1)}(Y)].
$$

All three terms are intertwined by the same contraction equality. The last term must not be omitted. These are explicit coefficient identities; no infinite-time scattering limit is being taken.

An elementary canonical diagnostic also shows why all commutators must be retained. In symmetric polynomial coordinates for $[q,p]=i\hbar$,

$$
\frac{i}{\hbar}\left[\frac{q^4}{24},p^3\right]_\star
=-\frac{q^3p^2}{2}+\frac{\hbar^2q}{4}.
$$

This is a finite algebra check of the quantum contraction term, not a substitute for a continuum loop computation.

## Auxiliary Smooth-Time Presentations

The stationary Hamiltonian construction above does not require a physical switching process. For comparison with smooth-time relative-source formulas, one may instead use the following auxiliary coordinates. Take observables in a finite time window $O$, with spatial support away from the physical walls but allowed to cross the artificial cut. Use a smooth auxiliary $\chi$ equal to one on a slightly larger time window and zero at sufficiently early and late times. Put

$$
\mathcal V_\chi=-\frac\lambda{24}\int dt\,dx\,
\chi(t)h(x)\phi(x)^4.
$$

The label is interpreted with the chosen off-shell local prescription. The interior time-ordering construction of [the existing interacting note](<../../../phin renormalized quantum observable algebra gluing.md>) applies to these compact vertex supports. It uses the full reflected propagator, including mixed-region entries after release. Its all-order bulk statement is separate from its unfinished history-family extension.

Define the relative source expression and its polynomial insertions by

$$
S_{\mathcal V}(F)=S(\mathcal V)^{-1}\star S(\mathcal V+F),
\qquad
\mathcal O_{\mathcal V}(F)=
\left.\frac{\hbar}{i}\frac d{ds}S_{\mathcal V}(sF)\right|_{s=0}.
$$

Take the algebra generated by these insertions and their finite products, with formal coefficient completion and the polynomial kernels produced by the contractions. Local derivative labels are permitted. The physical stress label is fixed by the canonical energy/contact prescription above.

For another test $\chi'$ equal to one on the same larger window, decompose

$$
\mathcal V_{\chi'}-\mathcal V_\chi=v_-+v_+,
$$

where $v_-$ is supported strictly before $O$ and $v_+$ strictly after $O$. Both have compact support. Causal factorization gives, for insertions in $O$,

$$
\mathcal O_{\mathcal V_{\chi'}}(F)
=U^{-1}\star\mathcal O_{\mathcal V_\chi}(F)\star U,
\qquad U=S_{\mathcal V_\chi}(v_-).
$$

The future change $v_+$ drops out. For the past change, insert
$S_{\mathcal V}(v_-+F)=S_{\mathcal V}(F)\star S_{\mathcal V}(v_-)$
in the relative-source definition and differentiate. This proves the displayed identity at every order, not just for the linear field. It also holds for all finite products.

Successive past changes obey

$$
S_{\mathcal V}(v_-)\star
S_{\mathcal V+v_-}(w_-)=S_{\mathcal V}(v_-+w_-).
$$

Thus the comparison maps compose. Identify presentations using these maps. The resulting local bulk algebra describes the stationary coupling in $O$ without choosing a physical switching process. This is the [algebraic adiabatic construction](https://arxiv.org/html/0901.2038#S6.SS3). It is an identification by isomorphisms, not a claim that two retarded observables written in the same free coordinates are identical. No expectation-value limit or time-slice surjectivity of the old finite-word class is being used.

The released and independently global prescriptions have the same contraction kernels and the same local extension rule. They therefore intertwine $S(\mathcal V)$, its formal inverse, the inserted series and the displayed $U$. Their bulk comparison consequently descends through changes of $\chi$. This proves the all-order constant-coupling comparison for the insertion-generated bulk sector. Time translation transports $O$, its tests and its source labels; the stationary $h$ and prescription preserve these identifications. The independent-convention source identities also intertwine every step.

This result includes bulk derivative insertions as algebra elements. We can also construct its time-translation generator in smooth time coordinates. Identifying this generator with the integral of the local stress tensor, and extending the construction to the independent regional histories, require further work.

## The Energy Generator in Auxiliary Coordinates

Choose the plateau of $\chi$ around $t=0$. Let $\nu(s)$ be $\chi'(s)$ on the past rising part and zero elsewhere. It is smooth, supported before the observation window, and $\int ds\,\nu(s)=1$. Define

$$
K_\chi=\lambda\,\mathcal O_{\mathcal V_\chi}
\left(\int ds\,\nu(s)V_*(s)\right),
\qquad
V_*(s)=\frac1{24}\int_0^\ell dx\,h(x)[\phi^0(s,x)^4]_*.
$$

This is an explicit interacting insertion. At order $\lambda$ it is the smooth time average of the free quartic potential; higher orders follow by differentiating the relative source series. All vertex tests are smooth and compact, and their spatial supports stay away from the physical walls. Thus the existing bulk construction defines every coefficient of $K_\chi$. No sharp-time stress product is used in this definition.

To see what it generates, let $\alpha_t^0$ translate free fields and tests, with $\chi_t(s)=\chi(s-t)$. For small $t$, write

$$
\mathcal V_{\chi_t}-\mathcal V_\chi=v_-^t+v_+^t,
\qquad C_\chi(t)=S_{\mathcal V_\chi}(v_-^t).
$$

The two changes lie before and after the observation window. Time-translation covariance followed by the past-change identity gives

$$
\alpha_t^0\bigl(\mathcal O_{\mathcal V_\chi}(F)\bigr)
=C_\chi(t)^{-1}\star
\mathcal O_{\mathcal V_\chi}(F_t)\star C_\chi(t).
$$

Therefore, while both insertion supports remain in the plateau,

$$
\alpha_{\lambda,t}^\chi(A)
=C_\chi(t)\star\alpha_t^0(A)\star C_\chi(t)^{-1}
$$

is exactly translation of the interacting insertion. The sign is fixed by the negative interaction-action label:

$$
\left.\frac d{dt}v_-^t\right|_{t=0}
=\lambda\int ds\,\nu(s)V_*(s),
\qquad C_\chi'(0)=\frac i\hbar K_\chi.
$$

This $C_\chi$ implements conjugation in the displayed direction. It is different from the earlier Dyson operator $U_s$, which obeys the negative-sign evolution equation.

For small $s,t$, the past changes obey
$v_-^{t+s}=v_-^t+\alpha_t^0v_-^s$.
Cancellation of adjacent relative source factors, with the future change removed by causal factorization, gives

$$
C_\chi(t+s)=C_\chi(t)\star\alpha_t^0(C_\chi(s)).
$$

Equivalently, extend $C_\chi$ to every finite time by

$$
C_\chi'(t)=\frac i\hbar C_\chi(t)\star\alpha_t^0(K_\chi),
\qquad C_\chi(0)=1.
$$

For $t>0$ the coefficient recursion is the ordered series

$$
C_\chi(t)=1+\sum_{n\ge1}\left(\frac i\hbar\right)^n
\int_{0<s_n<\cdots<s_1<t}\!\!ds_1\cdots ds_n\,
\alpha_{s_n}^0(K_\chi)\star\cdots\star\alpha_{s_1}^0(K_\chi).
$$

Each coefficient uses finitely many such integrals. The factors already have smooth spacetime tests, so the endpoints of these parameter integrals do not create the sharp vertex-time restriction of the original Dyson expression. The cocycle identity makes successive finite time translations compose. This is the relative-source construction of [Fredenhagen--Lindner, Section 3](https://arxiv.org/html/1306.6519#S3), with the interaction-action sign and $\hbar$ restored here.

Specify the enlargement used by these formulas. Begin with the bulk coefficient algebra and include its free time translates, time derivatives and the finite parameter integrals just displayed, together with their finite products. Denote this concrete polynomial class by $\mathfrak B^\chi$. The conjugations define dynamics on this class; they agree with interacting source translation in each plateau chart. Enlarging a time window and using the previous comparison maps gives consistent charts. This does not assert that the old finite-word algebra in one window generates every observable at every time.

The relative source series and $C_\chi$ are auxiliary expressions: at a fixed order in $\lambda$ they may contain negative powers of $\hbar$. Their connected insertion coefficients and their conjugation on observables have nonnegative powers. In the generator formula this follows directly from the factor of $\hbar$ in a commutator:

$$
\delta_\lambda^\chi A
=\delta_0A+\frac i\hbar[K_\chi,A]_\star,
\qquad
\delta_0A=\left.\frac d{dt}\alpha_t^0(A)\right|_{t=0}.
$$

Use the field-integral $H_0$ defined in the canonical prescription. Its action on each allowed polynomial coefficient is

$$
H_0F-FH_0=-i\hbar\,\delta_0F.
$$

The relation extends from the canonical fields to their finite products by the commutator derivation rule, and to their time integrals by linearity. It is now a property of the field energy. Merely adjoining a symbol obeying this relation would give an abstract energy extension and would not establish this realization.

In these smooth time coordinates set

$$
H_\lambda^\chi=H_0+K_\chi.
$$

Then

$$
\frac i\hbar[H_\lambda^\chi,A]=\delta_\lambda^\chi A,
\qquad
\delta_\lambda^\chi H_\lambda^\chi=0.
$$

The latter equality uses $\delta_0H_0=0$ and
$(i/\hbar)[K_\chi,H_0]=-\delta_0K_\chi$.
Thus we have an all-order conserved generator of the bulk dynamics. The comparison immediately below identifies it with the sum of the regional stress integrals in the same coordinates.

The construction is compatible with the bulk sewing comparison. Construct $K_{\chi,s}$ from the released regional contractions and $K_{\chi,\ell}$ independently on the whole interval. Splitting the smooth potential label into its regional expressions and using the same local extension rule gives

$$
\mathcal S_0(K_{\chi,s})=K_{\chi,\ell}.
$$

The free energy densities and their full contractions agree under the free sewing map, with the same physical-wall normalization. Thus
$\mathcal S_0(H_{0,s})=H_{0,\ell}$.
Applying the map to each coefficient of the ordered series proves

$$
\mathcal S_0(C_{\chi,s}(t))=C_{\chi,\ell}(t),
\qquad
\mathcal S_0(H_{\lambda,s}^\chi)=H_{\lambda,\ell}^\chi.
$$

This proves the comparison for the declared bulk recipes and their energy generators. The ordinary independent-history quotient is addressed below; no time-ordered quotient is being asserted.

Finally let $W=S_{\mathcal V_\chi}(v_-)$ compare two auxiliary tests. The same source cancellations give

$$
C_{\chi'}(t)=W^{-1}\star C_\chi(t)\star\alpha_t^0(W).
$$

Differentiating gives

$$
K_{\chi'}=W^{-1}\star K_\chi\star W
+\frac{\hbar}{i}W^{-1}\star\delta_0W,
\qquad
H_\lambda^{\chi'}=W^{-1}\star H_\lambda^\chi\star W.
$$

The extra free-derivative term is required. The auxiliary time average changes the energy coordinates by conjugation; it does not change the stationary physical coupling. Independent finite-renormalization conventions transport the whole insertion defining $K_\chi$ and hence these identities.

## Comparing the Two Energy Presentations

Once the regional stress energy has been identified with the canonical field Hamiltonian, the following calculation compares it with the smooth-time representation. It concerns auxiliary coordinates only. Introduce a comparison interaction $\lambda\chi(t)h(x)$ to calculate the embedding of the stationary theory in a free algebra. Let $U_\chi(t)$ denote its canonical interaction-picture series, and put

$$
V_\chi^{\mathrm H}(t)=U_\chi(t)^{-1}V_*(t)U_\chi(t),\qquad
\mathcal E_\chi(t)=
U_\chi(t)^{-1}\bigl(H_0+\lambda\chi(t)V_*(t)\bigr)U_\chi(t).
$$

Differentiate, using
$\dot U_\chi=-(i\lambda/\hbar)\chi V_*U_\chi$
and $\dot V_*=(i/\hbar)[H_0,V_*]$. The free-translation term cancels the commutator with $H_0$, leaving

$$
\frac d{dt}\mathcal E_\chi(t)
=\lambda\chi'(t)V_\chi^{\mathrm H}(t).
$$

Equivalently the local auxiliary identity is
$\partial_t e_\chi+\partial_x j_\chi
=\lambda\chi' h[\Phi_\chi^4]_*/24$.
It includes the quadratic and constant subtractions of that same quartic insertion.

In the past of the auxiliary support, $\mathcal E_\chi=H_0$. Integrate to a time in the plateau. Causality identifies the canonical insertion of $V_*(s)$ with the single relative-source insertion used to define $K_\chi$. Therefore

$$
\boxed{\quad
(E_1+E_2)^\chi
=H_0+\lambda\int ds\,\nu(s)
\mathcal O_{\mathcal V_\chi}\bigl(V_*(s)\bigr)
=H_0+K_\chi=H_\lambda^\chi.
\quad}
$$

This gives the desired equality in the same auxiliary presentation, with the same energy zero, at orders where the local stress-work identity has been established. The calculation below establishes the physical constant-coupling energy identity through the chosen endpoint. No assumption about the center of the observable algebra is needed. The stationary theory still has coupling $\lambda h(x)$ at every physical time; the auxiliary free past only computes a change of coordinates.

## The Completed Two-Vertex Calculation

The useful separation is between propagation and a vertex collision. Releasing the history changes propagation everywhere, including on the same side of the cut. A collision also remembers the local subtraction used to define its field polynomial. We now keep both changes explicitly.

All formulas in this section use the free Wick coordinates belonging to the propagation problem being evaluated. First this can be an isolated region; after release it is the sewn problem. Write $W_{XY}=W(X,Y)$, with the factor of $\hbar$ outside $W$, and let $C$ be the common symmetric massive flat-space subtraction. Set

$$
w_{AB}=\frac12(W_{AB}+W_{BA})-C_{AB},\qquad w_X=w_{XX}.
$$

The coincidence is taken in the interior of that propagation problem. The local insertions, written as ordinary polynomials in its Wick coordinates, are

$$\begin{aligned}
P_2(X)&=\phi_X^2+\hbar w_X,\\
P_4(X)&=\phi_X^4+6\hbar w_X\phi_X^2+3\hbar^2w_X^2,\\
P_{AB}&=\phi_A\phi_B+\hbar w_{AB}.
\end{aligned}$$

Here an ordinary monomial in the $\phi$ labels represents a joint $W$-normal product. Thus $P_4$ represents $[\phi^4]_*$, not another interaction. Its quadratic and scalar terms are respectively one- and two-loop contributions already at order $\lambda$. They cannot be removed by choosing an isolated-region vacuum zero.

Changing from isolated to released propagation replaces $w_i$ by $w_s$. The exact coordinate change is

$$
\exp\!\left(\frac{\hbar(w_s-w_i)}2\partial_\phi^2\right)
P_n(\phi;w_i)=P_n(\phi;w_s),\qquad n=2,4.
$$

For the quartic this retains both $6\hbar w_s\phi^2$ and $3\hbar^2w_s^2$. Apply this change at resolved interior points before approaching the artificial endpoint. The isolated image term diverges there; its separate endpoint value is not part of the sewing prescription.

There is an explicit finite answer after release. With $C(0;r)=K_0(m|r|)/(2\pi)$, the independently global interval gives

$$
w_\ell(x)=\frac1\pi\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}K_0(2mn\ell)
-\frac1{2\pi}\sum_{n\in\mathbb Z}K_0(2m|x+n\ell|).
$$

The same expression follows by integrating the released regional Euclidean response minus $1/(2\sqrt{m^2+\omega^2})$ over $d\omega/(2\pi)$. At an interior cut it is finite. For equal halves, $\ell=2L$, $a=L$,

$$
w_\Gamma=\frac1\pi\left[
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}K_0(4mnL)
-\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}K_0((4n+2)mL)\right].
$$

This exhibits the removal of the artificial reflecting image. It keeps the images of the physical walls. As an independent check, for $m=1.3$, $\ell=2$, $a=0.8$, the image sum and the regional-response integral both give $w_\Gamma=-0.02036983441009108844\ldots$.

### Every One- and Two-Vertex Coefficient

Put $v(Y)=h(y)P_4(Y)/24$. At a common observation time $t$, define

$$\begin{aligned}
R_1F&=\frac i\hbar\int_{t_0}^{t}ds\int dy\,[v(Y),F]_{\star},\\
R_2F&=\left(\frac i\hbar\right)^2
\int_{t_0<u<s<t}du\,ds\int dz\,dy\,
[v(Z),[v(Y),F]_{\star}]_{\star}.
\end{aligned}$$

The earlier vertex is the outer commutator. Integrals with vertices on either region are included. The physical coupling is constant; these are coefficients of evolution between two finite reference times, not a switch-on prescription.

For a polynomial $F$ in distinct field labels $X_b$, the entire integrand can be calculated by the finite operation

$$
\mathcal D_YF=
i\sum_{\substack{\mathbf r\ge0\\1\le r=\sum_b r_b\le4}}
\frac{\hbar^{r-1}}{\prod_b r_b!}
\frac{\partial_{\phi_Y}^{,r}P_4(Y)}{24}
\left(\prod_bW_{YX_b}^{r_b}-\prod_bW_{X_bY}^{r_b}\right)
\prod_b\partial_{\phi_{X_b}}^{r_b}F.
$$

Zero polynomial derivatives remove the impossible terms. Include the label $Y$ among the $X_b$ when applying $\mathcal D_Z$ to $\mathcal D_YF$. Then $R_1F$ and $R_2F$ are the displayed time integrals of $h(y)\mathcal D_YF$ and $h(z)h(y)\mathcal D_Z\mathcal D_YF$. This specifies all field powers, coefficients and orientations of the contractions; there are no unnamed two-loop diagrams left in these formulas.

For stress, begin with two resolved labels $A,B$. Define

$$
\alpha_A=W_{YA}-W_{AY},\quad
\alpha_B=W_{YB}-W_{BY},\quad
\beta_{AB}=W_{YA}W_{YB}-W_{AY}W_{BY}.
$$

Direct contraction gives

$$\begin{aligned}
\mathcal D_Y P_{AB}
={}&\frac i6\left(\alpha_A\phi_Y^3\phi_B+
\alpha_B\phi_Y^3\phi_A\right)
+\frac{i\hbar}2\beta_{AB}\phi_Y^2\\
&+\frac{i\hbar w_Y}2\left(\alpha_A\phi_Y\phi_B+
\alpha_B\phi_Y\phi_A\right)
+\frac{i\hbar^2w_Y}2\beta_{AB}.
\end{aligned}$$

The last term is an easily missed two-loop scalar contribution. The root scalar $\hbar w_{AB}$ itself commutes with a vertex, but the scalar produced by contracting the vertex's quadratic image term does not disappear.

Here is the complete two-vertex list in a compact form. Choose $t_Y,t_Z\in\{0,1\}$, put

$$
k=4-2t_Y,\quad l=4-2t_Z,\qquad
c_Y=\begin{cases}1&t_Y=0,\\6w_Y&t_Y=1,\end{cases}
\quad
c_Z=\begin{cases}1&t_Z=0,\\6w_Z&t_Z=1.\end{cases}
$$

The scalar part of a vertex has zero commutator. The remaining indices count lines from $Y$ to $A,B$ and from $Z$ to $A,B,Y$:

$$\begin{gathered}
a,b\in\{0,1\},\quad c\in\{0,\ldots,1-a\},\quad
d\in\{0,\ldots,1-b\},\quad e\ge0,\\
a+b\ge1,\quad c+d+e\ge1,\quad a+b+e\le k,\quad c+d+e\le l,\\
\mathcal L=a+b+c+d+e-2+t_Y+t_Z,\qquad 0\le\mathcal L\le2.
\end{gathered}$$

For every allowed choice, add

$$\begin{aligned}
&-\frac{c_Yc_Z}{24^2}\,
\frac{(k)_{a+b+e}(l)_{c+d+e}}{e!}\hbar^{\mathcal L}
\phi_A^{1-a-c}\phi_B^{1-b-d}
\phi_Y^{k-a-b-e}\phi_Z^{l-c-d-e}\\
&\qquad\times
\left(W_{YA}^{a}W_{YB}^{b}-W_{AY}^{a}W_{BY}^{b}\right)
\left(W_{ZA}^{c}W_{ZB}^{d}W_{ZY}^{e}
-W_{AZ}^{c}W_{BZ}^{d}W_{YZ}^{e}\right).
\end{aligned}$$

Here $(k)_r=k!/(k-r)!$. Integrate this sum over the ordered times with $h(y)h(z)$. There are 4 tree, 13 one-loop and 16 two-loop indexed terms. These counts keep the two root labels distinct and combine each commutator into a difference; they are not counts of unlabelled Feynman topologies. Independent direct Wick differentiation gives exactly the same polynomial in each loop degree.

Differentiate the root labels, holding the common evolution time and its integration endpoints fixed, using

$$\begin{aligned}
D_e&=\tfrac12(\partial_{t_A}\partial_{t_B}
+\partial_{x_A}\partial_{x_B}+m^2),\\
D_j&=-\tfrac12(\partial_{t_A}\partial_{x_B}
+\partial_{x_A}\partial_{t_B}),\\
D_p&=\tfrac12(\partial_{t_A}\partial_{t_B}
+\partial_{x_A}\partial_{x_B}-m^2).
\end{aligned}$$

Only then take $A,B\to X$. These derivatives define the free seeds $e_0,j_0,p_0$ with their scalar image terms and the already fixed energy zero. They do not differentiate an upper limit of a Dyson integral. The latter differentiation generates the contacts computed next.

The potential part of the stress already carries one power of $\lambda$, so it needs only one extra vertex. Its integrand, with $h(x)h(y)$ outside, is

$$
\sum_{\substack{t_X,t_Y\in\{0,1\}\\1\le r\le\min(4-2t_X,4-2t_Y)\\r-1+t_X+t_Y\le2}}
\frac{i c_Xc_Y}{24^2}\hbar^{r-1+t_X+t_Y}
\binom{4-2t_X}{r}\binom{4-2t_Y}{r}r!
(W_{YX}^{r}-W_{XY}^{r})
\phi_X^{4-2t_X-r}\phi_Y^{4-2t_Y-r}.
$$

The $r=4$, $t_X=t_Y=0$ contribution is three-loop and is outside the endpoint. The finite scalar in the undressed $\lambda v$ is inside it. The same $\mathcal D$ formula with root $\phi_X$ gives the interacting field; its two-vertex terms include the sunset and the finite tadpole insertions. With any finite root polynomial it gives the corresponding composite observable.

### The Finite Stress Contact

The remaining collision can be evaluated directly in real time. In a small neighborhood of the insertion, write $r=x-y$, $\tau=t-s$. Its singular contraction is

$$
W_\epsilon(\tau,r)=-\frac1{4\pi}
\log\!\left(r^2+(\epsilon+i\tau)^2\right),\qquad
\delta_\epsilon(r)=\frac{\epsilon}{\pi(r^2+\epsilon^2)}.
$$

At equal time, the pieces which can survive in a local contact are

$$
W_{Y\pi_X}\simeq\frac i2\delta_\epsilon(r),\qquad
W_{\pi_XY}\simeq-\frac i2\delta_\epsilon(r),\qquad
W_{Y\phi'_X}=W_{\phi'_XY}\simeq-\frac{r}{2\pi(r^2+\epsilon^2)}
+\partial_x w_{XY}.
$$

The massive remainder has no additional delta-derivative residue. Its spatial derivative is of order $r\log|r|$ and its product with $\delta_\epsilon$ tends to zero. The smooth image part is retained.

For the energy insertion the two momentum contractions have equal squares. Their difference is exactly zero before $\epsilon\to0$. The one-contraction terms, including the quadratic term of $P_4$, give

$$
L_t e_0(x)=-\partial_t v(t,x),\qquad
L_tF=\frac i\hbar[V(t),F].
$$

Thus the energy contact is the one written earlier, with no additional scalar or quadratic residue hidden in this collision.

For the current, the singular double contraction instead gives

$$
\frac{i\hbar}{2}
\left(-W_{Y\pi_X}W_{Y\phi'_X}
+W_{\pi_XY}W_{\phi'_XY}\right)_{\rm singular}
=\frac{\hbar}{8\pi}\delta_\epsilon'(r).
$$

Its normalization is fixed by $\int dr\,r\delta_\epsilon'(r)=-1$. It is not an arbitrary finite boundary parameter. The smooth image part supplies $\hbar w'(x)P_2(x)/4$ after the vertex label is integrated. This is exactly the missing image derivative in the ordinary polynomial chain rule:

$$
\partial_x\frac{P_4(x)}{24}
=\left(\frac{\phi_x^3}{6}+\frac{\hbar w_x\phi_x}{2}\right)\phi'_x
+\frac{\hbar w'_x}{4}P_2(x).
$$

Consequently the full current/vertex contact is

$$
\boxed{\quad
L_tj_0(x)=h(x)\partial_x\frac{P_4(x)}{24}
+\partial_x a(x),\qquad
a(x)=\frac{\hbar h(x)}{8\pi}P_2(x).
\quad}
$$

This calculation fixes the finite quantum correction to spatial stress. It also fixes its two-loop scalar piece $\hbar^2h w/(8\pi)$. In terms of the logarithmic stress convention above, $a$ is twice the quartic companion $\hbar h P_2/(16\pi)$. The contact calculation tells us where that correction belongs in the equal-time energy and momentum equations: subtract $\lambda a$ from the spatial stress, with no change of the canonical Hamiltonian density.

The same contact can be seen without keeping $\epsilon$. Put $u=r-\tau$, $v=r+\tau$. The singular parts of the one-vertex double-contraction integrands are

$$\begin{aligned}
\mathcal E_{\rm sing}(X,Y)
&=\frac{\hbar h(y)}{16\pi}\theta(\tau)
\bigl[\delta'(r-\tau)-\delta'(r+\tau)\bigr]P_2(Y),\\
\mathcal J_{\rm sing}(X,Y)
&=\frac{\hbar h(y)}{16\pi}\theta(\tau)
\bigl[\delta'(r-\tau)+\delta'(r+\tau)\bigr]P_2(Y).
\end{aligned}$$

For $\tau>0$, $\partial_t\mathcal E_{\rm sing}+\partial_x\mathcal J_{\rm sing}=0$. At the upper endpoint the energy's difference vanishes, while the current's sum is $2\delta'(r)$ and produces $\partial_x a$. These equations specify the collision together with its endpoint contribution. In particular, dropping a contact before taking a current to the interface would lose this term.

For two vertices, subtract these one-vertex collisions first. The simultaneous collision has four relative coordinates and at most two inverse powers from the stress derivatives; even after one divergence there is still a positive margin. Products with additional logarithms have the same conclusion. Thus there is no additional two-vertex primitive contact to choose. Contractions between two undifferentiated quartic vertices are powers of the logarithm and require no new ultraviolet counterterm in this two-dimensional reference prescription. Their finite bubble and sunset integrals remain in $R_2$; this statement does not set them to zero.

The retained stress coefficients are therefore completely specified by

$$\begin{aligned}
e_\lambda&=e_0+\lambda(R_1e_0+v)
+\lambda^2(R_2e_0+R_1v),\\
j_\lambda&=j_0+\lambda R_1j_0+\lambda^2R_2j_0,\\
T^{11}_\lambda&=p_0+\lambda(R_1p_0-v-a)
+\lambda^2(R_2p_0-R_1v-R_1a).
\end{aligned}$$

Read these equalities through two loops. In particular, writing $\alpha=W_{YX}-W_{XY}$ and $\beta=W_{YX}^2-W_{XY}^2$, the extra term needed at order $\lambda^2$ is explicitly

$$
R_1a(X)=\int_{t_0}^{t}ds\int dy\,h(x)h(y)
\left[
\frac{i\hbar}{24\pi}\alpha\phi_X\phi_Y^3
+\frac{i\hbar^2}{16\pi}\beta\phi_Y^2
+\frac{i\hbar^2w_Y}{8\pi}\alpha\phi_X\phi_Y
\right]
$$

at the retained loop orders. The scalar part of $a(X)$ commutes with the vertex. This displays the dressing of the finite stress correction; it is not enough to add its first-order value and omit $R_1a$.

### Sewing These Coefficients at the Cut

Differentiating these expressions also gives the same root derivatives, including the opposite outward derivatives at $a$. Near a collision crossing the cut, use a smooth partition $\eta+(1-\eta)=1$ on a common neighborhood before taking its sharp regional edge. The current contact gives, in particular,

$$
\partial_x(\eta a)+\partial_x((1-\eta)a)=\partial_xa.
$$

The artificial $\eta'a$ terms cancel, including the scalar part of $a$. There is no remaining delta potential, interface energy counterterm or freely adjustable interface current. The displayed light-cone delta derivatives have a specified action on the vertex label at an interior cut; the smooth time test acts on their root time. \mathcal S(e_{i,\lambda})=e_{\ell,\lambda}|_i,\qquad

Each equation holds at every retained coupling and loop degree, including its scalar coefficients. At the cut, the two outward currents cancel. The common history remains an operator. It has not been frozen when taking this limit.

The finite operation $\mathcal D$ establishes the same comparison for each polynomial generator. For ordinary products, use the usual Wick contraction between the already constructed insertions. Equality of every incoming coefficient and every contraction gives equality of every retained connected product graph, and hence of crossing commutators. Retain the free Wick factors before counting loops in a product. The external-history expansion and the actual section $B\mathcal R=1$ identify the kernel with the mismatch-slot ideal; each global polynomial recipe has a regional preimage. This proves the polynomial algebra comparison at the stated accuracy, including its stress and energy generators.

### Energy Exchange and the Actual Generator

There is a short calculation for every coefficient of the energy balance. Differentiating the ordered-time integrals gives

$$\begin{aligned}
\partial_tR_1F&=R_1\partial_tF+L_tF,\\
\partial_tR_2F&=R_2\partial_tF+R_1L_tF.
\end{aligned}$$

The contacts have just supplied $L_te_0=-\dot v$, while $L_tv=0$ since two equal-time potential polynomials commute. Use free energy conservation and the explicit stress coefficients to obtain

$$\begin{aligned}
\partial_te^{(1)}+\partial_xj^{(1)}
&=L_te_0+\dot v=0,\\
\partial_te^{(2)}+\partial_xj^{(2)}
&=R_1(L_te_0+\dot v)+L_tv=0.
\end{aligned}$$

These cancellations include the one- and two-loop terms because the contact was calculated with $P_4$, not with its classical highest-degree monomial. No integral of the sunset has been approximated in obtaining zero. The momentum calculation uses the other evaluated contact:

$$
L_tj_0-\partial_x(v+a)
=-h'(x)\frac{P_4(x)}{24}.
$$

Applying $1+\lambda R_1$ gives the momentum-work identity through $\lambda^2$. If the $a$ correction were omitted, its uncancelled residual would be $\lambda\partial_xa+\lambda^2\partial_xR_1a$.

Integrate the energy identities on the two regions. Their free physical-wall flux is zero, and the interaction is absent near those walls. The actual matched bulk-current limits therefore give

$$
\dot E_1^{(n)}=-J_{1\to2}^{(n)},\qquad
\dot E_2^{(n)}=+J_{1\to2}^{(n)},\qquad n=0,1,2,
$$

at every retained loop degree. Equivalently the prescribed-history power is $+[\dot q_i\Pi_i+\Pi_i\dot q_i]_*/2$ with the same bilinear prescription. This establishes equality of the two energy transfers, including finite contractions at the interface.

Finally compute the spatial integral, including its scalar term. The first-order coefficient obeys

$$
R_1H_0=\frac i\hbar\int_{t_0}^t ds\,[V(s),H_0]
=-\int_{t_0}^t ds\,\dot V(s)=V(t_0)-V(t).
$$

At second order integrate the later vertex time first:

$$\begin{aligned}
R_2H_0
&=-\frac i\hbar\int_{t_0}^t du\int_u^t ds\,
[V(u),\dot V(s)]\\
&=-\frac i\hbar\int_{t_0}^t du\,[V(u),V(t)-V(u)]
=-R_1V(t).
\end{aligned}$$

Hence

$$
\boxed{\quad
E_1(t)+E_2(t)
=H_0+\lambda V(t_0)
=H_\lambda
\quad}
$$

through $\lambda^2$ and two loops, with the same energy zero on both sides. The equality includes the finite $\lambda\hbar^2h w^2/8$ term in the potential. It is an equality with the spatial integral of the density whose coefficients were just sewn, not the addition of a separate energy variable. The canonical commutators with $\Phi$ and $\pi$ then show that this sum generates the same stationary interacting evolution. Ordinary finite products follow by the commutator product rule.

### Independent Conventions at This Order

The reference prescription requires the local Wick subtraction, its finite image conversion and the stress contact just calculated. Extra finite terms selected independently by the two regions must be transported with these data. They are not discarded because the reference two-vertex bulk integrals are finite.

For clarity, write the admitted local map as

$$
Z(X)=A X+\tfrac12B(X,X)+\tfrac16C(X,X,X)+\cdots,
$$

where $A=1+O(\hbar)$ is invertible and $B,C$ are symmetric multilinear maps. These letters in this paragraph label the finite map only. For the interaction-action label $\mathcal V_*=\lambda V_*$, put $\mathcal V_i=\lambda v_1+\lambda^2v_2+\cdots$. Then

$$
v_1=A^{-1}V_*,\qquad
v_2=-\tfrac12A^{-1}B(v_1,v_1).
$$

If the reference insertion is $F_*=F_0+\lambda F_1+\lambda^2F_2$, its regional label is $f_0+\lambda f_1+\lambda^2f_2$, with

$$\begin{aligned}
