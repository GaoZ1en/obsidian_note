The goal is to glue the classical observable algebras of the two halves of global AdS2, cut at the center. We will derive the regional response from the curved-space action, release the common history, and compare the resulting Poisson bracket with the independently computed global bracket. A massive example will keep the curvature-dependent propagation visible.

## Global AdS2 Example

Use the universal cover of global AdS2:

$$\begin{align}
ds^2&=\ell^2\sec^2\rho\,(-dt^2+d\rho^2),& -\frac\pi2<\rho<\frac\pi2,&\qquad t\in\mathbb R,\\
\mathcal R&=-\frac2{\ell^2}.
\end{align}$$

The time coordinate is not periodic. The two physical boundaries are at $\rho=\pm\pi/2$, and the center is $\rho=0$. Take a minimally coupled real scalar with $m>0$ and split the spatial interval into

$$\begin{align}
\Sigma_1&=(-\pi/2,0],&\Sigma_2&=[0,\pi/2).
\end{align}$$

Write $m^2\ell^2=\Delta(\Delta-1)$, with $\Delta>1$. At each physical boundary choose the fast falloff $\phi\sim(\cos\rho)^\Delta$; its coefficient can fluctuate, while the slower boundary-source term is absent. The regional action is

$$\begin{align}
S_i[\phi_i]&=-\frac12\int_{M_i}d^2x\sqrt{-g}\left(g^{\mu\nu}\partial_\mu\phi_i\partial_\nu\phi_i+m^2\phi_i^2\right)\\
&=\frac12\int dt\,d\rho\left[\dot\phi_i^2-(\partial_\rho\phi_i)^2-\Delta(\Delta-1)\sec^2\rho\,\phi_i^2\right].
\end{align}$$

The conformal factors cancel from the kinetic term, but not from the mass term. The equation is

$$\begin{align}
\mathcal L\phi_i&=0,& \mathcal L&=\partial_t^2-\partial_\rho^2+\Delta(\Delta-1)\sec^2\rho.
\end{align}$$

Thus the massive field feels a position-dependent potential. The massless choice would remove this term and reproduce the flat-strip equation, so the explicit example below uses $\Delta=2$.

At the cut prescribe $q_i(t)=\phi_i(t,0)$ and define

$$\begin{align}
\Pi_1&=\partial_\rho\phi_1|_0,&\Pi_2&=-\partial_\rho\phi_2|_0.
\end{align}$$

The action variation is

$$\begin{align}
\delta S_i&=-\int dt\,d\rho\,\mathcal L\phi_i\,\delta\phi_i +\left[\int_{\Sigma_i}d\rho\,\dot\phi_i\delta\phi_i\right]_{t_-}^{t_+} -\int dt\,\Pi_i\delta q_i.
\end{align}$$

At infinity, the omitted boundary term vanishes as $(\cos\rho)^{2\Delta-1}$. At the center the unit normal contains $1/\ell$, while the line measure contains $\ell$; they cancel. There is no extra radius factor in $\Pi_i$ as it appears in this variation.

### Regional Observable Algebra

For fixed $q_i$, let $\mathcal P_i[q_i]$ be the regional solutions with this history and the stated AdS boundary behavior. The symplectic form and Hamiltonian observables are

$$\begin{align}
\Omega_i&=\int_{\Sigma_i}d\rho\,\delta\dot\phi_i\wedge\delta\phi_i,\\
\mathcal H_i[q_i]&=\{F:\exists X_{F,i},\ X_{F,i}\mathbin{\cdot}\Omega_i=-\delta F\},\\
\{F,G\}_{i,q_i}&=X_{F,i}\mathbin{\cdot}X_{G,i}\mathbin{\cdot}\Omega_i.
\end{align}$$

Collect these into families depending on the prescribed history. Their bracket is evaluated at fixed $q_i$, so pure history functions have zero bracket. The independent product of the two regions also has zero cross bracket. To compose them, retain the regional response to changes of the history.

We use smooth smeared fields and histories and their finite sums and products. Point-field expressions below specify their smearing kernels. Sources are switched off near the temporal ends, with zero past data for retarded responses and zero future data for advanced responses. The displayed source density $f$ is integrated against $dt\,d\rho$: a covariant source $J$ in $\int\sqrt{-g}J\phi$ corresponds to $f=\ell^2\sec^2\rho\,J$.

### Algebra Gluing

Set $q_1=q_2=q$. Releasing $q$ in the summed action gives

$$\begin{align}
\Pi_1+\Pi_2&=0,&\partial_\rho\phi_1|_0&=\partial_\rho\phi_2|_0.
\end{align}$$

The field and its normal derivative now agree at the center. Joining the two fields therefore gives the uncut solution, and cutting any global solution reverses the construction. On a common time slice,

$$\begin{align}
\Omega_{\mathrm{glue}}&=\Omega_1+\Omega_2=\Omega_{\mathrm{global}}.
\end{align}$$

Let $\mathcal H_{\mathrm{com}}$ be the algebra generated by the regional smeared fields and the common history. Expressions agreeing on every matched solution represent the same glued observable:

$$\begin{align}
\mathcal I&=\{F\in\mathcal H_{\mathrm{com}}:F|_{\mathcal P_{\mathrm{glue}}}=0\},& \mathcal H_{\mathrm{glue}}&=\mathcal H_{\mathrm{com}}/\mathcal I.
\end{align}$$

This identifies observables, but their bracket must be calculated after release. We define the global algebra independently on the uncut AdS2 field, using the same smearings, including the field at the center. Its bracket comes from the uncut action.

### Regional Response and the Glued Bracket

Perturb the action by $\varepsilon G$, with

$$\begin{align}
\delta G&=\sum_i\int dt\,d\rho\,f_i\delta\phi_i+\int dt\,g\delta q.
\end{align}$$

At either causal prescription, write the response as

$$\begin{align}
\delta_G\phi_i&=G_i f_i+H_i\eta_G,\\
\delta_G\Pi_i&=\rho_i f_i+D_i\eta_G,&\eta_G&=\delta_Gq.
\end{align}$$

Here $G_i$ keeps the cut history fixed, $H_i$ propagates a change of history into the region, and $\rho_i,D_i$ give the corresponding cut flux. The perturbed interface equation is $\delta_G\Pi_1+\delta_G\Pi_2=g$. It gives

$$\begin{align}
K&=(D_1+D_2)^{-1},&\eta_G&=K\left(g-\sum_j\rho_jf_j\right),\\
\mathcal G_{ij}&=\delta_{ij}G_i-H_iK\rho_j,&\mathcal G_{iq}&=H_iK,\\
\mathcal G_{qj}&=-K\rho_j,&\mathcal G_{qq}&=K.
\end{align}$$

Each equation holds separately for $R$ and $A$. With $Z=(\phi_1,\phi_2,q)$, set

$$\begin{align}
\mathcal E&=\mathcal G^A-\mathcal G^R,& \{F,G\}_{\mathrm{glue}}&=\sum_{a,b}\int\frac{\delta F}{\delta Z_a}\mathcal E_{ab}\frac{\delta G}{\delta Z_b}.
\end{align}$$

The algebraic structure is the same as in the flat scalar example. Curvature changes the four regional responses. We now compute those responses rather than assuming that the flat kernels still apply.

### Explicit Regional Response

Fourier transform with $e^{-i\omega t}$ and use $\zeta_{R/A}=\omega\pm i0$. The radial equation away from a source is

$$\begin{align}
u''+[\zeta^2-\Delta(\Delta-1)\sec^2\rho]u&=0.
\end{align}$$

A solution with the fast left-boundary falloff is

$$\begin{align}
u(\rho)&=(\cos\rho)^\Delta\,{}_2F_1\left(\Delta-\zeta,\Delta+\zeta;\Delta+\frac12;\frac{1+\sin\rho}{2}\right).
\end{align}$$

It follows by substituting $u=(\cos\rho)^\Delta f((1+\sin\rho)/2)$ into the radial equation. Reflection gives the fast right-boundary solution $u(-\rho)$. Write $u_0=u(0)$ and $d_0=u'(0)$; explicitly,

$$\begin{align}
u_0&={}_2F_1\left(\Delta-\zeta,\Delta+\zeta;\Delta+\frac12;\frac12\right),\\
d_0&=\frac{\Delta^2-\zeta^2}{2\Delta+1}\,{}_2F_1\left(\Delta+1-\zeta,\Delta+1+\zeta;\Delta+\frac32;\frac12\right).
\end{align}$$

We also need the odd solution normalized at the center:

$$\begin{align}
s(\rho)&=\sin\rho\,(\cos\rho)^\Delta\,{}_2F_1\left(\frac{\Delta+1-\zeta}{2},\frac{\Delta+1+\zeta}{2};\frac32;\sin^2\rho\right),\\
s(0)&=0,&s'(0)&=1.
\end{align}$$

The two fixed-history kernels and the history responses are

$$\begin{align}
\widehat G_1(\rho,\rho')&=-\frac{u(\rho_<)s(\rho_>)}{u_0},&\rho,\rho'&\leq0,\\
\widehat G_2(\rho,\rho')&=\frac{s(\rho_<)u(-\rho_>)}{u_0},&\rho,\rho'&\geq0,\\
\widehat H_1(\rho)&=\frac{u(\rho)}{u_0},&\widehat H_2(\rho)&=\frac{u(-\rho)}{u_0},\\
\widehat\rho_i(\rho')&=-\widehat H_i(\rho'),&\widehat D_1=\widehat D_2&=\frac{d_0}{u_0},\\
\widehat K&=\frac{u_0}{2d_0}.
\end{align}$$

For example, $u s'-u's$ is constant and equals $u_0$ at the center. Hence $G_1$ has derivative jump $-1$ at its source, while vanishing at the cut and having the required physical-boundary falloff. Taking its outward derivative at the cut gives $-H_1$. The other formulas follow in the same way.

All products are formed at complex frequency before taking their causal limits. Removable expressions are interpreted by their limits. The inverse $K^{R/A}$ is used with the stated zero past or zero future data.

### Comparison with the Global Algebra

Independently solve the uncut source equation, with fast falloff at both AdS boundaries. A solution on the left of the source must be proportional to $u(\rho)$, and one on the right to $u(-\rho)$. Their derivative jump fixes

$$\begin{align}
\widehat G_{\mathrm{global}}(\rho,\rho')&=\frac{u(\rho_<)u(-\rho_>)}{2u_0d_0}.
\end{align}$$

No interface condition at $\rho=0$ was used in this calculation. For points in different halves, regional release gives directly

$$\begin{align}
\widehat H_1(\rho)\widehat K\widehat H_2(\rho')&=\frac{u(\rho)u(-\rho')}{2u_0d_0} =\widehat G_{\mathrm{global}}(\rho,\rho'),&\rho<0<\rho'.
\end{align}$$

For points in the left half, use

$$\begin{align}
u(-\rho)&=u(\rho)-2d_0s(\rho).
\end{align}$$

Both sides solve the radial equation and have the same value and derivative at zero. Therefore, for $\rho\leq\rho'\leq0$,

$$\begin{align}
\widehat G_1+\widehat H_1\widehat K\widehat H_1^{\mathsf T} &=-\frac{u(\rho)s(\rho')}{u_0}+\frac{u(\rho)u(\rho')}{2u_0d_0}\\
&=\frac{u(\rho)u(-\rho')}{2u_0d_0} =\widehat G_{\mathrm{global}}(\rho,\rho').
\end{align}$$

Reflection gives the right-half identity. The mixed and history blocks are

$$\begin{align}
\widehat H_i(\rho)\widehat K&=\widehat G_{\mathrm{global}}(\rho,0),\\
\widehat K\widehat H_j(\rho')&=\widehat G_{\mathrm{global}}(0,\rho'),\\
\widehat K&=\widehat G_{\mathrm{global}}(0,0).
\end{align}$$

These equalities hold separately for $R$ and $A$, and hence for every Poisson block. For assembly of the two regional fields,

$$\begin{align}
\mathrm{Ass}^*:\mathcal H_{\mathrm{global}}&\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathcal H_{\mathrm{glue}},& \mathrm{Ass}^*\{F,G\}_{\mathrm{global}}&=\{\mathrm{Ass}^*F,\mathrm{Ass}^*G\}_{\mathrm{glue}}.
\end{align}$$

Here the algebras are generated by the stated smeared observables. Global bulk integrals split into regional integrals. The source terms cancel in the advanced-minus-retarded response, so it preserves matching and annihilates expressions that vanish on all matched solutions. This makes the quotient bracket independent of the representative. Equality on the generators extends to their finite products.

### A Curvature-Dependent Crossing Bracket

For $\Delta=2$, or $m^2\ell^2=2$, the fast-boundary solution becomes elementary:

$$\begin{align}
u(\rho)&=\frac{3}{\zeta(1-\zeta^2)}\left[\zeta\cos\zeta(\rho+\pi/2)+\tan\rho\,\sin\zeta(\rho+\pi/2)\right],\\
u_0&=\frac{3\cos(\pi\zeta/2)}{1-\zeta^2},&d_0&=\frac{3\sin(\pi\zeta/2)}{\zeta},\\
\widehat D_i&=\frac{1-\zeta^2}{\zeta}\tan\frac{\pi\zeta}{2},& \widehat K&=\frac{\zeta}{2(1-\zeta^2)}\cot\frac{\pi\zeta}{2}.
\end{align}$$

Substitution checks the radial equation and $u(-\pi/2+\epsilon)\sim\epsilon^2$. The apparent singularities at $\zeta=0,\pm1$ in these expressions are removable. In particular, they do not add spurious low-frequency modes.

The fixed-history half has positive frequencies $3,5,7,\ldots$. The released $K$ supplies the center-active frequencies $2,4,6,\ldots$, and the full global kernel has frequencies $2,3,4,\ldots$. Modes which vanish at the center still occur through the regional bulk response; the center history alone is not the whole field.

We can also calculate the global bracket directly in time. Put $\tau=t-t'$ and

$$\begin{align}
Z(\tau;\rho,\rho')&=\frac{\cos\tau-\sin\rho\sin\rho'}{\cos\rho\cos\rho'}.
\end{align}$$

Before the first reflected signal arrives, the retarded kernel for $\Delta=2$ is

$$\begin{align}
G^R_{\mathrm{global}}(\tau;\rho,\rho')&=\frac12\theta(\tau-|\rho-\rho'|)Z(\tau;\rho,\rho'),\\
0<\tau&<\pi-|\rho+\rho'|.
\end{align}$$

Indeed $[\partial_\tau^2-\partial_\rho^2+2\sec^2\rho]Z=0$ away from the source and $Z=1$ on the direct lightcone. The factor $1/2$ gives the unit impulse. The advanced kernel is the time reflection. Consequently, inside that cone and before a reflected arrival,

$$\begin{align}
\{\phi(t,\rho),\phi(t',\rho')\}_{\mathrm{glue}} &=-\frac12\operatorname{sgn}(\tau)Z(\tau;\rho,\rho').
\end{align}$$

This agrees with the independently computed global bracket. The frequency formulas above also include all later reflected signals; the simple time formula here is only being used before their arrival. Its short-time normalization gives $\{\phi(t,\rho),\dot\phi(t,\rho')\}=\delta(\rho-\rho')$.

Choose

$$\begin{align}
\rho&=-\pi/6,&\rho'&=\pi/6,&\tau&=\arccos(1/4).
\end{align}$$

Then $\pi/3<\tau<\pi$, $Z=2/3$, and

$$\begin{align}
\{\phi_1(t,-\pi/6),\phi_2(t',\pi/6)\}_{\mathrm{glue}}&=-\frac13,\\
\{\phi_1(t,-\pi/6),\phi_2(t',\pi/6)\}_{\mathrm{ind}}&=0.
\end{align}$$

For a kernel source at $(t',\pi/6)$ there is no explicit history source, $g=0$, but the center still responds:

$$\begin{align}
\eta_G^R(t)&=[K^RH_2^{R\mathsf T}](t-t';\pi/6) =G^R_{\mathrm{global}}(\tau;0,\pi/6)=\frac1{4\sqrt3},\\
\eta_G^A(t)&=0.
\end{align}$$

Smooth smearings near the two events give the corresponding nonzero averaged bracket. At the center itself, the same calculation gives $E_{qq}(t,t')=-\tfrac12\operatorname{sgn}(\tau)\cos\tau$ for $0<|\tau|<\pi$, so the history-history bracket also contains the curved-space propagation.

The center is therefore removed as an artificial boundary in exactly the same way as in the flat examples. Curvature changes the response and the value of the bracket; it does not require an additional gluing variable.
