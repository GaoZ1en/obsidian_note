# Conclusion

We constructed the regional free-scalar theories from their covariant phase spaces, normalized their Neumann modes, and canonically quantized them before introducing any gluing interaction. A quadratic coupling of the two trace operators then turns the product of regional theories into a finite-dimensional Gaussian system after mode truncation.

The interval benchmark shows why the interface coupling must run with the cutoff. Matching the boundary response omitted by the regional truncation reconstructs the low joined spectrum more rapidly than a direct hard projection, and the same matched stiffness matrix reconstructs the selected cross-interface and interface-to-bulk vacuum covariances. The four-region and global $\mathrm{AdS}_2$ appendices provide compositional and curved-space checks without changing the main argument.

The established scope is the displayed free Gaussian models, isolated modes in fixed spectral windows, and the selected smeared equal-time observables. We have not proved convergence for arbitrary states or correlators, a unitary equivalence between regional and joined continuum Fock representations, a regulator-uniform ultraviolet theorem, or an interacting-QFT gluing construction.
