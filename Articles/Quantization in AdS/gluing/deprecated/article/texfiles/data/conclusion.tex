\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

The quadratic interface interaction supplies a single variational framework for gluing the examples studied here. For scalars it balances the normal derivatives and suppresses the field mismatch; for Maxwell theory the transition field dresses the tangential mismatch, preserves the independent cut gauge symmetries, and contributes essentially to the reduced CPS form. In each case the finite-coupling spectrum interpolates mode by mode between the decoupled and uncut endpoint theories. The Maxwell reduction also isolates the common holonomy pair from the local photon oscillators.

The finite-mode calculations sharpen this analytic picture. A scalar interface is a finite-rank stiffness update controlled by a boundary resolvent. Matching its omitted zero-energy response accelerates the interval and global-$\mathrm{AdS}_2$ spectra and gives a practical regulator for a nonseparable four-quadrant network. The Dirichlet interpolation shows that the cutoff generally generates every symmetry-allowed boundary quadratic form: its common and relative coefficients run independently even though the continuum path was parameterized by one $\kappa$. Maxwell theory is structurally different. Its interface term changes the kinetic metric, and exact finite-energy matching is energy dependent, so the scalar constant-coupling prescription cannot be transferred to it. Moreover, pure uncut Maxwell is obtained only in the strong limit at fixed unscaled gauge-invariant data; a CPS-normalized boundary scaling retains an additional interface scalar and defines a different limiting phase space.

Our conclusions are therefore at the level of reduced classical phase spaces, CPS-normalized mode spectra, exact finite-matrix identities, and fixed-window spectral convergence. We have not proved convergence of continuum correlators, a unitary identification of the limiting Fock representations, or an interacting gluing theorem. Natural next steps are to formulate the response matching directly for continuum Green functions and to represent the energy-dependent Maxwell correction by local auxiliary interface fields.
