\begin{abstract}

We study how a quantum field theory can be reconstructed by coupling two regional theories that have already been quantized separately. For a free scalar, a quadratic interface interaction couples the regional boundary field traces, but a finite mode truncation removes high-frequency modes that still contribute to the boundary response. We show that this missing response must be absorbed into a cutoff-dependent interface coupling. On two Neumann half intervals, the continuum finite-coupling problem is exactly solvable, and response matching at zero spatial energy improves the reconstruction of the joined low-energy spectrum from \(O(N^{-1})\) for direct hard projection to \(O(N^{-2})\) in a fixed spectral window. The same response-matched Hamiltonian also reproduces selected smeared equal-time vacuum two-point functions across the interface and in one-sided interface limits. Four-region and global AdS\(_2\) examples test composition and curvature dependence. 

\end{abstract}
