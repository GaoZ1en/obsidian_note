\begin{abstract}
We construct finite-coupling gluing interactions for free scalar and Maxwell theories on cut spatial regions. The quadratic interface interaction interpolates between decoupled region theories and the theory on the uncut geometry while preserving the covariant phase-space data required for canonical quantization. The construction is solved analytically for a scalar on a cut interval, a scalar in global $\mathrm{AdS}_2$, four scalar quadrants of a square, and Maxwell theory on a cut cylinder, with a separate boundary-penalty interpolation for Dirichlet data. We complement these solutions by a finite-mode truncation and diagonalization scheme. Scalar interfaces give finite-rank stiffness updates whose low-energy cutoff dependence is controlled by the omitted boundary resolvent, whereas the Maxwell transition field produces a generalized eigenvalue problem through a kinetic defect in the reduced symplectic form. The numerical benchmarks reproduce the analytic spectra in fixed energy windows, identify protected sectors, and show that a continuum one-parameter boundary family need not remain one-parameter after cutoff matching. The results establish finite-matrix identities and fixed-mode spectral convergence, not continuum correlator convergence or unitary equivalence of Fock representations.
\end{abstract}
