# Maxwell Gluing on an Interval Times a Circle

Consider source-free compact $U(1)$ Maxwell theory on

$$\begin{align}
[-L,L]\times S_R^1.
\end{align}$$

Unlike the $1+1$ model, this theory has one local transverse photon polarization. It also retains the axial Wilson-line/electric-flux sector found in `maxwell 1p1.md`.

## 1. Boundary problem

Use

$$\begin{align}
S=\dfrac1{2e^2}\int dt\,dx\,R,d\theta
\left(E_x^2+E_\theta^2-B^2\right),
\end{align}$$

where

$$\begin{align}
E_a&=\dot A_a-\partial_aA_0,\\
B&=\partial_xA_\theta-R^{-1}\partial_\theta A_x.
\end{align}$$

Choose relative, or perfect-conductor, outer boundary conditions

$$\begin{align}
A_0|_{x=\pm L}&=0, & A_\theta|_{x=\pm L}&=0.
\end{align}$$

Gauge parameters obey $\lambda|_{x=\pm L}=0$. These conditions make the variational problem well posed, preserve an axial open Wilson line, and give a simple self-adjoint transverse mode problem. They remove an independent constant angular holonomy. A boundary condition that leaves the outer tangential connection free would retain additional boundary charge/holonomy data and is not equivalent to the problem solved here.

## 2. Gauss law and interface data

The canonical momenta are $\Pi^a=E^a/e^2$. Variation of $A_0$ gives

$$\begin{align}
\partial_x\Pi^x+R^{-1}\partial_\theta\Pi^\theta&=0.
\end{align}$$

At the artificial cut the relevant data are:

- the tangential connection $A_\theta^\Gamma(\theta)$;
- the normal electric flux $\Pi_\Gamma^x(\theta)$;
- the interface gauge parameter $\lambda_\Gamma(\theta)$;
- the tangential electric data needed for the remaining canonical initial conditions.

Gluing identifies the two pullbacks of $A_\theta^\Gamma$, imposes continuity of the oriented normal electric flux in the absence of surface charge, and quotients the common interface gauge transformation. The normal potential $A_x$ is not itself matched as a gauge-invariant scalar. Allowing the common tangential connection to vary in the action also gives the corresponding magnetic matching condition, so the glued evolution has no artificial surface current.

In canonical terms, the regional product is reduced by the interface Gauss moment map. The relative gauge direction is removed rather than quantized as an edge photon. Charged interface matter would modify the normal-flux equation and supply genuine additional phase-space variables; none are added here.

## 3. Wilson observables

With the stated outer gauge group, the axial open Wilson line

$$\begin{align}
W_x(\theta)&=\exp\left[i\int_{-L}^{L}A_x(x,\theta)dx\right]
\end{align}$$

is gauge invariant. Its angular zero mode is conjugate to the uniform normal electric flux and gives the compact rotor sector.

Circular Wilson loops

$$\begin{align}
W_\theta(x)&=\exp\left[iR\int_0^{2\pi}A_\theta(x,\theta)d\theta\right]
\end{align}$$

are also gauge invariant. With $A_\theta=0$ at the outer boundaries, they do not supply an independent harmonic zero mode; their variation with $x$ is determined by the magnetic flux through the intervening cylinder. The interface loop is part of the tangential gluing data.

## 4. Transverse photon modes

Fourier transform along the circle and write $k_\theta=\ell/R$. After Gauss reduction, a transverse vector mode can be generated by a scalar Neumann eigenfunction $\psi(x)$:

$$\begin{align}
(A_x,A_\theta)&=\dfrac1k(ik_\theta\psi,-\partial_x\psi),\\
k^2&=k_x^2+k_\theta^2.
\end{align}$$

It is divergence free and has unit vector $L^2$ norm when $\psi$ has unit scalar norm. The outer condition $A_\theta=0$ becomes $\partial_x\psi=0$. The connected photon frequencies are therefore

$$\begin{align}
\Omega_{r\ell}^2&=\left(\dfrac{r\pi}{2L}\right)^2
+\dfrac{\ell^2}{R^2},\\
r&=0,1,2,\ldots, & \ell&\in\mathbb Z,
\end{align}$$

with $(r,\ell)=(0,0)$ excluded from the oscillator list. That excluded pair is the axial Wilson-line rotor, not a zero-frequency photon oscillator.

For $\ell\ne0$, the $r=0$ mode is a genuine transverse photon. For $\ell=0$, local photons begin at $r=1$.

## 5. Regional/global vector overlap

On a half interval let $u_n$ be normalized integer Neumann cosine modes and $f_j$ normalized half-integer cosine modes. Their scalar overlap is

$$\begin{align}
C^{\cos}_{0j}&=\sqrt2\dfrac{(-1)^{j+1}}{(j-1/2)\pi},\\
C^{\cos}_{nj}&=(-1)^{n+j}
\dfrac{2j-1}{\pi[n^2-(j-1/2)^2]},\qquad n\ge1.
\end{align}$$

The transverse-vector overlap $V^{(\ell)}$ includes both components:

$$\begin{align}
V_{nj}^{(\ell)}
&=\int_0^Ldy\,
\left(A_{x,n\ell}^*A_{x,j\ell}
+A_{\theta,n\ell}^*A_{\theta,j\ell}\right)\\
&=\dfrac{\omega_{n\ell}}{\Omega_{j\ell}}
C^{\cos}_{nj},
\end{align}$$

where

$$\begin{align}
\omega_{n\ell}^2&=(n\pi/L)^2+\ell^2/R^2,\\
\Omega_{j\ell}^2&=[(j-1/2)\pi/L]^2+\ell^2/R^2.
\end{align}$$

For $\ell=0$, take $n=1,2,\ldots$ in the photon sector. For $\ell\ne0$, include $n=0$. The script independently integrates both vector components and verifies this formula.

## 6. Finite canonical regulator

At a common radial cutoff, take the polar factor

$$\begin{align}
O_{N\ell}&=V_{N\ell}
\left[V_{N\ell}^{\mathrm T}V_{N\ell}\right]^{-1/2}.
\end{align}$$

The connected even radial tower matches a regional Neumann tower passively. The affected odd tower uses $O_{N\ell}$. In the affected/passive ordering,

$$\begin{align}
T_{N\ell}&=\operatorname{diag}(O_{N\ell},\mathbf1_N),\\
S_{N\ell}&=\operatorname{diag}
(T_{N\ell},T_{N\ell}^{-\mathrm T}).
\end{align}$$

The script verifies

$$\begin{align}
S_{N\ell}^{\mathrm T}JS_{N\ell}&=J
\end{align}$$

and exact intertwining of the finite regional and connected photon Hamiltonians. The associated Bogoliubov matrices obey the canonical identities, and the transformed global-vacuum covariance is pure.

The angular zero-mode rotor is reduced separately:

$$\begin{align}
a_x&=a_{x,1}+a_{x,2}, &
\Pi_x^{(1)}&=\Pi_x^{(2)}=\Pi_x,\\
\omega_{\mathrm{rotor}}&=\delta\Pi_x\wedge\delta a_x, &
H_{\mathrm{rotor}}&=\dfrac{e^2}2(2L)\Pi_x^2.
\end{align}$$

This agrees exactly with `maxwell 1p1.md`.

## 7. Verification and claim boundary

The script `numerics/maxwell_cylinder.wl` verifies at $N=8$ in the $\ell=0$ and $\ell=2$ channels:

- the closed transverse-vector overlap against direct high-precision integration;
- orthogonality of its polar factor;
- the channel symplectic identity and Hamiltonian intertwining;
- the Bogoliubov canonical identities and global-vacuum purity;
- exact gluing of the harmonic Wilson-line/electric-flux rotor.

Verified: the Gauss-reduced local photon spectrum, finite channel canonical quantization and harmonic flux sector for the stated outer boundary conditions.

Assumptions: relative outer boundary conditions and a global-first polar radial regulator are used; positive and negative angular channels are paired by the real-field condition.

Not verified: other electromagnetic boundary conditions, charged interface matter, a continuum Maxwell Shale--Stinespring sum, or non-Abelian gluing.
