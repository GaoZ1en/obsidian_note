## Introduction

In a nongravitational theory, a spacetime symmetry generated by a Killing vector $\xi$ gives a conserved quantity by integrating the stress current $\xi _{\nu}T^{\mu \nu}$ over a Cauchy surface. In a gravitational theory, by contrast, the corresponding conserved quantity is defined at the asymptotic boundary. The relation between these bulk and boundary descriptions is frequently used when matter is treated as a perturbative source, although the two quantities arise from apparently different constructions: one is a bulk integral of a local current, whereas the other is an asymptotic surface charge.

Reference \cite{Chen:2026bulkboundary} established this identification for generic non-electromagnetic matter perturbations on stationary asymptotically flat and asymptotically AdS backgrounds using the Wald formalism. In that setting the matter perturbation supplies the bulk stress current, and the induced first-order gravitational response supplies the boundary charge. This leaves open the perturbative order at which the gravitational fluctuation itself contributes to the conserved quantity. Once a first-order graviton is present, its quadratic stress tensor enters at the same order as a classical matter stress tensor, so the bulk current is no longer represented by the matter sector alone.

We address this problem in three-dimensional Einstein gravity with a negative cosmological constant. The background is global AdS$_3$, the metric obeys the Brown--Henneaux boundary conditions \cite{Brown:1986nw}, and the symmetry generator is an exact Killing vector of the background. We write $\kappa ^2=8\pi G$ and expand

$$\begin{align}
g_{\mu\nu} & =g^{(0)}_{\mu\nu}+\kappa h_{\mu\nu}+\kappa ^2k_{\mu\nu}+\mathcal{O}(\kappa ^3).
\end{align}$$

The matter action is allowed to be general and diffeomorphism covariant, subject to the boundary conditions stated in Sec. 1. It contains no negative powers of $\kappa$, and hence its leading classical Hilbert stress tensor is $T_{\mu\nu}=\mathcal{O}(\kappa ^0)=\mathcal{O}(G^0)$. Matter therefore first contributes at the same finite order as the quadratic graviton stress tensor.

We construct the conserved charge by applying the covariant phase-space formalism with boundaries \cite{Harlow:2019yfa} to the perturbative renormalized action. The timelike-boundary action and the corner potential are retained throughout the construction. At order $\kappa^{-1}$, the bulk current reduces on the linearized constraint surface to the usual linearized gravitational surface term. At order $\kappa^0$, it contains the sum of the matter stress current and the effective graviton stress current. The second-order Einstein equation converts this sum, together with the remaining superpotential terms, into an asymptotic surface integral. Combining it with the boundary and corner contributions gives

$$\begin{align}
H_{\xi}^{[-1]} & =H_{\xi,T}^{[-1]}, & H_{\xi}^{[0]} & =H_{\xi,T}^{[0]},
\end{align}$$

where $H_{\xi,T}$ is the background-subtracted Brown--York charge \cite{Brown:1992br}. Thus the identification between the bulk and boundary conserved quantities continues to hold when both the leading classical matter contribution and the graviton contribution are included.

The paper is organized as follows. Sec. 1 specifies the geometry, asymptotic conditions, matter assumptions, and perturbative action. Sec. 2 constructs the covariant phase-space charge and proves its coefficientwise agreement with the Brown--York charge. Appendix A collects the perturbative bulk, boundary, and corner coefficients. Appendix B proves the background-covariant current-reduction lemma used for the matter and graviton sectors, and Appendix C derives the underlying charge identity from the unexpanded theory.
