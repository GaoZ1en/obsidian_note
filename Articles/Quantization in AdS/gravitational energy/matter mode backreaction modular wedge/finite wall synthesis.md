# Transparent Finite Walls and the Modular-Wedge Area Term

## The story

The useful story is not that a brick wall *is* the RT surface.  It is that a
smooth global matter excitation may be cut by an artificial timelike wall
without changing its classical phase space, provided the excised collar is
retained as the second side of a transparent interface.  At finite cutoff,
the two sides cancel each other's variational sources and symplectic fluxes.
When the wall is fused onto the bifurcation curve, the artificial-wall
generator disappears, while the operation of removing the collar leaves a
boost corner.  That corner is the area term in

$$
\boxed{
H_{\xi_A,T}^{\infty,(2)}
=H_{\xi_A}^{\mathrm{matter},(2)}
+\frac{A_{\gamma_A}^{(2)}}{4G}
}.
\tag{0.1}
$$

Equivalently, in the modular-Hamiltonian notation,

$$
K_{A,\partial}^{(2)}
=\frac{\delta^{(2)}A_{\gamma_A}}{4G}
+K_{A,\mathrm{matter}}^{(2)}.
\tag{0.2}
$$

Throughout this project, the superscript $(2)$ denotes the
$\varepsilon^2$ Taylor coefficient.  If $\delta^{(2)}$ instead denotes the
full second derivative at $\varepsilon=0$, every quadratic term in (0.2)
acquires the same factor of two.

This mechanism is insensitive to whether the selected response is static,
time dependent, or rotating.  What does depend on the mode and cut is the
split into Brown--York, Einstein--Hilbert, descent, Hayward-joint and
Harlow--Wu endpoint pieces.  Those pieces should therefore be used as
diagnostics, not promoted individually to universal area terms.

The evidence now consists of three exact massless-scalar benchmarks:

| mode | new sector tested | smooth checks | finite-wall checks | endpoint lesson |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| complex $(n,m)=(0,0)$ | stationary diagonal response | 35 | 32 | symmetric-cut joint vanishes |
| real quadrature of $(0,0)$ | time dependence and $T_{tr}$ | 30 | 32 | general-time joint is nonzero |
| complex $(n,m)=(0,1)$ | rotation, $T_{t\phi}$ and $k_{t\phi}$ | 31 | 30 | symmetric-cut density is locally nonzero but integrates to zero |

## 1. Covariant setup

Let a diffeomorphism-covariant classical matter--gravity theory be expanded
about a background $(G,0)$ with a bifurcate Killing horizon generated by
$\xi$.  For the scalar benchmarks,

$$
g_{\mu\nu}
=G_{\mu\nu}+\kappa_{\mathrm p}^2\varepsilon^2
k_{\mu\nu}^{(2)}+O(\varepsilon^4),
\qquad
\Phi=\varepsilon\phi+O(\varepsilon^3),
\tag{1.1}
$$

and

$$
\mathcal E^{(1)}_{\mu\nu}[k^{(2)}]
=T_{\mu\nu}^{(2)},
\qquad
\nabla_\mu T^{(2)\mu\nu}=0.
\tag{1.2}
$$

The modular normalization is

$$
\kappa_\xi=2\pi,
\qquad
\kappa_{\mathrm p}^2=8\pi G.
\tag{1.3}
$$

Insert a timelike hypersurface $\mathcal H_{\chi_0}$ around the bifurcation
curve and write the original smooth spacetime as the union of the retained
wedge $M_+$ and the collar $M_-$.  Their normals point outward from their
respective regions.

## 2. Exact transparent-interface proposition

**Proposition 1 (interface insertion).**  Suppose the two sides have the
same local bulk Lagrangian and their boundary actions define canonical pairs
$(\gamma_{ab},\Pi^{ab})$ for gravity and $(\Phi,\pi)$ for matter.  On the
matching subspace

$$
\gamma_{ab}^+=\gamma_{ab}^-,
\qquad
\Pi_+^{ab}+\Pi_-^{ab}=0,
\qquad
\Phi_+=\Phi_-,
\qquad
\pi_++\pi_-=0,
\tag{2.1}
$$

the internal-wall source vanishes and the pullback of the sum of the two
symplectic currents vanishes.  Thus (2.1) is a Lagrangian correspondence and
inserting the transparent wall does not change the classical covariant
phase space of smooth configurations.

To see the first statement, the interface part of the on-shell variation is

$$
\delta I\big|_{\mathcal H}
=\int_{\mathcal H}
\left[
\frac12\Pi_+^{ab}\delta\gamma^+_{ab}
+\pi_+\delta\Phi_+
+\frac12\Pi_-^{ab}\delta\gamma^-_{ab}
+\pi_-\delta\Phi_-
\right].
\tag{2.2}
$$

Equation (2.1) makes (2.2) zero.  Antisymmetrizing a second variation gives

$$
\left.\left(\omega_++\omega_-\right)\right|_{\mathcal H}=0,
\tag{2.3}
$$

including off-diagonal metric and momentum variations.  This is an exact
algebraic statement; it uses neither stationarity nor axisymmetry.

## 3. Admissible-class wall-fusion theorem

**Theorem 2 (fixed-embedding fusion).**  Let $(\phi,k)$ belong to the smooth
weighted $\Delta=2$ class defined in `admissible scalar finite wall
theorem.md`: $z^{-2}\phi$ has bounded derivatives through order three, $k$
is Kruskal regular and Brown--Henneaux with bounded compactified derivatives,
the boundary stress is bounded, and the wall embedding and outer anchors are
fixed.  Then the transparent two-sided artificial-wall generator cancels
exactly at every finite $\chi_0$.  Removing the collar leaves the boost
corner

$$
\delta H_{\mathrm{fuse}}
=\frac{\kappa_\xi}{\kappa_{\mathrm p}^2}\,
\delta A_\gamma.
\tag{3.1}
$$

For $\kappa_\xi=2\pi$, (3.1) is $\delta A_\gamma/(4G)$.  Combining the
linearized constraint with Stokes' theorem gives the cut-independent smooth
identity

$$
\delta H_\xi^\infty
=\int_{\Sigma_A}d\Sigma_\mu\,
\xi_\nu\,\delta T^{\mu\nu}
+\frac{\kappa_\xi}{\kappa_{\mathrm p}^2}\delta A_\gamma.
\tag{3.2}
$$

For the matter-amplitude families (1.1), (3.2) is precisely (0.1) at the
$\varepsilon^2$ coefficient.

The analytic input that was conditional in the earlier proposition is now a
consequence of the admissible norm.  In regular Rindler frames,

$$
|k(e_i,e_j)|\leq C\operatorname{sech}^2u,
\qquad
|T(e_i,e_j)|\leq C\operatorname{sech}^4u.
\tag{3.3}
$$

Kruskal smoothness gives

$$
\mathfrak t_\chi
=\frac{\kappa_\xi}{2}\left[
\sinh^2\chi\,k_{00}
+(\operatorname{sech}\chi-1)k_{uu}
\right],
\tag{3.4}
$$

so the integrated source--corner transgression is $O(\chi_0^2)$.  The
collar matter energy is also $O(\chi_0^2)$.  Metric and endpoint tails are
$O(e^{-2|u|})$, scalar-stress tails are $O(e^{-4|u|})$, and both the omitted
wall tail and the outer anchor strip are $O(R^{-2})$ uniformly on a bounded
wall family.  The outer-cutoff and wall-fusion limits therefore commute in
this class.

A general cut still has a separate endpoint functional.  In the fully
Dirichlet problem it is the oriented variation

$$
\mathcal E_{\rm H}[C]
=\frac{\sigma_C}{\kappa_{\mathrm p}^2}
\int_C\left(\eta_0\,\delta\sqrt q
+\sqrt{q_0}\,\delta\eta\right),
\tag{3.5}
$$

whereas the Harlow--Wu problem uses the descent one-form
$\mathcal E_{\rm HW}[C]=\int_C\sqrt q\,\tau_\mu C_\Gamma^\mu$.  They are
not added.  On the fixed $\tau$ cuts in this project their densities are,
in the same orientation, proportional to
$\cosh\chi\,k_{\tau\chi}/\sinh\chi$ with relative factor two.

The endpoint functional corrects an endpoint-truncated **finite-wall
decomposition**.  It cancels between the two transparent sides and is
removed with the artificial interface.  It is not an additional term in the
smooth identity (3.2).  The real-quadrature general-time calculation proves
that it can be nonzero; the rotating $t=0$ example vanishes only after the
odd density is integrated.

The local origin of (3.1) is the bifurcation-surface data

$$
\xi\big|_\gamma=0,
\qquad
\nabla_\mu\xi_\nu\big|_\gamma
=\kappa_\xi\epsilon_{\mu\nu}.
\tag{3.6}
$$

The constraint potential therefore reduces to the area variation at the
inner cut.  The finite-wall variational argument says why this inner term is
the corner left by wall *removal*, rather than an uncancelled charge carried
by the artificial timelike wall.

The theorem applies to the declared smooth weighted class.  Extending it to
the full finite-energy Hilbert completion would require a separate
continuity and existence theorem for the sourced response map.

## 4. Universal structure versus mode-dependent diagnostics

The theorem and the three benchmarks establish the following separation.

### Structural

- transparent matter and gravitational source cancellation;
- transparent symplectic-flux cancellation;
- the local sourced Noether--Stokes identity;
- disappearance of the complete artificial-wall generator in the fused
  limit;
- survival of the separate boost corner as the RT-area response;
- vanishing collar and anchor-strip corrections uniformly in either order
  of limits.

### Not structural

- separate vanishing of the Brown--York, Einstein--Hilbert or descent
  densities;
- pointwise vanishing of the wall--cut joint;
- vanishing of the integrated joint away from a symmetric cut;
- equality of the Hayward and Harlow--Wu coefficients;
- stationarity, diagonality, or $k_{t\phi}=0$.

For the real quadrature and rotating mode the fused generator illustrates
the generic pattern

$$
\mathcal H_{\mathrm{BY}}\to0,
\qquad
\mathcal H_{\mathrm{EH}}\to-\kappa_\xi\ell_0,
\qquad
\mathcal H_{\mathrm{descent}}\to+\kappa_\xi\ell_0,
\tag{4.1}
$$

where $\ell_0$ is the first variation of the fused cut's area density.  The
original stationary diagonal mode happens to make all three displayed
pieces vanish separately at finite wall position; that extra zero is not
part of Theorem 2.

The endpoint lesson is equally sharp.  On a general cut the Hayward density
can have a nonzero integral.  Even on the symmetric cut, rotation can make
it locally nonzero while parity forces its integral to vanish.  The
Harlow--Wu fixed-cut coefficient is one half of the same oriented linear
normal-product density in these conventions, but it belongs to a different
variational problem.  The two are compared, not summed.

## 5. Manuscript spine

The article can be organized around one question:

> How does the RT area term arise when a smooth global matter excitation is
> represented using a finite timelike regulator wall?

A compact main-text chain is:

1. derive the sourced covariant Noether--Stokes identity and the smooth
   outer--matter--area balance;
2. show that a single-sided Dirichlet wall removes the amplitude variation,
   motivating the two-sided transparent correspondence;
3. prove Proposition 1 covariantly;
4. distinguish the finite artificial-wall generator from the wall-removal
   boost corner and state Theorem 2;
5. use the stationary, real-time-dependent and rotating scalar modes as
   increasingly discriminating tests;
6. end with the exact claim boundary: a classical fixed-embedding regulator
   result, not factorization or quantum JLMS.

The stationary mode can carry the detailed derivation in the main text.  The
real and rotating modes should appear as a compact robustness section or
appendix table, emphasizing the failures of accidental zeros rather than
repeating every integral.

  symplectic two-form, including off-diagonal data;

  three selected matter families;

## Assumptions

- classical local Einstein gravity with minimally coupled scalar matter;
- perturbation theory through the first matter-sourced metric response;
- a fixed interval, fixed wall embedding and fixed outer anchors;
- a smooth two-sided continuation across the transparent interface;
- regular centre, normalizable asymptotics, fixed boundary frame and no
  added homogeneous vacuum graviton in the selected response;
- a bounded modular-time band and finite admissible norm;
- the differentiable boundary and joint actions stated in the individual
  benchmarks.

## Not verified

- existence and continuity of the sourced response map on the full
  finite-energy scalar Hilbert completion;
- arbitrary matter theories, higher-curvature gravity or alternative
  boundary conditions;
- moving embeddings, simultaneous wall-shape variations or a generic
  nonorthogonal-corner phase space;
- a single-sided reflecting polarization containing the global modular
  orbit;
- sharp-wedge Hilbert-space factorization, matter loops, bulk entropy,
  graviton entropy or quantum JLMS.

## Claim tier

The achieved result is a **classical admissible-class fixed-embedding
finite-wall theorem** with exact transparent insertion, uniform regulator
estimates, commuting outer/wall limits and an explicit general-cut endpoint
functional.  It is stronger than a mode-verified conditional fusion claim
and weaker than a theorem on the full finite-energy Hilbert completion, a
moving-edge phase space or a quantum modular-Hamiltonian identity.
