## Finite-action variations and surface descent

### Bulk potential and timelike GHY identity

With signature $(-,+,+)$ and unit AdS radius, define

$$\begin{align}
\mathcal E_{\mu\nu}
:=R_{\mu\nu}-\frac12Rg_{\mu\nu}-g_{\mu\nu}.
\end{align}$$

The bulk variation is

$$\begin{align}
\delta S_{\rm bulk}
=-\frac1{2\kappa_{\rm p}^{2}}
\int_M\sqrt{-g}\,\mathcal E^{\mu\nu}\delta g_{\mu\nu}
+\int_{\partial M}\Theta_{\rm bulk}[g;\delta g],
\end{align}$$

where

$$\begin{align}
\Theta_{\rm bulk}
=\iota_n\boldsymbol\epsilon\,
n_\mu\vartheta^\mu,
\qquad
\vartheta^\mu
=\frac1{2\kappa_{\rm p}^{2}}
\left(\nabla_\nu\delta g^{\mu\nu}-\nabla^\mu\delta g\right).
\end{align}$$

For a timelike boundary with $K_{ij}=\gamma_i{}^\mu\gamma_j{}^\nu\nabla_\mu n_\nu$, the GHY variation gives

$$\begin{align}
\left.(\Theta_{\rm bulk}+\delta\ell_{\rm GHY})\right|_\Gamma
=-\frac12\Pi^{ij}\delta\gamma_{ij}+\mathrm dC_\Gamma .
\end{align}$$

The counterterm changes $\Pi^{ij}$ algebraically but produces no new normal derivative of $\delta g$.  For the outer AdS$_3$ cylinder,

$$\begin{align}
\Pi^{ij}_{R}
=\frac{\sqrt{-\gamma}}{\kappa_{\rm p}^{2}}
\left(K^{ij}-K\gamma^{ij}+\gamma^{ij}\right),
\end{align}$$

whereas the inner wall has no $+\gamma^{ij}$ counterterm contribution.

The endpoint one-form can be written covariantly as

$$\begin{align}
C_\Gamma[\delta g]
=-\frac1{2\kappa_{\rm p}^{2}}
\int_{\partial\Sigma}\sqrt q\,
\tau_\mu\gamma^{\mu\nu}n^\rho\delta g_{\nu\rho}.
\end{align}$$

This expression vanishes in Gaussian radial gauge, but the Brown--York source $-\Pi^{ij}\delta\gamma_{ij}/2$ need not.

### Boundary descent of a diffeomorphism

For an arbitrary spacetime vector $u$, the boundary contribution to the finite-action charge is generated by

$$\begin{align}
\mu_u^\mu
=\frac1{\kappa_{\rm p}^{2}}
\left[(K-1)\gamma^\mu{}_\nu u^\nu
-D^\mu(n_\nu u^\nu)\right]
\end{align}$$

on the renormalized outer cylinder; the $-1$ is omitted at an unrenormalized inner wall.  The cut contribution is the sum of the bulk surface form and

$$\begin{align}
\mathscr H_u
=\int_C\sqrt q\,\tau_\mu
\left(\mu_u^\mu-C_\Gamma^\mu[\mathcal L_ug]\right).
\end{align}$$

For a background Killing field $\xi$, the bulk first-order surface form is

$$\begin{align}
S_\xi^{\mu\nu}[h]
=\xi_\rho\nabla^{[\mu}h^{\nu]\rho}
+\frac12h\nabla^{[\mu}\xi^{\nu]}
-h^{\rho[\mu}\nabla_\rho\xi^{\nu]}.
\end{align}$$

At quadratic order the pure-$h$ current has the off-shell structure

$$\begin{align}
\mathcal H_{\xi,h}^{\mu}
=\xi_\nu T_h^{\mu\nu}
+\nabla_\nu S_{\xi,h}^{\mu\nu}
+\mathcal R_{\xi,h}^{\mu},
\end{align}$$

and the $k$-linear current satisfies

$$\begin{align}
\xi_\nu\mathcal E^{(1),\mu\nu}[k]
+\nabla_\nu S_{\xi,k}^{\mu\nu}[k]
=\nabla_\nu S_\xi^{\mu\nu}[k].
\end{align}$$

On the second-order constraint surface these relations combine the bulk stress current and both superpotentials into the cut expression used in Section \ref{sec:finite-action-cps}.

### Shape variation of the wall

For completeness, the shape derivative of the induced metric is

$$\begin{align}
\Delta_X\gamma_{ij}
=\delta\gamma_{ij}
+2D_{(i}\chi_{j)}+2K_{ij}\chi_\perp .
\end{align}$$

Substitution into $-\Pi^{ij}\Delta_X\gamma_{ij}/2$ and integration by parts gives

$$\begin{align}
\mathcal B_X
=-\frac12\Pi^{ij}\delta\gamma_{ij}
+(D_i\Pi^i{}_j)\chi^j
-\Pi^{ij}K_{ij}\chi_\perp
-D_i(\Pi^i{}_j\chi^j).
\end{align}$$

The last term is the wall-endpoint descent.  Varying the bulk domain contributes

$$\begin{align}
-\frac{\sqrt{-\gamma}}{\kappa_{\rm p}^{2}}
n_\mu\mathcal E^{\mu\nu}
(e_{j\nu}\chi^j+n_\nu\chi_\perp),
\end{align}$$

which yields the total momenta (\ref{eq:tangential-momentum}) and (\ref{eq:normal-momentum}).  This derivation shows explicitly why Brown--York momentum alone is not the off-shell embedding momentum.

### Joint variation

At a non-null joint, the Dirichlet corner variation can be organized as

$$\begin{align}
\delta S_J
=\frac{\sigma_J}{\kappa_{\rm p}^{2}}
\int_J\left(\eta\,\delta\sqrt q+\sqrt q\,\delta\eta\right).
\end{align}$$

After the adjacent GHY variations cancel the unwanted normal-variation term, the remaining endpoint potential may be chosen as

$$\begin{align}
\Theta_J
=\frac{\sigma_J}{\kappa_{\rm p}^{2}}
\int_J\eta\,\Delta_X\sqrt q,
\end{align}$$

up to a field-space exact one-form.  Its variation is the area--boost symplectic form (\ref{eq:hayward-pair}).
