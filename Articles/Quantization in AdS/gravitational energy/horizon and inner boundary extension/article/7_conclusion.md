## Discussion

We have extended the finite-action CPS construction of gravitational energy from a complete asymptotically AdS$_3$ Cauchy surface to a partial surface ending on a compact horizon or an anchored RT geodesic.  The extension keeps the same off-shell generator, action normalization, perturbative expansion, and asymptotic Brown--York matching as the parent construction.  Its new content is the inner boundary: the GHY descent, Brown--York source work, Harlow--Wu endpoint potential, Hayward joint, and embedding variation are all retained until the variational problem or regulator estimate removes them.

The result has three complementary formulations.  In a fixed-surface Hollands--Wald representative, the horizon Noether charge equals the quadratic extremal area through second order and the remaining bulk term is ordinary canonical energy.  In an arbitrary metric representative, the same quantity is canonical energy plus the finite-action horizon cocycle $\int\Upsilon_{\rm p}$.  In the moving-embedding extended presymplectic space, these two descriptions are sections related by $(\mathcal L_vg,-v)$ for a proper, source-preserving vector.  The cocycle is section dependent; only the completed energy is invariant.  This shows why the corner completion is required without promoting an auxiliary finite-rank symplectic cancellation to a universal physical edge mode or confusing the pullback construction with existence of a Hamiltonian polarization.

For the linear Brown--Henneaux sector, all regulator layers can be controlled.  The compact BTZ calculation fixes the inner orientation, source-to-corner transgression, and canonical-energy normalization.  In AdS-Rindler, the proved linked class $\alpha>7/2$, $\beta=\alpha$, $\gamma>2\alpha+3$ gives uniform bounds on the finite-action kernels and is independent of the schedule and taper inside the stated majorant class.  Independent $\beta$ remains a two-parameter wall-kernel problem.  The apparent unprojected anchor pole is removed by choosing an endpoint-fixing representative modulo the exact global AdS Killing fields.  The resulting theorem applies to the full metric tangent quotient $H^\sigma(S^1)/\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb R)$ for $\sigma>5/2$, not merely to a hand-selected finite mode block.

The completed energy is the Hessian of a classical Bañados/Schwarzian functional.  Its positive square representation identifies the exact global-Killing kernel and gives the unique closed extension to the endpoint-fixed $H^2$ section.  The same analysis shows that $H^2$ is optimal in the ordinary Sobolev scale and that the form is not coercive because energy can concentrate near an anchor.

Several stronger statements remain open but are not required for the theorem.  We have not constructed a single polarization containing simultaneously an arbitrary time-dependent nonuniform wall, a tilted joint, and all Brown--Henneaux modes.  The infinite-mode Hollands--Wald extension is a fixed-width weak Sobolev collar, not a $C^\infty$ Gaussian-null collar.  We have also not extended the theorem to finite-amplitude Bañados coadjoint orbits or to generic nonlinear horizon geometries.

The $D\geq4$ local-Rindler tensor test isolates the propagating-graviton issue.  On compact boost-time slabs its complete Einstein--GHY wall flux is $O(\epsilon)$ and no Harlow--Wu or orthogonal-joint term survives.  On slabs that approach separated null-horizon cuts, the same limit is the generally nonzero radiative null flux.  Hence the high-dimensional result is a diagnostic: HW gauge plus smoothness is compatible with a no-extra-wall-term limit only after the slab and boundary polarization are specified.  It is not a $d>2$ analogue of the Brown--Henneaux theorem.

Most importantly, the result is classical.  It supplies an action-derived and slice-covariant version of the quadratic gravitational subregion identity that underlies graviton JLMS, but it does not construct bulk or boundary von Neumann algebras, modular crossed products, graviton entropies, or quantum error-correcting maps.  Those structures require additional quantum input that is independent of the classical finite-action completion proved here.
