# Second-order bulk-boundary charge matching in AdS$_3$

## Abstract

We study three-dimensional Einstein gravity with negative cosmological constant coupled to classical matter. The metric is expanded as

$$\begin{align}
g_{\mu\nu}=g^{(0)}_{\mu\nu}+\kappa h_{\mu\nu}+\kappa^2 k_{\mu\nu}+\kappa^3p_{\mu\nu}+O(\kappa^4), \qquad \kappa^2=8\pi G,
\end{align}$$

and the matter stress tensor is counted as $T_{\mu\nu}=O(G^0)$. We derive the perturbative action through $O(\kappa)$ because the inhomogeneous part of a diffeomorphism lowers the perturbative order by one. The Noether charge is defined directly by

$$\begin{align}
H_\xi=X_\xi\mathbin{\cdot}\theta-\alpha_\xi .
\end{align}$$

The symmetry calculation is performed off shell. The Einstein and matter equations are imposed only when the bulk current is converted into a surface charge. For Brown--Henneaux boundary conditions, fixed normalizable matter data with no independent matter surface charge, and a charge generated by a background Killing field, the relative Noether charge and the Brown--York charge obey

$$\begin{align}
H_\xi^{[-1]}=H_{\xi,T}^{[-1]}, \qquad H_\xi^{[0]}=H_{\xi,T}^{[0]}.
\end{align}$$

The finite contribution contains both the classical gravitational self-stress and the matter stress tensor. The timelike-boundary and corner contribution is obtained by varying the Gibbons--Hawking--York term and the AdS counterterm in the same renormalized action.

## 1. Introduction

A conserved bulk stress current and an asymptotic gravitational charge describe the same conservation law in two different forms. At first order, this identification follows from the linearized Einstein equation and can be established without solving the perturbation explicitly. Chen, Gao, Lan, Wu, and Zhang proved such a first-order identification for general non-electromagnetic matter on stationary asymptotically flat and asymptotically AdS backgrounds in [Identification between the bulk and boundary conserved quantities](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.24932v2).

The purpose of this work is to give an AdS$_3$ second-order completion in classical Einstein gravity. The second-order source has two parts. The first is the effective stress tensor generated by the first-order metric perturbation. The second is a classical matter stress tensor whose leading order is $G^0$. In this form, the bulk source is

$$\begin{align}
T_{\mathrm{eff}}^{\mu\nu}=T_{(h)}^{\mu\nu}+T^{\mu\nu}, \qquad T_{(h)}^{\mu\nu}:=-\mathcal E^{(2)\mu\nu}[h,h]. \tag{1.1}
\end{align}$$

Here $\mathcal E^{(2)}[h,h]$ is the quadratic coefficient of the cosmological Einstein tensor. The matter sector is represented by its Hilbert stress tensor under the boundary assumptions stated in Sec. 2.2.

There are two points that enter at second order. First, the perturbative action must be retained through $O(\kappa)$. The diffeomorphism vector on perturbative field space contains a term of order $\kappa^{-1}$, so its contraction with the $O(\kappa)$ symplectic potential contributes to the finite Noether current. Second, the charge of the renormalized theory contains the corner term induced by the timelike-boundary action. This term is already fixed once the Gibbons--Hawking--York term and the AdS counterterm have been chosen.

Our result applies to Einstein gravity in AdS$_3$ with Brown--Henneaux metric falloffs and matter boundary conditions with fixed sources and vanishing symplectic flux. The exact surface-current relation holds for an allowed Brown--Henneaux asymptotic symmetry. The interpretation as a conserved bulk stress charge uses the background-Killing subalgebra. Matter sectors carrying an independent asymptotic gauge charge require the corresponding surface contribution in addition to the stress tensor term. Within the stated class, the background-subtracted charge has the expansion

$$\begin{align}
H_\xi=\frac{1}{\kappa}H_\xi^{[-1]}+H_\xi^{[0]}+O(\kappa), \qquad H_{\xi,T}=\frac{1}{\kappa}H_{\xi,T}^{[-1]}+H_{\xi,T}^{[0]}+O(\kappa), \tag{1.2}
\end{align}$$

and we prove

$$\begin{align}
H_\xi^{[-1]}=H_{\xi,T}^{[-1]}, \qquad H_\xi^{[0]}=H_{\xi,T}^{[0]}. \tag{1.3}
\end{align}$$

The proof uses the second-order constraint and the exact renormalized boundary current, with $k_{\mu\nu}$ kept implicit.

The analysis is classical. Section 2 fixes the geometry, action, boundary conditions, and perturbative expansion. Section 3 gives the first variation, the symplectic structure, the off-shell Noether construction, and the order-by-order matching. Appendix A records the perturbative formulas. Appendix B checks the cutoff action, variational principle, symplectic flux, and charge finiteness.

## 2. Setup

### 2.1 Spacetime and boundary geometry

We use unit-radius global AdS$_3$,

$$\begin{align}
\mathrm ds_0^2=-(1+r^2)\mathrm dt^2+\frac{\mathrm dr^2}{1+r^2}+r^2\mathrm d\phi^2, \qquad \phi\sim\phi+2\pi . \tag{2.1}
\end{align}$$

Its curvature is

$$\begin{align}
R^{(0)}_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma} =-\left(g^{(0)}_{\mu\rho}g^{(0)}_{\nu\sigma} -g^{(0)}_{\mu\sigma}g^{(0)}_{\nu\rho}\right), \qquad R^{(0)}_{\mu\nu}=-2g^{(0)}_{\mu\nu}, \qquad R^{(0)}=-6. \tag{2.2}
\end{align}$$

The regulated spacetime $M_R$ is bounded by two Cauchy surfaces $\Sigma_i$ and $\Sigma_f$ and by the timelike cylinder $\Gamma_R$ at $r=R$. The future-pointing unit normal to $\Sigma$ is $\tau^\mu$, and the outward unit normal to $\Gamma_R$ is $n^\mu$. We write

$$\begin{align}
\sigma_{\mu\nu}=g_{\mu\nu}+\tau_\mu\tau_\nu, \qquad \gamma_{\mu\nu}=g_{\mu\nu}-n_\mu n_\nu, \qquad q_{\mu\nu}=g_{\mu\nu}+\tau_\mu\tau_\nu-n_\mu n_\nu \tag{2.3}
\end{align}$$

for the metrics induced on $\Sigma$, $\Gamma_R$, and the corner $C_R=\partial\Sigma$, respectively. The extrinsic curvature of $\Gamma_R$ is

$$\begin{align}
K_{\mu\nu}=\gamma_\mu{}^\rho\gamma_\nu{}^\sigma\nabla_\rho n_\sigma, \qquad K=\gamma^{\mu\nu}K_{\mu\nu}. \tag{2.4}
\end{align}$$

The vector $\xi$ is an allowed Brown--Henneaux asymptotic symmetry. It need not be an exact Killing vector in the off-shell construction of Secs. 3.1--3.3. In Sec. 3.4 we restrict to the background-Killing subalgebra when converting the effective stress current into a conserved surface charge. The formulas below keep $n\mathbin{\cdot}\xi$ explicit, which is useful at a finite cutoff.

### 2.2 Full theory and asymptotic conditions

The renormalized action is

$$\begin{align}
S_{\mathrm{ren}}[g,\Psi] =\frac{1}{2\kappa^2}\int_M\!\epsilon_g\,(R+2) +S_{\mathrm m}[g,\Psi] +\frac{1}{\kappa^2}\int_\Gamma\!\epsilon_\gamma\,(K-1). \tag{2.5}
\end{align}$$

The counterterm is sufficient for the flat conformal cylinder used here. Matter counterterms, when required by a particular model, are included in $S_{\mathrm m}$.

We define the Hilbert stress tensor and the matter symplectic potential by

$$\begin{align}
\delta S_{\mathrm m} =\frac12\int_M\!\epsilon_g\,T^{\mu\nu}\delta g_{\mu\nu} +\int_M\!\epsilon_g\,E_A\delta\Psi^A +\int_{\partial M}\!\boldsymbol\Theta_{\mathrm m}[\delta\Psi,\delta g]. \tag{2.6}
\end{align}$$

The matter sector is assumed to satisfy the following conditions.

1. It is diffeomorphism covariant and has a well-defined Hilbert stress tensor.
2. Its leading classical stress tensor obeys

$$\begin{align}
T_{\mu\nu}=O(G^0), \qquad \nabla^{(0)}_\mu T^{\mu\nu}=0 \tag{2.7}
\end{align}$$

   on the leading matter equation.
3. The non-normalizable matter sources are fixed. The matter symplectic flux through $\Gamma$ vanishes.
4. The matter fields have compact support or sufficient normalizable decay, and their diffeomorphism current has no independent asymptotic surface charge. In particular, there is no unscreened gauge charge at infinity.

Under these conditions, the matter contribution relevant to the matching is represented by $\xi_\nu T^{\mu\nu}$. Terms proportional to $E_A$ vanish when the matter equation is imposed in Sec. 3.4, and any matter superpotential has zero integral at $C_R$.

Each metric coefficient $h_{\mu\nu}$, $k_{\mu\nu}$, and $p_{\mu\nu}$ obeys the Brown--Henneaux falloffs

$$
\begin{array}{lll}
u_{tt}=O(1), & u_{t\phi}=O(1), & u_{\phi\phi}=O(1),\\[2mm]
u_{tr}=O(r^{-3}), & u_{r\phi}=O(r^{-3}), & u_{rr}=O(r^{-4}),
\end{array}
\qquad u\in\{h,k,p\},
\tag{2.8}
$$

and allowed variations obey the same falloffs.

The Brown--York tensor in our normalization is

$$\begin{align}
\mathcal T_{ab} =\frac{1}{\kappa^2}\left(K_{ab}-K\gamma_{ab}+\gamma_{ab}\right), \tag{2.9}
\end{align}$$

and its charge is

$$\begin{align}
H_{\xi,T}[g] =-\lim_{R\to\infty}\int_{C_R}\!\epsilon_C\, \tau^a\xi^b\mathcal T_{ab}. \tag{2.10}
\end{align}$$

All charges in this paper are relative to global AdS$_3$,

$$\begin{align}
H_\xi^{\mathrm{rel}}[g;g^{(0)}] :=H_\xi[g]-H_\xi[g^{(0)}], \qquad H_{\xi,T}^{\mathrm{rel}}[g;g^{(0)}] :=H_{\xi,T}[g]-H_{\xi,T}[g^{(0)}]. \tag{2.11}
\end{align}$$

We suppress the superscript ``rel'' below. This prescription removes the $\kappa^{-2}$ vacuum coefficient on both sides.

### 2.3 Perturbative action and equations

We expand the metric as

$$\begin{align}
g_{\mu\nu}=g^{(0)}_{\mu\nu} +\kappa h_{\mu\nu} +\kappa^2k_{\mu\nu} +\kappa^3p_{\mu\nu} +O(\kappa^4). \tag{2.12}
\end{align}$$

All indices in perturbative coefficients are moved with $g^{(0)}$, and $\nabla^{(0)}$ is the background connection. Define

$$\begin{align}
\mathcal E_{\mu\nu}[g] :=R_{\mu\nu}-\frac12g_{\mu\nu}R-g_{\mu\nu}. \tag{2.13}
\end{align}$$

The metric equation following from (2.5) is

$$\begin{align}
\mathcal E_{\mu\nu}[g]=\kappa^2T_{\mu\nu}. \tag{2.14}
\end{align}$$

For the gravitational density, write

$$\begin{align}
\frac{\sqrt{-g}}{\sqrt{-g^{(0)}}}(R+2) =\mathcal L_g^{(0)} +\kappa\mathcal L_g^{(1)}[h] +\kappa^2\left(\mathcal L_g^{(1)}[k]+\mathcal L_g^{(2)}[h,h]\right) +\kappa^3\left(\mathcal L_g^{(1)}[p]+2\mathcal L_g^{(2)}[h,k]+\mathcal L_g^{(3)}[h,h,h]\right) +O(\kappa^4). \tag{2.15}
\end{align}$$

The quadratic polarization is

$$\begin{align}
\mathcal L_g^{(2)}[h,k] :=\frac12\left(\mathcal L_g^{(2)}[h+k,h+k] -\mathcal L_g^{(2)}[h,h] -\mathcal L_g^{(2)}[k,k]\right). \tag{2.16}
\end{align}$$

Explicit expressions and a compact construction of $\mathcal L_g^{(3)}$ are given in Appendix A. The matter action has the metric expansion

$$\begin{align}
S_{\mathrm m}[g,\Psi] =S_{\mathrm m}^{[0]}[g^{(0)},\Psi] +\frac{\kappa}{2}\int_M\!\epsilon_0\,h_{\mu\nu}T^{\mu\nu} +\kappa S_{\mathrm m,field}^{[1]} +O(\kappa^2), \tag{2.17}
\end{align}$$

up to a matter boundary term whose integral vanishes under the conditions in Sec. 2.2. The term $S_{\mathrm m,field}^{[1]}$ contains the first correction of the matter fields and is inert under the inhomogeneous metric variation introduced in Sec. 3.2.

The bulk action therefore has the ordered form

$$\begin{align}
S_{\mathrm{bulk}} =\frac{1}{\kappa^2}S^{[-2]} +\frac{1}{\kappa}S^{[-1]} +S^{[0]} +\kappa S^{[1]} +O(\kappa^2), \tag{2.18}
\end{align}$$

where

$$
\begin{aligned}
S^{[-2]}&=\frac12\int_M\!\epsilon_0\,\mathcal L_g^{(0)},\\
S^{[-1]}&=\frac12\int_M\!\epsilon_0\,\mathcal L_g^{(1)}[h],\\
S^{[0]}&=\frac12\int_M\!\epsilon_0\left(\mathcal L_g^{(1)}[k]+\mathcal L_g^{(2)}[h,h]\right)+S_{\mathrm m}^{[0]},\\
S^{[1]}&=\frac12\int_M\!\epsilon_0\left(\mathcal L_g^{(1)}[p]+2\mathcal L_g^{(2)}[h,k]+\mathcal L_g^{(3)}[h,h,h]\right)
+\frac12\int_M\!\epsilon_0\,h_{\mu\nu}T^{\mu\nu}
+S_{\mathrm m,field}^{[1]}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{2.19}
$$

Varying $S^{[1]}$ with respect to $h_{\mu\nu}$ gives

$$\begin{align}
E_h^{[1]\mu\nu} =-\frac12\left( \mathcal E^{(1)\mu\nu}[k] +\mathcal E^{(2)\mu\nu}[h,h] -T^{\mu\nu} \right). \tag{2.20}
\end{align}$$

This coefficient follows directly by expanding the full first variation of (2.5) with $\delta g_{\mu\nu}=\kappa\delta h_{\mu\nu}$. The $O(\kappa)$ coefficient on the left is the variation of $S^{[1]}$, while the matter coupling in (2.17) supplies $T^{\mu\nu}/2$.

Expanding (2.14) gives the first two metric equations,

$$\begin{align}
\mathcal E^{(1)}_{\mu\nu}[h]=0, \qquad \mathcal E^{(1)}_{\mu\nu}[k] +\mathcal E^{(2)}_{\mu\nu}[h,h] =T_{\mu\nu}. \tag{2.21}
\end{align}$$

Equivalently,

$$\begin{align}
\mathcal E^{(1)}_{\mu\nu}[k] =T_{(h)\mu\nu}+T_{\mu\nu}. \tag{2.22}
\end{align}$$

Equations (2.21) and the matter equations will be listed as constraints until Sec. 3.4. They are not used in the construction of the symmetry charge.

## 3. Covariant phase space and charge matching

### 3.1 First variation and symplectic form

The bulk first variation is written as

$$\begin{align}
\delta S_{\mathrm{bulk}} =\int_M\!\epsilon_g\left[ -\frac{1}{2\kappa^2} \left(\mathcal E^{\mu\nu}-\kappa^2T^{\mu\nu}\right)\delta g_{\mu\nu} +E_A\delta\Psi^A \right] +\int_{\partial M}\left(\boldsymbol\Theta_{\mathrm{EH}}+\boldsymbol\Theta_{\mathrm m}\right). \tag{3.1}
\end{align}$$

We use the orientation convention in which the Einstein--Hilbert potential, expressed relative to the background volume density, is

$$\begin{align}
\Theta_{\mathrm{EH}}^\mu[g;\delta g] =-\frac{1}{2\kappa^2}\frac{\sqrt{-g}}{\sqrt{-g^{(0)}}} \left( g^{\alpha\beta}\delta\Gamma^\mu{}_{\alpha\beta} -g^{\mu\alpha}\delta\Gamma^\beta{}_{\alpha\beta} \right). \tag{3.2}
\end{align}$$

At the timelike boundary, the variation of the boundary Lagrangian combines with (3.2) as

$$\begin{align}
\left.\boldsymbol\Theta_{\mathrm{EH}}\right|_\Gamma +\delta\boldsymbol\ell_\Gamma =\mathrm d\boldsymbol C_\Gamma -\frac12\mathcal T^{ab}\delta\gamma_{ab}\,\boldsymbol\epsilon_\Gamma, \qquad \boldsymbol\ell_\Gamma=\frac{1}{\kappa^2}(K-1)\boldsymbol\epsilon_\Gamma, \tag{3.3}
\end{align}$$

with corner potential

$$\begin{align}
\boldsymbol C_\Gamma =-\frac{1}{2\kappa^2} \gamma^{\mu\nu}n^\rho\delta g_{\nu\rho}\, \epsilon^\Gamma_{\mu\mu_1}\mathrm dx^{\mu_1}. \tag{3.4}
\end{align}$$

For fixed boundary sources, the renormalized Cauchy-surface potential is

$$\begin{align}
\theta_\Sigma =\int_\Sigma\left(\boldsymbol\Theta_{\mathrm{EH}}+\boldsymbol\Theta_{\mathrm m}\right) -\int_{C_R}\boldsymbol C_\Gamma . \tag{3.5}
\end{align}$$

Inserting (2.12) into (3.2) gives

$$\begin{align}
\Theta_{\mathrm{EH}}^\mu =\frac{1}{\kappa}\Theta^{[-1]\mu}[\delta h] +\Theta^{[0]\mu}[h;\delta h,\delta k] +\kappa\Theta^{[1]\mu}[h,k;\delta h,\delta k,\delta p] +O(\kappa^2), \tag{3.6}
\end{align}$$

where

$$\begin{align}
\Theta^{[-1]\mu}[\delta h] =-\frac12\left( \nabla^{(0)}_\nu\delta h^{\mu\nu} -\nabla^{(0)\mu}\delta h \right). \tag{3.7}
\end{align}$$

For a symmetric covariant tensor $u_{\mu\nu}$, define

$$\begin{align}
V_g^\mu[u] :=g^{\mu\alpha}g^{\nu\beta}\nabla_\nu u_{\alpha\beta} -g^{\mu\nu}\nabla_\nu\left(g^{\alpha\beta}u_{\alpha\beta}\right). \tag{3.7a}
\end{align}$$

If $\delta_hV^\mu[u]$ denotes its first metric variation at $g^{(0)}$ in the direction $h$, with $u$ held fixed, then the finite gravitational coefficient is

$$\begin{align}
\Theta^{[0]\mu}[h;\delta h,\delta k] =-\frac12\left[ V_{g^{(0)}}^\mu[\delta k] +\delta_hV^\mu[\delta h] +\frac12h\,V_{g^{(0)}}^\mu[\delta h] \right]. \tag{3.7b}
\end{align}$$

The leading matter potential $\Theta_{\mathrm m}^{[0]}$ is added to this coefficient in the full Cauchy-surface potential.

The higher coefficients are obtained by coefficient extraction from (3.2). The part needed below is

$$\begin{align}
\Theta_h^{[1]\mu}[\delta h] :=\left[\kappa^1\right] \Theta_{\mathrm{EH}}^\mu
\big[g;\delta g_{\mu\nu}=\kappa\delta h_{\mu\nu}\big]. \tag{3.8}
\end{align}$$

This definition retains the $h^2\delta h$ and $k\delta h$ terms generated by the full covariant potential.

For two variations $\delta_1$ and $\delta_2$, the renormalized symplectic current and form are

$$
\begin{aligned}
\boldsymbol\omega(\delta_1,\delta_2)
&=\delta_1\left(\boldsymbol\Theta_{\mathrm{EH}}+\boldsymbol\Theta_{\mathrm m}\right)[\delta_2]
-\delta_2\left(\boldsymbol\Theta_{\mathrm{EH}}+\boldsymbol\Theta_{\mathrm m}\right)[\delta_1],\\
\Omega_\Sigma(\delta_1,\delta_2)
&=\int_\Sigma\boldsymbol\omega(\delta_1,\delta_2)
-\int_{C_R}\left(\delta_1\boldsymbol C_\Gamma[\delta_2]-\delta_2\boldsymbol C_\Gamma[\delta_1]\right).
\end{aligned}
\tag{3.9}
$$

The coefficient $\Theta^{[-1]}$ is independent of the perturbative fields, so its field-space exterior derivative vanishes. The leading nonzero gravitational symplectic form is the finite coefficient built from the $h\,\delta h$ part of $\Theta^{[0]}$. Appendix B shows that (3.9) is finite and independent of the Cauchy surface under the boundary conditions of Sec. 2.2.

### 3.2 Diffeomorphisms and the Noether charge

The diffeomorphism vector on perturbative field space is

$$\begin{align}
X_\xi=\frac{1}{\kappa}X_\xi^{[-1]}+X_\xi^{[0]}+O(\kappa), \tag{3.10}
\end{align}$$

with action

$$
\begin{array}{lll}
X_\xi^{[-1]}\mathbin{\cdot}\delta h_{\mu\nu}=\mathcal L_\xi g^{(0)}_{\mu\nu},
&X_\xi^{[-1]}\mathbin{\cdot}\delta k_{\mu\nu}=0,
&X_\xi^{[-1]}\mathbin{\cdot}\delta p_{\mu\nu}=0,\\[1mm]
X_\xi^{[0]}\mathbin{\cdot}\delta h_{\mu\nu}=\mathcal L_\xi h_{\mu\nu},
&X_\xi^{[0]}\mathbin{\cdot}\delta k_{\mu\nu}=\mathcal L_\xi k_{\mu\nu},
&X_\xi^{[0]}\mathbin{\cdot}\delta p_{\mu\nu}=\mathcal L_\xi p_{\mu\nu},\\[1mm]
X_\xi^{[-1]}\mathbin{\cdot}\delta\Psi^A=0,
&X_\xi^{[0]}\mathbin{\cdot}\delta\Psi^A=\mathcal L_\xi\Psi^A.&
\end{array}
\tag{3.11}
$$

It reconstructs the full metric transformation:

$$\begin{align}
\kappa X_\xi\mathbin{\cdot}\delta h_{\mu\nu} +\kappa^2X_\xi\mathbin{\cdot}\delta k_{\mu\nu} +\kappa^3X_\xi\mathbin{\cdot}\delta p_{\mu\nu} =\mathcal L_\xi g^{(0)}_{\mu\nu} +\kappa\mathcal L_\xi h_{\mu\nu} +\kappa^2\mathcal L_\xi k_{\mu\nu} +\kappa^3\mathcal L_\xi p_{\mu\nu} +O(\kappa^4). \tag{3.12}
\end{align}$$

Diffeomorphism covariance gives a boundary functional $\alpha_\xi$ through

$$\begin{align}
X_\xi\mathbin{\cdot}\delta S_{\mathrm{ren}} =\alpha_\xi\big|_{\Sigma_f}-\alpha_\xi\big|_{\Sigma_i}. \tag{3.13}
\end{align}$$

For the bulk Lagrangian form, $\alpha_{\xi,\mathrm{bulk}}$ is the pullback of $\xi\mathbin{\cdot}\boldsymbol L_{\mathrm{bulk}}$. The timelike-boundary part is fixed by

$$\begin{align}
\left. \left(\xi\mathbin{\cdot}\boldsymbol L_{\mathrm{bulk}} +X_\xi\mathbin{\cdot}\delta\boldsymbol\ell_\Gamma\right) \right|_\Gamma =\mathrm d\boldsymbol\mu_\xi+\boldsymbol\nu_\xi, \tag{3.14}
\end{align}$$

where $\boldsymbol\nu_\xi$ vanishes for the allowed asymptotic symmetry and fixed sources.

With the boundary descent form included, the functional in (3.13) is

$$\begin{align}
\alpha_\xi =\int_\Sigma\xi\mathbin{\cdot}\boldsymbol L_{\mathrm{bulk}} -\int_{C_R}\boldsymbol\mu_\xi . \tag{3.14a}
\end{align}$$

Writing $S_{\mathrm m}^{[0]}=\int_M\boldsymbol\epsilon_0\mathcal L_{\mathrm m}^{[0]}$, its bulk coefficients through finite order are

$$
\begin{aligned}
\boldsymbol\alpha_{\xi,\mathrm{bulk}}
&=\frac{1}{\kappa^2}\boldsymbol\alpha_{\xi,\mathrm{bulk}}^{[-2]}
+\frac{1}{\kappa}\boldsymbol\alpha_{\xi,\mathrm{bulk}}^{[-1]}
+\boldsymbol\alpha_{\xi,\mathrm{bulk}}^{[0]}+O(\kappa),\\
\boldsymbol\alpha_{\xi,\mathrm{bulk}}^{[-2]}
&=\xi\mathbin{\cdot}\left(\frac12\mathcal L_g^{(0)}\boldsymbol\epsilon_0\right),\\
\boldsymbol\alpha_{\xi,\mathrm{bulk}}^{[-1]}
&=\xi\mathbin{\cdot}\left(\frac12\mathcal L_g^{(1)}[h]\boldsymbol\epsilon_0\right),\\
\boldsymbol\alpha_{\xi,\mathrm{bulk}}^{[0]}
&=\xi\mathbin{\cdot}\left[
\left\{\frac12\left(\mathcal L_g^{(1)}[k]
+\mathcal L_g^{(2)}[h,h]\right)
+\mathcal L_{\mathrm m}^{[0]}\right\}\boldsymbol\epsilon_0
\right].
\end{aligned}
\tag{3.14b}
$$

The Noether charge is

$$\begin{align}
H_\xi=X_\xi\mathbin{\cdot}\theta_\Sigma-\alpha_\xi. \tag{3.15}
\end{align}$$

At a finite cutoff it separates as

$$
\begin{aligned}
H_\xi&=H_{\xi,\mathrm{bulk}}+H_{\xi,\Gamma},\\
H_{\xi,\mathrm{bulk}}
&=\int_\Sigma\left[
X_\xi\mathbin{\cdot}
\left(\boldsymbol\Theta_{\mathrm{EH}}+\boldsymbol\Theta_{\mathrm m}\right)
-\xi\mathbin{\cdot}\boldsymbol L_{\mathrm{bulk}}
\right],\\
H_{\xi,\Gamma}
&=\int_{C_R}\left(\boldsymbol\mu_\xi-X_\xi\mathbin{\cdot}\boldsymbol C_\Gamma\right).
\end{aligned}
\tag{3.16}
$$

Equations (3.10)--(3.16) are identities on the perturbative field space. No metric equation, matter equation, or gauge condition enters their derivation.

For the boundary Lagrangian in (3.3), direct variation gives

$$\begin{align}
\boldsymbol\mu_\xi =\frac{1}{\kappa^2} \left[(K-1)\gamma^\mu{}_\nu\xi^\nu -D^\mu(n_\nu\xi^\nu)\right] \epsilon^\Gamma_{\mu\mu_1}\mathrm dx^{\mu_1}. \tag{3.17}
\end{align}$$

Using

$$\begin{align}
\gamma^{\mu\nu}n^\rho\nabla_\nu\xi_\rho =D^\mu(n_\rho\xi^\rho)-K^\mu{}_\nu\xi^\nu, \tag{3.18}
\end{align}$$

the boundary and corner contribution becomes

$$\begin{align}
\boldsymbol\mu_\xi-X_\xi\mathbin{\cdot}\boldsymbol C_\Gamma =\mathcal J_{\xi,\Gamma}^\mu \epsilon^\Gamma_{\mu\mu_1}\mathrm dx^{\mu_1}, \tag{3.19}
\end{align}$$

where

$$\begin{align}
\mathcal J_{\xi,\Gamma}^\mu =\frac{1}{2\kappa^2} \left[ -K^\mu{}_\nu\xi^\nu +2(K-1)\gamma^\mu{}_\nu\xi^\nu -D^\mu(n\mathbin{\cdot}\xi) +\gamma^{\mu\nu}n^\rho\nabla_\rho\xi_\nu \right]. \tag{3.20}
\end{align}$$

The Einstein--Hilbert surface current obtained from the bulk part of (3.16) is

$$\begin{align}
\mathcal J_{\xi,\mathrm{EH}}^\mu =\frac{1}{2\kappa^2} \left[ -K^\mu{}_\nu\xi^\nu +D^\mu(n\mathbin{\cdot}\xi) -\gamma^{\mu\nu}n^\rho\nabla_\rho\xi_\nu \right]. \tag{3.21}
\end{align}$$

Their sum is the Brown--York current:

$$\begin{align}
\mathcal J_{\xi,\mathrm{EH}}^\mu +\mathcal J_{\xi,\Gamma}^\mu =\frac{1}{\kappa^2} \left[-K^\mu{}_\nu+(K-1)\gamma^\mu{}_\nu\right]\xi^\nu =-\mathcal T^\mu{}_\nu\xi^\nu =:\mathcal J_{\xi,T}^\mu. \tag{3.22}
\end{align}$$

This is an exact off-shell identity of the full fields. In particular, every coefficient of its $\kappa$ expansion is fixed by the same renormalized action.

The finite coefficient $H_{\xi,\Gamma}^{[0]}$ is obtained by expanding the exact form (3.19). This coefficient extraction automatically includes boundary-action terms of order $O(\kappa)$ that are lowered by $X_\xi^{[-1]}$.

### 3.3 Off-shell perturbative bulk current

After the background coefficient is removed by (2.11), the bulk current has the expansion

$$\begin{align}
\mathcal H_{\xi,\mathrm{bulk}}^\mu =\frac{1}{\kappa}\mathcal H_{\xi,\mathrm{bulk}}^{[-1]\mu} +\mathcal H_{\xi,\mathrm{bulk}}^{[0]\mu} +O(\kappa). \tag{3.23}
\end{align}$$

The leading coefficient is

$$\begin{align}
\mathcal H_{\xi,\mathrm{bulk}}^{[-1]\mu} =\xi_\nu\mathcal E^{(1)\mu\nu}[h] +\nabla^{(0)}_\nu S_\xi^{[-1]\mu\nu}, \qquad S_\xi^{[-1]\mu\nu} =\frac12\left(A_\xi^{(1)\mu\nu}[h]+\frac12hA_\xi^{(0)\mu\nu}\right). \tag{3.24}
\end{align}$$

The tensors $A_\xi^{(0)}$, $A_\xi^{(1)}$, and their second-order extension are listed in Appendix A.

The finite current obtained from $S^{[0]}$ and the homogeneous part of $X_\xi$ is

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal H_{\xi,\mathrm{bulk,tr}}^{[0]\mu}
={}&\xi_\nu T_{(h)}^{\mu\nu}
+\xi_\nu T^{\mu\nu}
+\nabla^{(0)}_\nu
\left(S_{\xi,h}^{\mu\nu}+S_{\xi,k,0}^{\mu\nu}\right)
+\mathcal R_{\xi,h}^\mu
+\mathcal R_{\xi,\mathrm m}^\mu,
\end{aligned}
\tag{3.25}
$$

where

$$\begin{align}
\mathcal R_{\xi,h}^\mu =\xi^\rho h^{\mu\sigma}\mathcal E^{(1)}_{\sigma\rho}[h] -\frac12h\xi_\nu\mathcal E^{(1)\mu\nu}[h]. \tag{3.26}
\end{align}$$

The $k$-dependent tensor appearing before the constraint is imposed is

$$\begin{align}
S_{\xi,k,0}^{\mu\nu} =\xi^{[\mu}\left( \nabla^{(0)}_\rho k^{\nu]\rho} -\nabla^{(0)\nu]}k \right). \tag{3.26a}
\end{align}$$

The term $\mathcal R_{\xi,\mathrm m}^\mu$ is proportional to the leading matter equations, up to a matter superpotential with vanishing asymptotic integral.

At finite order, diffeomorphism covariance of the ordered action reads

$$\begin{align}
X_\xi^{[-1]}\mathbin{\cdot}\delta S^{[1]} +X_\xi^{[0]}\mathbin{\cdot}\delta S^{[0]} =\alpha_{\xi,\mathrm{bulk}}^{[0]}\big|_{\Sigma_f} -\alpha_{\xi,\mathrm{bulk}}^{[0]}\big|_{\Sigma_i}. \tag{3.26b}
\end{align}$$

The inhomogeneous contraction $X_\xi^{[-1]}\mathbin{\cdot}\delta S^{[1]}$ adds

$$\begin{align}
\Delta\mathcal H_{\xi,\mathrm{inh}}^{[0]\mu} =\Theta_h^{[1]\mu}[\mathcal L_\xi g^{(0)}] \tag{3.27}
\end{align}$$

to the local finite current. Its possible surface contribution is obtained from the finite coefficient of the full Einstein--Hilbert surface tensor. One finds

$$
\begin{aligned}
S_{\xi,\mathrm{EH}}^{[0]\mu\nu}
=\frac12\bigg(&A_\xi^{(1)\mu\nu}[k]
+A_\xi^{(2)\mu\nu}[h,h]
+\frac12hA_\xi^{(1)\mu\nu}[h]\\
&+\left(\frac12k+s_2\right)A_\xi^{(0)\mu\nu}\bigg),
\qquad
s_2=-\frac14h_{\rho\sigma}h^{\rho\sigma}+\frac18h^2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{3.28}
$$

The identities

$$\begin{align}
D_{1,\xi}^{[\mu\nu]}=\frac12A_\xi^{(1)\mu\nu}[h], \qquad D_{2,\xi}^{[\mu\nu]}=\frac12A_\xi^{(2)\mu\nu}[h,h], \tag{3.29}
\end{align}$$

give

$$\begin{align}
S_{\xi,\mathrm{inh}}^{[0]\mu\nu} :=S_{\xi,\mathrm{EH}}^{[0]\mu\nu} -S_{\xi,h}^{\mu\nu}-S_{\xi,k}^{\mu\nu} =\frac12A_\xi^{(2)\mu\nu}[h,h]-D_{2,\xi}^{[\mu\nu]} =0. \tag{3.30}
\end{align}$$

Thus the $O(\kappa)$ Lagrangian is required for the local finite Noether current, while its selected inhomogeneous contraction produces no additional antisymmetric Einstein--Hilbert surface tensor. The timelike-boundary contribution in (3.19) remains part of the total charge.

For the background-Killing specialization used next, $\mathcal L_\xi g^{(0)}=0$, so the local term (3.27) also vanishes. Equations (3.26b)--(3.30) retain the complete off-shell result for the larger Brown--Henneaux asymptotic-symmetry space.

### 3.4 Identification of the bulk and boundary charges

We now specialize $\xi$ to a background Killing field and impose the leading matter equations and the metric constraints (2.21). The first constraint removes the equation term in (3.24) and sets $\mathcal R_{\xi,h}^\mu=0$. The matter equation sets $\mathcal R_{\xi,\mathrm m}^\mu=0$. The second constraint gives

$$\begin{align}
T_{(h)}^{\mu\nu}+T^{\mu\nu} =\mathcal E^{(1)\mu\nu}[k]. \tag{3.31}
\end{align}$$

The linearized Bianchi identity and the Killing equation imply

$$\begin{align}
\nabla^{(0)}_\mu\left(T_{(h)}^{\mu\nu}+T^{\mu\nu}\right)=0, \qquad \nabla^{(0)}_\mu\left[\xi_\nu \left(T_{(h)}^{\mu\nu}+T^{\mu\nu}\right)\right]=0. \tag{3.31a}
\end{align}$$

For a background Killing field, the contracted linearized Einstein tensor is a divergence,

$$\begin{align}
\xi_\nu\mathcal E^{(1)\mu\nu}[k] =\nabla^{(0)}_\nu\widehat S_{\xi,k}^{\mu\nu}, \tag{3.32}
\end{align}$$

with

$$
\begin{aligned}
\widehat S_{\xi,k}^{\mu\nu}
={}&\xi^{[\mu}\nabla^{(0)\nu]}k
-\xi^{[\mu}\nabla^{(0)}_\rho k^{\nu]\rho}
+\xi_\rho\nabla^{(0)[\mu}k^{\nu]\rho}\\
&+\frac12k\nabla^{(0)[\mu}\xi^{\nu]}
-k^{\rho[\mu}\nabla^{(0)}_\rho\xi^{\nu]}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{3.33}
$$

Combining $\widehat S_{\xi,k}$ with the $k$-dependent surface term already present in (3.25) gives

$$\begin{align}
S_{\xi,k}^{\mu\nu} =\xi_\rho\nabla^{(0)[\mu}k^{\nu]\rho} +\frac12k\nabla^{(0)[\mu}\xi^{\nu]} -k^{\rho[\mu}\nabla^{(0)}_\rho\xi^{\nu]}. \tag{3.34}
\end{align}$$

The on-constraint bulk contribution is therefore

$$
\begin{aligned}
H_{\xi,\mathrm{bulk}}
&=\frac{1}{\kappa}H_{\xi,\mathrm{bulk}}^{[-1]}
+H_{\xi,\mathrm{bulk}}^{[0]}+O(\kappa),\\
H_{\xi,\mathrm{bulk}}^{[-1]}
&=\lim_{R\to\infty}\int_{C_R}\!\epsilon_C^{(0)}\,
\tau_\mu^{(0)}n_\nu^{(0)} S_\xi^{[-1]\nu\mu},\\
H_{\xi,\mathrm{bulk}}^{[0]}
&=\lim_{R\to\infty}\int_{C_R}\!\epsilon_C^{(0)}\,
\tau_\mu^{(0)}n_\nu^{(0)}
\left(S_{\xi,h}^{\nu\mu}+S_{\xi,k}^{\nu\mu}\right).
\end{aligned}
\tag{3.35}
$$

The perturbative surface tensors in (3.35) already contain the inverse-metric and volume-density corrections, so the displayed coefficient integrals use the background corner measure and normals.

The first line of (3.22) holds before the perturbative expansion. Expanding it after the common background subtraction gives, coefficient by coefficient,

$$
\begin{aligned}
H_\xi^{[-1]}
&=H_{\xi,\mathrm{bulk}}^{[-1]}+H_{\xi,\Gamma}^{[-1]}
=H_{\xi,T}^{[-1]},\\
H_\xi^{[0]}
&=H_{\xi,\mathrm{bulk}}^{[0]}+H_{\xi,\Gamma}^{[0]}
=H_{\xi,T}^{[0]}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{3.36}
$$

At leading order, the boundary coefficient fixed by (3.20) is

$$\begin{align}
H_{\xi,\Gamma}^{[-1]} =H_{\xi,T}^{[-1]}-H_{\xi,\mathrm{bulk}}^{[-1]}. \tag{3.36a}
\end{align}$$

For the linearized Brown--Henneaux sector used here, the standard first-order calculation gives $H_{\xi,\mathrm{bulk}}^{[-1]}=H_{\xi,T}^{[-1]}$. Equation (3.36a) therefore gives $H_{\xi,\Gamma}^{[-1]}=0$.

At finite order, the required corner coefficient is explicitly

$$\begin{align}
H_{\xi,\Gamma}^{[0]} =\left[\kappa^0\right]
\lim_{R\to\infty}\int_{C_R} \left(\boldsymbol\mu_\xi-X_\xi\mathbin{\cdot}\boldsymbol C_\Gamma\right). \tag{3.37}
\end{align}$$

Equations (3.17), (3.4), and (3.20) determine this coefficient from the renormalized action. Equivalently, taking the finite coefficient of the exact identity (3.22) gives

$$\begin{align}
H_{\xi,\Gamma}^{[0]} =H_{\xi,T}^{[0]}-H_{\xi,\mathrm{bulk}}^{[0]}. \tag{3.38}
\end{align}$$

The right-hand side of (3.38) is the evaluated coefficient of (3.37). It contains no free improvement parameter. Combining (3.35) and (3.37) proves the finite matching in (3.36).

The $\kappa^{-2}$ coefficient is the charge of the reference background. Definition (2.11) sets

$$\begin{align}
H_\xi^{[-2]}=H_{\xi,T}^{[-2]}=0. \tag{3.39}
\end{align}$$

Thus the first nontrivial term is $O(\kappa^{-1})$, and the classical matter stress and gravitational self-stress first enter the $O(\kappa^0)$ relation.

## 4. Conclusion

We have extended the first-order bulk-boundary identification of Chen et al. to second order in metric perturbations around AdS$_3$ for the conserved charges generated by background Killing fields in classical Einstein gravity. The finite bulk source is the sum of the gravitational self-stress $T_{(h)}=-\mathcal E^{(2)}[h,h]$ and a classical matter stress tensor $T=O(G^0)$.

The perturbative action was retained through $O(\kappa)$, and its direct variation supplied the second-order Einstein constraint in the finite Noether current. The diffeomorphism charge was constructed off shell from $H_\xi=X_\xi\mathbin{\cdot}\theta-\alpha_\xi$. The constraints were imposed only when the bulk current was converted to a surface integral.

The Gibbons--Hawking--York term and the AdS counterterm determine the corner potential $C_\Gamma$ and the boundary descent form $\mu_\xi$. Their contribution combines with the Einstein--Hilbert surface current into the Brown--York current as an exact full-field identity. This gives

$$\begin{align}
H_\xi^{[-1]}=H_{\xi,T}^{[-1]}, \qquad H_\xi^{[0]}=H_{\xi,T}^{[0]}.
\end{align}$$

Matter sources, boundary flux, and long-range gauge charges enlarge the boundary phase space and require their own boundary terms. The fixed-source, zero-flux sector considered here isolates the second-order gravitational backreaction and its matching to the asymptotic energy.

## Appendix A. Perturbative formulas

### A.1 Inverse metric and volume density

For (2.12), the inverse metric is

$$
\begin{aligned}
g^{\mu\nu}
={}&g^{(0)\mu\nu}-\kappa h^{\mu\nu}
+\kappa^2\left(h^\mu{}_\rho h^{\rho\nu}-k^{\mu\nu}\right)\\
&+\kappa^3\left(-p^{\mu\nu}+h^\mu{}_\rho k^{\rho\nu}
+k^\mu{}_\rho h^{\rho\nu}
-h^\mu{}_\rho h^\rho{}_\sigma h^{\sigma\nu}\right)
+O(\kappa^4).
\end{aligned}
\tag{A.1}
$$

The volume density is

$$\begin{align}
\frac{\sqrt{-g}}{\sqrt{-g^{(0)}}} =1+\kappa v_1+\kappa^2v_2+\kappa^3v_3+O(\kappa^4), \tag{A.2}
\end{align}$$

where

$$
\begin{aligned}
v_1={}&\frac12h,\\
v_2={}&\frac12k+\frac18h^2-\frac14h_{\mu\nu}h^{\mu\nu},\\
v_3={}&\frac12p+\frac14hk-\frac12h_{\mu\nu}k^{\mu\nu}
+\frac1{48}h^3-\frac18h\,h_{\mu\nu}h^{\mu\nu}
+\frac16h_\mu{}^\nu h_\nu{}^\rho h_\rho{}^\mu.
\end{aligned}
\tag{A.3}
$$

### A.2 Gravitational density through cubic order

On the AdS$_3$ background,

$$\begin{align}
\mathcal L_g^{(0)}=-4, \qquad \mathcal L_g^{(1)}[u] =\nabla^{(0)}_\mu\nabla^{(0)}_\nu u^{\mu\nu} -\nabla^{(0)2}u. \tag{A.4}
\end{align}$$

The quadratic density used in (2.15) is

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal L_g^{(2)}[u,u]
={}&\frac12u^2-u_{\mu\nu}u^{\mu\nu}
+u^{\mu\nu}\nabla^{(0)}_\mu\nabla^{(0)}_\nu u
-\frac14\nabla^{(0)}_\mu u\nabla^{(0)\mu}u\\
&-\nabla^{(0)}_\mu u^{\mu\nu}\nabla^{(0)}_\rho u_\nu{}^\rho
+\nabla^{(0)\mu}u\nabla^{(0)}_\nu u_\mu{}^\nu
-u^{\mu\nu}\nabla^{(0)}_\nu\nabla^{(0)}_\rho u_\mu{}^\rho\\
&-u^{\mu\nu}\nabla^{(0)}_\rho\nabla^{(0)}_\nu u_\mu{}^\rho
+\frac12u\nabla^{(0)}_\mu\nabla^{(0)}_\nu u^{\mu\nu}
+u^{\mu\nu}\nabla^{(0)2}u_{\mu\nu}\\
&-\frac12u\nabla^{(0)2}u
-\frac12\nabla^{(0)}_\mu u_{\nu\rho}\nabla^{(0)\rho}u^{\mu\nu}
+\frac34\nabla^{(0)}_\rho u_{\mu\nu}\nabla^{(0)\rho}u^{\mu\nu}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{A.5}
$$

The cubic coefficient can be generated without fixing a gauge. For a pure $h$ perturbation define

$$
\begin{aligned}
C_1[h]^\rho{}_{\mu\nu}
&=\frac12\left(\nabla^{(0)}_\mu h^\rho{}_\nu
+\nabla^{(0)}_\nu h^\rho{}_\mu
-\nabla^{(0)\rho}h_{\mu\nu}\right),\\
C_2[h]^\rho{}_{\mu\nu}
&=-h^\rho{}_\lambda C_1[h]^\lambda{}_{\mu\nu},\\
C_3[h]^\rho{}_{\mu\nu}
&=h^\rho{}_\lambda h^\lambda{}_\sigma C_1[h]^\sigma{}_{\mu\nu}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{A.6}
$$

The Ricci coefficients are

$$
\begin{aligned}
R^{(1)}_{\mu\nu}
&=\nabla^{(0)}_\rho C_1^\rho{}_{\mu\nu}
-\nabla^{(0)}_\nu C_1^\rho{}_{\mu\rho},\\
R^{(2)}_{\mu\nu}
&=\nabla^{(0)}_\rho C_2^\rho{}_{\mu\nu}
-\nabla^{(0)}_\nu C_2^\rho{}_{\mu\rho}
+C_1^\rho{}_{\rho\lambda}C_1^\lambda{}_{\mu\nu}
-C_1^\rho{}_{\nu\lambda}C_1^\lambda{}_{\mu\rho},\\
R^{(3)}_{\mu\nu}
&=\nabla^{(0)}_\rho C_3^\rho{}_{\mu\nu}
-\nabla^{(0)}_\nu C_3^\rho{}_{\mu\rho}
+C_1^\rho{}_{\rho\lambda}C_2^\lambda{}_{\mu\nu}
+C_2^\rho{}_{\rho\lambda}C_1^\lambda{}_{\mu\nu}\\
&\quad-C_1^\rho{}_{\nu\lambda}C_2^\lambda{}_{\mu\rho}
-C_2^\rho{}_{\nu\lambda}C_1^\lambda{}_{\mu\rho}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{A.7}
$$

Writing $\mathcal R^{(n)}$ for the coefficient obtained after also expanding the inverse metric, the pure cubic density is

$$\begin{align}
\mathcal L_g^{(3)}[h,h,h] =\mathcal R^{(3)} +\frac12h\,\mathcal R^{(2)} +\left(\frac18h^2-\frac14h_{\rho\sigma}h^{\rho\sigma}\right)\mathcal R^{(1)} -4\left(\frac1{48}h^3-\frac18h\,h_{\rho\sigma}h^{\rho\sigma} +\frac16h_\mu{}^\nu h_\nu{}^\rho h_\rho{}^\mu\right). \tag{A.8}
\end{align}$$

Together with the polarization (2.16), equations (A.1)--(A.8) reproduce every coefficient in (2.15).

### A.3 Linearized Einstein operator

The derivative ordering produced directly by xPert gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal E^{(1)}_{\mu\nu}[u]
={}&2u_{\mu\nu}-g^{(0)}_{\mu\nu}u
-\frac12\nabla^{(0)}_\mu\nabla^{(0)}_\nu u
+\frac12\nabla^{(0)}_\rho\nabla^{(0)}_\mu u_\nu{}^\rho
+\frac12\nabla^{(0)}_\rho\nabla^{(0)}_\nu u_\mu{}^\rho\\

\end{aligned}
\tag{A.9}
$$

Commuting the first two double derivatives with the AdS curvature gives the equivalent divergence-first form used in the surface-charge calculation.

### A.4 Surface tensors

Define

$$
\begin{aligned}
A_\xi^{(0)\mu\nu}
&=\nabla^{(0)\mu}\xi^\nu-\nabla^{(0)\nu}\xi^\mu,\\
A_\xi^{(1)\mu\nu}[u]
&=-u^{\mu\rho}\nabla^{(0)}_\rho\xi^\nu
+u^{\nu\rho}\nabla^{(0)}_\rho\xi^\mu
+\xi^\rho\left(\nabla^{(0)\mu}u^\nu{}_\rho
-\nabla^{(0)\nu}u^\mu{}_\rho\right),\\
A_\xi^{(2)\mu\nu}[h,h]
&=h^\mu{}_\rho h^{\rho\sigma}\nabla^{(0)}_\sigma\xi^\nu
-h^\nu{}_\rho h^{\rho\sigma}\nabla^{(0)}_\sigma\xi^\mu\\
&\quad+\xi^\lambda\left[
h^{\mu\rho}\left(\nabla^{(0)\nu}h_{\rho\lambda}
-\nabla^{(0)}_\rho h^\nu{}_\lambda\right)
-h^{\nu\rho}\left(\nabla^{(0)\mu}h_{\rho\lambda}
-\nabla^{(0)}_\rho h^\mu{}_\lambda\right)
\right].
\end{aligned}
\tag{A.10}
$$

The quadratic $h$ surface tensor is

$$\begin{align}
S_{\xi,h}^{\mu\nu} =s_2\nabla^{(0)[\mu}\xi^{\nu]} +\frac12hD_{1,\xi}^{[\mu\nu]} +D_{2,\xi}^{[\mu\nu]}, \tag{A.11}
\end{align}$$

where

$$
\begin{aligned}
D_{1,\xi}^{\mu\nu}
&=-h^{\mu\rho}\nabla^{(0)}_\rho\xi^\nu
+g^{(0)\mu\sigma}C_1[h]^\nu{}_{\sigma\rho}\xi^\rho,\\
D_{2,\xi}^{\mu\nu}
&=h^\mu{}_\lambda h^{\lambda\rho}\nabla^{(0)}_\rho\xi^\nu
-h^{\mu\rho}C_1[h]^\nu{}_{\rho\sigma}\xi^\sigma
+g^{(0)\mu\rho}C_2[h]^\nu{}_{\rho\sigma}\xi^\sigma.
\end{aligned}
\tag{A.12}
$$

Antisymmetrization has weight one half. Direct canonicalization gives (3.29). It also gives

$$\begin{align}
S_{\xi,k}^{\mu\nu} =\frac12A_\xi^{(1)\mu\nu}[k] +\frac14kA_\xi^{(0)\mu\nu}, \tag{A.13}
\end{align}$$

which is equivalent to (3.34).

## Appendix B. Finiteness and boundary flux

### B.1 Vacuum subtraction and the cutoff action

For the background (2.1),

$$\begin{align}
\sqrt{-g^{(0)}}=r, \qquad \sqrt{-\gamma^{(0)}}=R\sqrt{1+R^2}, \qquad K^{(0)}=\frac{1+2R^2}{R\sqrt{1+R^2}}. \tag{B.1}
\end{align}$$

Per unit $t$ and $\phi$ coordinate volume, the regulated bulk and timelike-boundary contributions are

$$\begin{align}
S_{\mathrm{bulk}}^{(0)}(R)=-\frac{R^2}{\kappa^2}, \qquad S_\Gamma^{(0)}(R) =\frac{1+2R^2-R\sqrt{1+R^2}}{\kappa^2}. \tag{B.2}
\end{align}$$

Their sum has the finite limit

$$\begin{align}
S_{\mathrm{bulk}}^{(0)}(R)+S_\Gamma^{(0)}(R) =\frac{1}{2\kappa^2}+O(R^{-2}). \tag{B.3}
\end{align}$$

The finite vacuum value is removed from charges by (2.11).

### B.2 Perturbative falloff check

Under (2.8), the raised radial components obey

$$\begin{align}
u^{rr}=O(1), \qquad u^{ra}=O(r^{-3}), \qquad u^{ab}=O(r^{-4}), \qquad u\in\{h,k,p\}, \tag{B.4}
\end{align}$$

where the first estimate uses $g^{(0)rr}=O(r^2)$. The combinations appearing in the cutoff boundary expansion satisfy

$$\begin{align}
r^4u_{rr}=O(1), \qquad rD^{(0)a}u_{ra}=O(r^{-4}), \qquad r^2\gamma^{(0)ab}u_{ra}u_{rb}=O(r^{-6}). \tag{B.5}
\end{align}$$

To display the cancellation at each perturbative order, define $u_\partial:=\gamma^{(0)ab}u_{ab}$. The large-$r$ boundary densities through finite order are

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal I_\Gamma^{[-1]}[h]
={}&-\frac12h_\partial+\frac r2\partial_rh_\partial
-r^4h_{rr}-rD^{(0)a}h_{ra},\\
\mathcal I_\Gamma^{[0]}[h,k]
={}&-\frac12k_\partial+\frac r2\partial_rk_\partial
-r^4k_{rr}-rD^{(0)a}k_{ra}
+r^2\gamma^{(0)ab}h_{ra}h_{rb}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{B.5a}
$$

Tangential traces and their radial derivatives are $O(r^{-2})$. Hence every perturbative coefficient of the Gibbons--Hawking--York plus counterterm action through the order used in the text is finite at the cutoff boundary.

The same conclusion follows directly from the variational identity (3.3). Brown--Henneaux conditions fix the leading conformal metric, so the source variation in

$$\begin{align}
-\frac12\int_\Gamma\!\mathcal T^{ab}\delta\gamma_{ab}\,\boldsymbol\epsilon_\Gamma
\tag{B.6}
\end{align}$$

has a finite limit. The remaining term is the finite corner variation $\int_{C_R}\boldsymbol C_\Gamma$. Starting from the finite background value (B.3), integration of this finite variation along an allowed path in field space proves finiteness of the renormalized action in the sector considered here.

### B.3 Symplectic flux

Taking an antisymmetrized second variation of (3.3) gives the flux of the renormalized symplectic current through $\Gamma$. The fixed leading boundary metric and the matter condition in Sec. 2.2 imply

$$\begin{align}
\lim_{R\to\infty}\int_{\Gamma_R}\boldsymbol\omega_{\mathrm{ren}}(\delta_1,\delta_2)=0. \tag{B.7}
\end{align}$$

Stokes' theorem then gives

$$\begin{align}
\Omega_{\Sigma_f}(\delta_1,\delta_2) =\Omega_{\Sigma_i}(\delta_1,\delta_2). \tag{B.8}
\end{align}$$

The matter contribution is finite because the sources are fixed and the normalizable data have zero flux. The same assumptions set the asymptotic integral of a matter superpotential to zero.

### B.4 Charge finiteness

Equation (3.22) writes the complete gravitational surface current as the Brown--York current before any perturbative expansion. The Brown--Henneaux falloffs make $\mathcal T_{ab}$ finite after the common background subtraction. Therefore

$$\begin{align}
\lim_{R\to\infty}\int_{C_R} \left(\mathcal J_{\xi,\mathrm{EH}}^\mu +\mathcal J_{\xi,\Gamma}^\mu\right) \epsilon^\Gamma_{\mu\mu_1}\mathrm dx^{\mu_1} =H_{\xi,T} \tag{B.9}
\end{align}$$

is finite. Since (3.22) holds for the full fields, the $\kappa^{-1}$ and $\kappa^0$ coefficients used in (3.36) are finite separately.
