# Global AdS$_3$ Interval Modular-Wedge Corner Benchmark

## Claim Target

The purpose of this benchmark is to test, in one explicit Brown--Henneaux state, whether the finite-action covariant-phase-space corner term used in this project realizes the arbitrary-gauge completion of canonical energy in equation (4.36) of [[Generalized entropy of gravitational fluctuations (2501.08308v2)]]:

$$\begin{align}
E_{\mathrm{can}}[h^{\mathrm{HW}}]-E_{\mathrm{can}}[h] =\int_{\gamma_A}\Upsilon.
\end{align}$$

The target is only an explicit global AdS$_3$ interval-wedge identity. It is not a general proof of JLMS, a nonperturbative definition of subregion algebras, or a proof that the crossed-product assumptions in [[Holography in the linearized quantum gravity regime and modular crossed product (2607.27337v1)]] hold.

## Formalism and Assumptions

We use Einstein gravity in AdS$_3$ with unit AdS radius,

$$\begin{align}
ds^2=-(1+r^2)dt^2+\frac{dr^2}{1+r^2}+r^2d\phi^2,
\end{align}$$

Brown--Henneaux boundary conditions, and the finite-renormalized-action covariant phase space of the current gravitational-energy project. In particular, the calculation must keep the full quadratic current

$$\begin{align}
\xi_\nu T_h^{\mu\nu}+\nabla_\nu S_{\xi,h}^{[0]\mu\nu}
\end{align}$$

and the charge split between the asymptotic and inner boundaries of

$$\begin{align}
\partial\Sigma_A=(\partial\Sigma_A)_\infty\cup\gamma_A.
\end{align}$$

The linear equation $E^{(1)}[h]=0$ is not identified with the second-order backreaction equation

$$\begin{align}
E^{(1)}[k]+E^{(2)}[h,h]=0.
\end{align}$$

The present calculation concerns the $h$-quadratic canonical-energy/corner sector. Solving for $k$ is a later, separate check of the full second-order charge law.

## Interval Geometry

For the interval

$$\begin{align}
A=\{t=0,\ -a\leq\phi\leq a\},\qquad 0<a<\frac\pi2,
\end{align}$$

the RT geodesic is

$$\begin{align}
r_{\mathrm{RT}}(\phi)=\frac{\cos a}{\sqrt{\cos^2\phi-\cos^2a}}.
\end{align}$$

The bulk modular Killing field with surface gravity $2\pi$ is

$$\begin{align}
\xi_A=\frac{2\pi}{\sin a}\left[ \left(\frac{r\cos t\cos\phi}{\sqrt{1+r^2}}-\cos a\right)\partial_t +\sqrt{1+r^2}\sin t\cos\phi\,\partial_r -\frac{\sqrt{1+r^2}}{r}\sin t\sin\phi\,\partial_\phi \right].
\end{align}$$

The script verifies, component by component,

$$\begin{align}
\mathcal L_{\xi_A}G=0, \qquad \xi_A|_{\gamma_A}=0, \qquad -\frac12\nabla_\mu\xi_{A\nu}\nabla^\mu\xi_A^\nu\big|_{\gamma_A}=(2\pi)^2,
\end{align}$$

and checks the geodesic equation for $r_{\mathrm{RT}}(\phi)$.

## Explicit Real Brown--Henneaux Mode

The complex normalized left primary is generated by

$$\begin{aligned}
\zeta_{-,0}^t&=\frac{i\sqrt{3G}}{3}e^{-2i(t-\phi)}\frac{r^4}{(1+r^2)^2},\\
\zeta_{-,0}^r&=-\frac{\sqrt{3G}}{3}e^{-2i(t-\phi)}\frac{r(3+2r^2)}{1+r^2},\\
\zeta_{-,0}^\phi&=-\frac{i\sqrt{3G}}{3}e^{-2i(t-\phi)}\frac{3+r^2}{1+r^2},
\end{aligned}$$

with $h_{-,0}=\mathcal L_{\zeta_{-,0}}G$ and $\Omega[h_{-,0},h_{-,0}^*]=-i$. The script independently verifies that this Lie derivative equals the explicit primary tensor recorded in the linearized-gravity project. It also verifies the vector-level identity for the real quadrature below. This extra check is essential: an earlier version had the opposite radial sign, which still defined a pure diffeomorphism but was not a real phase of this Brown--Henneaux primary. All formulas below use the corrected vector.

For the concrete benchmark we set $a=\pi/4$ and take the unit real quadrature

$$\begin{align}
h_R=\frac{ih_{-,0}-ih_{-,0}^*}{\sqrt2}, \qquad \zeta_R=\frac{i\zeta_{-,0}-i\zeta_{-,0}^*}{\sqrt2}.
\end{align}$$

At $t=0$, the tangential boundary components of $\zeta_R$ vanish at $\phi=\pm\pi/4$. Thus the large diffeomorphism does not move the two interval anchors. This condition is why this phase and interval size were selected.

## Local Extremal-Gauge Completion and Regulated Inner Term

Because $h_R=\mathcal L_{\zeta_R}G$ locally, one may choose a smooth completion satisfying

$$\begin{align}
v=-\zeta_R
\end{align}$$

in a neighbourhood of $\gamma_A$. Then $h_R+\mathcal L_vG=0$ there, which is stronger than the linear extremality condition $\delta K_{(k)}=\delta K_{(l)}=0$ on $\gamma_A$. Away from the RT surface, $v$ must be extended so as not to remove the physical Brown--Henneaux data at the asymptotic boundary. Only the near-$\gamma_A$ jet is used in the present inner-boundary calculation.

Using the general Einstein Iyer--Wald form

$$\begin{align}
\delta Q_v[\mathcal L_{\xi_A}h_R]-\iota_v\theta[G;\mathcal L_{\xi_A}h_R]
\end{align}$$

and contracting it with the inner normal pointing out of the wedge, introduce

$$\begin{align}
x=\tan\phi, \qquad x_c(R)=\sqrt{\frac{R^2-1}{R^2+1}},
\end{align}$$

where $r_{\mathrm{RT}}(\phi_c)=R$. The qualification "general" matters here: $v$ is not a Killing field. The formula for $k_\xi$ simplified using the Killing equation in the asymptotic-charge calculation cannot be reused with $\xi\mapsto v$. The script independently checks at three bulk points that the divergence of the form above equals the Lee--Wald symplectic current.

The regulated inner integrand reduces to the smooth polynomial

$$\begin{align}
\frac{dH_{\gamma}}{dx} =\frac{-5+13x^2-11x^4+3x^6}{16}.
\end{align}$$

An antiderivative is

$$\begin{align}
F(x)=\frac1{16}\left(-5x+\frac{13}{3}x^3-\frac{11}{5}x^5+\frac37x^7\right),
\end{align}$$

so the regulated inner integral of the Iyer--Wald form is

$$\begin{align}
K_{v,\gamma}^{\mathrm{IW}}(R)=2F(x_c(R)).
\end{align}$$

Its large-cutoff behavior is

$$\begin{align}
K_{v,\gamma}^{\mathrm{IW}}(R) =-\frac{32}{105}+\frac1{3R^6}+O(R^{-8}).
\end{align}$$

There is an orientation subtlety here. Equation (4.25) of 2501.08308v2 writes the Stokes boundary as

$$\begin{align}
\int_B k-\int_{\gamma^{(0)}}k.
\end{align}$$

Thus the paper's displayed $\gamma^{(0)}$ orientation is opposite to the outward-inner-boundary orientation used by the script. Equation (4.37) contains the form

$$\begin{align}
-\left(\delta Q_v[\mathcal L_{\xi_A}h_R] -\iota_v\theta[G;\mathcal L_{\xi_A}h_R]\right)
\end{align}$$

when $h_R+\mathcal L_vG=0$ near $\gamma_A$. The minus sign in the form and the minus sign converting the paper's $\gamma$ orientation to the script's outward orientation cancel. Therefore

$$\begin{align}
\int_{\gamma_A}\Upsilon =\lim_{R\to\infty}K_{v,\gamma}^{\mathrm{IW}}(R) =-\frac{32}{105}
\end{align}$$

in the orientation convention of equation (4.36).

## Coupling and Symplectic-Current Convention Map

The two calculations do not use the same normalization:

$$\begin{align}
\kappa_{2501}^2=16\pi G, \qquad \kappa_{\mathrm p}^2=8\pi G, \qquad \delta g_{\mathrm{phys}}=\kappa_{\mathrm p}h_{\mathrm p}=h_R.
\end{align}$$

After expressing both currents in terms of the same physical metric variations, the script evaluates the two displayed bulk symplectic currents directly at three independent points and finds

$$\begin{align}
\omega_{\mathrm p}=-\omega_{2501}.
\end{align}$$

Consequently the project-convention corner form should be defined as $\Upsilon_{\mathrm p}:=-\Upsilon_{2501}$ if one wants the literal identity $\Delta E_{\mathrm{can,p}}=\int\Upsilon_{\mathrm p}$. The numerical target in project conventions is therefore

$$\begin{align}
\int_{\gamma_A}\Upsilon_{\mathrm p} =\frac{32}{105}.
\end{align}$$

## Raw Project Surface Tensor Is Not yet $\Upsilon$

To compare with the current gravitational-energy formalism, write

$$\begin{align}
g=G+\kappa h_{\mathrm p}+\kappa^2k_{\mathrm p}+O(\kappa^3), \qquad \kappa=\sqrt{8\pi G},
\end{align}$$

and use the exact-diffeomorphism representative

$$\begin{align}
h_{\mathrm p}=\mathcal L_{\zeta_R/\kappa}G, \qquad k_{\mathrm p}=\frac12\mathcal L_{\zeta_R/\kappa}^2G.
\end{align}$$

This keeps $E^{(1)}[h_{\mathrm p}]=0$ conceptually separate from the second-order equation $E^{(1)}[k_{\mathrm p}]+E^{(2)}[h_{\mathrm p},h_{\mathrm p}]=0$. Diffeomorphism covariance of the exact AdS metric implies both equations for this representative. Independently, the script expands the coordinate Einstein tensor of $G+\epsilon h_{\mathrm p}+\epsilon^2k_{\mathrm p}$ through $O(\epsilon^2)$ and checks the background, linear, and second-order coefficients at three high-precision bulk points.

Substituting these fields into the project's raw inner tensor $S_{\xi_A,h}^{[0]}+S_{\xi_A,k}^{[0]}$ gives

$$\begin{align}
\frac{dH_{\gamma,\mathrm{raw}}^{[0]}}{dx} =-\frac18(1-x^2)^2(1+x^2).
\end{align}$$

It vanishes at both anchors and integrates to

$$\begin{align}
\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{dH_{\gamma,\mathrm{raw}}^{[0]}}{dx}\,dx =-\frac{16}{105}.
\end{align}$$

The former logarithmic-anchor obstruction is therefore withdrawn: it was an artifact of the incorrect radial sign. The raw tensor still cannot be identified with $\Upsilon$. It is a representative of the full second-order modular Noether charge and contains area/backreaction data, whereas $\Upsilon$ is a gauge-section difference of canonical energies. Their finite values already differ in this corrected benchmark.

The current project's $C_\Gamma$ is defined on the asymptotic timelike cutoff and is precisely what completes the outer charge to Brown--York. As currently written, it does not define an inner brick-wall variational principle at $\gamma_A$ or the associated endpoint joints. Therefore the present finite-action formalism does not yet provide a project-internal object that can simply be called $H_{\xi_A,\gamma_A}^{[0]}=\int\Upsilon_{\mathrm p}$.

## Why the Globally Trivializing Diffeomorphism Is Not Admissible

It is tempting to use $v=-\zeta_R$ everywhere. This makes $h_R+\mathcal L_vG=0$ globally, but it also removes the physical Brown--Henneaux boundary graviton and is therefore large at infinity. The script retains, rather than drops, its asymptotic Iyer--Wald term and finds

$$\begin{align}
\frac{dK_{v,B}^{\mathrm{IW}}}{d\phi} =4\cos2\phi\left(\cos2\phi-\sqrt2\cos3\phi\right),
\end{align}$$

$$\begin{align}
K_{v,B}^{\mathrm{IW}}=\pi-\frac{16}{5}\neq0.
\end{align}$$

Together with the outward inner term, the total potential is

$$\begin{align}
K_{v,B}^{\mathrm{IW}}+K_{v,\gamma}^{\mathrm{IW}} =\pi-\frac{368}{105}.
\end{align}$$

This is a useful falsification check: the local extremal-gauge completion cannot be replaced by a global large diffeomorphism while continuing to drop the outer boundary term.

## An Explicit Proper Regulated Completion

To resolve the anchor issue without changing the Brown--Henneaux state, choose

$$\begin{align}
v_L=-f_L(r)\zeta_R, \qquad f_L(r)=\frac{L^2}{L^2+r^2}.
\end{align}$$

For every finite $L$, $v_L$ decays at infinity: its radial component is $O(r^{-1})$ and its tangential components are $O(r^{-2})$. At every fixed interior point of the RT curve, $v_L\to-\zeta_R$ as $L\to\infty$. The full equation-(4.37) form must now retain both terms,

$$\begin{align}
\Upsilon[v_L] =k_{[\xi_A,v_L]}[h_R+\mathcal L_{v_L}G] -k_{v_L}[\mathcal L_{\xi_A}h_R],
\end{align}$$

with the paper's orientation converted as above. Let $\mathcal u_L(x)$ denote the exact rational density produced by the companion script, so that $\int\Upsilon[v_L]=\int_{-1}^{1}\mathcal u_L(x)\,dx$. The script verifies symbolically that

$$\begin{align}
\lim_{L\to\infty}\mathcal u_L(x) =\frac{-5+13x^2-11x^4+3x^6}{16}
\end{align}$$

for every fixed $-1<x<1$. The transition layer has width $1-x=O(L^{-2})$; with $x=1-z/L^2$, the script finds

$$\begin{align}
\lim_{L\to\infty}\frac1{L^2} \mathcal u_L(1-z/L^2)=0,
\end{align}$$

so there is no finite leading anchor-layer remainder at that scale. High-precision integrations give

| $L$ | $\int_{-1}^{1}\Upsilon[v_L]$ |
|---:|---:|
| 2 | $-0.301129431945035$ |
| 4 | $-0.304663636532284$ |
| 8 | $-0.304764943840477$ |
| 16 | $-0.304762440002153$ |
| 32 | $-0.304761947834711$ |

Converging to $-32/105$. This supplies an explicit proper completion and a controlled numerical anchor-limit check. The local limiting integral is exact; interchange of the integral and $L\to\infty$ limit is supported here by the symbolic interior/boundary-layer checks and high-precision convergence, not yet by a general dominated-convergence theorem for mode sums.

## Current Status and Next Falsification Test

The originally proposed literal equality with the raw project surface charge is incorrect. For the explicit proper family above, the 2501 Iyer--Wald identity and the regulated limit instead give the project-convention statement

$$\begin{align}
\boxed{ E_{\mathrm{can,p}}[h_R+\mathcal L_vG]-E_{\mathrm{can,p}}[h_R] =\int_{\gamma_A}\Upsilon_{\mathrm p} =\frac{32}{105}
}
\end{align}$$

at the level of the controlled limit described above. This finite number is not produced by the project's raw $H_{\xi_A,\gamma_A}^{[0]}$, whose corrected integral is $-16/105$. The obstruction is now conceptual rather than divergent: the current finite-action setup has an asymptotic $C_\Gamma$ but no inner brick-wall action/joint prescription, and the raw full second-order Noether representative is not the canonical-energy gauge cocycle. Establishing a literal project-internal equality therefore still requires extending the inner variational principle; it cannot be obtained by relabelling the raw tensor.

Verified: the interval geometry, modular normalization, vector-level real Brown--Henneaux phase, fixed anchors, the local extremal-gauge condition, the Lee--Wald/Iyer--Wald divergence identity at three independent bulk points, the opposite project/2501 bulk-current sign, the polynomial local $\Upsilon$ integrand, its primitive and exact finite limit, the nonzero outer term for the global large completion, the explicit proper family $v_L$, its exact rational finite-$L$ density, its symbolic interior and anchor-layer limits, its high-precision integrated convergence, the background/linear/second-order Einstein coefficients at three high-precision points, and the regular raw-project tensor.

Assumptions: unit AdS radius; $a=\pi/4$; the stated real left-moving quadrature; the regulated completion $v_L=-L^2\zeta_R/(L^2+r^2)$; the displayed inner-normal orientation; the exact-diffeomorphism representative for $k_{\mathrm p}$ in the raw project comparison.
