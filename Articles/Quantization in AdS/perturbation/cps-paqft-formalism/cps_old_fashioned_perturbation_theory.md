# CPS-normalized old-fashioned perturbation theory

Date: 2026-07-15

## 0. Direct verdict

Old-fashioned perturbation theory (OFPT) is a stationary-background spectral frontend for the same renormalized theory described by the CPS/canonical and pAQFT notes. It is not a replacement for the covariant backend.

Correct under the following precise conditions:

1. The background and theory-defining boundary condition $\mathcal B$ are preserved by a complete timelike Killing field $\xi$. Its one-parameter flow $\Phi_t$ supplies the evolution parameter $t$; only in coordinates adapted to this flow do we write $\xi=\partial_t$.
2. The free CPS is closed, its symplectic flux through the boundary vanishes on allowed variations, and the vector field induced by $\xi$ on the free CPS is Hamiltonian. After fixing the normalization of $\xi$ and the additive constant in its charge, the corresponding generator $H_0\equiv H_{\xi,0}$ has a self-adjoint realization on the chosen state space.
3. The positive-energy modes are complete and CPS normalized, so they define a Fock representation of the free algebra.
4. The interaction contribution to the same $\xi$-generator is represented by a renormalized composite operator $V_{\rm R}$ with the bulk- and boundary-local counterterms required by the same renormalization conditions as the covariant calculation.
5. Spatially integrated matrix elements are finite after the stated regulator and vacuum subtraction. Exact degeneracies are treated by degenerate perturbation theory rather than by singular nondegenerate denominators.
6. The present construction is for gauge-free matter on a fixed stationary background. Constraints, BV-BRST, dynamical gravity, edge modes, and nonstationary backgrounds require additional structure.

Under these conditions the two operational frontends are

$$\begin{align}
\text{CPS-normalized free algebra} \longrightarrow
\begin{cases}
G_F^{\mathcal B},\ T_n^{\rm ren},\ \text{Wick diagrams}, & \text{covariant/time-ordered frontend},\\
H_0,\ V_{\rm R},\ \text{free-energy projectors}, & \text{OFPT spectral frontend}.
\end{cases}
\end{align}$$

They agree only when they use the same $\mathcal B$, state, composite-operator prescription, counterterms, and physical renormalization conditions.

## 1. CPS data and the Hamiltonian representation of the stationary flow

The CPS formalism does not by itself select a preferred time coordinate. Suppose instead that the stationary background and the allowed field space are preserved by a complete timelike Killing field $\xi$. Let $\Phi_t$ denote its one-parameter flow. Here $t$ is the group parameter along this chosen flow, not an additional preferred time built into the CPS. Once the normalization of $\xi$ has been fixed, adapted coordinates may be chosen so that $\xi=\partial_t$ and the stationary metric takes the form

$$\begin{align}
ds^2=-N^2dt^2+h_{ij}(dx^i+N^i dt)(dx^j+N^jdt).
\end{align}$$

For the scalar examples below the shift is zero. The free CPS form determines the causal propagator and the normalization of the positive-energy modes,

$$\begin{align}
\Omega_0[u_\alpha,u_\beta^*]&=-i\delta_{\alpha\beta}, & E^{\mathcal B}&=(\Omega_0^{\mathcal B})^{-1},\\
\widehat\phi(x)&=\sum_\alpha\left(a_\alpha u_\alpha(x)+a_\alpha^\dagger u_\alpha^*(x)\right), & [a_\alpha,a_\beta^\dagger]&=\delta_{\alpha\beta}.
\end{align}$$

The spacetime flow generated by $\xi$ induces a vector field $X_\xi$ on the free CPS. If this action is Hamiltonian, its charge satisfies

$$\begin{align}
\delta H_{\xi,0}=\iota_{X_\xi}\Omega_0, \qquad H_0\equiv H_{\xi,0}=\sum_\alpha\omega_\alpha\left(a_\alpha^\dagger a_\alpha+\frac12\right).
\end{align}$$

Thus $H_0$ is not an independent noncovariant input: it is the Hamiltonian representative of the $\xi$-flow on the free CPS. After quantization it generates translations of operators in the same parameter $t$. All later interaction-picture and OFPT formulas use this chosen flow parameter.

The free Fock basis supplies the resolution of the identity used in OFPT,

$$\begin{align}
H_0|A\rangle=E_A^{(0)}|A\rangle, \qquad \mathbf 1=\sum_A|A\rangle\langle A|,
\end{align}$$

with sums replaced by spectral integrals when the free spectrum has a continuous part. CPS therefore fixes not only the commutator kernel used by Feynman diagrams but also the normalization of every OFPT intermediate state.

## 2. The renormalized interaction Hamiltonian

Relative to the same $\xi$-flow, split the renormalized generator into free and interaction parts. For nonderivative scalar interactions in a zero-shift stationary metric, the interaction contribution is

$$\begin{align}
V_{\rm R}(t) =\int_{\Sigma_t}d^dx\,N\sqrt h\left[ \frac{g_3}{3!}[\phi^3]_{\rm R} +\frac{g_4}{4!}[\phi^4]_{\rm R} +\mathcal H_{{\rm ct},{\rm bulk}} \right] +\int_{\partial\Sigma_t}d^{d-1}x\,N_\partial\sqrt\sigma\, \mathcal H_{{\rm ct},\partial}^{\mathcal B}.
\end{align}$$

The symbol $[\phi^k]_{\rm R}$ denotes a renormalized composite field. Normal ordering with respect to the chosen free state may remove selected tadpole and vacuum contractions, but it is not a substitute for the full local renormalization prescription.

For derivative interactions, constrained systems, or a nonzero shift, $H_{\rm int}$ must be obtained by the Legendre transform after computing the corrected canonical momentum. In those cases $H_{\rm int}=-L_{\rm int}$ is not a valid general rule, and contact terms needed to match covariant time ordering must be retained.

## 3. Dyson expansion and intermediate-state denominators

In the interaction picture,

$$\begin{align}
V_I(t)&=e^{iH_0t}V_{\rm R}(0)e^{-iH_0t},\\
U_I(t_f,t_i)&=T\exp\left[-i\int_{t_i}^{t_f}dt\,V_I(t)\right].
\end{align}$$

At second order,

$$\begin{align}
U_I^{(2)}(t_f,t_i) =(-i)^2\int_{t_i}^{t_f}dt_1\int_{t_i}^{t_1}dt_2\,V_I(t_1)V_I(t_2).
\end{align}$$

Inserting the free resolution of the identity between the two vertices gives a sum over on-shell free multi-particle states. In an adiabatic scattering problem this produces the resolvent expansion

$$\begin{align}
T(E)&=V_{\rm R}+V_{\rm R}\frac{1}{E-H_0+i0}V_{\rm R} +V_{\rm R}\frac{1}{E-H_0+i0}V_{\rm R}\frac{1}{E-H_0+i0}V_{\rm R}+\cdots,\\
T_{fi}^{(2)}(E_i)&=\sum_A\frac{\langle f|V_{\rm R}|A\rangle\langle A|V_{\rm R}|i\rangle} {E_i-E_A^{(0)}+i0}.
\end{align}$$

Every OFPT term has:

1. a spatial matrix element at each vertex;
2. a free on-shell Fock state between adjacent vertices;
3. an energy-deficit denominator for each intermediate time interval;
4. a sum over all time orderings and all intermediate sectors allowed by the field operator.

Global energy need not be conserved at an individual ordered vertex. It is restored in the full transition amplitude, or replaced by the spectral eigenvalue condition in a bound-state calculation.

## 4. Energy levels and effective Hamiltonians

Write

$$\begin{align}
H=H_0+gV_1+g^2V_2+O(g^3),
\end{align}$$

where $V_1$ and $V_2$ include counterterms at the corresponding coupling order. For an isolated free eigenstate $|A\rangle$,

$$\begin{align}
E_A^{(1)}&=\langle A|V_1|A\rangle,\\
E_A^{(2)}&=\langle A|V_2|A\rangle +\sum_{B\neq A}\frac{|\langle B|V_1|A\rangle|^2}{E_A^{(0)}-E_B^{(0)}}.
\end{align}$$

Vacuum-subtracted gaps, rather than bare absolute vacuum energies, are normally the finite observables. If $P$ projects onto a degenerate free-energy sector and $Q=1-P$, the correct second-order operator on that sector is

$$\begin{align}
H_{\rm eff} =E_0P+gPV_1P +g^2\left[ PV_2P +PV_1Q\frac{1}{E_0-QH_0Q}QV_1P \right]+O(g^3).
\end{align}$$

One must diagonalize $H_{\rm eff}$ inside $P$. A zero denominator caused by degeneracy is a mixing or resonance problem; divergence of the high-energy sum over $B$ is a UV-renormalization problem. They require different repairs.

## 5. Relation to Feynman diagrams

For a stationary state, the CPS-normalized Feynman propagator already contains the time-ordered decomposition

$$\begin{align}
G_F(x,y) =\theta(t_x-t_y)\sum_\alpha u_\alpha(x)u_\alpha^*(y) +\theta(t_y-t_x)\sum_\alpha u_\alpha(y)u_\alpha^*(x).
\end{align}$$

In flat space with mostly-minus signature,

$$\begin{align}
\frac{i}{(q^0)^2-\omega_{\mathbf q}^2+i0} =\frac{i}{2\omega_{\mathbf q}} \left[ \frac{1}{q^0-\omega_{\mathbf q}+i0} -\frac{1}{q^0+\omega_{\mathbf q}-i0} \right].
\end{align}$$

After the vertex-time integrations, the two poles become distinct time orderings and energy denominators. A covariant graph is recovered only after all orderings, particle-number sectors, symmetry factors, and derivative-interaction contact terms have been included.

This correspondence has three limitations:

1. Individual OFPT terms are not manifestly covariant.
2. A hard energy cutoff on intermediate states is not automatically equivalent to a local covariant regulator.
3. The covariant and OFPT answers may be compared only after matching finite bulk and boundary counterterms.

## 6. Relation to the pAQFT backend

The pAQFT layer remains responsible for defining the local interacting theory through renormalized time-ordered products and the Bogoliubov map. In a stationary representation, OFPT evaluates matrix elements of that theory after choosing a state and a Hamiltonian spectral decomposition.

The matching chain is

$$\begin{align}
T_n^{\rm ren} \longrightarrow
[\mathcal H_{\rm int}]_{\rm R} \longrightarrow
V_{\rm R}(t) \longrightarrow
\langle A|V_{\rm R}|B\rangle \longrightarrow
\text{OFPT sums}.
\end{align}$$

Consequently:

1. causal factorization and the local counterterm classification are inherited from the $T_n^{\rm ren}$ layer;
2. the Principle of Perturbative Agreement must hold when a boundary-compatible quadratic term is moved between $H_0$ and $V_{\rm R}$;
3. Ward identities must be checked after summing the relevant intermediate sectors and inserting the renormalized current or charge;
4. OFPT does not extend the present scalar backend to gauge theory or gravity.

## 7. Global AdS interpretation

For a standard-quantized scalar in global AdS$_3$,

$$\begin{align}
\omega_{n,m}=\Delta+2n+|m|, \qquad E_{\{N_\alpha\}}^{(0)}=\sum_\alpha N_\alpha\omega_\alpha.
\end{align}$$

The discrete spectrum makes energy shifts and mixing matrices natural observables. In AdS/CFT units with radius one, the global Hamiltonian maps to the CFT dilatation operator, so a bulk energy shift of a primary state is its anomalous dimension.

Standard global AdS does not provide an ordinary asymptotic S-matrix because the boundary reflects excitations. The primary OFPT outputs are therefore:

1. finite-time transition amplitudes;
2. vacuum-subtracted energy gaps;
3. anomalous dimensions and operator mixing;
4. resonant effective Hamiltonians;
5. spectral self-energies and pole shifts.

A secular term $t e^{-iE_0t}$ is the perturbative expansion of an energy shift or a mixing problem only after the resonant sector has been identified. The corresponding resummation is encoded by diagonalizing $H_{\rm eff}$ or by an equivalent normal-form construction.

## 8. Practical workflow

For a new stationary scalar example:

1. Specify $(M,g,S,\mathcal B)$, the boundary action, allowed field space, state, and renormalization conditions.
2. Verify the boundary-compatible CPS and normalize all positive-energy modes.
3. Construct the self-adjoint $H_0$ and its Fock basis.
4. Define the renormalized spatial interaction $V_{\rm R}$, including bulk and boundary counterterms.
5. Compute spatial mode-overlap matrix elements and their selection rules.
6. Choose the target: finite-time transition amplitude, isolated energy shift, degenerate mixing matrix, or scattering amplitude when an adiabatic limit exists.
7. Insert the complete free-energy projectors and include every particle-number sector and time ordering.
8. Separate exact degeneracies from UV growth of the high-energy sum.
9. Match the regulator and finite counterterms to the covariant $T_n^{\rm ren}$ prescription.
10. Check the result against a Feynman/Wick calculation, a Schwinger-Dyson identity, or a Ward identity.

## 9. Scope boundary

Implemented here:

1. the CPS-to-Fock-to-resolvent construction for stationary gauge-free matter;
2. nondegenerate and degenerate energy perturbation theory;
3. the relation between OFPT denominators and covariant propagator time orderings;
4. the renormalization-matching conditions required by the pAQFT backend.

Not implemented here:

1. a general Hamiltonian construction for nonstationary backgrounds;
2. a hard-cutoff renormalization theorem for arbitrary AdS interactions;
3. BV-BRST or constrained OFPT;
4. dynamical gravity or boundary edge modes;
5. a proof of an all-orders global AdS spectral expansion.
