# Global AdS3 scalar boundary sources from CPS

Date: 2026-07-09

## 0. Direct verdict

Correct under the following precise conditions:

1. The theory is a real scalar on fixed global $\mathrm{AdS}_3$, with no gauge constraint, no dynamical gravity, and standard quantization.
2. The source is the non-normalizable boundary coefficient $\phi_{(0)}(t,\varphi)$, or $\phi_{(0)}(\tau,\varphi)$ after Wick rotation.
3. The quantized Hilbert space is still the normalizable CPS phase space $\mathcal S_0^{\rm norm}$ at $\phi_{(0)}=0$.
4. The bulk-to-boundary propagator is not an additional primitive. It is the differential of the regular Euclidean Dirichlet source-to-bulk map, viewed inside the independent renormalized asymptotic radial data space.

The CPS-first pipeline is

$$\begin{align}
\boxed{ \mathcal S_0^{\rm norm},\Omega_0 \to G_\nu \to \text{independent asymptotic radial data} \to \text{regular source-to-bulk graph }K_\Delta \to \text{boundary correlators}
}.
\end{align}$$

Here $G_\nu$ is the internal bulk propagator fixed by the normalizable CPS quantization, while $K_\Delta$ is the external boundary-source leg obtained by allowing $\delta\phi_{(0)}\neq0$.

## 1. Normalizable CPS data

Use global $\mathrm{AdS}_3$ coordinates $(t,r,\varphi)$:

$$\begin{align}
ds^2=-(1+r^2)dt^2+\frac{dr^2}{1+r^2}+r^2d\varphi^2, \qquad \varphi\sim\varphi+2\pi .
\end{align}$$

The Euclidean section is

$$\begin{align}
ds_E^2=(1+r^2)d\tau^2+\frac{dr^2}{1+r^2}+r^2d\varphi^2 .
\end{align}$$

Set the AdS radius to one and write

$$\begin{align}
m^2=\Delta(\Delta-2)=\nu^2-1, \qquad \Delta=1+\nu, \qquad \nu>0 .
\end{align}$$

Near the conformal boundary,

$$\begin{align}
\phi(t,r,\varphi) \sim r^{\Delta-2}\phi_{(0)}(t,\varphi) + r^{-\Delta}\phi_{(\Delta)}(t,\varphi) + \cdots .
\end{align}$$

This two-term notation is literal for generic noninteger $\nu$. When $\nu\in\mathbb N$, the asymptotic solution can also contain a holographic logarithm,

$$\begin{align}
\phi=r^{\Delta-2}(\phi_{(0)}+\cdots)+r^{-\Delta}\left[\phi_{(\Delta)}+\widetilde\phi_{(\Delta)}\log r\right]+\cdots,
\end{align}$$

where $\widetilde\phi_{(\Delta)}$ is a local functional of the source. In that case response and boundary-limit formulas below mean their renormalized finite part after subtracting the local holographic logarithms. These source-determined logarithms are distinct from the $\delta\nu\log r$ generated by a finite anomalous-dimension shift later in perturbation theory.

The normalizable free solution space is

$$\begin{align}
\mathcal S_0^{\rm norm} = \{\phi\text{ solves }P_\nu\phi=0,\ \phi_{(0)}=0\}.
\end{align}$$

On the global time slice $\Sigma_t$, the free scalar CPS symplectic form is

$$\begin{align}
\Omega_0(\delta_1\phi,\delta_2\phi) = \int_0^\infty dr\int_0^{2\pi}d\varphi\, \frac{r}{1+r^2} \left( \delta_1\phi\,\partial_t\delta_2\phi - \delta_2\phi\,\partial_t\delta_1\phi \right).
\end{align}$$

The normalizable modes are written directly in $(t,r,\varphi)$ coordinates as

$$\begin{align}
u_{n,m}(t,r,\varphi) = e^{-i\omega_{n,m}t}e^{im\varphi}R_{n,m}(r), \qquad \omega_{n,m}=\Delta+2n+|m|.
\end{align}$$

Write $N=(n,m)$. The CPS/KG normalization is

$$\begin{align}
\Omega_0[u_N,u_M^*]=-i\delta_{NM}, \qquad \Omega_0[u_N,u_M]=0.
\end{align}$$

The source-free quantum field is expanded as

$$\begin{align}
\hat\phi_0(X) = \sum_N\left(\hat a_Nu_N(X)+\hat a_N^\dagger u_N^*(X)\right), \qquad [\hat a_N,\hat a_M^\dagger]=\delta_{NM}.
\end{align}$$

Inverting $\Omega_0$ on $\mathcal S_0^{\rm norm}$ gives the causal kernel $E$. After choosing the positive-frequency splitting used in the free CPS quantization, the Euclidean or Feynman bulk propagator is the corresponding two-point kernel. In Euclidean $\mathrm{AdS}_3$,

$$\begin{align}
G_\nu(X,Y) = \frac{e^{-\nu\rho(X,Y)}}{4\pi\sinh\rho(X,Y)} .
\end{align}$$

Equivalently,

$$\begin{align}
G_\nu(X,Y)=\sum_N u_{N,E}(X)u_{N,E}^*(Y).
\end{align}$$

This is the propagator used for internal quantum lines.

## 2. Extend CPS to boundary-source variations

Boundary sources require a larger asymptotic solution space than $\mathcal S_0^{\rm norm}$:

$$\begin{align}
\delta\phi \sim r^{\Delta-2}\delta\phi_{(0)} + r^{-\Delta}\delta\phi_{(\Delta)} + \cdots .
\end{align}$$

For Euclidean boundary sources, use boundary coordinates $(\tau,\varphi)$ and write the measure explicitly. Put a cutoff at $r=r_c$. In the orientation convention of this note, the source one-form is represented by

$$\begin{align}
\Theta_{r_c}^{\partial}(\delta\phi) = -\int d\tau\,d\varphi\, r_c(1+r_c^2)\,\partial_r\phi\,\delta\phi + \delta S_{\rm ct,r_c}.
\end{align}$$

The factor $r_c(1+r_c^2)$ is the usual cutoff-surface measure and normal combination in $(\tau,r,\varphi)$ coordinates. The sign fixes the convention in which the final response is $+2\nu\phi_{(\Delta)}$. With the asymptotic expansion

$$\begin{align}
\phi = r^{\Delta-2}\phi_{(0)} + r^{-\Delta}\phi_{(\Delta)} + \cdots ,
\end{align}$$

the bare cutoff contribution has the form

$$\begin{align}
-r(1+r^2)\partial_r\phi\,\delta\phi =& \text{local divergent terms} + \Delta\,\phi_{(\Delta)}\delta\phi_{(0)} - (\Delta-2)\phi_{(0)}\delta\phi_{(\Delta)} + \cdots .
\end{align}$$

The leading divergence is local in the source. It is removed by the standard local counterterm, in this orientation,

$$\begin{align}
S_{\rm ct,r_c} = \frac{\Delta-2}{2} \int d\tau\,d\varphi\,\sqrt\gamma\,\phi^2 + S_{\rm local}[\phi_{(0)}],
\end{align}$$

whose variation contributes

$$\begin{align}
\delta S_{\rm ct,r_c} =& \text{opposite local divergent terms} + (\Delta-2) \left( \phi_{(\Delta)}\delta\phi_{(0)} + \phi_{(0)}\delta\phi_{(\Delta)} \right) + \delta S_{\rm local}[\phi_{(0)}] + \cdots .
\end{align}$$

Adding the two pieces cancels both the divergent term and the unwanted $\phi_{(0)}\delta\phi_{(\Delta)}$ term. The finite nonlocal part is

$$\begin{align}
\Theta_{\rm ren}^{\partial}(\delta\phi) = \int d\tau\,d\varphi\, 2(\Delta-1)\phi_{(\Delta)}\,\delta\phi_{(0)} + \delta S_{\rm local}[\phi_{(0)}].
\end{align}$$

Let $\mathcal P_{\rm asym}^{\rm ren}$ denote the renormalized asymptotic radial data space on which $\phi_{(0)}$ and $\phi_{(\Delta)}$ are independent coordinates. Since $\nu=\Delta-1$, the finite one-form on this space is

$$\begin{align}
\Theta_{\rm asym}^{\rm ren} =\int d\tau\,d\varphi\,2\nu\,\phi_{(\Delta)}\,\delta\phi_{(0)} +\delta S_{\rm local}[\phi_{(0)}],
\end{align}$$

and its renormalized asymptotic/radial symplectic form is

$$\begin{align}
\Omega_{\rm asym}^{\rm ren} =\delta\Theta_{\rm asym}^{\rm ren} =2\nu\int d\tau\,d\varphi\,\delta\phi_{(\Delta)}\wedge\delta\phi_{(0)}.
\end{align}$$

Here $\delta^2S_{\rm local}=0$; a finite local source counterterm changes the polarization by an exact one-form but does not change this two-form, assuming no boundary-of-boundary contribution.

For the Euclidean Dirichlet problem, interior regularity defines the solution graph

$$\begin{align}
\iota_{\rm reg}:\phi_{(0)}\longmapsto \left(\phi_{(0)},\phi_{(\Delta)}^{\rm reg}[\phi_{(0)}]\right) \subset\mathcal P_{\rm asym}^{\rm ren}.
\end{align}$$

Only after pullback to this graph is the one-form the variation of the renormalized on-shell action:

$$\begin{align}
\iota_{\rm reg}^{*}\Theta_{\rm asym}^{\rm ren}=\delta S_{E,\rm ren}^{\rm os}, \qquad \iota_{\rm reg}^{*}\Omega_{\rm asym}^{\rm ren}=\delta^2S_{E,\rm ren}^{\rm os}=0.
\end{align}$$

Thus $(\phi_{(0)},2\nu\phi_{(\Delta)})$ is a canonical pair on the independent asymptotic radial data space. On the regular Euclidean graph the response $\phi_{(\Delta)}^{\rm reg}[\phi_{(0)}]$ is a functional of the source and is not an independent coordinate. The graph is Lagrangian in $\mathcal P_{\rm asym}^{\rm ren}$; this does not imply that the Lorentzian normalizable CPS form $\Omega_0$ vanishes. The source-free Lorentzian quantum theory is recovered by imposing the theory-defining boundary condition $\phi_{(0)}=0$ and quantizing $\mathcal S_0^{\rm norm}$.

## 3. Source variations and $K_\Delta$

For a Euclidean boundary test source $\eta(\tau,\varphi)$, define the corresponding tangent variation to the regular Euclidean solution graph by

$$\begin{align}
\delta_\eta\phi_{(0)}(\tau,\varphi)=\eta(\tau,\varphi), \qquad P_\nu\,\delta_\eta\phi=0, \qquad \delta_\eta\phi\text{ is regular in the interior}.
\end{align}$$

The bulk component is linear in $\eta$:

$$\begin{align}
\delta_\eta\phi(X) = \int d\tau'\,d\varphi'\, K_\Delta(X;\tau',\varphi')\eta(\tau',\varphi') .
\end{align}$$

Therefore

$$\begin{align}
K_\Delta(X;\tau',\varphi') = \frac{\delta\phi_{\rm cl}(X)}{\delta\phi_{(0)}(\tau',\varphi')} .
\end{align}$$

It solves

$$\begin{align}
P_{\nu,X}K_\Delta(X;\tau',\varphi')=0
\end{align}$$

with distributional boundary behavior

$$\begin{align}
K_\Delta(X;\tau',\varphi') \sim r^{\Delta-2}\delta(\tau-\tau')\delta_{2\pi}(\varphi-\varphi') + r^{-\Delta}K_{(\Delta)}(\tau,\varphi;\tau',\varphi') + \cdots .
\end{align}$$

The source-response map is

$$\begin{align}
\mathcal G_\Delta(\tau,\varphi;\tau',\varphi') = 2\nu\,K_{(\Delta)}(\tau,\varphi;\tau',\varphi') = \frac{\delta\langle\mathcal O(\tau,\varphi)\rangle}{\delta\phi_{(0)}(\tau',\varphi')}
\end{align}$$

up to local contact terms.

## 4. Boundary limit of the CPS-normalized bulk propagator

The same $K_\Delta$ is obtained by pushing one endpoint of the CPS-normalized bulk propagator to the boundary:

$$\begin{align}
K_\Delta(X;\tau',\varphi') = 2\nu
\lim_{r'\to\infty} (r')^\Delta G_\nu(X; \tau',r',\varphi') .
\end{align}$$

In Lorentzian notation, define the boundary wavefunction of a normalized mode by

$$\begin{align}
b_N(t,\varphi) := \lim_{r\to\infty}r^\Delta u_N(t,r,\varphi).
\end{align}$$

The retarded source kernel has the mode-sum form

$$\begin{align}
K_R(X;t',\varphi') = 2\nu\,i\,\theta(t-t') \sum_N \left[ u_N(X)b_N^*(t',\varphi') - u_N^*(X)b_N(t',\varphi') \right].
\end{align}$$

The Euclidean kernel used below is the Wick-rotated positive-frequency sum,

$$\begin{align}
K_\Delta(X;\tau',\varphi') = 2\nu \sum_N u_{N,E}(X)b_{N,E}^*(\tau',\varphi') .
\end{align}$$

In Euclidean global coordinates, define

$$\begin{align}
P(X;\tau',\varphi') = \sqrt{1+r^2}\cosh(\tau-\tau') - r\cos(\varphi-\varphi') .
\end{align}$$

Then the standard-quantization kernel is

$$\begin{align}
K_\Delta(X;\tau',\varphi') = \frac{C_\Delta}{[2P(X;\tau',\varphi')]^\Delta}, \qquad C_\Delta = \frac{\Gamma(\Delta)}{\pi\Gamma(\Delta-1)} = \frac{\nu}{\pi}.
\end{align}$$

The normalization follows from the boundary limit of $G_\nu$:

$$\begin{align}
2\nu \lim_{R\to\infty} R^\Delta \frac{e^{-\nu\operatorname{arccosh}(RP)}}{4\pi\sqrt{(RP)^2-1}} = \frac{\nu}{\pi(2P)^\Delta}, \qquad \Delta=\nu+1 .
\end{align}$$

## 5. Boundary operator and extrapolate rule

The renormalized one-point function is the response coordinate

$$\begin{align}
\langle\mathcal O(\tau,\varphi)\rangle_{\phi_{(0)}} = 2\nu\,\phi_{(\Delta)}(\tau,\varphi)
\end{align}$$

up to local contact terms. The free two-point kernel is therefore

$$\begin{align}
\langle\mathcal O(\tau,\varphi)\mathcal O(\tau',\varphi')\rangle_0 = \frac{2\nu^2/\pi} {\left[ 2\left(\cosh(\tau-\tau')-\cos(\varphi-\varphi')\right) \right]^\Delta}.
\end{align}$$

Equivalently, for non-contact correlators,

$$\begin{align}
\mathcal O(t,\varphi) = 2\nu\lim_{r\to\infty}r^\Delta\hat\phi_0(t,r,\varphi).
\end{align}$$

This extrapolate rule is the boundary limit of the normalizable CPS field together with the same $2\nu$ source-response normalization.

## 6. Perturbative output rules

For bulk interactions, write

$$\begin{align}
\phi = \phi_{\rm cl}[\phi_{(0)}] + \phi_{\rm q}, \qquad (\phi_{\rm q})_{(0)}=0.
\end{align}$$

The CPS-first perturbative rules are:

1. Quantize only the normalizable field $\phi_{\rm q}$ using $\Omega_0$.
2. Use $G_\nu$ for internal bulk contractions of $\phi_{\rm q}$.
3. Use $K_\Delta$ for external boundary-source legs.
4. Integrate bulk vertices with the AdS volume element.
5. Add local bulk and boundary counterterms according to the chosen renormalization scheme.

Before taking a boundary limit, fix the mass and operator renormalization conditions. Let $G_{\rm ren}(X,Y)$ be the full renormalized bulk two-point function and $Z_{\mathcal O}$ the chosen finite normalization of the boundary operator.

If the physical conformal weight is held fixed, $m_{\rm phys}^2=\Delta(\Delta-2)$ and the finite mass counterterm is chosen so that the renormalized pole and falloff remain $\Delta$. Then

$$\begin{align}
\langle\mathcal O_R(x)\mathcal O_R(y)\rangle_{\rm ren} =Z_{\mathcal O}^2(2\nu)^2
\lim_{r,r'\to\infty}r^\Delta(r')^\Delta G_{\rm ren}(X,Y), \qquad \nu=\Delta-1.
\end{align}$$

For the one-loop $\phi^4$ tadpole in the companion note this condition is $\delta m_{\rm eff}^2=0$; the loop fixes the finite mass counterterm rather than the falloff.

If the finite mass shift is retained, define

$$\begin{align}
\nu_R=\nu+\delta\nu,\qquad \delta\nu=\frac{\delta m_{\rm eff}^2}{2\nu},\qquad \Delta_R=1+\nu_R.
\end{align}$$

The theory then uses the renormalized standard-quantization falloffs $r^{\Delta_R-2}$ and $r^{-\Delta_R}$, and

$$\begin{align}
\langle\mathcal O_R(x)\mathcal O_R(y)\rangle_{\rm ren} =Z_{\mathcal O}^2(2\nu_R)^2
\lim_{r,r'\to\infty}r^{\Delta_R}(r')^{\Delta_R}G_{\rm ren}(X,Y).
\end{align}$$

Since

$$\begin{align}
r^{\Delta_R}=r^\Delta\left[1+\delta\nu\log r+O(\lambda^2)\right],
\end{align}$$

applying the old fixed-$\Delta$ limit to $G^{(1)}$ alone leaves radial logarithms and does not produce the renormalized boundary correlator in this scheme. In either branch, the same result can equivalently be drawn with two external boundary legs defined using the corresponding renormalized falloff.

## 7. Fixed conventions

This note fixes the following conventions for later source computations:

1. Boundary expansion:

$$\begin{align}
\phi(t,r,\varphi) \sim r^{\Delta-2}\phi_{(0)}(t,\varphi) + r^{-\Delta}\phi_{(\Delta)}(t,\varphi).
\end{align}$$

2. Source-response normalization:

$$\begin{align}
\langle\mathcal O\rangle_{\phi_{(0)}}=2\nu\,\phi_{(\Delta)}
\end{align}$$

up to local contact terms.

3. Bulk-to-boundary kernel:

$$\begin{align}
K_\Delta(X;\tau',\varphi') = \frac{\nu/\pi} {\left[ 2\left( \sqrt{1+r^2}\cosh(\tau-\tau') - r\cos(\varphi-\varphi') \right) \right]^\Delta}.
\end{align}$$

4. Fixed-physical-$\Delta$ extrapolate rule:

$$\begin{align}
\mathcal O(t,\varphi) = 2\nu\lim_{r\to\infty}r^\Delta\hat\phi_0(t,r,\varphi)
\end{align}$$

for non-contact correlators, after subtracting local holographic logarithms when present. If the physical dimension is shifted, replace $(\nu,\Delta,\mathcal O)$ by $(\nu_R,\Delta_R,\mathcal O_R)$.

5. Diagrammatic roles:

$$\begin{align}
\text{internal bulk line}=G_\nu, \qquad \text{external boundary leg}=K_\Delta.
\end{align}$$
