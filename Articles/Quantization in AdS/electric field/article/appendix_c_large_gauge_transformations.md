# Appendix C: Large gauge transformations

In this appendix, we record the gauge-charge argument showing that gauge transformations with boundary parameter $\Lambda=O(r^{0})$ do not define additional physical degrees of freedom in the Maxwell phase space used in this paper.

Consider the infinitesimal gauge transformation

$$\begin{align}
\delta_{\Lambda}A_{\mu}&=\nabla_{\mu}\Lambda.
\end{align}$$

The corresponding Noether charge is

$$\begin{align}
Q_{\Lambda}
&=\int_{\Sigma}\mathrm{d}^{2}x\sqrt{\sigma}\,\tau_{\mu}F^{\mu\nu}\nabla_{\nu}\Lambda \\
&=\int_{\Sigma}\mathrm{d}^{2}x\sqrt{\sigma}\,\tau_{\mu}\left[\nabla_{\nu}\left(F^{\mu\nu}\Lambda\right)-\left(\nabla_{\nu}F^{\nu\mu}\right)\Lambda\right].
\end{align}$$

On the Maxwell solution space, the second term vanishes, so the charge reduces to a boundary contribution. Introducing the regulated slice $\Sigma_{r_{\infty}}$ and its large circle $H_{r_{\infty}}=\Sigma\cap\{r=r_{\infty}\}$, we obtain

$$\begin{align}
Q_{\Lambda}
&=\lim_{r_{\infty}\to\infty}\int_{\Sigma_{r_{\infty}}}\mathrm{d}^{2}x\sqrt{\sigma}\,\tau_{\mu}\nabla_{\nu}\left(F^{\mu\nu}\Lambda\right) \\
&=\lim_{r_{\infty}\to\infty}\int_{H_{r_{\infty}}}\mathrm{d}x\sqrt{h}\,\hat{n}_{a}\sigma^{a}{}_{\nu}\tau_{\mu}F^{\mu\nu}\Lambda.
\end{align}$$

Here $h$ is the induced metric on $H_{r_{\infty}}$, and $\hat n^a$ is the outward-pointing unit normal vector tangent to $\Sigma$. In the present geometry,

$$\begin{align}
h_{mn}\mathrm{d}x^{m}\mathrm{d}x^{n}&=r^{2}\mathrm{d}\phi^{2}, &
\hat{n}^{a}&=\left(\sqrt{1+r^{2}},0\right).
\end{align}$$

Therefore the boundary term is controlled by

$$\begin{align}
Q_{\Lambda}
&\propto \lim_{r_{\infty}\to\infty}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\mathrm{d}\phi\,rF^{tr}\Lambda.
\end{align}$$

Using the asymptotic conditions of Section 1,

$$\begin{align}
F^{tr}&=o(r^{-1}), &
\Lambda&=O(r^{0}),
\end{align}$$

we find

$$\begin{align}
Q_{\Lambda}
&\propto \lim_{r_{\infty}\to\infty}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\mathrm{d}\phi\,r\,o(r^{-1})\,O(r^{0}) \\
&=\lim_{r_{\infty}\to\infty}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\mathrm{d}\phi\,o(r^{0})=0.
\end{align}$$

Thus the gauge transformation generated by $\Lambda=O(r^{0})$ carries vanishing Noether charge on the phase space.

The same conclusion holds for the symplectic form. Since a pure gauge variation has vanishing field strength, the bilinear symplectic pairing with an arbitrary solution $A$ becomes

$$\begin{align}
\Omega[\nabla\Lambda,A]
&=\int_{\Sigma}\mathrm{d}^{2}x\sqrt{\sigma}\,\tau_{\mu}\left(F^{\mu\nu}_{\nabla\Lambda}A_{\nu}-F^{\mu\nu}\nabla_{\nu}\Lambda\right) \\
&=-\int_{\Sigma}\mathrm{d}^{2}x\sqrt{\sigma}\,\tau_{\mu}F^{\mu\nu}\nabla_{\nu}\Lambda \\
&=-Q_{\Lambda}=0.
\end{align}$$

Therefore these gauge directions are null directions of the symplectic form and do not belong to the physical oscillator sector. In particular, within the asymptotic phase space used in this manuscript, they do not label distinct winding sectors.
