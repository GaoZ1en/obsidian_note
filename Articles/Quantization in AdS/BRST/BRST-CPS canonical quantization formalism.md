# BRST-CPS Canonical Quantization Formalism

This note fixes the free and linear gauge-theory layer of the CPS canonical quantization formalism. The basic object is not an ordinary symplectic solution space with extra ghost oscillators. It is a differential graded symplectic system

$$\begin{align}
(\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{ext}},\Omega _{\mathrm{ext}},s,Q_{\mathrm{BRST}}),
\end{align}$$

where $s$ has ghost number $+1$, preserves the graded symplectic structure, and is generated by the BRST charge. Physical observables and states are obtained only after taking ghost-number-zero cohomology.

The construction is tested below by three complementary theories:

- Maxwell theory tests a propagating gauge field and the BRST quartet mechanism;
- U(1) Chern-Simons theory tests a topological bulk with physical boundary gauge modes;
- linearized Einstein gravity in AdS$_3$ tests a tensor gauge field whose local bulk cohomology is trivial but whose Brown-Henneaux boundary cohomology is non-trivial.

## 1. Three Related Phase Spaces

The classical gauge-invariant theory starts from the solution space $\mathcal{S}_{0}$ with presymplectic form $\Omega _{0}$. Once boundary conditions have been fixed, the proper gauge algebra is

$$\begin{align}
\mathfrak{g}_{\mathrm{prop}} :=\left\{\lambda\ \middle|\ R_{\lambda}\text{ preserves the boundary conditions and }
\iota _{R_{\lambda}}\Omega _{0,\mathrm{ren}}=0\right\}.
\end{align}$$

The ordinary reduced phase space is

$$\begin{align}
\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{red}}=\mathcal{S}_{0}/\mathfrak{g}_{\mathrm{prop}}.
\end{align}$$

The BRST construction replaces the quotient by a cohomological resolution. It enlarges the fields before reduction and keeps the gauge-fixing sector until the BRST cohomology is taken. These two procedures must not be mixed by first deleting the gauge modes from the bosonic solution space and then adding an unreduced ghost sector.

For perturbative quantization one should distinguish:

1. the BV odd symplectic space of bulk fields and antifields, used to encode the equations, gauge symmetry, and gauge fixing;
2. the even graded BFV or BRST-CPS symplectic space on Cauchy data, used for canonical brackets and the BRST charge;
3. the ghost-number-zero cohomology, which carries the physical observable algebra and the induced physical symplectic structure.

## 2. Field Content and Off-Shell Differential

For a bosonic irreducible linear gauge theory, write the fields as

$$\begin{align}
\Phi ^I=(\phi ^i,c^{\alpha},\bar c_{\alpha},B_{\alpha}).
\end{align}$$

Their ghost numbers are

$$
\begin{array}{c|cccc}
&\phi ^i&c^{\alpha}&\bar c_{\alpha}&B_{\alpha}\\
\hline
\operatorname{gh}&0&1&-1&0\\
\text{Grassmann parity}&0&1&1&0
\end{array}
$$

Let $R^{i}{}_{\alpha}$ be the linear gauge generator. The off-shell BRST differential is

$$\begin{align}
s\phi ^i&=R^{i}{}_{\alpha}c^{\alpha}, & sc^{\alpha}&=0,\\
s\bar c_{\alpha}&=B_{\alpha}, & sB_{\alpha}&=0.
\end{align}$$

Thus $s^{2}=0$ without using any equation of motion. For a gauge condition $\chi ^{\alpha}(\phi)=0$, choose the gauge-fixing fermion

$$\begin{align}
\Psi=\int _M\bar c_{\alpha}\left(\chi ^{\alpha}(\phi)+\frac{\xi}{2}B^{\alpha}\right).
\end{align}$$

The gauge-fixed action is

$$\begin{align}
S_{\Psi}=S_{0}+s\Psi,
\end{align}$$

and remains BRST invariant off shell. Eliminating $B$ is allowed after the off-shell complex and its boundary conditions have been fixed, but it turns some later identities into on-shell identities.

## 3. Graded Covariant Phase Space

Use the trigrading by spacetime form degree, field-space form degree, and ghost number. The differentials $d$, $\delta$, and $s$ obey their graded commutation relations. Vary the gauge-fixed Lagrangian as

$$\begin{align}
\delta L_{\Psi}=E_{I}\,\delta\Phi ^I+d\theta _{\mathrm{ext}}, \qquad \omega _{\mathrm{ext}}=\delta\theta _{\mathrm{ext}}, \qquad \Omega _{\mathrm{ext}}=\int _{\Sigma}\omega _{\mathrm{ext}}.
\end{align}$$

The form $\Omega _{\mathrm{ext}}$ is an even graded symplectic form. A non-degenerate ghost-antighost pairing is required: it supplies the graded canonical brackets needed for the BRST charge and is not a physical positive-definite norm.

If

$$\begin{align}
sL_{\Psi}=dB_{s},
\end{align}$$

the BRST Noether current is

$$\begin{align}
J_{s}=\iota _s\theta _{\mathrm{ext}}-B_{s}.
\end{align}$$

With the Hamiltonian convention $\iota _{X_F}\Omega=-\delta F$, the canonical relation takes the form

$$\begin{align}
\iota _s\Omega _{\mathrm{ext}} =-\delta Q_{\mathrm{BRST}}+\mathcal{F}_{\partial\Sigma}.
\end{align}$$

For proper gauge transformations and closed boundary conditions, $\mathcal{F}_{\partial\Sigma}=0$. If the boundary term is non-zero, it is part of the asymptotic or edge charge and cannot be discarded when defining the physical cohomology.

The consistency conditions used in this note are

$$\begin{align}
s^{2}&=0, & \mathcal{L}_{s}\Omega _{\mathrm{ext}}&=0, & \{Q_{\mathrm{BRST}},Q_{\mathrm{BRST}}\}_{\mathrm{gr}}&=0.
\end{align}$$

## 4. Physical Cohomology and the Induced Symplectic Form

Classically, the BRST complex resolves the algebra of functions on the reduced phase space:

$$\begin{align}
\mathcal{O}_{\mathrm{phys}} \simeq H^{0}(s,\mathcal{O}_{\mathrm{ext}}) \simeq C^{\infty}(\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{red}}),
\end{align}$$

under the usual regularity and exactness assumptions. After canonical quantization,

$$\begin{align}
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{phys}} &=H^{0}(Q_{\mathrm{BRST}},\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{ext}})\\
&=\frac{\ker\left(Q_{\mathrm{BRST}}:\mathcal{H}^{0}_{\mathrm{ext}}\to \mathcal{H}^{1}_{\mathrm{ext}}\right)}
{\operatorname{im}\left(Q_{\mathrm{BRST}}:\mathcal{H}^{-1}_{\mathrm{ext}}\to \mathcal{H}^{0}_{\mathrm{ext}}\right)}.
\end{align}$$

BRST invariance makes the symplectic pairing descend to cohomology. For $s$-closed degree-zero representatives $u$ and $v$,

$$\begin{align}
\Omega _{H^{0}}([u],[v])=\Omega _{\mathrm{ext}}(u,v).
\end{align}$$

Changing $u$ by an exact representative does not change this pairing because

$$\begin{align}
\Omega _{\mathrm{ext}}(sw,v) =\pm\Omega _{\mathrm{ext}}(w,sv)=0,
\end{align}$$

provided the relevant boundary flux vanishes. The benchmark condition is therefore not that every unphysical variable be a null vector of $\Omega _{\mathrm{ext}}$, but that the induced form on $H^{0}$ agree with the reduced CPS form.

## 5. Contractible Quartet Normal Form

For each non-zero Faddeev-Popov mode, the non-physical sector can be put into a canonical normal form with coordinates

$$\begin{align}
(\lambda,b;c,\bar c),
\end{align}$$

where $\lambda$ is a gauge coordinate and $b$ is the gauge-fixing or scalar-polarization coordinate. Choose the graded canonical brackets

$$\begin{align}
\{\lambda,b\}&=1, & \{\bar c,c\}_{\mathrm{gr}}&=1,
\end{align}$$

and define $sF=\{F,\mathcal{Q}\}_{\mathrm{gr}}$ with

$$\begin{align}
\mathcal{Q}=bc.
\end{align}$$

Then

$$\begin{align}
s\lambda&=c, & sc&=0, & s\bar c&=b, & sb&=0.
\end{align}$$

The associated graded symplectic form is schematically

$$\begin{align}
\Omega _{\mathrm{quartet}} =\delta b\wedge\delta\lambda +\delta\bar c\wedge\delta c,
\end{align}$$

with the wedge products understood in the total grading. Define the contracting homotopy

$$\begin{align}
\kappa=\lambda\frac{\partial}{\partial c} +\bar c\frac{\partial}{\partial b}.
\end{align}$$

It obeys

$$\begin{align}
\{s,\kappa\}=N_{\mathrm{quartet}},
\end{align}$$

where $N_{\mathrm{quartet}}$ counts quartet excitations. Hence, if $s\mathcal{X}=0$ and $N_{\mathrm{quartet}}\mathcal{X}=n\mathcal{X}$ with $n>0$,

$$\begin{align}
\mathcal{X}=s\left(\frac{1}{n}\kappa\mathcal{X}\right).
\end{align}$$

The positive-excitation subcomplex is therefore acyclic even though its symplectic form is non-degenerate. The full quartet Fock factor retains only its vacuum class at ghost number zero. Thus no cohomology class is generated by a single ghost or by a ghost-number-zero ghost-antighost composite.

## 6. Canonical Quantization

First quantize the complete graded symplectic system. Bosonic coordinates obey commutators and odd ghost coordinates obey graded anticommutators determined by $\Omega _{\mathrm{ext}}^{-1}$. Promote $Q_{\mathrm{BRST}}$ to a ghost-number-one operator satisfying

$$\begin{align}
Q_{\mathrm{BRST}}^{2}=0, \qquad [Q_{\mathrm{BRST}},\widehat{\mathcal{O}}]_{\mathrm{gr}} =i\widehat{s\mathcal{O}}.
\end{align}$$

The extended state space is generally indefinite. If $Q_{\mathrm{BRST}}$ is compatible with the adjoint operation, exact states pair trivially with closed states, so the inner product descends to the quotient. The quartet identity implies that the physical Fock space is generated only by creation operators representing non-trivial classes in $H^{0}(Q_{\mathrm{BRST}})$.

## 7. Boundary Conditions, Zero Modes, and Topology

The ghost domain is part of the definition of the theory. It must satisfy all of the following:

1. $R_{c}\phi$ is tangent to the chosen field boundary conditions;
2. the BRST transformations preserve the boundary conditions of $\bar c$ and $B$;
3. the symplectic flux and the Hamiltonian boundary term are controlled;
4. the Faddeev-Popov operator maps the selected domain into the selected gauge-condition space.

The proper gauge algebra is determined by the renormalized presymplectic kernel, not only by whether the gauge parameter vanishes pointwise at the boundary. The physical boundary symmetry algebra is

$$\begin{align}
\mathfrak{g}_{\mathrm{bdy}} =\mathfrak{g}_{\mathrm{allowed}}/\mathfrak{g}_{\mathrm{prop}}.
\end{align}$$

Parameters in $\mathfrak{g}_{\mathrm{bdy}}$ must not be included as bulk BRST redundancies. They instead act by non-trivial boundary charges or are incorporated through a boundary BFV complex.

If the Faddeev-Popov operator has a kernel, the quartet homotopy does not exist on that kernel. The remaining modes must be classified as one of:

- reducibility parameters, such as a constant U(1) parameter with $dc=0$;
- residual proper gauge transformations, which require a separate quotient;
- topological cohomology classes;
- physical edge or asymptotic-symmetry modes.

## 8. Validation Contract

Each model benchmark must verify the same list.

| Gate | Required evidence |
|---|---|
| Complex | The allowed fields and boundary conditions are closed under $s$. |
| Nilpotency | $s^{2}=0$ off shell, or the exact on-shell limitation is stated. |
| Symplectic compatibility | $\mathcal{L}_{s}\Omega _{\mathrm{ext}}=0$ and the boundary term in $\iota_s\Omega$ is identified. |
| Quartet | Every non-zero unphysical mode belongs to a contractible BRST subcomplex. |
| Cohomology | $H^{0}$ reproduces the independently known reduced CPS degrees of freedom. |
| Pairing | The form induced on $H^{0}$ equals the reduced or renormalized CPS form. |
| Zero modes | Faddeev-Popov zero modes and topology are treated separately. |
| Boundary modes | Charge-carrying large gauge transformations survive in boundary cohomology. |

## 9. Interface with CPS-pAQFT

At the free level, the gauge-fixed operator supplies a Green-hyperbolic extended complex and a graded causal propagator. The extended free algebra is quantized first, and its physical algebra is

$$\begin{align}
\mathfrak{A}_{\mathrm{phys}}=H^{0}(\widehat{s},\mathfrak{A}_{\mathrm{ext}}).
\end{align}$$

The CPS form fixes the causal bracket and the normalization of the BRST charge. Interactions are introduced only after this free cohomological layer is stable. The interacting extension requires the BV quantum master equation, renormalized time-ordered products, and anomaly control; these are not consequences of the free quartet argument alone.

## Scope

The validated scope is free or linear irreducible gauge theory around a fixed background, including charge-carrying boundaries. Non-linear gauge algebras, reducible theories, Gribov problems, interacting BRST anomalies, and the full renormalized BV-pAQFT construction require additional checks.
