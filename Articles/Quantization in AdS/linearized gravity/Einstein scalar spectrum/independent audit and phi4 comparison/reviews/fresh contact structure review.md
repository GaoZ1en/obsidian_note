# Fresh independent review: contact rank, exact kernels, and left–right exchange

## Decisive conclusions

The following results were derived without reading any existing candidate/structure note, package, or other script. Only the math-computation skill and its Sage reference were read before exact CAS checks.

Let
\[
\mathcal A_{N,P}=\{f(z_1,\ldots,z_N):D_Lf=0,\ \deg f=P\},\qquad
\mathcal H_{N,P}=(\mathcal A_{N,P}\otimes\mathcal A_{N,P})^{S_N},
\]
and write \(D_N(P)=\dim\mathcal H_{N,P}\), \(r_N(P)=\operatorname{rank}W\), and \(T=\binom N2\). All statements below use the Bargmann norm and pair Casimir in the question, real \(\Delta>1\), and finite, strictly positive \(c_k\).

1. **The exact kernel is independent of the positive coefficients \(c_k\).** At every finite \(P\), it is an intersection of exact matched-channel kernels, not a statement about eigenvalues merely becoming small.
2. **Useful finite-sector bounds hold for every \(N\ge3\):** define
   \[
   b_M(n)=\dim\bigl[(\operatorname{Sym}^n\mathbb C^M)^{\otimes2}\bigr]^{S_M},
   \quad S_M\text{ acts by coordinate permutations},
   \qquad R_N(P)=\sum_{n=0}^{P}b_{N-2}(n).
   \]
   Then
   \[
   \boxed{\left\lceil\frac{R_N(P)}{\binom N2}\right\rceil
   \le r_N(P)\le\min\{D_N(P),R_N(P)\}.}
   \]
3. **At fixed \(N\ge3\), the rank is \(\Theta(P^{2N-5})\), whereas the physical dimension is \(\Theta(P^{2N-4})\).** Consequently the fraction of *exact* zero modes tends to one, with nonzero fraction \(\Theta(P^{-1})\). This is stronger than an upper rank bound alone.
4. **For \(N=3\), the rank can be determined exactly:**
   \[
   \boxed{r_3(0)=1,\qquad r_3(P)=P\quad(P\ge1).}
   \]
   This holds for every \(\Delta>1\), not only generically in \(\Delta\). The exact finite-sector zero fraction is given below.
5. **Global left–right antisymmetry is sufficient for a zero mode for \(N=3\), but not for \(N=4\).** An explicit counterexample works for every \(N\ge4\), every \(P\ge3\), every \(\Delta>1\), and all positive \(c_k\).
6. **No all-\(N\ge4\) exact finite rank formula or sharp leading rank coefficient is claimed here.** The all-\(N\) inequalities and limiting exact-zero fraction are proved; the sharper \(N=3\) formula is proved separately.

## 1. Primary spaces and the single-pair decomposition

The left primary space is the degree-\(P\) polynomial representation on the standard \((N-1)\)-dimensional representation of \(S_N\):
\[
\mathcal A_{N,P}\simeq\operatorname{Sym}^P(\mathrm{Std}_{S_N}),\qquad
 d_N(P):=\dim\mathcal A_{N,P}=\binom{P+N-2}{N-2}.
\]
Translation-invariant coordinates give this identification directly; it is independent of \(\Delta\). The inherited Hilbert inner product is positive, and all permutation matrices and Casimir projectors can be represented in a real orthonormal basis.

For pair \(12\), the Casimir eigenvalues are
\[
\lambda_k=(\Delta+k)(\Delta+k-1),\qquad k=0,\ldots,P,
\]
which are strictly increasing under the assumed \(\Delta>1\). Write \(E_k\) for the projector on the left primary space with this eigenvalue. Its eigenspace has dimension
\[
 m_{N,P}(k)=\binom{P-k+N-3}{N-3}.
\]
One way to see the multiplicity is the pair Clebsch–Gordan decomposition
\[
D^+_{\Delta/2}\otimes D^+_{\Delta/2}
 =\bigoplus_{k\ge0}D^+_{\Delta+k}.
\]
After choosing \(k\), couple that fused site to the remaining \(N-2\) sites and impose the total-primary condition. The remaining degree is \(n=P-k\), and there are \(N-2\) independent relative coordinates.

More precisely, under the spectator permutation group \(S_{N-2}\), this multiplicity space is
\[
\mathcal B_{P-k}\simeq\operatorname{Sym}^{P-k}(\mathbb C^{N-2}_{\rm perm}).
\]
The fused-site weight depends on \(k\), but this spectator representation does not: the spectators' coordinates relative to the fused coordinate transform as the permutation representation. Pair exchange \((12)\) acts by \((-1)^k\).

These facts can also be obtained using pair lowest vectors \((z_1-z_2)^k\) and pair raising descendants. The raising operator
\[
J^+_{12}=z_1^2\partial_1+z_2^2\partial_2+\Delta(z_1+z_2)
\]
preserves the ideal generated by \((z_1-z_2)^k\), so every pair-\(k\) vector is divisible by that power.

## 2. Positivity reduces the rank question to one pair

On \(\mathcal K=\mathcal A_{N,P}\otimes\mathcal A_{N,P}\), define
\[
 S=\frac1{N!}\sum_{g\in S_N}U_g\otimes U_g,
 \qquad Q=\sum_{k=0}^P E_k\otimes E_k.
\]
Both are orthogonal projectors. The physical space is \(\operatorname{ran}S\). For \(v\) physical, positivity and permutation symmetry give
\[
\langle v,Wv\rangle
 =T\sum_{k=0}^P c_k\|(E_k\otimes E_k)v\|^2.
\]
Therefore
\[
\ker(W|_{\mathcal H_{N,P}})=\ker(Q|_{\mathcal H_{N,P}}),
\qquad
r_N(P)=\operatorname{rank}(QS)=\operatorname{rank}(SQS).
\]
Only positivity, not a common lower bound on the \(c_k\), was used. The sum is finite on each fixed-degree sector.

Let \(H=S_2\times S_{N-2}\) be the stabilizer of pair \(12\). Since \(S\) lands in \(H\)-invariants and \(Q\) commutes with \(H\),
\[
\operatorname{ran}(QS)\subseteq(\operatorname{ran}Q)^H.
\]
On each matched \(k\) block, the pair exchange acts as \((-1)^{2k}=1\); hence
\[
\dim(\operatorname{ran}Q)^H
 =\sum_{k=0}^P\dim(\mathcal B_{P-k}\otimes\mathcal B_{P-k})^{S_{N-2}}
 =R_N(P).
\]
This proves the upper rank bound.

For the lower bound, all eigenvalues of \(SQS\) lie in \([0,1]\), so
\[
 r_N(P)\ge\operatorname{Tr}(SQ).
\]
In a real basis,
\[
\operatorname{Tr}(SQ)
 =\frac1{N!}\sum_{g\in S_N}\sum_{k=0}^P
       \bigl[\operatorname{Tr}(E_kU_g)\bigr]^2.
\]
**Every term is nonnegative.** Retaining only \(g\in H\), and using the spectator characters, gives
\[
\operatorname{Tr}(SQ)
 \ge\frac{2(N-2)!}{N!}\sum_{k=0}^P b_{N-2}(P-k)
 =\frac{R_N(P)}{T}.
\]
This proves the lower bound and explains why equal left/right degree and identical real representations are important. For unequal degrees the corresponding products of traces need not have a common sign.

## 3. Explicit character formulas and fixed-\(N\) asymptotics

These bounds require only finite character sums, not diagonalization. For a partition \(\lambda\vdash m\) with \(a_j\) cycles of length \(j\), set
\[
 z_\lambda=\prod_j j^{a_j}a_j!.
\]
Then
\[
 D_N(P)=\sum_{\lambda\vdash N}\frac1{z_\lambda}
 \left([t^P]\frac{1-t}{\prod_j(1-t^j)^{a_j}}\right)^2,
\]
\[
 b_M(n)=\sum_{\lambda\vdash M}\frac1{z_\lambda}
 \left([t^n]\frac1{\prod_j(1-t^j)^{a_j}}\right)^2.
\]
Equivalently, \(b_M(n)\) counts \(S_M\)-orbits of pairs of weak \(M\)-compositions of \(n\).

For fixed \(N\ge3\), the identity term dominates the first character sum:
\[
 D_N(P)=\frac1{N!}\binom{P+N-2}{N-2}^{\!2}+O(P^{2N-6})
 =\frac{P^{2N-4}}{N!((N-2)!)^2}+O(P^{2N-5}).
\]
Indeed, every nonidentity permutation on the standard representation has maximal eigenvalue multiplicity at most \(N-2\), so its degree-\(P\) symmetric-power character is \(O(P^{N-3})\).

Similarly,
\[
 R_N(P)=\frac{P^{2N-5}}
 {(N-2)!((N-3)!)^2(2N-5)}+O(P^{2N-6}).
\]
For \(N=3\), this statement follows directly from \(R_3(P)=P+1\).

Define
\[
 A_N=\frac{N(N-1)(N-2)^2}{2N-5},\qquad
 B_N=\frac{2(N-2)^2}{2N-5}.
\]
The proven all-\(N\) asymptotic sandwich is
\[
 \frac{B_N}{P}\bigl(1+O(P^{-1})\bigr)
 \le\frac{r_N(P)}{D_N(P)}
 \le\frac{A_N}{P}\bigl(1+O(P^{-1})\bigr).
\]
In particular,
\[
\boxed{\frac{\dim\ker W}{D_N(P)}\longrightarrow1,
\qquad 1-\frac{\dim\ker W}{D_N(P)}=\Theta(P^{-1}).}
\]
The exact finite-sector zero fraction obeys
\[
\max\{0,1-R_N(P)/D_N(P)\}
\le\frac{\dim\ker W}{D_N(P)}
\le1-\frac{\lceil R_N(P)/T\rceil}{D_N(P)}.
\]
These are fixed-\(N\) results; they do not justify a simultaneous large-\(N\), large-\(P\) limit.

For example,
\[
R_4(P)=\frac{(P+1)(P+2)(2P+3)}{12}
       +\frac{\lfloor P/2\rfloor+1}{2},
\]
so \(\lceil R_4(P)/6\rceil\le r_4(P)\le\min(D_4(P),R_4(P))\). The asymptotic nonzero fraction is between \((8/3)P^{-1}(1+O(P^{-1}))\) and \(16P^{-1}(1+O(P^{-1}))\); a sharp coefficient is not established by this argument.

## 4. Exact rank theorem for \(N=3\)

Here every pair-\(k\) eigenspace in \(\mathcal A_{3,P}\) is one-dimensional. Let
\[
 A=C_{12},\quad B=C_{23},\quad C=C_{13},\qquad
 A+B+C=sI,
\]
where
\[
 s=P(P+3\Delta-1)+3\Delta(\Delta-1).
\]
Identify \(\mathcal A_{3,P}\otimes\mathcal A_{3,P}\) with matrices using a real orthonormal basis. Then \(S_3\) acts by conjugation and the matched-pair subspace consists of matrices diagonal in the \(A\)-eigenbasis. Since \(A\) has \(P+1\) distinct eigenvalues, every such matrix is \(f(A)\) for a unique polynomial of degree at most \(P\).

The adjoint of the single-pair restriction is group averaging (twirling). On these diagonal matrices it is
\[
 \Gamma(f(A))=\frac13\bigl(f(A)+f(B)+f(C)\bigr).
\]
We now show that, for \(P\ge1\), its kernel is exactly the span of \(f(t)=t-s/3\).

### 4.1 Irreducible tridiagonality, derived directly

Use translation-invariant coordinates
\[
 r=z_1-z_2,\qquad u=(z_1+z_2)/2-z_3.
\]
On degree-\(P\) polynomials,
\[
 A=r^2\partial_r^2-\frac{r^2}{4}\partial_u^2
   +2\Delta r\partial_r+\Delta(\Delta-1).
\]
Its eigenvector \(v_k\), with eigenvalue \(\lambda_k\), can be normalized to
\[
 v_k=r^ku^{P-k}+O(r^{k+2}).
\]
Also
\[
 B=-(u-r/2)^2(\partial_r\partial_u-\tfrac12\partial_u^2)
   +\Delta(u-r/2)(-\partial_r+\tfrac32\partial_u)
   +\Delta(\Delta-1).
\]
This lowers \(r\)-valuation by at most one. Therefore \(Bv_k\) has no component \(v_j\) with \(j<k-1\). Self-adjointness and orthogonality of the \(v_k\) imply the other band bound, so \(B\) is tridiagonal in this basis. Its coefficient of \(v_{k-1}\) is
\[
 -k(P-k+\Delta),\qquad k=1,\ldots,P,
\]
which never vanishes. Thus \(B\) is irreducibly tridiagonal. In this same basis \(C=sI-A-B\), so its off-diagonal entries are minus those of \(B\).

### 4.2 There is only one twirling relation

Suppose \(f(A)+f(B)+f(C)=0\), with
\(f(t)=a_mt^m+a_{m-1}t^{m-1}+\cdots\), \(a_m\ne0\), \(m\le P\).

If \(m\ge2\) is even, the \(m\)-th off-diagonal of \(f(B)+f(C)\) is
\[
 2a_m\prod_{j=i}^{i+m-1}B_{j,j+1},
\]
which is nonzero. This is impossible.

If \(m\ge3\) is odd, the leading off-diagonal cancels, but the \((m-1)\)-th off-diagonal equals a nonzero product of adjacent \(B\)-entries times
\[
 a_m\sum_{j=i}^{i+m-1}(s-\lambda_j)+2a_{m-1}.
\]
There are at least two consecutive allowed values of \(i\), since \(m\le P\). The sums differ by
\(\lambda_{i+m}-\lambda_i>0\), so they cannot both vanish. This is also impossible.

Hence \(m\le1\). The sole relation is \(A+B+C=sI\), namely \(f(t)\propto t-s/3\). The diagonal space has dimension \(P+1\), so
\[
\operatorname{rank}\Gamma=P\quad(P\ge1).
\]
The ranks of the restriction and its adjoint agree, proving \(r_3(P)=P\). At \(P=0\), the physical sector is one-dimensional and has nonzero interaction.

### 4.3 Exact zero-mode fraction for \(N=3\)

The \(S_3\) character formula reduces to
\[
 D_3(P)=\frac{(P+1)^2+3\mathbf1_{P\text{ even}}
                   +2\mathbf1_{P\bmod3\ne2}}{6}.
\]
Thus, for \(P\ge1\),
\[
\boxed{\frac{\dim\ker W}{D_3(P)}
=1-\frac{6P}{(P+1)^2+3\mathbf1_{P\text{ even}}
                   +2\mathbf1_{P\bmod3\ne2}}.}
\]
Its expansion is
\[
 1-\frac6P+\frac{12}{P^2}+O(P^{-3}).
\]
Global exchange-odd states are therefore only part of the large kernel; the exact kernel is not exhausted by this symmetry argument.

## 5. Left–right antisymmetry: sufficient for \(N=3\), false for \(N\ge4\)

Let \(\tau\) exchange the two chiral polynomial factors. For \(N=3\), each matched block is spanned by \(v_k\otimes v_k\), which is \(\tau\)-even. Hence
\[
 \tau F=-F\quad\Longrightarrow\quad QF=0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad WF=0.
\]
For \(N\ge4\), the pair-\(k\) multiplicity is usually larger than one. A matched block then contains exchange-odd tensors, so the preceding argument does not extend.

An explicit physical counterexample uses centered variables
\[
 x_i=z_i-\frac1N\sum_jz_j,\qquad
 y_i=w_i-\frac1N\sum_jw_j,
\]
and
\[
 s_2(x)=\sum_i x_i^2,\quad s_2(y)=\sum_i y_i^2,
\]
\[
\boxed{F=s_2(y)\sum_i x_i^3y_i-s_2(x)\sum_i x_iy_i^3.}
\]
It is translation invariant in both chiralities, simultaneously \(S_N\)-invariant, of bidegree \((3,3)\), and odd under \(x\leftrightarrow y\).

For any \(N\ge4\), choose
\[
 x=(0,0,1,-1,0,\ldots),\qquad
 y=(1,1,0,-2,0,\ldots).
\]
These vectors are already centered, and both pair coincidences hold: \(x_1=x_2\), \(y_1=y_2\). Nevertheless,
\[
 s_2(x)=2,\quad s_2(y)=6,\quad
 \sum_i x_i^3y_i=2,\quad\sum_i x_iy_i^3=8,
\qquad F=-4.
\]
Every pair-\(k>0\) component vanishes at the corresponding coincidence because it is divisible by the pair difference. Therefore the double coincidence receives contributions only from the matched \((k_L,k_R)=(0,0)\) block. Its nonzero value proves
\[
 (E_0\otimes E_0)F\ne0,
 \qquad \langle F,WF\rangle>0.
\]
Thus \(F\) is not a zero mode, uniformly for all \(\Delta>1\) and positive coefficients.

This failure persists at every \(P\ge3\): set
\[
 F_P=F\left(\sum_i x_iy_i\right)^{P-3}.
\]
It remains physical and exchange odd, and its value at the same double coincidence is
\(-4\,2^{P-3}\ne0\). In contrast, the displayed degree-three \(F\) is identically zero when \(N=3\).

## 6. Exact kernels versus scale-dependent soft eigenvalues

At each fixed \(P\), strict positivity of every relevant \(c_k\) makes the kernel statement exact. It gives no uniform positive lower bound on the remaining spectrum as \(P\to\infty\).

The distinction can be made quantitative. Suppose, for example, that \(c_k\to0\). For a fixed cutoff \(K\), split
\[
 W=W_{\le K}+W_{>K}.
\]
The same single-pair argument gives
\[
 \operatorname{rank}W_{\le K}
 \le\sum_{k=0}^{\min(K,P)}b_{N-2}(P-k)
 =O_K(P^{2N-6}),
\]
while
\[
 0\le W_{>K}\le T\sup_{k>K}c_k\,I.
\]
Min–max therefore implies that at most \(O_K(P^{2N-6})\) eigenvalues exceed \(T\sup_{k>K}c_k\). Since the full positive rank is \(\Theta(P^{2N-5})\), for every fixed positive threshold, **all but a vanishing fraction of the positive eigenvalues lie below that threshold** as \(P\to\infty\). Explicitly, the positive-only empirical measure
\[
 \mu_P^+=\frac1{r_N(P)}\sum_{\lambda_j(W)>0}\delta_{\lambda_j(W)}
\]
satisfies
\[
 \boxed{\mu_P^+\ \Rightarrow\ \delta_0\qquad(c_k\to0).}
\]
The tail estimate proves that its mass outside every neighborhood of zero tends to zero; the support is uniformly bounded because a sequence converging to zero is bounded. These soft eigenvalues are still not exact zero modes at finite \(P\). This positive-only weak law is a separate result from the exact-zero fraction tending to one.

Conversely, for \(c_k=1\), write \(W_0\) for the interaction. Then
\[
 \operatorname{Tr}_{\rm phys}W_0=T\operatorname{Tr}(SQ)\ge R_N(P),
 \qquad 0\le W_0\le T I,\qquad r_N(P)\le R_N(P).
\]
The mean of its positive eigenvalues is at least one. More precisely, for \(0<\epsilon<1\), the number of eigenvalues larger than \(\epsilon\) is at least
\[
 \frac{(1-\epsilon)R_N(P)}{T-\epsilon}.
\]
Thus a fixed fraction of the nonzero spectrum is order one in this normalization. This does not establish a uniform spectral gap for *all* positive eigenvalues.

### 6.1 Weighted trace and the physical \(1/k\) coefficients

There is also a useful general weighted trace bound. Set
\[
 Q_c=\sum_{k=0}^P c_kE_k\otimes E_k,\qquad
 R_c(P)=\sum_{k=0}^P c_k\,b_{N-2}(P-k).
\]
The same nonnegative-square expansion, with weights \(c_k\), gives
\(\operatorname{Tr}_{\rm phys}W\ge R_c(P)\). For the upper bound let \(S_H\) be the stabilizer-invariant projector. Since \(\operatorname{ran}S\subseteq\operatorname{ran}S_H\), we have \(S\le S_H\), and positivity of \(Q_c\) implies
\[
 \operatorname{Tr}_{\rm phys}W
 =T\operatorname{Tr}(SQ_c)
 \le T\operatorname{Tr}(S_HQ_c)=T R_c(P).
\]
Thus
\[
 \boxed{R_c(P)\le\operatorname{Tr}_{\rm phys}W\le T R_c(P).}
\]

For the particular coefficients
\[
 c_k=\frac1{8\pi(2\Delta+2k-1)}
     =\frac1{16\pi(k+a)},\qquad a=\Delta-\frac12>0,
\]
put \(M=N-2\), \(d=2N-6=2M-2\). The identity-character term gives
\[
 b_M(n)=\frac{n^d}{M!((M-1)!)^2}+O(n^{d-1})\quad(M\ge2),
\]
while \(b_1(n)=1\) exactly. The elementary harmonic sum
\[
 \sum_{k=0}^P\frac{(P-k)^d}{k+a}
   =P^d\log P+O(P^d)
\]
(with numerator interpreted as one for \(d=0\)) therefore gives
\[
 \boxed{R_c(P)=\frac{P^{2N-6}\log P}
 {16\pi(N-2)!((N-3)!)^2}+O(P^{2N-6}).}
\]
The character remainder contributes only \(O(P^{d-1}\log P)\) when \(d\ge2\), which is absorbed in the displayed error. Combining the weighted trace sandwich with the rank theorem/bounds proves
\[
 \boxed{\frac1{r_N(P)}\sum_{\lambda_j>0}\lambda_j
       =\Theta\!\left(\frac{\log P}{P}\right).}
\]
The same coefficients satisfy the positive-only weak law above. This last estimate concerns the **mean** positive eigenvalue; it does not identify the typical eigenvalue, a rescaling limit, or a spectral gap. No sharp leading coefficient of the mean is inferred from the trace sandwich.
