# Einstein–scalar solutions on the global-AdS vacuum orbit

This note extends the solution organization in [perturbation](perturbation.md). Its companion, [Einstein scalar spectra on the vacuum orbit](Einstein%20scalar%20spectra%20on%20the%20vacuum%20orbit.md), derives the order-$G$ action and calculates the scalar-primary spectrum. The independent vacuum-graviton data are the $a$ of [all order perturbation result](../all%20order%20perturbation%20result.md), not additional matter sources.

**Main result.** A finite allowed diffeomorphism transports the **entire sourced Einstein–scalar solution**, not only the vacuum metric and the probe scalar. Thus one reference sourced calculation produces a family exact in the chosen vacuum-orbit transformation. This is an organization of solutions by independent seed data, not a product decomposition of the interacting phase space.

## 1. Conventions and the meaning of an arbitrary background

Use the conventions of the existing scalar calculation:

$$
\kappa^2=16\pi G>0,\qquad L_{\mathrm{AdS}}=1,\qquad
 ds_0^2=-(1+r^2)dt^2+\frac{dr^2}{1+r^2}+r^2d\varphi^2,
\qquad \varphi\sim\varphi+2\pi.
$$

The equations are

$$
\mathcal E_{\mu\nu}[g]:=R_{\mu\nu}-\frac12g_{\mu\nu}R-g_{\mu\nu}
 =\frac{\kappa^2}{2}T_{\mu\nu}[g,\phi],
\qquad P_g\phi:=(\Box_g-m^2)\phi=0,
$$

$$
T_{\mu\nu}[g,\phi]
=\nabla_\mu\phi\nabla_\nu\phi
-\frac12g_{\mu\nu}\big((\nabla\phi)^2+m^2\phi^2\big).
$$

Keep a smooth center, the fixed boundary cylinder, Brown–Henneaux metric falloffs, and the differentiated source-free standard scalar condition

$$
 m^2=\Delta(\Delta-2),\qquad \Delta>1,\qquad
 \phi=r^{-\Delta}\alpha+r^{-(2-\Delta)}\beta+\cdots,
 \qquad \beta=\delta\beta=0.
$$

Only proper diffeomorphisms are quotiented. Physical boundary gravitons are retained. For the existing logarithm-free de Donder representative also retain the exclusion $\Delta\ne\Delta_*=(1+\sqrt5)/2$. The finiteness of a spectral coefficient at $\Delta_*$ does not remove this gauge-accessibility restriction.

Write

$$
 \mathscr U_a:=\Phi_a^*=e^{\kappa\mathcal L_{\zeta[a]}},
 \qquad \bar g[a]=\mathscr U_a g^{(0)}.
 \tag{1.1}
$$

An admissible $a$ means that the chosen flow is a smooth, invertible, time-orientation-preserving map of the global-AdS spacetime with the stated boundary behavior; alternatively all formulas can be read as formal Lie series. For finite transformations, transport the regulator surfaces, endpoint prescription, gauge representative, and homogeneous-response convention as well as the fields. A single exponential is not asserted to cover the whole diffeomorphism group. Compositions of allowed flows give the same results.

Thus “arbitrary $\bar g[a]$” means arbitrary points in this **smooth global-AdS vacuum orbit**, with a specified representative $\Phi_a$. It does not mean BTZ, conical quotients, different holonomies/topologies, singular flows, or arbitrary off-shell metrics. Even on one orbit the metric alone does not choose a preferred time generator: changing $\Phi_a$ by an AdS isometry can change the chosen clock and mode labels.

## 2. Independent seeds and the full transport theorem

Let $v$ be a real free scalar solution on $g^{(0)}$, with coefficients $b_I,b_I^\dagger$ in the existing normalized KG basis. Introduce a separate real bookkeeping parameter $b$ multiplying the whole scalar seed; it is not one of the oscillator labels. Put

$$
 \lambda=\kappa^2b^2,\qquad
 g_{\mathrm{s}}(b)=g^{(0)}+\sum_{r\ge1}\lambda^r k_r[v],
 \qquad
 \phi_{\mathrm{s}}(b)=b\,\Psi(\lambda),
 \quad \Psi(\lambda)=v+\sum_{r\ge1}\lambda^r\psi_r[v].
 \tag{2.1}
$$

The suffix ${\mathrm{s}}$ denotes the reference **sourced branch**: no independent higher-order gravitational or scalar homogeneous amplitudes are added. Their particular source-dependent values need not vanish. In particular the nonzero asymptotic mass generated by scalar stress is not an independently added vacuum mass.

**Transport theorem.** Suppose (2.1) satisfies the coupled equations, the chosen response prescription and boundary conditions to some retained order. Define

$$
 \boxed{\quad
 g[a,b]=\mathscr U_a g_{\mathrm{s}}(b),\qquad
 \phi[a,b]=\mathscr U_a\phi_{\mathrm{s}}(b).
 \quad}
 \tag{2.2}
$$

Then it satisfies the transported problem to exactly the same order, without an expansion in $a$. Conversely, applying $\mathscr U_a^{-1}$ recovers the reference problem. In particular,

$$
\begin{aligned}
g[a,b]&=\bar g[a]+\kappa^2b^2\mathscr U_a k_1
                   +\kappa^4b^4\mathscr U_a k_2+\cdots,\\
\phi[a,b]&=b\mathscr U_a v+\kappa^2b^3\mathscr U_a\psi_1
                         +\kappa^4b^5\mathscr U_a\psi_2+\cdots.
\end{aligned}
\tag{2.3}
$$

**Proof.** Tensorial covariance gives, including off shell,

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal E[\mathscr U_a g]&=\mathscr U_a\mathcal E[g],\\
T[\mathscr U_a g,\mathscr U_a\phi]&=\mathscr U_a T[g,\phi],\\
P_{\mathscr U_a g}(\mathscr U_a\phi)&=\mathscr U_a(P_g\phi).
\end{aligned}
\tag{2.4}
$$

Pull back the reference equations and their residuals. Boundary restrictions and initial data also pull back, so no new equation is left over. Invertibility gives the converse. The proof applies coefficient by coefficient to formal series, or exactly to an existing nonlinear solution. It does **not** prove convergence of a formal series or existence of a reference solution outside its established domain. $\square$

The two mechanisms are therefore separated as requested:

$$
\begin{array}{c|l}
\bar g[a],\;b\mathscr U_a v
 &\text{independent vacuum gravity and its transport of the probe scalar},\\
\kappa^2b^2\mathscr U_a k_1
 &\text{gravity sourced by that scalar},\\
\kappa^2b^3\mathscr U_a\psi_1
 &\text{the sourced gravity acting back on the scalar},\\
\kappa^{2r}b^{2r}\mathscr U_a k_r,\;
\kappa^{2r}b^{2r+1}\mathscr U_a\psi_r
 &\text{successive matter-response orders}.
\end{array}
$$

The background is not $\bar g$ alone once matter is present: $\mathscr U_a g_{\mathrm{s}}$ generally has non-vacuum curvature. No claim that a scalar-sourced metric is diffeomorphic to empty AdS is involved.

At fixed $\zeta[a]$, expansion of (2.3) reproduces the original notation:

$$
\begin{aligned}
g[a,b]&=g^{(0)}+\kappa\mathcal L_\zeta g^{(0)}
 +\kappa^2\left(\frac12\mathcal L_\zeta^2g^{(0)}+b^2k_1\right)\\
&\quad+\kappa^3\left(\frac16\mathcal L_\zeta^3g^{(0)}+b^2\mathcal L_\zeta k_1\right)+O(\kappa^4),\\
\phi[a,b]&=bv+\kappa b\mathcal L_\zeta v
 +\kappa^2\left(\frac b2\mathcal L_\zeta^2v+b^3\psi_1\right)+O(\kappa^3).
\end{aligned}
\tag{2.5}
$$

Thus $h=\mathcal L_\zeta g^{(0)}$ and $k=\tfrac12\mathcal L_\zeta^2g^{(0)}+b^2k_\phi$, with $k_1=k_\phi$. In particular $h=0$ never implied $k_\phi=0$. All mixed background/matter coefficients are explicitly the Lie-series coefficients of the reference response. If $a=a(\kappa)$, its Taylor terms must additionally be expanded, just as in the vacuum note.

## 3. The reference EOM recursion

Let $\mathcal L_0=D\mathcal E|_{g^{(0)}}$. In this section $D^j$ is the ordinary $j$th Frechet derivative, so factorials are explicit; the existing coefficient $\mathcal E^{(2)}[k,k]$ equals $\tfrac12D^2\mathcal E[k,k]$.

After extracting the scalar factor $b$, the equations become

$$
 \mathcal E[g(\lambda)]=\frac\lambda2T[g(\lambda),\Psi(\lambda)],
 \qquad P_{g(\lambda)}\Psi(\lambda)=0.
 \tag{3.1}
$$

At the first sourced order,

$$
 \boxed{\quad
 \mathcal L_0 k_1=\frac12T_0[v,v],\qquad
 P_0\psi_1=-DP_0[k_1]v.
 \quad}
 \tag{3.2}
$$

Here $T_0[u,v]$ is the polarized stress tensor used in the reference note, normalized by $T_0[v,v]=T_0[v]$. The scalar operator variation is

$$
 DP_0[k]v
 =-k^{\mu\nu}\nabla_\mu\nabla_\nu v
 -\left(\nabla_\mu k^{\mu\nu}-\frac12\nabla^\nu k\right)\nabla_\nu v.
 \tag{3.3}
$$

The next order is

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal L_0k_2={}&-\frac12D^2\mathcal E_0[k_1,k_1]
 +\frac12D_gT_0[k_1;v,v]+T_0[v,\psi_1],\\
P_0\psi_2={}&-DP_0[k_2]v-DP_0[k_1]\psi_1
              -\frac12D^2P_0[k_1,k_1]v.
\end{aligned}
\tag{3.4}
$$

For arbitrary order let $g_{<r}=g^{(0)}+\sum_{j=1}^{r-1}\lambda^jk_j$ and $\Psi_{<r}=v+\sum_{j=1}^{r-1}\lambda^j\psi_j$. Then

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal L_0 k_r
 &=\frac12[\lambda^{r-1}]T[g_{<r},\Psi_{<r}]
   -[\lambda^r]\mathcal E[g_{<r}],\\
P_0\psi_r
 &=-[\lambda^r]\big(P_{g_{<r}+\lambda^rk_r}\Psi_{<r}\big).
\end{aligned}
\tag{3.5}
$$

At each step the right sides contain only previously obtained data, with $k_r$ obtained before $\psi_r$. Their compatibility follows from the exact identities

$$
 \nabla^\mu\mathcal E_{\mu\nu}=0,
 \qquad \nabla^\mu T_{\mu\nu}=(P_g\phi)\nabla_\nu\phi.
$$

Expanding these identities proves conservation of the effective metric source with respect to $\nabla^{(0)}$ after lower equations have been imposed. It does not license setting the metric's constraint data to zero: a nonzero scalar energy fixes nonzero gravitational constraint data.

Given the already selected particular-response inverses, (3.5) is a finite-order recursive construction. The KG inverse can, for example, be fixed by zero homogeneous correction on a chosen reference Cauchy slice. Its oscillator components solve an inhomogeneous equation; resonant forcing gives secular terms rather than a division by zero. The gravity inverse must include constraints and the prescribed homogeneous branch. Its existence on every new higher-order source is an analytic input, not a consequence of the leading finite-source kernel. Higher-order radial resonances must be checked before insisting on the original no-log class. We establish the recursion and its exact transport, not an all-order global well-posedness theorem.

## 4. Transporting the response, rather than selecting it again

For a retained finite scalar-mode source space $\mathcal V_0$, use the map $G_{\mathcal V_0}$ in [Einstein scalar response prescription](Einstein%20scalar%20response%20prescription.md):

$$
 \mathcal L_0G_{\mathcal V_0}J=J,\qquad
 k_1=\frac12G_{\mathcal V_0}T_0[v,v].
$$

Its domain is the stated span of polarized scalar stresses, not all off-shell tensor histories. Put $\mathcal V_a=\mathscr U_a\mathcal V_0$ and

$$
 G_{\mathcal V_a}=\mathscr U_aG_{\mathcal V_0}\mathscr U_a^{-1}.
 \tag{4.1}
$$

Differentiating (2.4) proves

$$
 \mathcal L_a\mathscr U_a=\mathscr U_a\mathcal L_0,
 \quad C_{\bar g[a]}\mathscr U_a=\mathscr U_a C_{g^{(0)}},
 \quad k_{1,a}[\mathscr U_a v]=\mathscr U_a k_1[v],
 \tag{4.2}
$$

where $C_g[k]_\nu=\nabla^\mu(k_{\mu\nu}-\tfrac12g_{\mu\nu}\operatorname{tr}_g k)$ is de Donder gauge. This proves the gauge condition as well as Einstein's equation on the moved background. The same construction applies to the chosen KG response.

In a displayed finite Gram-kernel implementation, transport the independent source basis, its particular responses, and the auxiliary integration region and contractions together. A new unrelated choice of source representatives can add homogeneous gravity and need not give the same branch. Transport also applies to a reciprocal spectral exchange pairing; one must not infer reciprocity of an arbitrary finite-source or retarded kernel from (4.1). The action calculation uses the reciprocal pairing already selected by the reference spectrum problem.

The fast scalar condition is preserved. If the asymptotic diffeomorphism acts by $x_A\mapsto f_A(x_A)$, $A=L,R$, with $x_L=t-\varphi$, $x_R=t+\varphi$, then

$$
 R_a\sim \frac{r}{\sqrt{f_L'f_R'}},\qquad
 \alpha_a=(f_L'f_R')^{\Delta/2}\,\alpha\circ(f_L,f_R),
 \quad \beta_a=0.
 \tag{4.3}
$$

This uses the compensating radial transformation which preserves the fixed conformal boundary cylinder. No slow scalar source is turned on. A smooth logarithm-free transformation preserves the specified reference asymptotic class to the order at which that class exists.

## 5. What is, and is not, split

At $(g^{(0)},0)$ the linearized equations separate into vacuum gravity and KG. Nonlinearly, adding $k_r^{\mathrm{hom}}$ or $\psi_r^{\mathrm{hom}}$ changes the choice of independent seeds. Within a formal local slice, those independent data can be absorbed order by order into $a$ and the scalar amplitudes, while proper gauge is removed. This is a coordinate choice on solutions. We do not infer a global, canonical projection from the full Einstein–scalar solution space onto its vacuum subspace.

In particular varying $a$ drags matter as well. With $Q=(g_{\mathrm{s}},\phi_{\mathrm{s}})$,

$$
 \delta(\mathscr U_aQ)=\mathscr U_a\big(\delta Q+\mathcal L_{\chi_a}Q\big),
 \quad \chi_a=\kappa\frac{1-e^{-\kappa\operatorname{ad}_{\zeta}}}
                         {\kappa\operatorname{ad}_{\zeta}}\delta\zeta.
 \tag{5.1}
$$

Covariance of the full boundary-completed CPS form consequently gives

$$
 \Omega_{\mathscr U_aQ,\Sigma_a}(\delta_1,\delta_2)
 =\Omega_{Q,\Sigma_0}
   (\delta_1Q+\mathcal L_{\chi_1}Q,\delta_2Q+\mathcal L_{\chi_2}Q),
 \qquad \Sigma_a=\Phi_a^{-1}\Sigma_0.
 \tag{5.2}
$$

There are matter–gravity cross terms. Large-diffeomorphism directions have matter-dependent charges; they are not null directions to quotient away. The transported solution family therefore does not imply $\Omega=\Omega_{\mathrm{vac}}(a)+\Omega_{\mathrm{scalar}}(b)$, or a tensor-product factorization of the full quantum theory. Slice independence of $\Omega$ requires the reference no-flux and boundary/corner conditions; it is not a claim that the symplectic **potential** is slice independent.

## 6. Clocks and modes

Define $(\tau_a,R_a,\theta_a)=(t,r,\varphi)\circ\Phi_a$ and the pulled-back vector fields

$$
 K_a=\Phi_a^*\partial_t=e^{\kappa\operatorname{ad}_\zeta}\partial_t,
 \qquad J_a=\Phi_a^*\partial_\varphi.
 \tag{6.1}
$$

Then $\mathcal L_{K_a}\bar g[a]=\mathcal L_{J_a}\bar g[a]=0$. A reference mode $u_I$ becomes

$$
 u_I^{[a]}(x)=u_I(\tau_a(x),R_a(x),\theta_a(x)),
 \quad i\mathcal L_{K_a}u_I^{[a]}=\omega_Iu_I^{[a]},
 \quad -i\mathcal L_{J_a}u_I^{[a]}=j_Iu_I^{[a]}.
 \tag{6.2}
$$

The KG/CPS normalization is unchanged on transported slices. Transporting the positive-frequency space also specifies the vacuum and normal ordering used in the companion calculation.

Generically $\mathcal L_{\partial_t}\bar g[a]\ne0$. Accordingly one cannot call the transported modes eigenmodes of the **unmoved** $\partial_t$. The companion transports the reference connected order-$G$ spectrum for $K_a$ and computes fixed-boundary-frame energy expectation values. Its Floquet statement additionally requires a periodic spacelike Cauchy foliation anchored at the original boundary time. Smooth invertibility and periodicity of $\Phi_a$ alone do not imply that the coordinate surfaces $t=\mathrm{const}$ are Cauchy surfaces of $\bar g[a]$. None of these choices discards physical boundary gravitons.

## 7. Result and limits of the claim

The extension from $a=0$ to the admitted vacuum orbit is proved by (2.4), including source response, feedback, mixed Lie coefficients, gauges and transported boundary data. No new Einstein inversion is required merely because $a\ne0$. The next order in scalar backreaction still requires solving the reference recursion; its existence, asymptotic class and long-time control are separate issues. The spectral result is for the same connected scalar-primary branch as the reference calculation, not all graviton–matter mixing sectors.

The transport theorem preserves the reference calculation's evidence level as well as its coefficients. In particular, the all-index completion in [the OFPT note, section 9](OFPT%20two%20particle%20energy%20shifts.md#9-analytic-completion-for-arbitrary-radial-level-and-spin) uses a stated high-spin inversion input. Covariance does not replace that input or turn finite-level checks into an independent all-index OFPT summation.

The companion contains executable checks: a component calculation of the off-shell KG covariance identity for the actual Brown–Henneaux seed, the action coefficient, reference spectral coefficients, and nontrivial finite boundary transformations. They check the written implementation; the all-$a$ statement is the covariance proof above, not a finite numerical extrapolation.
