# Bunch--Davies Restriction and KMS

## 1. Direct verdict

The global Euclidean/Bunch--Davies Maxwell state restricts to the local
gauge-invariant characteristic algebra constructed in `04`--`06`, and that
restriction is KMS for static time at

$$
\boxed{\beta_t=2\pi L.}
\tag{1.1}
$$

Equivalently, in dimensionless static time $\tau=t/L$ the inverse temperature
is $\beta_\tau=2\pi$. For characteristic coefficients, the restricted
quasifree covariance is

$$
\boxed{
\mu_{\rm BD}(c_1,c_2)
=4\sum_{P,j,m}\int_0^\infty d\omega\,
\omega\coth(\pi\omega)
\operatorname{Re}(\overline{c_{1,jm}^P}c_{2,jm}^P).}
\tag{1.2}
$$

Thus T5 is `proved` on the local-observable/BD-continuity characteristic
algebra defined below. The state is obtained by algebraic restriction of the
global state, equivalently by affine-horizon positive frequency. It is not
defined by a partial trace or a static inside/outside Hilbert tensor product.

There is a genuine infrared boundary: (1.2) is not finite on every vector in
the larger $(\omega+\omega^2)$ CPS completion. A regular BD state on that
entire nonlocal completion is therefore not asserted.

## 2. The algebra on which the state is regular

Calculation `06` associates to every compact gauge-invariant field-strength
smearing $[\eta]\in\mathscr T_F$ the characteristic Hamiltonian vector

$$
x_\eta:=R_-\mathbb E_F\eta.
\tag{2.1}
$$

Let

$$
\mathcal X_{\rm loc}
:=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb R}\{x_\eta:[\eta]\in\mathscr T_F\}.
\tag{2.2}
$$

Its spectral coefficients are bounded at $\omega=0$ and rapidly decreasing at
large $\omega$ by (3.3)--(3.5) of `06`. Define the BD-continuity norm

$$
\|c\|_{\rm BD}^2
:=2\sum_{P,j,m}\int_0^\infty d\omega\,
\left[\omega+\omega^2+\omega\coth(\pi\omega)\right]|c_{jm}^P|^2.
\tag{2.3}
$$

Let $\mathcal X_{\rm BD}$ be the completion of $\mathcal X_{\rm loc}$ in
(2.3). It is invariant under static translations and exact scattering, and
$\Omega$ is continuous on it. The relevant characteristic algebra is

$$
\boxed{
\mathfrak A_{\rm char}^{\rm loc}
:=\mathfrak W(\mathcal X_{\rm BD},\Omega).}
\tag{2.4}
$$

It contains the Weyl observables generated by all compact local Maxwell
field-strength smearings. No pointwise endpoint observable is added.

## 3. Definition by global restriction and affine horizon frequency

Define $\varpi_{\rm BD}$ first as the global Euclidean/Hadamard Maxwell state,
restricted algebraically to the local static-patch field-strength algebra and
then carried to (2.4) by T1--T3. This definition is gauge invariant.

For computation, use its equivalent horizon characterization. Extend the
affine coordinate $V$ across the complete global cosmological horizon and
restrict at the end to the northern half $V<0$. For each physical polarization
and angular channel, affine positive-frequency modes are

$$
p_\kappa(V)=\frac{e^{-i\kappa V}}{\sqrt{4\pi\kappa}},
\qquad \kappa>0.
\tag{3.1}
$$

They are normalized by the affine version of the characteristic form,

$$
\Omega(f_1,f_2)
=\int dV\,(f_1\partial_Vf_2-f_2\partial_Vf_1).
\tag{3.2}
$$

The affine vacuum derivative two-point function is

$$
\boxed{
\left\langle\partial_Vf(V)\partial_{V'}f(V')\right\rangle_{\rm BD}
=-\frac{1}{4\pi(V-V'-i0)^2}.}
\tag{3.3}
$$

In four-dimensional Maxwell theory the Hodge star on two-forms is conformally
invariant. Euclidean regularity therefore selects the same affine
positive-frequency horizon distribution for the two gauge-invariant
radiative polarizations. Conversely, the characteristic uniqueness theorem
T1 reconstructs a unique static-patch bulk two-point distribution from this
horizon restriction. Hence the affine prescription is equivalent here to the
global Euclidean definition, rather than an independent state postulate.

## 4. Mellin transform and exact thermal coefficients

On $\mathcal H^-$,

$$
V=-e^{-v},
\qquad x:=-V=e^{-v}>0.
\tag{4.1}
$$

A positive static-frequency mode is

$$
q_\omega(V)
=\frac{e^{-i\omega v}}{\sqrt{4\pi\omega}}
=\frac{x^{i\omega}}{\sqrt{4\pi\omega}},
\qquad \omega>0.
\tag{4.2}
$$

With the usual positive regulator, the Mellin integrals are

$$
\int_0^\infty dx\,x^{i\omega-1}e^{-i\kappa x}
=e^{+\pi\omega/2}\Gamma(i\omega)\kappa^{-i\omega},
\tag{4.3}
$$

$$
\int_0^\infty dx\,x^{i\omega-1}e^{+i\kappa x}
=e^{-\pi\omega/2}\Gamma(i\omega)\kappa^{-i\omega}.
\tag{4.4}
$$

The null symplectic product then gives

$$
\boxed{
\alpha_{\omega\kappa}
=\frac1{2\pi}\sqrt{\frac\omega\kappa}\,
e^{\pi\omega/2}\Gamma(i\omega)\kappa^{-i\omega},}
\tag{4.5}
$$

$$
\boxed{
\beta_{\omega\kappa}
=-\frac1{2\pi}\sqrt{\frac\omega\kappa}\,
e^{-\pi\omega/2}\Gamma(i\omega)\kappa^{-i\omega}.}
\tag{4.6}
$$

Their ratio is

$$
\beta_{\omega\kappa}=-e^{-\pi\omega}\alpha_{\omega\kappa}.
\tag{4.7}
$$

Using

$$
|\Gamma(i\omega)|^2
=\frac\pi{\omega\sinh(\pi\omega)},
\qquad
\int_0^\infty\frac{d\kappa}{\kappa}
e^{-i(\omega-\omega')\ln\kappa}
=2\pi\delta(\omega-\omega'),
\tag{4.8}
$$

one obtains the exact canonical normalization

$$
\int_0^\infty d\kappa\,
(\alpha_{\omega\kappa}\overline{\alpha_{\omega'\kappa}}
-\beta_{\omega\kappa}\overline{\beta_{\omega'\kappa}})
=\delta(\omega-\omega')
\tag{4.9}
$$

and the static occupation number

$$
\boxed{
\int_0^\infty d\kappa\,
\beta_{\omega\kappa}\overline{\beta_{\omega'\kappa}}
=n_\omega\delta(\omega-\omega'),
\qquad
n_\omega=\frac1{e^{2\pi\omega}-1}.}
\tag{4.10}
$$

There is no $aa$ anomalous covariance. Equations (4.9)--(4.10) apply
diagonally to both Maxwell polarizations and all $j,m$; the center scattering
phase has unit modulus and does not alter the occupation factor.

## 5. Restricted quasifree state

Relative to the static annihilation operators of `05`, the restricted state
satisfies

$$
\langle a_\alpha^\dagger(\omega)a_{\alpha'}(\omega')\rangle_{\rm BD}
=n_\omega\delta_{\alpha\alpha'}\delta(\omega-\omega'),
\tag{5.1}
$$

$$
\langle a_\alpha(\omega)a_{\alpha'}^\dagger(\omega')\rangle_{\rm BD}
=(1+n_\omega)\delta_{\alpha\alpha'}\delta(\omega-\omega'),
\qquad
\langle aa\rangle_{\rm BD}=0.
\tag{5.2}
$$

Since $1+2n_\omega=\coth(\pi\omega)$, these equations give (1.2). The Weyl
functional is

$$
\boxed{
\varpi_{\rm BD}(W(c))
=\exp\!\left[-\frac14\mu_{\rm BD}(c,c)\right]
=\exp\!\left[-\sum_{P,j,m}\int_0^\infty d\omega\,
\omega\coth(\pi\omega)|c_{jm}^P|^2\right].}
\tag{5.3}
$$

Its antisymmetric two-point part is still $i\Omega/2$, so its commutator is
the Maxwell Peierls bracket proved in `06`. For a local Maxwell observable,
put $c=c_\eta$ from (3.3) of `06`; (5.3) is finite and gauge independent.

Transforming (3.3) with $V=-e^{-v}$ gives the stationary radiative correlator

$$
\boxed{
\left\langle\partial_vf(v)\partial_{v'}f(v')\right\rangle_{\rm BD}
=-\frac1{16\pi\sinh^2((v-v'-i0)/2)}.}
\tag{5.4}
$$

The Maxwell field-strength two-point function follows by applying the local
electric/magnetic dictionary $D$ of `02`; no potential gauge or edge
oscillator enters.

## 6. KMS proof

Static evolution in physical time acts as

$$
(T_t c)(\omega)=e^{-i\omega t/L}c(\omega).
\tag{6.1}
$$

For $\beta_t=2\pi L$, (4.10) obeys detailed balance,

$$
\boxed{
n_\omega=e^{-\beta_t\omega/L}(1+n_\omega).}
\tag{6.2}
$$

Let $\mathcal X_{\rm an}$ be the finite-angular, compact
$\omega$-support-away-from-zero core in $\mathcal X_{\rm BD}$. Then
$T_zc=e^{-i\omega z/L}c$ is entire for $c\in\mathcal X_{\rm an}$. For Weyl
polynomials generated by this core, the quasifree two-point functions are
analytic in the strip

$$
0<\operatorname{Im}z<2\pi L,
\tag{6.3}
$$

continuous at its boundaries in the distributional sense, and (6.2) gives

$$
\boxed{
\varpi_{\rm BD}\!\left(A\alpha_{t+i2\pi L}(B)\right)
=\varpi_{\rm BD}\!\left(\alpha_t(B)A\right).}
\tag{6.4}
$$

Wick's rule extends the two-point boundary relation to the polynomial
analytic algebra; the Weyl Gaussian formula extends it directly to Weyl
products. Since $\mathcal X_{\rm an}$ is dense in (2.3), regular GNS
continuity extends the boundary relation to the von Neumann algebra generated
by $\mathfrak A_{\rm char}^{\rm loc}$. Individual Weyl orbits need not be
point-norm continuous in the abstract CCR norm; no such false continuity
claim is used.

Equation (5.4) gives the same proof in position space: the meromorphic
function is periodic under $v-v'\mapsto v-v'+2\pi i$, while the opposite
boundary prescriptions at the two edges of the strip exchange operator
ordering. Periodicity alone without the boundary prescription would not be a
KMS proof.

## 7. Infrared boundary and the static ground state

As $\omega\to0$,

$$
\omega\coth(\pi\omega)\longrightarrow\frac1\pi.
\tag{7.1}
$$

Thus BD continuity requires $c\in L^2(d\omega)$ at zero, whereas the T2 CPS
norm requires only $\sqrt\omega c\in L^2$. For example,

$$
c(\omega)=\chi(\omega)\omega^{-3/4},
\tag{7.2}
$$

with $\chi$ smooth and supported near zero, belongs to the CPS space but has
infinite BD covariance. Therefore the statement

> the BD restriction is a regular quasifree state on every vector of the full
> CPS Hilbert completion

is `false`. It is regular on (2.4), which contains all compact local Maxwell
observables used in T3.

The static ground state of `05` has covariance weight $\omega$ rather than
$\omega\coth(\pi\omega)$. It is a different quasifree state. The KMS result is
an algebraic comparison of two states under the same static automorphisms, not
a claim that a formal density matrix has been obtained by tracing the global
vacuum.

## 8. Theorem T5

**Theorem 8.1.** The global Euclidean/Bunch--Davies Maxwell state restricts,
through the gauge-invariant Maxwell/master map and T1--T3, to the quasifree
state (5.3) on $\mathfrak A_{\rm char}^{\rm loc}$. This state is KMS at
$\beta_t=2\pi L$ for the static automorphisms (6.1).

**Proof.** Euclidean regularity is equivalent to affine positive frequency on
the complete cosmological horizon for the conformally invariant Maxwell field.
The Mellin transform (4.3)--(4.10) gives the normalized static occupation
number and covariance (1.2). Compact local source vectors lie in the BD norm
by `06`, section 3. Detailed balance proves strip analyticity and the boundary
condition on the dense analytic algebra, and continuity gives the extension.
The T1--T3 maps make the construction gauge invariant and identify its
commutator with the bulk Maxwell Peierls bracket. $\square$

## 10. Literature and claim boundary

The Gibbons--Hawking temperature, static-patch KMS property, affine/Euclidean
characterization, and Maxwell horizon photon two-point function are known.
The project-specific statement is only their compatibility with the exact
constraint-complete characteristic algebra, symplectic normalization, and
Peierls map constructed in `00`--`06`.

This calculation does not establish a new thermodynamic temperature, a static
entropy, a type-I tensor factor, or a partial-trace density matrix. It also
does not classify the endpoint-memory extension; that is the next task in
`08`.
