# Question

Consider a one-parameter family of Lagrangians

$$\begin{align}
\mathbf L_\lambda[\phi], \qquad
\lambda\in I.
\end{align}$$

The useful question is not whether a particular deformation is integrable. The structural question is how the covariant phase space data changes with $\lambda$:

1. the symplectic potential and symplectic form,
1. Hamiltonian vector fields and charges,
1. the distinction between bare and transported symmetries,
1. the resulting charge algebra.

The discussion below assumes that the background structures are held fixed when building the phase space. If the deformation also changes boundary conditions, field space, or the background sources, those changes must be included separately.

# Covariant Phase Space Data

For each fixed $\lambda$, vary the Lagrangian $d$-form:

$$\begin{align}
\delta \mathbf L_\lambda =\mathbf E_\lambda(\phi)\cdot\delta\phi +d\mathbf\Theta_\lambda(\delta\phi).
\end{align}$$

The presymplectic current is

$$\begin{align}
\mathbf\omega_\lambda(\delta_1,\delta_2) =\delta_1\mathbf\Theta_\lambda(\delta_2) -\delta_2\mathbf\Theta_\lambda(\delta_1),
\end{align}$$

or, in field-space notation,

$$\begin{align}
\mathbf\omega_\lambda=\delta\mathbf\Theta_\lambda.
\end{align}$$

On a Cauchy slice $\Sigma$,

$$\begin{align}
\Theta_{\Sigma,\lambda} =\int_\Sigma\mathbf\Theta_\lambda, \qquad
\Omega_{\Sigma,\lambda} =\int_\Sigma\mathbf\omega_\lambda =\delta\Theta_{\Sigma,\lambda}.
\end{align}$$

In a gauge theory this is a presymplectic form before quotienting gauge degeneracies and before adding possible boundary or corner terms.

# Flow of the Symplectic Form

Since $\delta$ and $\partial_\lambda$ commute,

$$\begin{align}
\partial_\lambda\Omega_{\Sigma,\lambda}=\partial_\lambda\delta\Theta_{\Sigma,\lambda}=\delta\left(\partial_\lambda\Theta_{\Sigma,\lambda}\right).
\end{align}$$

Thus the deformation is controlled by the field-space one-form

$$\begin{align}
B_{\Sigma,\lambda}:=\partial_\lambda\Theta_{\Sigma,\lambda} =\int_\Sigma\partial_\lambda\mathbf\Theta_\lambda,
\end{align}$$

and the basic identity is

$$\begin{align}
\partial_\lambda\Omega_{\Sigma,\lambda} =\delta B_{\Sigma,\lambda}.
\end{align}$$

This is the correct general statement. It does not say

$$\begin{align}
\partial_\lambda\Omega_{\Sigma,\lambda}=0.
\end{align}$$

The symplectic form can change in a fixed set of field coordinates. What is special is that the change is exact in field space.

There is a related on-shell current statement. Differentiating the variational identity and antisymmetrizing in two linearized variations gives

$$\begin{align}
d(\partial_\lambda\mathbf\omega_\lambda)\approx0.
\end{align}$$

This only says that the $\lambda$-variation of the symplectic current is on-shell closed. It does not by itself prove that the integrated symplectic form is unchanged. Turning the current statement into a boundary statement requires locality, cohomological assumptions, and control of zero modes and boundary contributions.

# Canonical Transport

Assume for the moment that gauge degeneracies have been quotiented out and that $\Omega_{\Sigma,\lambda}$ is nondegenerate on the reduced phase space. If the one-form $B_{\Sigma,\lambda}$ is in the image of $\Omega_{\Sigma,\lambda}$, define a phase-space vector field $Y_\lambda$ by

$$\begin{align}
\iota_{Y_\lambda}\Omega_{\Sigma,\lambda} =-B_{\Sigma,\lambda}.
\end{align}$$

Using Cartan's formula,

$$\begin{align}
\mathcal L_{Y_\lambda}\Omega_{\Sigma,\lambda}=\delta\iota_{Y_\lambda}\Omega_{\Sigma,\lambda}+\iota_{Y_\lambda}\delta\Omega_{\Sigma,\lambda}=-\delta B_{\Sigma,\lambda},
\end{align}$$

so

$$\begin{align}
(\partial_\lambda+\mathcal L_{Y_\lambda})
\Omega_{\Sigma,\lambda} =0.
\end{align}$$

This is the local Moser-type statement for the deformation. The symplectic form is not necessarily fixed in the original variables, but it can be locally trivialized by a $\lambda$-dependent canonical transport generated by $Y_\lambda$.

The statement is local. It may fail globally because of topology of phase space, zero modes, changes of boundary conditions, singular points of $\Omega_{\Sigma,\lambda}$, or obstructions to solving for a globally defined $Y_\lambda$.

# Hamiltonian Charges

At fixed $\lambda$, a phase-space vector field $X_\lambda$ is Hamiltonian if there is a functional $H_\lambda$ such that

$$\begin{align}
\iota_{X_\lambda}\Omega_{\Sigma,\lambda} =-\delta H_\lambda.
\end{align}$$

There are two useful ways to discuss the deformation.

## Bare Vector Field

In the bare picture, the geometric action of the vector field is held fixed:

$$\begin{align}
\partial_\lambda X=0.
\end{align}$$

Differentiating the Hamiltonian relation gives

$$\begin{align}
\iota_X\partial_\lambda\Omega_{\Sigma,\lambda} =-\delta(\partial_\lambda H_\lambda).
\end{align}$$

Using $\partial_\lambda\Omega_{\Sigma,\lambda}=\delta B_{\Sigma,\lambda}$,

$$\begin{align}
\iota_X\delta B_{\Sigma,\lambda} =-\delta(\partial_\lambda H_\lambda).
\end{align}$$

Since

$$\begin{align}
\mathcal L_XB_{\Sigma,\lambda} =\iota_X\delta B_{\Sigma,\lambda} +\delta(\iota_XB_{\Sigma,\lambda}),
\end{align}$$

one obtains

$$\begin{align}
\delta(\partial_\lambda H_\lambda) =-\mathcal L_XB_{\Sigma,\lambda} +\delta(\iota_XB_{\Sigma,\lambda}).
\end{align}$$

Therefore there is no universal formula

$$\begin{align}
\partial_\lambda H_\lambda=\iota_XB_{\Sigma,\lambda}
\end{align}$$

unless $\mathcal L_XB_{\Sigma,\lambda}$ vanishes, or is otherwise an exact variation whose contribution is accounted for.

## Transported Vector Field

In the transported picture, the vector field is pushed along by the same canonical transport as the symplectic form:

$$\begin{align}
(\partial_\lambda+\mathcal L_{Y_\lambda})X_\lambda=0.
\end{align}$$

Then

$$\begin{align}
(\partial_\lambda+\mathcal L_{Y_\lambda})
\Omega_{\Sigma,\lambda}=0
\end{align}$$

implies that the Hamiltonian relation is preserved:

$$\begin{align}
\delta\left[ (\partial_\lambda+\mathcal L_{Y_\lambda})H_\lambda \right]=0.
\end{align}$$

After fixing additive constants, one may choose

$$\begin{align}
(\partial_\lambda+\mathcal L_{Y_\lambda})H_\lambda=0.
\end{align}$$

Thus transported charges are constant along the canonical flow, while their expression as functions of the original coordinates can still change.

# Noether Charges and Hamiltonian Charges

A Noether charge is tied to a transformation of the fields under which the Lagrangian changes by a total derivative:

$$\begin{align}
\delta_\xi\mathbf L_\lambda=d\mathbf M_{\xi,\lambda}.
\end{align}$$

The corresponding Noether current is

$$\begin{align}
\mathbf J_{\xi,\lambda} =\mathbf\Theta_\lambda(\delta_\xi\phi) -\mathbf M_{\xi,\lambda}.
\end{align}$$

If the symmetry is Hamiltonian on phase space, then its charge also satisfies

$$\begin{align}
\iota_{X_\xi}\Omega_{\Sigma,\lambda} =-\delta H_{\xi,\lambda}.
\end{align}$$

The converse need not hold. A Hamiltonian conserved charge may generate a flow on phase space without coming from a spacetime Noether symmetry. This distinction is important when discussing integrable hierarchies or other families of conserved Hamiltonians.

# Charge Algebra

If two transported Hamiltonian vector fields satisfy

$$\begin{align}
(\partial_\lambda+\mathcal L_{Y_\lambda})X_\lambda=0, \qquad
(\partial_\lambda+\mathcal L_{Y_\lambda})Z_\lambda=0,
\end{align}$$

then their bracket is transported as well:

$$\begin{align}
(\partial_\lambda+\mathcal L_{Y_\lambda})[X_\lambda,Z_\lambda]=0.
\end{align}$$

In a local patch where the canonical transport exists, the charge algebra is therefore preserved in the transported frame, up to additive constants, central extensions, and boundary improvements.

In the bare frame, the charge densities and even the integrated Hamiltonians may deform. The algebra may still remain unchanged if the deformation terms are total derivatives or if they are absorbed by boundary counterterms, but this is an additional statement to check in each example.

# Practical Checklist

For a concrete deformation, the clean workflow is:

1. Compute $\mathbf\Theta_\lambda$ and $\Omega_{\Sigma,\lambda}$ at fixed $\lambda$.
1. Compute $B_{\Sigma,\lambda}=\int_\Sigma\partial_\lambda\mathbf\Theta_\lambda$.
1. Check whether $\delta B_{\Sigma,\lambda}$ is nonzero in the chosen variables.
1. If the reduced symplectic form is nondegenerate, solve $\iota_{Y_\lambda}\Omega_{\Sigma,\lambda}=-B_{\Sigma,\lambda}$.
1. Decide whether the relevant symmetry is being kept bare or transported.
1. For each charge, verify the Hamiltonian relation with the actual $\Omega_{\Sigma,\lambda}$.
1. Check boundary terms before claiming conservation or algebra preservation.

# Limitations

The discussion assumes a fixed phase-space problem. If the deformation changes the space of allowed solutions, the asymptotic conditions, the set of gauge degeneracies, or the boundary symplectic structure, then the formula

$$\begin{align}
\partial_\lambda\Omega_{\Sigma,\lambda} =\delta B_{\Sigma,\lambda}
\end{align}$$

is still the local variational identity, but it is not by itself a statement about an identified family of reduced phase spaces. The identification of phase spaces is precisely the extra structure supplied locally by $Y_\lambda$, when it exists.
