# Boundary Covariant Phase Space

Finite-boundary covariant phase space treats the action, boundary term, symplectic potential, and Hamiltonian charge as one geometric package.

## Finite Boundary Structure

For an action on a region $M$ with boundary $\Gamma$,

$$\begin{align}
S=\int_M L+\int_\Gamma \ell,
\end{align}$$

the variation has a bulk equation-of-motion term and boundary terms. A boundary term $\ell$ is chosen together with boundary data so that the variational principle is well posed.

## Boundary Symplectic Potential

The symplectic potential on a Cauchy surface $\Sigma$ can receive a corner correction from $\partial\Sigma$:

$$\begin{align}
\theta_\Sigma=\int_\Sigma \Theta-\int_{\partial\Sigma} C.
\end{align}$$

Here $\Theta$ is the bulk presymplectic potential current, while $C$ records the boundary correction induced by $\ell$.

## Charges

For a symmetry generator $\xi$, the Hamiltonian charge is governed by contraction with the presymplectic form. With boundaries, the charge may contain both a bulk constraint part and a boundary part. Integrability depends on the boundary conditions and on whether $\xi$ is field-dependent.

## Models

- Maxwell theory: the CPS charge reproduces the stress-tensor charge for spacetime symmetries.
- Higher-curvature gravity: smooth-boundary, corner, and null-boundary terms become part of the variational principle.
- Gravitational Chern-Simons theory: the variation contains a Cotton-tensor bulk term and a boundary symplectic potential.
- Liouville and JT gravity: low-dimensional models where boundary dynamics and charges are especially visible.
