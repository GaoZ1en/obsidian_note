## representations of conformal group in $\displaystyle{d}$ dimensions

given an infinitesimal conformal transformation parametrized by $\displaystyle{\omega_{g}}$, we seek a matrix representation $\displaystyle{T_{g}}$ such that $\displaystyle{\Phi(x)}$ transforms as

$$
\tag{3.2.1}
\begin{align}
\Phi'(x') & =(1-i\omega_{g}T_{g})\Phi(x)
\end{align}
$$

we start by studying the subgroup of the Poincare group that leaves the $\displaystyle{x=0}$ invariant, that is, Lorentz group. we then introduce a matrix representation $\displaystyle{S_{\mu \nu}}$ to define the action of the infinitesimal Lorentz transformation on the field $\displaystyle{\Phi(0)}$

$$
\tag{3.2.2}
\begin{align}
L_{\mu \nu}\Phi(0) & =S_{\mu \nu}\Phi(0)
\end{align}
$$

$\displaystyle{S_{\mu \nu}}$ is the spin operator associated with the field $\displaystyle{\Phi(x)}$. next, by use of the commutation relations of the Poincare group, we translate $\displaystyle{L_{\mu \nu}}$ to non-zero value of $\displaystyle{x}$

$$
\tag{3.2.3}
\begin{align}
e^{ix^{\rho}P_{\rho}}L_{\mu \nu}e^{-ix^{\rho}P_{\rho}} & =S_{\mu \nu}-x_{\mu}O_{\nu}+x_{\nu}P_{\mu}
\end{align}
$$

where we have used the Baker-Campbell-Hausdorff formula

$$
\tag{3.2.4}
\begin{align}
e^{-A}Be^{A} & = \sum^{\infty}_{n=0} \frac{1}{n!}[B,A]_{n} \\
[B,A]_{n} & =[[B,A]_{n-1},A],\quad [B,A]_{0}=B
\end{align}
$$

then we can write the action of the generators as

$$
\tag{3.2.5}
\begin{align}
P_{\mu}\Phi(x) & =-i\partial_{\mu}\Phi(x) \\
L_{\mu \nu}\Phi(x) & =-(x_{\mu}\partial_{\nu}-x_{\nu}\partial_{\mu})\Phi(x)+S_{\mu \nu}\Phi(x)
\end{align}
$$

we follow the same procedure for the conformal group. the subgroup that leaves $\displaystyle{x=0}$ invariant is generated by rotations, dilatations and SCTs. we then denote by $\displaystyle{S_{\mu \nu},\tilde{\Delta},\kappa_{\mu}}$ the respective values of the generators $\displaystyle{L_{\mu \nu},D,K_{\mu}}$ at $\displaystyle{x=0}$. these must form a matrix representation of the reduced algebra

$$
\tag{3.2.6}
\begin{align}
[\tilde{\Delta},S_{\mu \nu}] & =0 \\
[\tilde{\Delta},\kappa_{\mu}] & =-i\kappa_{\mu} \\
[\kappa_{\mu},\kappa_{\nu}] & =0 \\
[\kappa_{\rho},S_{\mu \nu}] & =i(\eta_{\rho \mu}\kappa_{\nu}-\eta_{\rho \nu}\kappa_{\mu}) \\
[S_{\mu \nu},S_{\rho \sigma}] & =i(\eta_{\nu \rho}S_{\mu \sigma}+\eta_{\mu \sigma}S_{\nu \rho}-\eta_{\nu \sigma}S_{\mu \rho}-\eta_{\mu \rho}S_{\nu \sigma})
\end{align}
$$

then

$$
\tag{3.2.7}
\begin{align}
e^{ix^{\rho}P_{\rho}}De^{-ix^{\rho}P_{\rho}} & =D+x^{\nu}P_{\nu} \\
e^{ix^{\rho}P_{\rho}}K_{\mu}e^{-ix^{\rho}P_{\rho}} & =K_{\mu}+2x_{\mu}D-2x^{\nu}L_{\mu \nu}+2x_{\mu}x^{\nu}P_{\nu}-x^{2}P_{\mu}
\end{align}
$$

then we can write the action of the generators as

$$
\tag{3.2.8}
\begin{align}
D\Phi(x) & =(-ix^{\nu}\partial_{\nu}+\tilde{\Delta})\Phi(x) \\
K_{\mu}\Phi(x) & =\left(\kappa_{\mu}+2x_{\mu}\tilde{\Delta}-2x^{\nu}S_{\mu \nu}-i2x_{\mu}x^{\nu}\partial_{\nu}+ix^{2}\partial_{\mu}\right)\Phi(x)
\end{align}
$$

if we demand that the field $\displaystyle{\Phi(x)}$ belong to an irreducible representation of the Lorentz group, then by Schur's lemma, any matrix that commutes with $\displaystyle{S_{\mu \nu}}$ must be proportional to the identity. thus, the only possible representation of the conformal group is the trivial one, that is, $\displaystyle{\tilde{\Delta}}$ is a multiple of the identity, that is, $\displaystyle{\tilde{\Delta}=-i\Delta}$. and $\displaystyle{\kappa_{\mu}}$ must vanish. thus, the action of the conformal group on the spinless field is

$$
\tag{3.2.9}
\begin{align}
\phi(x)\to \phi'(x') & =\left|\frac{\partial x'}{\partial x}\right|^{-\Delta/d}\phi(x)
\end{align}
$$

a field transforming like this is called quasi-primary.
