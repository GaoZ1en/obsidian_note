# N=1 Supersymmetry Reference

## Core Data

Four-dimensional $\mathcal N=1$ supersymmetry is organized by:

- metric, sigma matrices, and spinor contractions;
- off-shell versus on-shell transformations;
- component Lagrangians derived from superspace;
- $F$- and $D$-flatness;
- holomorphy and chiral spurions;
- Weyl-fermion anomaly sums.

## Algebra And Chiral Multiplet

The super-Poincare algebra is

$$\begin{align}
\{Q_\alpha,\bar Q_{\dot\beta}\}=2(\sigma^\mu)_{\alpha\dot\beta}P_\mu,\qquad \{Q_\alpha,Q_\beta\}=0,\qquad \{\bar Q_{\dot\alpha},\bar Q_{\dot\beta}\}=0.
\end{align}$$

For an off-shell chiral multiplet $(\phi,\psi_\alpha,F)$, a common convention is

$$\begin{align}
\delta\phi&=\sqrt2\,\epsilon^\alpha\psi_\alpha,\\
\delta\psi_\alpha&=i\sqrt2(\sigma^\mu\bar\epsilon)_\alpha\partial_\mu\phi+\sqrt2\,\epsilon_\alpha F,\\
\delta F&=i\sqrt2\,\bar\epsilon_{\dot\alpha}(\bar\sigma^\mu)^{\dot\alpha\beta}\partial_\mu\psi_\beta.
\end{align}$$

## Superspace And Wess-Zumino Models

Component fields are most naturally related to superspace by:

- chiral and full superspace coordinates;
- chiral superfields and vector superfields;
- superspace actions;
- theta integration;
- auxiliary-field elimination;
- Wilsonian versus 1PI effective descriptions.

For Wess-Zumino models, Yukawa terms and scalar-potential terms come from superpotential derivatives.

## SUSY Breaking

For an $\mathcal N=1$ model, supersymmetric vacua are governed by:

- $F$-flatness equations;
- $D$-flatness equations modulo gauge transformations;
- flat directions and gauge-invariant coordinates;
- vacuum energy after auxiliary fields are eliminated;
- exact versus metastable SUSY breaking;
- rank assumptions in ISS-style models.

## Holomorphy Pattern

The Wilsonian superpotential is constrained by:

- Wilsonian rather than 1PI data;
- couplings and scales treated as chiral spurions;
- global charges, including $R$-charges;
- holomorphic monomials with the correct charges and dimension;
- constants fixed by weak-coupling matching, decoupling limits, or regularity.

This constrains the superpotential. The Kahler potential can still renormalize.

## Anomaly Matching Rule

Anomaly matching compares the same global anomaly in two descriptions of the infrared theory. The practical rule is:

$$\begin{align}
\text{sum over all left-handed Weyl fermions.}
\end{align}$$

For a chiral superfield $\Phi=(\phi,\psi_\Phi,F)$,

$$\begin{align}
R_{\psi_\Phi}=R_\Phi-1.
\end{align}$$

For $U(1)[SU(K)]^2$, a Weyl fermion in representation $r$ contributes $q\,T(r)$. For $SU(K)$,

$$\begin{align}
T(\square)=T(\bar\square)=\frac12.
\end{align}$$

For $[SU(K)]^3$, the fundamental and anti-fundamental contribute with opposite signs.

## SQCD And Seiberg Duality

The SQCD duality dictionary contains:

- electric color rank and flavor rank;
- global symmetry group;
- chiral superfields and their gauge and flavor representations;
- superfield and Weyl-fermion $R$-charges;
- color and flavor spectator multiplicities;
- electric and magnetic anomaly coefficients;
- magnetic superpotential and meson normalization.
