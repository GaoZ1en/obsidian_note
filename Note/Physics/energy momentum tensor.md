Suppose we have a field theory action that couples to gravity (we do not consider the dynamics of gravity itself)

$$\begin{align}
S & =\int \mathrm{d}^{d}x\sqrt{ -g }\mathcal{L}[\phi,g_{\mu \nu}]
\end{align}$$

take a variation of the action

$$\begin{align}
\delta S & =\int \mathrm{d}^{d}x\sqrt{ -g } \left( \frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial \phi}-\nabla _{\mu} \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \nabla _{\mu}\phi} \right)\delta \phi+\int \mathrm{d}^{d}x\sqrt{ -g }\left(  \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial g_{\mu \nu}}+\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{L}g^{\mu \nu} \right)\delta g_{\mu \nu} \\
 & +\int _{\Sigma _{f}-\Sigma _{i}} \mathrm{d}^{d-1}x\sqrt{ \sigma }\tau _{\mu} \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \nabla _{\mu}\phi}\delta \phi
\end{align}$$

consider a infinitesimal diffeomorphism generated by the vector $\displaystyle{\xi}$

$$\begin{align}
X_{\xi} & =\int \mathrm{d}^{d}x\sqrt{ -g }\left(\mathcal{L}_{\xi}\phi \frac{\delta}{\delta \phi}+\mathcal{L}_{\xi}g_{\mu \nu}\frac{\delta}{\delta g_{\mu \nu}}\right)
\end{align}$$

then act on the action, we have

$$\begin{align}
X_{\xi}\cdot \delta S & =\int _{\Sigma _{f}-\Sigma _{i}} \mathrm{d}^{d-1}x\sqrt{ -\sigma }\tau _{\mu}\xi ^{\mu}\mathcal{L}
\end{align}$$

then the corresponding Noether charge is

$$\begin{align}
H_{\xi} & =\int _{\Sigma}\mathrm{d}^{d-1}x\sqrt{ \sigma }\tau ^{\mu} \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \nabla _{\mu}\phi}\mathcal{L}_{\xi}\phi-\tau _{\mu}\xi ^{\mu}\mathcal{L} \\
 & =\int _{\Sigma}\mathrm{d}^{d-1}x\sqrt{ \sigma }\tau _{\mu}\xi _{\nu} \left( \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \nabla _{\mu}\phi} \nabla ^{\nu}\phi-g^{\mu \nu}\mathcal{L}\right) \\
 & =\int _{\Sigma}\mathrm{d}^{d-1}x\sqrt{ \sigma }\tau _{\mu}\xi _{\nu}T^{\mu \nu}
\end{align}$$

by Noether theorem, we have

$$\begin{align}
X_{\xi}\cdot \Omega & =-\delta H_{\xi}
\end{align}$$

we only need to argue that

$$\begin{align}
-2\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial g_{\mu \nu}} & = \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \nabla _{\mu}\phi}\nabla ^{\nu}\phi
\end{align}$$

take a Lie derivative of the Lagrangian

$$\begin{align}
\mathcal{L}_{\xi}\mathcal{L} & =\xi ^{\rho}\nabla _{\rho}\mathcal{L} \\
 & =\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \phi}\mathcal{L}_{\xi}\phi+\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \nabla _{\mu}\phi}\mathcal{L}_{\xi}(\nabla _{\mu}\phi)+\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial g_{\mu \nu}}\mathcal{L}_{\xi}g_{\mu \nu} \\
 & =\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \phi}\xi ^{\rho}\nabla _{\rho}\phi+\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \nabla _{\mu}\phi} (\xi ^{\rho}\nabla _{\rho}\nabla _{\mu}\phi+\nabla _{\rho}\phi \nabla _{\mu}\xi ^{\rho})+ 2\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial g_{\mu \nu}}g_{\rho \nu}\nabla _{\mu}\xi ^{\rho} \\
\implies \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \nabla _{\mu}\phi}\nabla _{\rho}\phi & +2 \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial g_{\mu \nu}}g_{\rho \nu}=0
\end{align}$$

this argument naturally generalizes to theories with other field components by requiring the general covariance of the Lagrangian.
