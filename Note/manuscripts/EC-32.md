## 1. Specify the Three-Dimensional Action

Let $e^a$ be a real invertible coframe, $a=0,1,2$, with

$$\begin{align}
g=\eta_{ab}e^a\otimes e^b,\qquad \eta=\operatorname{diag}(-1,1,1),\qquad \epsilon_{012}=+1,\qquad \mathrm{vol}=e^0\wedge e^1\wedge e^2. \tag{1.1}
\end{align}$$

All raised epsilon indices use this metric. In particular, raising its time index changes a sign. A Lorentz connection can be dualized to three one-forms $\omega^a$ by defining its matrix components as $\Omega^a{}_b=\epsilon^a{}_{cb}\omega^c$. Thus

$$\begin{align}
T^a=\mathrm de^a+\epsilon^a{}_{bc}\omega^b\wedge e^c,\qquad R^a=\mathrm d\omega^a+\frac12\epsilon^a{}_{bc}\omega^b\wedge\omega^c. \tag{1.2}
\end{align}$$

This connection is metric-compatible. Its coordinate connection is fixed by the tetrad postulate with the first lower slot as derivative slot. Its scalar curvature will be denoted $\mathcal R(\Gamma)$; a bar will always mean the Levi-Civita connection of the same metric. Ordinary exterior derivatives, not antisymmetrized torsionful covariant derivatives, define the curvatures of gauge one-forms.

Choose the following real two-by-two generators:

$$
J_0=\frac12\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\-1&0\end{pmatrix},\quad
J_1=\frac12\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix},\quad
J_2=\frac12\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&-1\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{1.3}
$$

Direct multiplication gives $[J_a,J_b]=\epsilon_{ab}{}^cJ_c$ and $\operatorname{Tr}(J_aJ_b)=\eta_{ab}/2$. This trace is an invariant Lie-algebra bilinear form, not a positive Hilbert norm. Write $\omega=\omega^aJ_a$ and $e=e^aJ_a$. For a matrix-valued connection define

$$\begin{align}
\mathcal C(A)=\operatorname{Tr}\left(A\wedge\mathrm dA+\frac23A\wedge A\wedge A\right),\qquad C(\omega)=\omega_a\wedge\mathrm d\omega^a+\frac13\epsilon_{abc}\omega^a\wedge\omega^b\wedge\omega^c. \tag{1.4}
\end{align}$$

The explicit trace gives $C(\omega)=2\mathcal C(\omega)$. This factor will determine the levels and central coefficients below.

The real bulk action is

$$\begin{align}
I_{\mathrm{MB}}=\int_M\left[2a_0e_a\wedge R^a-\frac{\lambda}{3}\epsilon_{abc}e^a\wedge e^b\wedge e^c +\alpha_3 C(\omega)+\alpha_4 e_a\wedge T^a\right]. \tag{1.5}
\end{align}$$

The coframe and connection are independent variables. There is no matter field in this model. The four constants are additional theory data, with dimensions $[a_0]=L^{-1}$, $[\lambda]=L^{-3}$, $[\alpha_3]=1$ and $[\alpha_4]=L^{-2}$ when $[e^a]=L$. The action coupling lambda is not itself a geometric cosmological constant.

Varying the two fields independently gives

$$\begin{align}
2a_0R^a+2\alpha_4T^a-\lambda\epsilon^a{}_{bc}e^b\wedge e^c=0,\qquad 2\alpha_3R^a+2a_0T^a+\alpha_4\epsilon^a{}_{bc}e^b\wedge e^c=0, \tag{1.6}
\end{align}$$

and the local boundary potential

$$\begin{align}
\Theta_{\mathrm{MB}}=-2a_0e_a\wedge\delta\omega^a-\alpha_3\omega_a\wedge\delta\omega^a-\alpha_4e_a\wedge\delta e^a. \tag{1.7}
\end{align}$$

For example, $\delta R^a=D_\omega\delta\omega^a$; moving this derivative past the one-form e produces the first term in the potential. The variation of $e_a\wedge T^a$ produces both its connection equation and its coframe potential. It is not permissible to impose zero torsion before taking this independent variation.

The orientation also fixes the Einstein normalization:

$$\begin{align}
2e_a\wedge R^a=\mathcal R(\Gamma)\mathrm{vol},\qquad \epsilon_{abc}e^a\wedge e^b\wedge e^c=6\mathrm{vol}. \tag{1.8}
\end{align}$$

These identities hold for arbitrary Lorentz-antisymmetric curvature, without a torsionless pair symmetry. In the Einstein limit $\alpha_3=\alpha_4=0$, the connection equation sets $T=0$. Choosing $a_0=1/(16\pi G_3)>0$ and $\lambda=-a_0/\ell^2$ then gives the metric action $(16\pi G_3)^{-1}\int(\bar R+2/\ell^2)\mathrm{vol}$. Here $[G_3]=L$, unlike the four-dimensional Newton constant.

We work classically in trivialized annular bundle sectors. The bosonic global gauge interpretation will use $PSL(2,\mathbb R)\times PSL(2,\mathbb R)$, with specified $SL(2,\mathbb R)$ matrix lifts for calculations. The invariant polynomial in (1.4) is Lie-algebra data and descends to the adjoint quotient; a fundamental matrix holonomy trace still depends on the lift. No quantization of levels, all-bundle sum or global quantum Chern–Simons theory is assumed.

## 2. A Nondegenerate Torsional AdS Branch

Set $\Delta=\alpha_3\alpha_4-a_0^2$ and assume $\Delta\ne0$. Solving the two independent form equations gives

$$\begin{align}
T^a=\frac p2\epsilon^a{}_{bc}e^b\wedge e^c,\qquad R^a=\frac q2\epsilon^a{}_{bc}e^b\wedge e^c,\qquad p=\frac{\alpha_3\lambda+a_0\alpha_4}{\Delta},\quad q=-\frac{a_0\lambda+\alpha_4^2}{\Delta}. \tag{2.1}
\end{align}$$

Thus $a_0q+\alpha_4p-\lambda=0$ and $\alpha_3q+a_0p+\alpha_4=0$. These are not equations obtained by assuming a spinor source. They follow from this selected gravitational action. The scalar p is not the projective one-form of the metric-affine chapter.

Subtracting the contorsion gives $\omega^a=\bar\omega^a+(p/2)e^a$. The torsion-free curvature is consequently

$$\begin{align}
\bar R^a=\frac12\left(q-\frac{p^2}{4}\right)\epsilon^a{}_{bc}e^b\wedge e^c, \qquad \Lambda_{\mathrm{eff}}=q-\frac{p^2}{4}=-\frac1{\ell^2}<0. \tag{2.2}
\end{align}$$

This defines the positive AdS radius ell. The metric scalar is $\bar R=-6/\ell^2$, whereas the full connection scalar is $\mathcal R(\Gamma)=6q$. They need not coincide.

Introduce

$$\begin{align}
q_\pm=-\frac p2\pm\frac1\ell,\qquad A_\pm=\omega+q_\pm e. \tag{2.3}
\end{align}$$

Their curvature is proportional to $q+pq_\pm+q_\pm^2=0$, so both are flat on the bulk solution. Conversely, since $q_+-q_-=2/\ell\ne0$,

$$\begin{align}
e=\frac{A_+-A_-}{q_+-q_-},\qquad \omega=\frac{q_+A_--q_-A_+}{q_+-q_-}. \tag{2.4}
\end{align}$$

This rewriting is restricted to the open sector in which the reconstructed coframe is invertible. A general Chern–Simons configuration need not belong to that gravity sector.

Define the boundary-completed action by the signed pair

$$\begin{align}
I_{\mathrm{CS}}=\frac{k_+}{4\pi}\int_M\mathcal C(A_+)-\frac{k_-}{4\pi}\int_M\mathcal C(A_-), \qquad \kappa_\pm=\frac{k_\pm}{8\pi}. \tag{2.5}
\end{align}$$

The coefficients k are classical couplings, conventionally called levels. Expanding actual ordered products of one-forms gives

$$\begin{align}
C(\omega+s e)=C(\omega)+2s e_a\wedge R^a+s^2e_a\wedge T^a +\frac{s^3}{3}\epsilon_{abc}e^a\wedge e^b\wedge e^c-s\,\mathrm d(\omega_a\wedge e^a). \tag{2.6}
\end{align}$$

Matching coefficients yields

$$
\begin{aligned}
\alpha_3&=\kappa_+-\kappa_-,&a_0&=\kappa_+q_+-\kappa_-q_-,\\
\alpha_4&=\kappa_+q_+^2-\kappa_-q_-^2,&-\lambda&=\kappa_+q_+^3-\kappa_-q_-^3.
\end{aligned}
\tag{2.7}
$$

In particular,

$$\begin{align}
\Delta=-\kappa_+\kappa_-(q_+-q_-)^2,\qquad k_\pm=4\pi\left[\ell\left(a_0+\frac{\alpha_3p}{2}\right)\pm\alpha_3\right], \qquad I_{\mathrm{CS}}=I_{\mathrm{MB}}-a_0\int_{\partial M}\omega_a\wedge e^a. \tag{2.8}
\end{align}$$

Both the factor eight in kappa and the sign of this boundary completion have therefore been derived. The primitive $\omega_a\wedge e^a$ depends on the frame/trivialization. It is not automatically the global covariant primitive required by the conditional charge-invariance statement in [AST-19](<AST-19.md#L465-L472>). We will compute the charges of the displayed completed action, rather than infer them from bulk equality up to a divergence. Before restricting boundary data its potential is

$$\begin{align}
\Theta_{\mathrm{CS}}=-\alpha_3\omega_a\wedge\delta\omega^a -a_0(\omega_a\wedge\delta e^a+e_a\wedge\delta\omega^a)-\alpha_4e_a\wedge\delta e^a. \tag{2.9}
\end{align}$$

The ordered relation is $\Theta_{\mathrm{MB}}-\Theta_{\mathrm{CS}}=\delta(a_0\omega_a\wedge e^a)$ in this patch. Independent coframe and connection variations have not been identified with one another.

A concrete nondegenerate family will be used below. Choose $\ell>0$ and a dimensionless $\mathcal N>0$, and set

$$\begin{align}
a_0=\frac{3\mathcal N}{4\pi\ell},\quad \alpha_3=\frac{\mathcal N}{2\pi},\quad \alpha_4=-\frac{3\mathcal N}{8\pi\ell^2},\quad \lambda=-\frac{15\mathcal N}{16\pi\ell^3}. \tag{2.10}
\end{align}$$

Then $\Delta=-3\mathcal N^2/(4\pi^2\ell^2)$, $p=1/\ell$, $q=-3/(4\ell^2)$, $k_+=6\mathcal N$ and $k_-=2\mathcal N$. Torsion and full Lorentz curvature are both nonzero, while the metric remains locally AdS with radius ell. The full scalar is $-9/(2\ell^2)$, not $-6/\ell^2$.

The axial torsion in (2.1) has $t_\mu=T^\rho{}_{\mu\rho}=0$ despite $p\ne0$. The trace $\tau_\mu^{\mathrm{AST19}}=T^\rho{}_{\rho\mu}$ is its negative, not a time coordinate. This p is therefore neither an affine projective trace nor an arbitrary Standard Model internal field. Its independence of the overall scale $\mathcal N$ also shows that classical torsion need not universally be a loop-suppressed effect. This is a comparison of specified theories, not a calculation contradicting a conditional five-dimensional loop model. The scale $\mathcal N$ is not a derived gauge rank.

## 3. Select the Boundary Sources and Compute Their Finite Response

Take an annular radial patch with real time t and $\phi\sim\phi+2\pi$. Time and radial distance have length units; phi is dimensionless. Orient the bulk by $\mathrm dt\wedge\mathrm dr\wedge\mathrm d\phi$. A constant-time slice has orientation $\mathrm dr\wedge\mathrm d\phi$, its outer circle has $+\mathrm d\phi$, and the outer timelike boundary has induced orientation $-\mathrm dt\wedge\mathrm d\phi$. These different signs must not be conflated.

Use the explicit basis

$$\begin{align}
L_0=J_2,\qquad L_1=J_1-J_0,\qquad L_{-1}=-J_0-J_1, \qquad [L_m,L_n]=(m-n)L_{m+n}, \tag{3.1}
\end{align}$$

where the displayed bracket applies to the three basis generators. Their traces are $\operatorname{Tr}L_0^2=1/2$ and $\operatorname{Tr}(L_1L_{-1})=-1$.

For the two sectors let $\sigma_+=+1$, $\sigma_-=-1$, $x_s=\sigma_s\phi$, and orient each circle $C_s=\sigma_sC_\phi$ by increasing its own x. The negative sector also uses the inner automorphism

$$
W=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\-1&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
WL_1W^{-1}=L_{-1},\quad WL_{-1}W^{-1}=L_1,\quad WL_0W^{-1}=-L_0.
\tag{3.2}
$$

It preserves the trace. The opposite signed bulk coefficient and reversed circle orientation then give a positive $k_s$ in each sector's own canonical formulas. Omitting either convention changes the reported charge sign.

In a fixed radial gauge set $b=\exp(\rho L_0)$, where rho increases outward. Use $A_+=b^{-1}a_+b+b^{-1}\mathrm db$ and $A_-=ba_-b^{-1}+b\mathrm db^{-1}$. In the negative sector the reduced connection is the W-conjugate of the aligned connection below. Its spatial component in physical phi coordinates is therefore $a_{\phi,-}=-L_{-1}+u_-L_1$; the positive one is $a_{\phi,+}=L_1-u_+L_{-1}$.

In each aligned sector impose the Drinfeld–Sokolov form

$$\begin{align}
a_x=L_1-uL_{-1},\qquad u=\frac{2\pi}{k}\mathcal L,\qquad a_t=\mu L_1-\mu' L_0+\left(\frac{\mu''}{2}-\mu u\right)L_{-1}. \tag{3.3}

A prime means $\partial_x$. The real periodic function mu, of dimension $L^{-1}$, is the prescribed chiral source. The dimensionless density $\mathcal L$ is the response. For $\mu_\pm=1/\ell$, the physical components obey $a_{t,+}=a_{\phi,+}/\ell$ and $a_{t,-}=-a_{\phi,-}/\ell$. These are selected mixed/chiral boundary conditions, not simultaneous arbitrary independent Dirichlet sources for boundary coframe and spin connection. Equation (2.9) records the unrestricted potential before this selection. An independent spin-current/zweibein source problem would require its own ensemble and Ward calculation.

All phase-space and black-hole variations below hold the bulk couplings fixed, hence also p, q, ell and $k_\pm$. Comparing different values of $\mathcal N$ in (2.10) compares theories; it is not an extended first law with varied couplings.

At any finite radial cutoff, varying the Chern–Simons action on shell gives the outer radial piece

$$\begin{align}
\delta I_{\mathrm{rad}}^{(r_c)} =\sum_s\frac{k_s}{4\pi}\int\mathrm dt\,\mathrm dx_s\, \operatorname{Tr}(a_t\delta a_x-a_x\delta a_t). \tag{3.4}
\end{align}$$

The sign includes the induced outer-boundary orientation. At fixed b the radial conjugations cancel inside this trace exactly, not only to leading asymptotic order. Direct multiplication of (3.3) gives

$$\begin{align}
\operatorname{Tr}(a_t\delta a_x-a_x\delta a_t) =\frac12\partial_x^2\delta\mu-2u\delta\mu, \qquad \delta I_{\mathrm{rad,ren}}=-\sum_s\int\mathrm dt\,\mathrm dx_s\,\mathcal L_s\delta\mu_s. \tag{3.5}
\end{align}$$

The second derivative integrates to zero on the periodic circle. Thus the completed action has a finite cutoff-independent radial source response. The local transgression in (2.8) and the chiral ensemble specify the variational renormalization scheme; no extra finite functional of the sources is added. A field-independent action reference is not determined by this variation. We have not computed a complete finite quantum partition function or replaced this prescription by a general metric-Dirichlet counterterm series.

Equation (3.5) is the outer radial contribution. Temporal endcaps and an inner boundary must either have fixed data or be included explicitly. In the driven experiment below their symplectic contributions are retained, rather than discarded because a source vanishes at the temporal endpoints.

## 4. Classical CPS, Ward Identities and the Canonical Central Term

For one sector with coefficient $k/(4\pi)$, the local potential and Iyer–Wald current are

$$\begin{align}
\Theta=-\frac{k}{4\pi}\operatorname{Tr}(A\wedge\delta A),\qquad \omega_{\mathrm{IW}}(\delta_1,\delta_2)=-\frac{k}{2\pi}\operatorname{Tr}(\delta_1A\wedge\delta_2A). \tag{4.1}
\end{align}$$

Here omega with the IW subscript is a field-space current, not the Lorentz connection. The published canonical convention is

$$\begin{align}
\Omega_\Sigma=-\int_\Sigma\omega_{\mathrm{IW}},\qquad \delta H_X=\Omega_\Sigma(X,\delta). \tag{4.2}
\end{align}$$

Reversing the order of the two arguments without changing the sign is an error. The separate covariant xCPS calculation checks the kinetic first variation, boundary potential and this current sign; the full non-Abelian coframe/connection variation is independently checked in the form algebra.

On flat solutions and linearized-flat variations, $X_\Lambda A=D_A\Lambda$ gives

\delta H_\Lambda=\frac{k}{2\pi}\oint_C\operatorname{Tr}(\Lambda\delta A). \tag{4.3}
\end{align}$$

This follows by integrating $\operatorname{Tr}(D_A\Lambda\wedge\delta A)=\mathrm d\operatorname{Tr}(\Lambda\delta A)$, with $D_A\delta A=\delta F=0$. For outer charges take transformations trivial at the inner boundary. The radial gauge need only be fixed near the outer boundary; fixing it globally on every physical variation would obscure the edge symplectic pairing. The independent seed epsilon is held fixed. ## 5. A Driven Construction with an Evaluated Bulk–Boundary Balance

Work in the positive sector, so $x=\phi$, about the massless outer data $\mathcal L_0=0$, $\mu_0=v=1/\ell$. Let $0\le t\le\mathcal T$, $\mathcal T>0$, and define

$$\begin{align}
y=x+vt,\qquad f(t)=\sin^2\frac{\pi t}{\mathcal T},\qquad \mathcal I(t)=\frac t2-\frac{\mathcal T}{4\pi}\sin\frac{2\pi t}{\mathcal T},\qquad \dot{\mathcal I}=f. \tag{5.1}
\end{align}$$

Two real solution-tangent variations are

$$
\begin{aligned}
\delta_1\mathcal L&=A\cos2y,&\delta_1\mu&=0,\\
\delta_2\mu&=Bf(t)\cos2y,&

\end{aligned}
\tag{5.2}
$$

This experiment uses the enlarged space of solutions and external sources: the second variation is not tangent to one fixed-source phase space. Here A is dimensionless and $[B]=L^{-1}$. Substitution gives $(\partial_t-v\partial_x)\delta_i\mathcal L=-k\partial_x^3\delta_i\mu/(4\pi)$ for both variations. The cubic source term is essential for the second one. Both sources vanish at the temporal endpoints, but the second state variation does not return to zero.

This is not just a pair satisfying a boundary PDE. At $u_0=0$, the residual seeds

produce exactly (5.2) by (4.5)–(4.6). Extend their matrix parameters into a radial annulus with coordinate $0\le s\le1$ using $h(s)=3s^2-2s^3$. Inside invariant traces one may remove the fixed radial conjugation. In that frame the background has $a_s=0$, $a_x=L_1$, $a_t=vL_1$, and

$$\begin{align}

\end{align}$$

All three components of linearized flatness vanish, including the radial ones. Through the invertible map (2.4) these are linearized gravity solutions; sufficiently small perturbation parameters preserve triad invertibility. There is no compensating inner flux because the variations vanish there. This is the oriented Stokes balance in the published $\Omega=-\delta\Theta$ convention, including the temporal endcaps. Globally setting $\delta A_s=0$ on every variation would have erased (5.5); a globally nontrivial inner transformation could instead conceal an inner-boundary contribution. Neither shortcut is used.

## 6. A Stationary Construction: Torsional BTZ Geometry and Charges

Consider $r>r_+>r_->0$, with

$$\begin{align}
f(r)=\frac{(r^2-r_+^2)(r^2-r_-^2)}{\ell^2r^2},\qquad w(r)=-\frac{r_+r_-}{\ell r^2},\qquad e^0=\sqrt f\,\mathrm dt,\quad e^1=\frac{\mathrm dr}{\sqrt f},\quad e^2=r(\mathrm d\phi+w\,\mathrm dt). \tag{6.1}
\end{align}$$

The coframe volume is $r\,\mathrm dt\wedge\mathrm dr\wedge\mathrm d\phi$, with the orientation already chosen. Computing the Levi-Civita connection from the actual metric and then using the tetrad postulate yields, in our dual convention,

$$\begin{align}
\bar\omega^0=\sqrt f\,\mathrm d\phi,\qquad \bar\omega^1=-\frac w{\sqrt f}\mathrm dr,\qquad \bar\omega^2=\frac r{\ell^2}\mathrm dt+rw\,\mathrm d\phi. \tag{6.2}
\end{align}$$

Set $\omega^a=\bar\omega^a+(p/2)e^a$. Dedicated component covariant differentiation gives $Q=0$, exactly the torsion and full constant-curvature tensor in (2.1), the scalar $6q$, and zero torsion trace. It also gives flat $A_\pm=\bar\omega\pm e/\ell$, although the Lorentz curvature is not zero. Local Cartan flatness is therefore not absence of metric or torsional gravitational geometry.

The directly computed invariant traces are

$$\begin{align}
\operatorname{Tr}[(A_{\phi,\pm})^2]=\frac{(r_+\mp r_-)^2}{2\ell^2},\qquad u_\pm=\frac{(r_+\mp r_-)^2}{4\ell^2}. \tag{6.3}
\end{align}$$
