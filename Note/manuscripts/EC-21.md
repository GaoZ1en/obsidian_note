# Supersymmetry and Tensor–Spinor Geometry

DRAFT — independent reconstruction; bounded review recorded.

Supersymmetry requires a parity assignment and odd generators whose graded brackets contain spacetime translations. Writing a vector as a bispinor, or adjoining commuting twistor coordinates, does not supply those data. We construct a flat graded incidence correspondence, prove that its supertranslations descend to projective supertwistor space, and calculate its Berezinian and helicity weights. This connects three precise objects: a complex chiral function algebra, an open holomorphic correspondence, and conditional massless representation characters. Their different reality, equation-of-motion and interaction assumptions will remain visible.

The ordinary spinor and twistor conventions are those of [AST-07](<AST-07.md>), [AST-13](<AST-13.md>) and [AST-14](<AST-14.md>). The basic superspace action of [AST-15](<AST-15.md>) is not repeated; the new work is the graded incidence map and its representation geometry.

## 1. Parity, Spin Spaces and Physical Normalization

Use signature $(-,+,+,+)$, orientation $dt\wedge dx\wedge dy\wedge dz$ and $c=\hbar=1$. Physical coordinates have length units and momentum has inverse-length units. The unnormalized Pauli map and alternating matrices are

$$
X(v)=v^0I+v^i\sigma_i,\qquad \det X(v)=-\eta(v,v),\qquad
\epsilon_{AB}=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\-1&0\end{pmatrix},\quad
(\epsilon^{AB})=(\epsilon_{AB})^{-1}.
\tag{1.1}
$$

An unprimed upper spinor transforms by $A\in SL(2,\mathbb C)$, its lower-index dual by $A^{-T}$, and the primed upper space by $\overline A$ on the Lorentzian real form. On a complexification the two spin spaces are independent. The physical Dirac convention is

$$\begin{align}
\rho(A)=\operatorname{diag}((A^\dagger)^{-1},A),\qquad \{\gamma^a,\gamma^b\}=-2\eta^{ab}I,\qquad \gamma_5=\operatorname{diag}(-I,+I). \tag{1.2}
\end{align}$$

Thus the fundamental unprimed upper space is the positive-gamma-five block. Geometric spinor components and ordinary twistor coordinates below commute. Fermionic classical fields have Grassmann-odd coefficients; quantum fields require an operator algebra and a state. Spinor indices alone specify neither statistics nor a quantum theory.

For a supercommutative algebra, homogeneous elements obey $fg=(-1)^{|f||g|}gf$, with parity $|f|\in\mathbb Z_2$. Odd coordinates satisfy $\xi_i\xi_j=-\xi_j\xi_i$ and $\xi_i^2=0$. All odd derivatives are left derivatives:

$$\begin{align}
\partial^L_{\xi_i}(fg)=(\partial^L_{\xi_i}f)g+ (-1)^{|f|}f\partial^L_{\xi_i}g, \qquad [U,V]_{\rm gr}=UV-(-1)^{|U||V|}VU. \tag{1.3}
\end{align}$$

For example $\partial_2^L(\xi_1\xi_2)=-\xi_1$. Consequently $\{\partial_i^L,\xi_j\}=\delta_{ij}$ and $\{\partial_1^L,\partial_2^L\}=0$, while their ordinary commutator on $\xi_1\xi_2$ is $-2$. Replacing odd derivatives by commuting ones changes the algebra, even if the number of coordinate labels is unchanged.

## 2. What a Fixed-Null-Momentum Multiplet Proves

The physical extended algebra, with no central charge in the massless modules considered here, has

$$\begin{align}
\{Q^{AI},(Q^{BJ})^\dagger\}=2\delta^{IJ}P^{AB'},\qquad P=X(p)=EI+\mathbf p\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma, \qquad \{Q,Q\}=0. \tag{2.1}
\end{align}$$

The primed label on the adjoint is understood in the displayed momentum matrix. In a positive Hilbert representation $P$ must be positive semidefinite. For a nonzero future null momentum,

$$\begin{align}
P=uu^\dagger,\qquad u^\dagger u=2E,\qquad u_A:=\epsilon_{AB}u^B. \tag{2.2}
\end{align}$$

The lowered $u_A$ is a covector momentum spinor. It transforms by $A^{-T}$; this is the epsilon map, not complex conjugation. Projectivizing its direction forgets the energy normalization.

Let $c_I^\dagger$ be exterior multiplication and $c_I$ its positive-Hilbert adjoint contraction on a finite exterior space. Then

$$\begin{align}
\{c_I,c_J^\dagger\}=\delta_{IJ},\qquad \{c_I,c_J\}=\{c_I^\dagger,c_J^\dagger\}=0, \qquad Q^{AI}=\sqrt2\,u^A c_I^\dagger. \tag{2.3}
\end{align}$$

Multiplying the CAR gives (2.1) directly. The charges have units $L^{-1/2}$. The state count per Clifford vacuum is $2^N$; choosing which nonzero charge is creation fixes a helicity ladder $h_r=h_0+r/2$ with multiplicity $\binom Nr$. Overall vacuum parity, internal representation and CPT completion remain additional data. At $p=(E,0,0,E)$, $u=(\sqrt{2E},0)^T$, so the active charge has anticommutator $4E$ and the other spin component vanishes by positivity.

This is a fixed-momentum on-shell representation. It is not a many-particle Fock space, an arbitrary off-shell momentum module, a superspace coordinate construction or a local supersymmetry theory. The exact CAR checks for $N=1,2,4,8$ and the generic rank-one charge check at $N=4$ are available in the [corrected representation evidence](<../checks/EC-21-parent/on-shell-multiplets-corrected-results.md>); their proof is the exterior multiplication/contraction identity just used.

## 3. A Complex Chiral Supertranslation Algebra

Now choose, explicitly, the split complex space $\mathbb C^{4|2N}$ with even coordinates $y^{AA'}$ and odd coordinates $\vartheta^{AI}$, $A,A'=0,1$, $I=1,\ldots,N$. Its functions are holomorphic even-coordinate functions tensored with the exterior algebra in $2N$ generators. This is a chiral complex space, not the full real $4|4N$ superspace with a prescribed conjugation.

For comparison with physical coordinates choose an arbitrary $L_0>0$ and put

$$\begin{align}
y^{AA'}=iX(x)^{AA'}/L_0. \tag{3.1}
\end{align}$$

Odd coordinates may likewise be made dimensionless by dividing physical odd coordinates of dimension $L^{1/2}$ by $\sqrt{L_0}$. The reference length fixes coordinate units, not a physical coupling or ultraviolet scale.

Define complex graded derivations

$$\begin{align}
p_{AA'}=\frac{\partial}{\partial y^{AA'}},\qquad q_{AI}=\frac{\partial^L}{\partial\vartheta^{AI}},\qquad \widetilde q_{A'}{}^I=2\vartheta^{AI}p_{AA'}. \tag{3.2}
\end{align}$$

The tilde is not a Hilbert adjoint or a reality condition. Their algebra is

$$\begin{align}
\{q_{AI},q_{BJ}\}=0,\qquad \{\widetilde q_{A'}{}^I,\widetilde q_{B'}{}^J\}=0,\qquad \{q_{AI},\widetilde q_{B'}{}^J\}=2\delta_I{}^Jp_{AB'}, \qquad [p,q]=[p,\widetilde q]=[p,p]=0. \tag{3.3}
\end{align}$$

To prove the mixed bracket, apply the left product rule to $\partial_{\vartheta^{AI}}(\vartheta^{CJ}p_{CB'}f)$. Its first term is $\delta_A^C\delta_I^Jp_{CB'}f$; the second cancels the reverse composition. In the tilde–tilde bracket the even derivatives commute and the two odd coefficients anticommute. These arguments work for every $N$ and every function in this algebra; no field equation is used.

The phases in (3.2) have been fixed rather than identified silently with physical charges. If $T^{AA'}=(I\partial_t-\sigma_i\partial_i)^{AA'}$, raising both indices of $p$ with the inverse epsilon gives

$$\begin{align}
p^{AA'}=-\frac{iL_0}{2}T^{AA'}. \tag{3.4}
\end{align}$$

Rescaling the raised $q$ by $\sqrt{2/L_0}$ and the raised tilde by $-\sqrt{2/L_0}$ therefore gives the operator bracket $2iT^{AA'}$ used in [AST-15](<AST-15.md#L124-L169>). On positive-frequency waves it is $2X(p)$. This is an algebraic basis comparison, not a positive-adjoint structure on the chiral function algebra. Pullback actions, field coefficients and particle states can be contragredient; a holomorphic derivative is not identified with the creator in (2.3) merely from its index.

Closure here is off shell in the kinematic sense of acting on arbitrary chiral functions. It has not supplied an off-shell component action, auxiliary fields or their elimination. Those require additional choices, as the separate chiral-multiplet action in AST-15 illustrates.

## 4. Dual Supertwistor Incidence and Its Fibres

Rename the dual twistor coordinates of [AST-14](<AST-14.md#L28-L70>) as $(\mu^{A'},\lambda_A)$; they were called $(\omega^{A'},\pi_A)$ there. Adjoin $N$ odd homogeneous coordinates $\chi^I$, all of projective weight one:

$$\begin{align}
(\mu,\lambda,\chi)\sim(t\mu,t\lambda,t\chi),\qquad t\in\mathbb C^*,\qquad \lambda\ne0. \tag{4.1}
\end{align}$$

The chosen split open supertwistor model has dimension $3|N$. Its body is $\mathbb{PT}^{\circ}=\{[\mu,\lambda]\in\mathbb{CP}^3:\lambda\ne0\}$. Define

$$\begin{align}
\mu^{A'}=y^{AA'}\lambda_A, \qquad \chi^I=\vartheta^{AI}\lambda_A. \tag{4.2}
\end{align}$$

Because $y=iX/L_0$, the even part is exactly $\mu=i(X^T/L_0)\lambda$, the book's dual incidence, not its opposite primed-covector convention. The odd coordinates are new Grassmann directions; the commuting lambda and mu have not been relabelled fermions.

The correspondence space is $\mathcal F_N=\mathbb C^{4|2N}\times\mathbb{CP}^1$. Projection to chiral superspace has projective-line fibres. Projection by (4.2) to supertwistor space has affine fibres of dimension $2|N$: at fixed nonzero lambda there are two independent even equations on four y entries, and $N$ independent odd equations on $2N$ theta entries. On $\lambda_0=1$, $z=\lambda_1$, these are

$$\begin{align}
\mu^{A'}=y^{0A'}+zy^{1A'},\qquad \chi^I=\vartheta^{0I}+z\vartheta^{1I}. \tag{4.3}
\end{align}$$

Their fibre directions are the even and odd vector fields

$$\begin{align}
V_{A'}=\partial_{y^{1A'}}-z\partial_{y^{0A'}},\qquad W_I=\partial^L_{\vartheta^{1I}}-z\partial^L_{\vartheta^{0I}}. \tag{4.4}
\end{align}$$

They annihilate all incidence coordinates, and all their graded brackets vanish: they do not differentiate z and their remaining coefficients are constant along the fibres. This proves integrability on this flat patch. The other lambda patch gives the same leaves. The excluded lambda-zero locus is essential: the displayed incidence Jacobians lose rank there. No projection of three-dimensional ordinary twistor space onto four-dimensional spacetime has been invented.

The chiral generators descend explicitly to homogeneous degree-zero vector fields:

$$\begin{align}
\widehat q_{AI}=\lambda_A\partial^L_{\chi^I},\qquad \widehat{\widetilde q}_{A'}{}^I=2\chi^I\partial_{\mu^{A'}},\qquad \widehat p_{AA'}=\lambda_A\partial_{\mu^{A'}}. \tag{4.5}
\end{align}$$

Indeed $q\chi=\delta\lambda$, $q\mu=0$, $\widetilde q\mu=2\delta\chi$, $\widetilde q\chi=0$, and $p\mu=\delta\lambda$. These are precisely the pullbacks of (4.5). Equality on the algebra generators and the graded Leibniz rule prove intertwining on all polynomial functions, and the holomorphic chain rule extends it on the stated domains. Each operator commutes with the projective Euler derivation, since its coefficient and derivative have opposite weights. It therefore acts on each homogeneous sheaf $\mathcal O(D)$.

For a concrete sign-sensitive example at $N\ge2$, let $F=\chi^1\chi^2\mu^{0'}$. Then

$$\begin{align}
\widehat q_{0,2}F=-\lambda_0\chi^1\mu^{0'},\qquad \{\widehat q_{0,1},\widehat{\widetilde q}_{0'}{}^1\}F =2\lambda_0\chi^1\chi^2. \tag{4.6}
\end{align}$$

The latter is $2\widehat p_{00'}F$. One composition vanishes because $(\chi^1)^2=0$; the other supplies the translation. This is genuine graded closure, not the ordinary commutativity of two coordinate shifts. Exact new exterior-algebra checks at $N=2$ cover the incidence intertwiner, fibre annihilators, projective weights and these negative controls; the preceding proofs state the general-N reason.

## 5. The Projective Berezinian Is a Separate Geometric Calculation

On $\lambda_0\ne0$ use even coordinates $(z,a,b)=(\lambda_1,\mu^{0'},\mu^{1'})/\lambda_0$ and odd coordinates $\eta^I=\chi^I/\lambda_0$. On the other patch,

$$\begin{align}
z'=1/z,\qquad a'=a/z,\qquad b'=b/z,\qquad \eta'^I=\eta^I/z. \tag{5.1}
\end{align}$$

The even Jacobian determinant is $-z^{-4}$. Its even-to-odd upper-right block is zero; the odd diagonal block is $z^{-1}I_N$. The lower-left odd block does not enter the resulting triangular Berezinian:

$$\begin{align}
\operatorname{Ber}J=\frac{\det J_{\rm even}}{\det J_{\rm odd}} =-z^{N-4}. \tag{5.2}
\end{align}$$

This is a reciprocal odd determinant, not the determinant of a $(3+N)$-dimensional commuting Jacobian. Repeating the transition multiplies the two Berezinians to one. Relabelling the four even homogeneous coordinates gives the same weight on the other projective overlaps, so for this specified split projective superspace

$$\begin{align}
\operatorname{Ber}(\mathbb{CP}^{3|N})\simeq\mathcal O(N-4). \tag{5.3}
\end{align}$$

For $N=4$ the transition is the constant minus sign, removable by local volume-frame choices, and the holomorphic Berezinian line is trivial. For $N=8$ its degree is four, not zero. This is a statement about this supermanifold's volume line. It does not manufacture a physical integration contour, action, positive norm or preferred interacting theory. In particular it is not a proof that four-dimensional physics must have $N=4$ supersymmetry.

## 6. Homogeneous Coefficients and Both Helicity Branches

Let a supertwistor overlap representative have integer total degree D. Its finite odd expansion is

$$\begin{align}
\mathcal F_D(Z,\chi)=\sum_{r=0}^{N}\frac1{r!} \chi^{I_1}\cdots\chi^{I_r}f_{I_1\cdots I_r}(Z), \qquad f_r(tZ)=t^{D-r}f_r(Z). \tag{6.1}
\end{align}$$

The coefficients are antisymmetric in the internal labels. This formula can be read for Cech cochains and their quotient classes, not only for global functions. We retain an open twistor domain and the contour/holomorphic extension assumptions of AST-14.

In the book's dual convention a coefficient of weight w has the conditional zero-rest-mass helicity

$$\begin{align}
h=-\frac{w+2}{2},\qquad w=D-r,\qquad h_r=\frac{r-D-2}{2}. \tag{6.2}
\end{align}$$

Here is a forward construction checking both signs. With $D\lambda=\lambda_0d\lambda_1-\lambda_1d\lambda_0$, the nonnegative-helicity branch $w=-m-2$, $m\ge0$, uses

$$\begin{align}
\phi_{A_1\cdots A_m}(x)=\frac1{2\pi i} \oint\lambda_{A_1}\cdots\lambda_{A_m} f_{-m-2}(iX^T\lambda/L_0,\lambda)\,D\lambda. \tag{6.3}
\end{align}$$

For the opposite branch $w=m-2$, use instead

$$\begin{align}
\widetilde\phi_{A'_1\cdots A'_m}(x)=\frac1{2\pi i} \oint\frac{\partial^m f_{m-2}}{ \partial\mu^{A'_1}\cdots\partial\mu^{A'_m}} (iX^T\lambda/L_0,\lambda)\,D\lambda. \tag{6.4}
\end{align}$$

Both integrands have total weight zero. Differentiating the first with respect to spacetime introduces another lambda, whose alternating contraction vanishes. In the second, the spacetime derivative produces a lambda times another mu derivative; the raised primed contraction of two symmetric mu derivatives vanishes. These are the two zero-rest-mass equations, not a construction of interacting potentials. Patch coboundaries give zero under the same extension and contour conditions.

Take a dimensionless $\nu>0$ and the actual overlap functions

$$\begin{align}
f^{(+)}_{-m-2}=\frac{e^{-\nu\mu^{0'}/\lambda_0}} {\lambda_0^{m+1}\lambda_1},\qquad f^{(-)}_{m-2}=\frac{\lambda_0^{m-1}}{\lambda_1} e^{-\nu\mu^{0'}/\lambda_0}. \tag{6.5}
\end{align}$$

On $\lambda=(1,\zeta)$, incidence gives $\mu^{0'}=i[t+z+(x+iy)\zeta]/L_0$. A positively oriented finite circle about $\zeta=0$ has no moving pole. Residues yield

$$\begin{align}
\phi_{0\cdots0}=e^{-i\nu(t+z)/L_0},\qquad \widetilde\phi_{0'\cdots0'}=(-\nu)^m e^{-i\nu(t+z)/L_0}, \tag{6.6}
\end{align}$$

with all other components zero. The negative branch has been evaluated explicitly for $m=1,2,4$, including weights $-1,0,2$. Nonzero residues show these overlap representatives are not coboundaries in the chosen domain.

For $m=2$, suppressing its independently adjustable amplitude, the primed result reconstructs

$$\begin{align}
F^- =e^{-i\nu(t+z)/L_0}(dt+dz)\wedge(dx+i\,dy), \qquad dF^-=0,\quad \partial_\mu(F^-)^{\mu\nu}=0. \tag{6.7}
\end{align}$$

Its momentum points along $-z$. Rotation by angle alpha about that momentum sends the polarization $(1,i,0)/\sqrt2$ to $e^{+i\alpha}$ times itself. With active phase $e^{-ih\alpha}$, its helicity is $-1$, exactly the w=0 label in (6.2). The unprimed branch gives the opposite circular polarization and helicity. The contour profiles are dimensionless; physical field amplitudes restore the appropriate field units. A real field requires its frequency-conjugate partner, not a pointwise reality condition on one holomorphic representative.

The open domain cannot be discarded. The standard projective-space theorem gives $H^1(\mathbb{CP}^3,\mathcal O(k))=0$ for every integer k. In this split model the odd expansion reduces the corresponding compact degree-D H-one group to a finite sum of those zero groups. Trivial Berezinian at $N=4$ does not change that fact. Nontrivial Minkowski wave classes belong to suitable open domains, not to that compact group. This global cohomology statement is theorem input, not a finite numerical check or a Hilbert-space completeness claim.

## 7. Exterior Characters, Parity and CPT

If the odd coordinates transform in an internal vector space V, the degree-r monomials transform in $\Lambda^rV$. A scalar superfield's coefficient tensor transforms in the dual $\Lambda^rV^*$. For $V=\mathbb C^4$ with $SU(4)$ action, the two sequences are

$$
\begin{array}{c|ccccc}
r&0&1&2&3&4\\ \hline
\Lambda^rV&1&4&6&\overline4&1\\
\Lambda^rV^*&1&\overline4&6&4&1
\end{array}.
\tag{7.1}
$$

The actual exterior characters obey $\overline{\chi_r}=\chi_{4-r}$; multiplicities are $\binom Nr$. Distinguishing monomials from coefficients prevents a hidden conjugate-representation change. An R symmetry rotates the supercharges/internal odd directions while preserving their pairing. It is not automatically the gauge group acting on matter. An additional gauge representation, connection and action must be supplied separately.

For $N=4,D=0$, and $N=8,D=2$, (6.2) gives

$$
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
(N,D)&h_r&\text{multiplicities in increasing }r\\ \hline
(4,0)&-1,-\tfrac12,0,\tfrac12,1&1,4,6,4,1\\
(8,2)&-2,-\tfrac32,-1,-\tfrac12,0,\tfrac12,1,\tfrac32,2
&1,8,28,56,70,56,28,8,1
\end{array}.
\tag{7.2}
$$

Thus the first begins at negative-one helicity and the second at negative-two. They have respectively $8+8$ and $128+128$ bosonic/fermionic state multiplicities when a physical spin-statistics interpretation is assigned. These are the familiar linearized vector and spin-two helicity characters. They do not by themselves define Yang–Mills or supergravity interactions, a gauge group or a reality structure.

An overall parity choice must not be suppressed. Since $2h_r=r-D-2$, physical spin-statistics requires

$$\begin{align}
(-1)^{F_r}=(-1)^{r+D}. \tag{7.3}
\end{align}$$

Equivalently choose the Clifford vacuum/superfield parity to be D modulo two. Degree parity alone has a freely shiftable vacuum convention. At $N=2,D=-1$, the four-state list is one fermion at $-1/2$, two scalar states at zero, and one fermion at $+1/2$, rather than bosons at even r.

CPT reverses helicity and conjugates internal charges. At the helicity-character level, comparing r with $N-r$ gives the reflected weight

$$\begin{align}
D_{\rm CPT}=N-D-4,\qquad h_r(D_{\rm CPT})=-h_{N-r}(D). \tag{7.4}
\end{align}$$

This follows by direct substitution; self-reflection of the helicity list requires $D=(N-4)/2$. For odd N this is not an integer degree in the present untwisted projective model. At $N=1$, degrees $-2$ and $-1$ give respectively $(0,+1/2)$ and $(-1/2,0)$: the two characters pair rather than each being a neutral CPT-complete chiral module.

Even when the degree is self-reflected, this is only a necessary helicity-character condition. It does not construct an antiunitary CPT map, its square, a positive inner product or a Lorentzian contour/reality operation. In particular the $N=2$ four-state character is not automatically a neutral full hypermultiplet. A neutral full hypermultiplet has four real scalar fields and two Weyl fermions, with four bosonic and four fermionic on-shell degrees of freedom. A permitted half-hypermultiplet instead requires suitable pseudoreal gauge-representation and reality data. Neither alternative follows from an exterior-degree table alone.

The number $N-4$ also occurred in the Berezinian line. The two calculations explain their limited relation: one is a projective volume weight and the other a helicity-reflection weight. Their numerical agreement does not supply the missing CPT or interaction structure.

## 8. A Curved Consistency Test and the Affine Boundary

A flat incidence formula does not become a supersymmetry on an arbitrary background by substituting a position-dependent coframe. A rigid background requires the relevant spinors to satisfy its chosen Killing-type equations and their integrability conditions. Local supersymmetry additionally transforms dynamical gauge/gravitational fields and satisfies supergravity torsion/closure constraints.

There is an elementary obstruction for the particularly restrictive parallel-spinor condition. Take a torsion-free metric-compatible four-dimensional connection of constant sectional curvature K, with

$$\begin{align}
R_{abcd}=K(\eta_{ac}\eta_{bd}-\eta_{ad}\eta_{bc}), \qquad R_{ab}=3K\eta_{ab},\qquad R=12K. \tag{8.1}
\end{align}$$

K has units $L^{-2}$. With the fixed spin convention $\Omega=-\omega_{ab}\gamma^{ab}/4$, its spin curvature on an orthonormal 01-plane is

$$\begin{align}
\mathcal F_{01}=-\frac14R_{ab01}\gamma^{ab} =\frac K2\gamma^{01},\qquad \mathcal F_{01}^2=\frac{K^2}{4}I. \tag{8.2}
\end{align}$$

A parallel spinor must obey $\mathcal F_{01}\varepsilon=0$. If $K\ne0$, (8.2) forces $\varepsilon=0$. The Ricci/scalar/Bianchi contractions and the actual spin matrix were checked separately. This excludes a nonzero parallel spinor in this model, not all supersymmetry on anti-de Sitter or other curved backgrounds. Generalized Killing equations with extra curvature/flux terms are different equations and require their own model and reality conditions.

There is also a global issue: a local spinor basis does not prove that a chosen manifold admits the required global spin bundle or Killing spinors. In general metric–affine geometry the [MAG-04](<MAG-04.md>) distinction remains essential. Strict ordinary Clifford compatibility with the full tangent connection forces nonmetricity zero. A chosen Lorentz projection can define an ordinary spin connection, but its curvature is not generally the antisymmetric part of full affine curvature; the difference contains the symmetric-connection square. Using that projection is additional coupling input, not a full-affine spin lift.

Likewise the odd-index structure coefficient in a superspace graded bracket is not automatically the ordinary spacetime torsion of an Einstein–Cartan connection. Promoting transformation parameters to functions produces derivative-of-parameter terms; their existence alone proves neither invariance of an action nor closure of a specified supergravity system. The flat construction here supplies neither those missing fields nor a universal curved correspondence.

## 9. What the Construction Establishes

We have an explicit complex graded translation algebra, a flat dual incidence map intertwining it, integrable $2|N$ incidence fibres and projectively well-defined induced generators. Its projective volume transition and its conditional helicity/reflection weights were calculated rather than guessed from index counts. Nonzero opposite-helicity contour examples and the constant-curvature parallel-spinor obstruction make the physical boundaries testable.

The representation choices can organize useful linearized multiplets. Choosing a consistent interacting theory still requires an action, gauge representation, reality/CPT data, dynamics and, on curved backgrounds, the appropriate symmetry equations. No Standard Model gauge group, number of generations, symmetry-breaking pattern or ultraviolet completion has been deduced.
