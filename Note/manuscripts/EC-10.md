# Equilibrium and Nonequilibrium Quantum Matter in a Projected Geometry Channel

Status: DRAFT — bounded review recorded.

A thermal state needs a Hamiltonian, a clock, a trace space and an ensemble. None of these is fixed merely by specifying area eigenvalues. We use the explicitly gauge-invariant lepton–Higgs/geometry channel constructed in [EC-09](<EC-09.md>) to calculate its Gibbs state, area fluctuations, KMS correlation, susceptibility, quench and thermal relaxation. The same noncommuting area observable connects the equilibrium and driven calculations. A separate relativistic Standard Model free-gas baseline states precisely what can be imported from [EC-05](<EC-05.md>); it is not identified with this two-state system.

Physical spacetime keeps signature $(-,+,+,+)$. The positive finite Hilbert-space inner product and imaginary-time continuation below do not change that signature. Set $c=1$, retain $\hbar$ and $k_B$, and define $\beta=(k_BT)^{-1}>0$. The gravitational normalization is $\kappa=8\pi G$, with constant real $\gamma\ne0$. Our time variable $\tau$ is an external model clock, not a solution of the gravitational Hamiltonian constraint.

## 1. Project First, Then Specify the Trace

We inherit, rather than repeat, the exterior-Fock and graph construction of [EC-09](<EC-09.md>). One left-handed lepton and one Higgs excitation form a weak singlet and Abelian-neutral composite $F_a$ that remains a spatial-frame doublet. A fixed boundary spin-$1/2$ leg $r$ is attached at vertex $v$; one dynamical link $h:v\to w$ has the full Haar space $L^2(SU(2),dh)$. Exactly one composite occupies $v$ or $w$. The far end of the boundary leg is retained as boundary data. Spatial-frame rotations are distinct from weak rotations.

The two normalized joint Gauss invariants are

$$\begin{align}
\Psi_v(h)=|s\rangle_{r,F_v},\qquad \Psi_w(h)=(I\otimes h)|s\rangle_{r,F_w},\qquad |s\rangle=\frac{|\uparrow\downarrow\rangle-|\downarrow\uparrow\rangle}{\sqrt2}. \tag{1}
\end{align}$$

Position sectors are orthogonal and each function has pointwise norm one, so normalized Haar measure gives $\langle\Psi_x,\Psi_y\rangle=\delta_{xy}$. In this chosen sector they also exhaust the Gauss invariants. If $F$ occupies $v$, the empty vertex $w$ requires link spin zero; the two doublets at $v$ have one singlet. If $F$ occupies $w$, its doublet requires link spin $1/2$ there; the boundary doublet at $v$ again gives one singlet. This uses the $SU(2)$ coupling rule and Peter–Weyl decomposition. Exact generator kernels for link spins $0,1/2,1,3/2,2$ reproduce these two channels; those finite checks are not a proof of the general decomposition theorem.

Let $P_G$ be the Haar Gauss projector within this fixed one-composite boundary sector. It has rank two. Choose a positive transfer energy $J>0$; the covariant pair-transfer operator compresses to

$$
\mathcal H_{\rm inv}=\operatorname{span}\{\Psi_v,\Psi_w\},\qquad
H_0=-J\sigma_x,
\qquad A=\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\0&a\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 a=|\kappa\gamma|\hbar\frac{\sqrt3}{2}>0.
\tag{2}
$$

The embedded surface defining $A$ crosses only the dynamical link once transversely, away from vertices. Link spin is zero in $\Psi_v$ and $1/2$ in $\Psi_w$; the boundary leg is not crossed. This is the stated kinematical puncture-area prescription, not a universal physical spectrum. Within this sector $A=aN_w$, where $N_w$ projects onto composite occupation at $w$. Matter location and geometric link spin are therefore correlated rather than independently thermally populated.

The unbiased partition function can be written

$$\begin{align}
Z_0=\operatorname{Tr}_{\mathcal H_{\rm inv}}e^{-\beta H_0} =\operatorname{Tr}_{\mathcal H_{\rm patch}}(P_Ge^{-\beta H_{\rm tr}}). \tag{3}
\end{align}$$

Here $H_{\rm tr}$ is the bounded gauge-covariant transfer operator of [EC-09](<EC-09.md>). Its unprojected Haar-space thermal trace is not finite: infinitely many nonsinglet link sectors remain with bounded energies. Finite rank after the specified Gauss projection and boundary/matter restriction is essential. Every biased Hamiltonian below is defined on $\mathcal H_{\rm inv}$; no trace-class statement on an unrestricted graph space is implied.

A gravitational scalar constraint equal to zero is not a thermal Hamiltonian simply because its kernel is interesting. Nor is a positive-temperature Gibbs trace justified for an arbitrary unbounded negative clock generator. We have chosen an ordinary, bounded two-level generator for a declared external time.

## 2. The Projected Gibbs State and Its Thermodynamics

Since $H_0^2=J^2I$, its eigenvalues are $-J,+J$ and its exponential sums directly:

$$\begin{align}
Z_0=2\cosh(\beta J),\qquad \rho_0=\frac{e^{-\beta H_0}}{Z_0} =\frac12\big[I+m\sigma_x\big],\qquad m=\tanh(\beta J). \tag{4}
\end{align}$$

Thus this is a density matrix in the normalized joint invariant basis, not the tensor product of two separately projected geometry and matter Gibbs states. Its off-diagonal matrix elements are $m/2$. At high temperature it approaches $I_2/2$ on the correlated sector, not a maximally mixed state on an unrestricted tensor product.

Differentiating $\log Z_0$ gives the mean energy and heat capacity:

$$\begin{align}
U_0=-Jm,\qquad \operatorname{Var}(H_0)=J^2\operatorname{sech}^2(\beta J),\qquad \frac{C_V}{k_B}=(\beta J)^2\operatorname{sech}^2(\beta J). \tag{5}
\end{align}$$

The Helmholtz free energy and von Neumann thermal entropy are

$$\begin{align}
F_0=-\beta^{-1}\log Z_0,\qquad \frac{S_0}{k_B}=\log[2\cosh(\beta J)]-\beta J\tanh(\beta J). \tag{6}
\end{align}$$

The entropy tends to zero as $\beta J\to\infty$ and to $k_B\log2$ as $\beta J\to0$. This finite entropy bound is a channel dimension, not a black-hole area law. At every finite positive temperature $Z_0$ is positive and analytic; this system has no thermodynamic phase transition.

There is no full grand-canonical Standard Model ensemble here. The composite number is fixed to one. Adding $-\mu N$ with $N=I$ only multiplies $Z_0$ by $e^{\beta\mu}$ and leaves $\rho_0$ unchanged. A genuine equilibrium chemical potential requires an identified conserved charge and an ensemble containing its different allowed values. The area does not commute with $H_0$ and is not such a charge.

## 3. Area Response Is Not Its Equal-Time Variance

Introduce the centered area $X=A-aI/2=-a\sigma_z/2$. An external bias $f$, with energy-per-area units, changes the specified Hamiltonian to

$$\begin{align}
H_f=H_0-fX=-J\sigma_x+\frac{fa}{2}\sigma_z,\qquad r=\sqrt{J^2+(fa/2)^2},\qquad Z_f=2\cosh(\beta r). \tag{7}
\end{align}$$

This is a source coupling, not an area chemical potential. From $H_f^2=r^2I$, its Gibbs matrix is $\rho_f=[I-\tanh(\beta r)H_f/r]/2$. Equivalently $\langle X\rangle_f=\beta^{-1}\partial_f\log Z_f$, so

$$\begin{align}
\langle A\rangle_f=\frac a2+\frac{fa^2}{4r}\tanh(\beta r),\qquad \chi_A=\left.\frac{\partial\langle A\rangle_f}{\partial f}\right|_0 =\frac{a^2}{4J}\tanh(\beta J). \tag{8}
\end{align}$$

At zero bias, $\langle A\rangle=a/2$ and $A^2=aA$, hence $\operatorname{Var}(A)=a^2/4$. Nevertheless

$$\begin{align}
\beta\operatorname{Var}(A)-\chi_A =\frac{a^2}{4J}\big[\beta J-\tanh(\beta J)\big]>0. \tag{9}
\end{align}$$

For $x>0$, $x-\tanh x$ is positive because it vanishes at zero and has derivative $\tanh^2x$. The shortcut $\chi_A=\beta\operatorname{Var}(A)$ is therefore wrong here. Differentiating a matrix exponential inserts an imaginary-time-evolved observable when $[H_0,A]\ne0$, rather than replacing that insertion by its value at one time. The general response framework is given in [EC-05](<EC-05.md>); the actual insertion integral for this area will be evaluated next.

The high-temperature expansion is $\chi_A=\beta a^2/4-\beta^3a^2J^2/12+O(\beta^5)$, recovering the classical variance formula at leading order. At zero temperature $\chi_A\to a^2/(4J)$, whereas $\beta\operatorname{Var}(A)$ diverges. The remaining equal-time area fluctuations are quantum fluctuations of a non-energy eigenobservable, not an arbitrarily large static compliance.

## 4. The Same Area Satisfies KMS and Kubo Response

Let $X(\tau)=e^{iH_0\tau/\hbar}Xe^{-iH_0\tau/\hbar}$ and define the unsymmetrized connected correlation $C(\tau)=\operatorname{Tr}[\rho_0X(\tau)X]$. The mean of $X$ is zero. Direct Pauli multiplication gives

$$\begin{align}
C(\tau)=\frac{a^2}{4}\left[ \cos(\omega_0\tau)-im\sin(\omega_0\tau)\right],\qquad \omega_0=\frac{2J}{\hbar}. \tag{10}
\end{align}$$

Writing $p_g=(1+m)/2$, $p_e=(1-m)/2$ exhibits its two transition weights: $C=(a^2/4)(p_ge^{-i\omega_0\tau}+p_ee^{i\omega_0\tau})$. Because $p_e/p_g=e^{-2\beta J}$,

$$\begin{align}
C(\tau-i\beta\hbar)=C(-\tau). \tag{11}
\end{align}$$

This is the KMS relation in our correlation convention. On $0\le\lambda\le\beta$, its imaginary-time insertion is

$$\begin{align}
C_E(\lambda)=\langle X(-i\hbar\lambda)X\rangle =\frac{a^2}{4}\frac{\cosh[J(\beta-2\lambda)]}{\cosh(\beta J)}, \qquad \int_0^\beta C_E(\lambda)\,d\lambda =\frac{a^2}{4J}\tanh(\beta J)=\chi_A. \tag{12}
\end{align}$$

The integral is consequential: its time dependence is precisely what the equal-time variance shortcut omitted. The time-ordered imaginary-time function is extended periodically from this interval; simply evaluating the displayed cosh expression outside the interval is not that periodic extension. $A$ is fermion-parity even, although one constituent of the underlying channel is a lepton. Its bosonic KMS period is $\beta\hbar$; this does not turn real physical time into a compact direction or select a global spacetime thermal torus.

For a weak perturbation $-f(\tau)X$, define $\delta\langle A(\tau)\rangle=\int d\tau'\,\chi_R(\tau-\tau')f(\tau')$. The retarded response is

$$\begin{align}
\chi_R(\tau)=\frac{i}{\hbar}\theta(\tau) \langle[X(\tau),X]\rangle =\theta(\tau)\frac{a^2m}{2\hbar}\sin(\omega_0\tau). \tag{13}
\end{align}$$

With adiabatic convergence, $\lim_{\epsilon\downarrow0}\int_0^\infty e^{-\epsilon\tau}\chi_R(\tau)d\tau=\chi_A$. The damping factor is necessary for this undamped finite-system oscillation. The matching static limit holds for this off-diagonal area observable and these specified limits, not as a universal interchange of isolated and isothermal limits.

For clarity about fluctuation–dissipation normalization, use $\mathcal S_A(\omega)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{i\omega\tau}C(\tau)d\tau$. Then

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal S_A(\omega)&=\frac{\pi a^2}{2}
 [p_g\delta(\omega-\omega_0)+p_e\delta(\omega+\omega_0)],\\
\chi_R''(\omega)&=\frac{\pi a^2m}{4\hbar}
 [\delta(\omega-\omega_0)-\delta(\omega+\omega_0)],\\
\mathcal S_A(\omega)&=\frac{2\hbar}{1-e^{-\beta\hbar\omega}}\chi_R''(\omega).
\end{aligned}
\tag{14}
$$

Both delta-function coefficients satisfy the last equality. In particular the negative-frequency weight is suppressed by $e^{-2\beta J}$. This is a discrete equilibrium spectrum, not a continuum noise spectrum or an assumption about a gravitational bath.

## 5. Quench, Coherent Response and an Explicitly Chosen Bath

Prepare $\rho_0$ and suddenly change the source from zero to a constant $f$. The density matrix is continuous at the quench. With the centered energy convention of (7), mean work done on the system is

$$\begin{align}
W_{\rm on}=\operatorname{Tr}[\rho_0(H_f-H_0)]=-f\langle X\rangle_0=0, \qquad \Delta F=-\beta^{-1}\log\frac{\cosh(\beta r)}{\cosh(\beta J)}\le0. \tag{15}
\end{align}$$

Using $-fA$ instead would add the same scalar $-fa/2$ to $W_{\rm on}$ and $\Delta F$, without changing states or dissipated work. Thus zero mean work here is an explicit centered-source bookkeeping choice, not a free-energy loophole.

If the system is isolated after the quench, unitary evolution preserves its entropy and its new mean energy $U_{\rm in}=-Jm$. There is no heat exchange. Its centered area is

$$\begin{align}
\langle X\rangle_{\rm iso}(\tau) =\frac{fa^2Jm}{4r^2}\left[1-\cos(2r\tau/\hbar)\right]. \tag{16}
\end{align}$$

For small $f$ this becomes $f\chi_A[1-\cos(\omega_0\tau)]$, agreeing with the integrated step response in (13). At finite $f$ its time average generally differs from $\langle X\rangle_f$. Unitary oscillations do not establish thermalization.

To obtain relaxation we now add, rather than infer, a bath model. In the eigenbasis $H_f|g\rangle=-r|g\rangle$, $H_f|e\rangle=r|e\rangle$, choose jumps $L_-=|g\rangle\langle e|$, $L_+=L_-^\dagger$ and positive rates

$$\begin{align}
\Gamma_\downarrow>0,\qquad \Gamma_\uparrow=\Gamma_\downarrow e^{-2\beta r},\qquad \dot\rho=-\frac i\hbar[H_f,\rho] +\Gamma_\downarrow\mathcal D[L_-]\rho +\Gamma_\uparrow\mathcal D[L_+]\rho, \quad \mathcal D[L]\rho=L\rho L^\dagger-\frac12\{L^\dagger L,\rho\}. \tag{17}
\end{align}$$

This is a declared Markovian, secular weak-coupling thermal bath prescription. Its complete positivity uses the standard Lindblad theorem and nonnegative rates; the approximation and rates are not derived from quantum geometry. The jumps act inside the already invariant channel, so they do not violate its Gauss condition. Their rates have inverse external-time units.

Writing $p=\rho_{ee}$ and $c=\rho_{ge}$ gives the evaluated equations

$$\begin{align}
\dot p=\Gamma_\uparrow(1-p)-\Gamma_\downarrow p, \qquad \dot c=\left(\frac{2ir}{\hbar}-\frac{\Gamma_\uparrow+\Gamma_\downarrow}{2}\right)c. \tag{18}
\end{align}$$

The unique stationary state is $\rho_f$, with $p_{\rm eq}=1/(1+e^{2\beta r})$. Detailed balance is the equality $\Gamma_\uparrow(1-p_{\rm eq})=\Gamma_\downarrow p_{\rm eq}$; coherences decay as well as rotate. In particular,

$$\begin{align}
U(\tau)=-r\tanh(\beta r) +[U_{\rm in}+r\tanh(\beta r)] e^{-(\Gamma_\uparrow+\Gamma_\downarrow)\tau}. \tag{19}
\end{align}$$

The Hamiltonian is fixed during this bath stage, so subsequent work is zero. Heat absorbed by the system at complete relaxation is

$$\begin{align}
Q_\infty=U_f-U_{\rm in} =-r\tanh(\beta r)+J\tanh(\beta J)\le0. \tag{20}
\end{align}$$

The bath removes energy because $r\ge J$ and $x\tanh(\beta x)$ increases for positive $x$. System entropy may decrease; system-plus-bath entropy production is instead

$$\begin{align}
\Sigma=\frac{S_f-S_0}{k_B}-\beta Q_\infty =\log\frac{\cosh(\beta r)}{\cosh(\beta J)} =D(\rho_0\Vert\rho_f)=\beta(W_{\rm on}-\Delta F)\ge0, \tag{21}
\end{align}$$

where $D(\rho\Vert\sigma)=\operatorname{Tr}[\rho(\log\rho-\log\sigma)]$. The equality follows by inserting $\log\rho_f=-\beta H_f-\log Z_f$ and the corresponding expression for $\rho_0$; it does not require commuting Gibbs matrices. Positivity also follows directly here from $r\ge J$ and increasing cosh.

For an evaluated example choose energy, area and time units with $J=a=\hbar=1$, and set $\beta=1,f=2$. Then $r=\sqrt2$, $Z_0=3.08616127$, $Z_f=4.35636711$, $\chi_A=0.19039854$ while $\beta\operatorname{Var}(A)=0.25$. The relaxation heat is $Q_\infty=-0.49477275$, and $\Sigma=0.34471047$. The system entropy falls from $0.36533386k_B$ to $0.21527157k_B$, consistently with positive total entropy production. These numbers test one declared channel, not an electroweak or cosmological process.

## 6. A Precisely Scoped Standard Model Thermal Baseline

The preceding state contains one selected lepton–Higgs composite and a boundary geometry representation, not all Standard Model excitations. For comparison take a homogeneous ideal relativistic gas of the empirical minimal Standard Model content in an unbroken-phase reference description, in a large periodic box on flat spacetime. The quoted densities are its thermodynamic-limit values. Assume all included masses are negligible compared with $T$, negligible chemical potentials, thermal occupation of the stated species, no extra sterile states, and no interaction/curvature correction in the leading expression. For this auxiliary free-gas baseline only, use natural units $k_B=\hbar=c=1$.

Two helicities per gauge generator give $2(8+3+1)=24$ bosonic states; the complex Higgs doublet adds four real states, so $g_B=28$. Three generations of 15 independent Weyl components each contribute one particle and one antiparticle helicity, so $g_F=3\cdot15\cdot2=90$. This 90 counts thermal one-particle states, not 90 independent Weyl currents. The actual radial integrals are

$$\begin{align}
\int_0^\infty\frac{x^3\,dx}{e^x-1}=\frac{\pi^4}{15},\qquad \int_0^\infty\frac{x^3\,dx}{e^x+1}=\frac{7\pi^4}{120}. \tag{22}
\end{align}$$

They follow, for example, by expanding the occupation factors into exponentials and integrating $x^3e^{-nx}$, producing $6\zeta(4)$ and $6(1-2^{-3})\zeta(4)$. Including the radial phase-space factor $1/(2\pi^2)$ gives

$$\begin{align}
g_*=28+\frac78\,90=\frac{427}{4}=106.75,\qquad \varepsilon=\frac{\pi^2}{30}g_*T^4,\qquad P=\frac{\varepsilon}{3},\qquad s=\frac{2\pi^2}{45}g_*T^3. \tag{23}
\end{align}$$

These counts use the actual representation dictionary of [EC-04](<EC-04.md>) and the state-counting convention of [EC-05](<EC-05.md>). They do not follow from a graph's valence, area or singlet dimension. Interactions modify the ideal result; QCD confinement, electroweak thermal behavior, chemical equilibration and heavy thresholds require their own calculations. An added sterile species changes the result only if it is actually present and thermally populated. A mean axial current is not automatically conserved, so it does not automatically admit an equilibrium chemical potential.

Neither (22) nor the finite Gibbs calculation derives a universal area-modified Standard Model dispersion relation, a thermal circle for arbitrary quantum spacetime, a phase diagram or a regulator-independent quantum-gravity temperature. The finite response, quench and bath explicitly depend on the chosen generator, projected boundary sector, source, clock and ensemble. Remaining gravitational constraints, physical relational area observables and a full canonical/covariant interacting thermal theory remain outside this construction.
