# Canonical Quantization of Supergravity

DRAFT — independent reconstruction; bounded review recorded.

Canonical supergravity requires odd gauge constraints, not merely fermionic labels on a bosonic state space. Their brackets must close with the other constraints; quantization must then respect ordering, operator domains and a defined physical inner product. We work through a complete auxiliary example: the locally supersymmetric $N=2$ spinning particle on a Riemannian interval. Its graded constraint algebra, differential-form realization, polynomial BRST operator and physical kernel are computed explicitly. Boundary conditions and two finite-cutoff prescriptions expose failures that an abstract CAR calculation alone would miss. This worldline model is not a quantization of four-dimensional supergravity.

## 1. Fix the Action and the Level of the Claim

For physical four-dimensional spacetime retain signature $(-,+,+,+)$ and the book's Clifford relation $\{\gamma^a,\gamma^b\}=-2\eta^{ab}$. The target used below instead has the positive metric $dx^2$, $0\le x\le L$. It is an auxiliary configuration space, not a one-dimensional slice asserted to carry the degrees of freedom of four-dimensional gravity. Its one complex odd pair gives two real worldline supercharges.

The normalization distinction matters. In the reduced real Barbero system of [EC-07](<EC-07.md#L202-L271>),

$$\begin{align}
\{A_a{}^i(X),E^b{}_j(Y)\}=\kappa\gamma_{\rm BI}
\delta_a^b\delta^i_j\delta^{(3)}(X-Y),\qquad \kappa=8\pi G. \tag{1.1}
\end{align}$$

The corresponding formal quantum scale contains $\hbar\kappa\gamma_{\rm BI}$; with $c=1$, $G\hbar$ has area units. The boundary assumptions and fermionic reduction leading to such variables cannot be dropped. Neither $\kappa$ nor the Barbero–Immirzi parameter occurs in our interval model. Its explicit $\hbar$ factors will come from its own symplectic normalization, not from a gravitational flux identification.

The canonical and reduction conventions are those of [AST-11](<AST-11.md>); the left odd derivatives follow [AST-15](<AST-15.md#L24-L50>). The relational clock in [EC-08](<EC-08.md#L79-L131>) and the bounded simplicity/vertex constructions of [EC-17](<EC-17.md>) are separate systems, not missing pieces silently appended to this one.

## 2. A Specified Locally Supersymmetric Worldline Action

Let the gauge parameter $\tau$ be dimensionless. The even target pair is $(x,p)$; $\xi,\bar\xi$ are dimensionless complex odd variables. Define

$$\begin{align}
H=\frac12p^2,\qquad Q=\bar\xi p,\qquad \bar Q=\xi p,\qquad J_{\rm cl}=\bar\xi\xi. \tag{2.1}
\end{align}$$

Choose the first-order action

$$\begin{align}
S=\int d\tau\left[ p\dot x+\frac{i\hbar}{2}(\bar\xi\dot\xi-\dot{\bar\xi}\xi) -eH-i\bar\chi Q-i\chi\bar Q-\hbar A J_{\rm cl} \right]. \tag{2.2}
\end{align}$$

Here e and A are real even multipliers, while $\chi,\bar\chi$ are odd worldline gravitini. They are not four-dimensional Rarita–Schwinger fields. Compactly supported variations in $\tau$ define the bulk problem. Target-endpoint domains will be chosen at the operator level below; no reflected-path integral is assumed. With x in length units, p has action/length units, e has $L^2/\text{action}$ units, $\chi$ has length units and A is dimensionless. Every term in (2.2) has action units.

The multiplier momenta vanish as primary constraints; their preservation gives $H,Q,\bar Q,J_{\rm cl}=0$. The first-order odd kinetic term also produces second-class momentum relations. Eliminate those relations before using the following graded Dirac bracket.

To make the normalization explicit, use

$$\begin{align}
\Theta=p\,dx+\frac{i\hbar}{2}\big(\bar\xi\,d\xi-(d\bar\xi)\xi\big), \qquad \omega=-d\Theta. \tag{2.3}
\end{align}$$

Fix the graded coefficient/differential ordering as written, with symmetric products of odd-coordinate one-forms. In the coordinate blocks $(x,p)$ and $(\xi,\bar\xi)$, the component matrices and Poisson tensor are

$$
W_{xp}=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\-1&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
W_{\rm odd}=-i\hbar\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
\Pi=-W^{-1}.
\tag{2.4}
$$

This is an even graded symplectic structure with odd coordinates, not the odd BV antibracket. Use a right derivative in the first bracket slot and a left derivative in the second. For homogeneous F, the odd contribution is explicitly

$$\begin{align}
\{F,G\}_{D,\rm odd}=-\frac i\hbar(-1)^{|F|+1} \left[(\partial^L_\xi F)(\partial^L_{\bar\xi}G) +(\partial^L_{\bar\xi}F)(\partial^L_\xi G)\right]. \tag{2.5}
\end{align}$$

Add $F_xG_p-F_pG_x$ for the even pair. Thus

$$\begin{align}
\{x,p\}_D=1,\qquad \{\xi,\bar\xi\}_D=\{\bar\xi,\xi\}_D=-i/\hbar. \tag{2.6}
\end{align}$$

The symmetric odd block is consequential. An ungraded antisymmetric replacement would give the wrong bracket symmetry. The actual inverse blocks and $i\hbar$ rescalings have been checked separately.

Direct substitution gives the complete nonzero secondary-constraint brackets:

$$\begin{align}
\{Q,\bar Q\}_D=-\frac{2i}{\hbar}H,\qquad \{J_{\rm cl},Q\}_D=-\frac i\hbar Q,\qquad \{J_{\rm cl},\bar Q\}_D=+\frac i\hbar\bar Q. \tag{2.7}
\end{align}$$

Also $\{Q,Q\}_D=\{\bar Q,\bar Q\}_D=0$, and H commutes with all four constraints. For example differentiating $J_{\rm cl}=\bar\xi\xi$ with a left xi derivative gives $-\bar\xi$; the even-F sign in (2.5) then produces the first J bracket. This checks both the coefficient and the grading. Exact checks include the graded antisymmetry and all constraint-triple Jacobi identities.

For local gauge parameters choose the even generator

$$\begin{align}
G=\rho H+i\bar\varepsilon Q+i\varepsilon\bar Q+\hbar\alpha J_{\rm cl}, \qquad \delta F=\{F,G\}_D, \tag{2.8}
\end{align}$$

where $\rho,\alpha$ are even and $\varepsilon,\bar\varepsilon$ odd. Write $\mathcal H_T=eH+i\bar\chi Q+i\chi\bar Q+\hbar A J_{\rm cl}$. Define the multiplier variations by coefficient comparison in

$$\begin{align}
\delta_{\rm mult}\mathcal H_T=\partial_\tau G+\{G,\mathcal H_T\}_D. \tag{2.9}
\end{align}$$

The partial derivative here differentiates the explicit parameter functions only. This law makes the canonical action variation an endpoint term; compact support removes it. Closure of the constraint span and graded Jacobi give closure of the Hamiltonian gauge distribution. In the U(1) subgroup, $\xi\mapsto e^{-i\alpha}\xi$, $\bar\xi\mapsto e^{i\alpha}\bar\xi$ and $A\mapsto A+\dot\alpha$. This U(1) rotates worldline fermions; it is not an electromagnetic field on the target.

First class does not mean regular. On the positive target, the body of $H=0$ has $p=0$ and $dH=0$. We do not assert a smooth classical quotient or a proper BFV resolution of that irregular constraint variety. What follows is a specified quantum constraint module and its degree-zero operator BRST complex. That distinction precedes, rather than follows, any use of reduction language.

## 3. Quantization and the Form-Degree Choice

On $L^2\Omega^\bullet([0,L])$, exterior multiplication by the orthonormal one-form is $a^\dagger$ and contraction is a. They satisfy $\{a,a^\dagger\}=1$. Quantization of (2.6) gives

$$\begin{align}
[\hat x,\hat p]=i\hbar,\qquad \{\hat\xi,\hat{\bar\xi}\}=i\hbar(-i/\hbar)=1, \qquad \hat\xi=a,\quad\hat{\bar\xi}=a^\dagger. \tag{3.1}
\end{align}$$

The first commutator in (3.1) is the local differential-expression identity for momentum on compactly supported interior tests, not a self-adjoint momentum or global Weyl representation compatible with the interval endpoints. Indeed the absolute Hamiltonian-domain modes $e_0,e_1$ below have a momentum symmetry defect $2i\sqrt2\hbar/L$. The closed operators used here are the Hodge constraints, not an unqualified square of such a momentum.

We choose normal ordering for the primary model: $\hat J=\hat N_f=a^\dagger a$. There is no nonzero classical number silently set equal to $\bar\xi\xi$. Define

$$\begin{align}
\hat Q=-i\hbar d,\qquad \hat{\bar Q}=i\hbar\delta,\qquad \hat H=\frac{\hbar^2}{2}\Delta,\qquad \Delta=d\delta+\delta d. \tag{3.2}
\end{align}$$

Here delta will be the actual Hilbert adjoint of d with the stated domains, not merely a formal integration-by-parts symbol. On their common invariant core,

$$\begin{align}
\hat Q^2=\hat{\bar Q}^{\,2}=0,\quad \{\hat Q,\hat{\bar Q}\}=2\hat H,\quad [\hat J,\hat Q]=\hat Q,\quad [\hat J,\hat{\bar Q}]=-\hat{\bar Q}. \tag{3.3}
\end{align}$$

The factors are precisely $i\hbar$ times (2.7), for this ordered constraint algebra. This is not an exact quantization map for every classical polynomial. The two self-adjoint combinations $S_1=(\hat Q+\hat{\bar Q})/\sqrt2$ and $S_2=i(\hat Q-\hat{\bar Q})/\sqrt2$ obey $S_1^2=S_2^2=\hat H$.

Ordering and degree projection are separate input. Weyl ordering of the classical odd bilinear gives

$$\begin{align}
\frac12(a^\dagger a-aa^\dagger)=N_f-\frac12, \tag{3.4}
\end{align}$$

not the chosen $J=N_f$. Used without a finite correction it has no zero eigenvalue on the two-state fibre. Adding the half-unit correction implements the declared normal-ordered charge; it is not a classical Grassmann-body equation. More generally $J_\sigma=N_f-\sigma$ selects degree $\sigma=0,1$. The degree-one constraint is an explicitly different charge prescription, used below as a diagnostic, not the primary action (2.2) unchanged. The integer-charge operators obey $e^{2\pi iJ_\sigma}=1$; no fermionic path-integral measure or large-gauge determinant has been derived from that operator identity.

There is no clock constraint $p_T+H$ here. Tau is a gauge parameter. Replacing H by that expression while keeping $S_i^2=H$ would not give the displayed first-class algebra without additional clock-sector generators. A clock supermultiplet and its actual closed constraints would have to be constructed separately.

## 4. Domains, an Explicit Physical State and Its Norm

Write a state as $f(x)+g(x)dx$, with positive target inner product

$$\begin{align}
\langle(f,g),(h,k)\rangle=\int_0^L(\bar f h+\bar g k)\,dx. \tag{4.1}
\end{align}$$

This auxiliary Riemannian norm must not be confused with an indefinite Lorentzian spinor pairing. Put $\mathcal D=d+\delta$, acting by $\mathcal D(f,g)=(-g',f')$. Its boundary pairing is

$$\begin{align}
\langle\Psi,\mathcal D\Phi\rangle-\langle\mathcal D\Psi,\Phi\rangle =[\bar g h-\bar f k]_0^L. \tag{4.2}
\end{align}$$

For the absolute complex, choose $D(d)=H^1\oplus L^2$ and $D(\delta)=L^2\oplus H^1_0$. Thus $d^\dagger=\delta$ and $\mathcal D$ has the self-adjoint domain of H-one pairs with $g(0)=g(L)=0$. The adjoint boundary condition is the same, not just a vanishing formal surface term. Its squared operator has

$$\begin{align}
D(\Delta_{\rm abs})=\{(f,g)\in H^2\oplus H^2:
 f'(0)=f'(L)=0,\ g(0)=g(L)=0\}. \tag{4.3}
\end{align}$$

For the relative complex, exchange the endpoint restriction: $D(d)=H^1_0\oplus L^2$, $D(\delta)=L^2\oplus H^1$. Then

$$\begin{align}
D(\Delta_{\rm rel})=\{(f,g)\in H^2\oplus H^2:
 f(0)=f(L)=0,\ g'(0)=g'(L)=0\}. \tag{4.4}
\end{align}$$

Here $H^1_0$ has zero endpoint trace. Finite spans of the eigenmodes below give a dense common invariant core for the H, Q, Qbar and J constraint brackets; closed operators are not claimed to be bounded identities on every L-two vector.

For $\Psi\in D(\hat H)$,

$$\begin{align}
2\langle\Psi,\hat H\Psi\rangle =\|\hat Q\Psi\|^2+\|\hat{\bar Q}\Psi\|^2. \tag{4.5}
\end{align}$$

Hence H-zero states are exactly the joint Q and Qbar kernel on this domain. Solving $f'=g'=0$ and then imposing the boundary conditions gives

$$\begin{align}
\Psi_{\rm abs}=L^{-1/2},\qquad \Psi_{\rm rel}=L^{-1/2}dx, \qquad \|\Psi_{\rm abs}\|=\|\Psi_{\rm rel}\|=1. \tag{4.6}
\end{align}$$

The primary $J=N_f$ retains the absolute scalar, so its physical Hilbert space is one-dimensional with positive norm. It retains no relative harmonic state. The relative one-form instead satisfies the separately chosen $J_1=N_f-1$ constraint. A constant one-form is not in the absolute domain, despite being annihilated by the formal differential expression in the interior.

There is an actual projector in this auxiliary problem. Compact U(1) averaging projects onto the chosen degree,

$$\begin{align}
P_\sigma=\frac1{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}e^{i\alpha(N_f-\sigma)}d\alpha. \tag{4.7}
\end{align}$$

For the absolute primary model, the harmonic heat limit followed by $P_0$ gives the scalar kernel $K_{\rm phys}(x,y)=1/L$, with all one-form components zero. Direct integration gives $\int_0^L K(x,z)K(z,y)dz=K(x,y)$. Its quadratic form is $|\int f dx|^2/L\ge0$. This is a rank-one interval projector, not a construction of the gravitational rigging map or a spin-foam physical projector.

## 5. A Polynomial BRST Operator with the Right Ghost Parities

First solve the primary multiplier constraints by taking wavefunctions independent of e, A and the gravitini. To keep units explicit choose a positive momentum unit $P_*$ and write $h=\hat H/P_*^2$, $q=\hat Q/P_*$, $\bar q=\hat{\bar Q}/P_*$. All three rescaled constraints and J are dimensionless.

Use odd ghosts c,z for the even constraints h,J, and commuting even ghosts u,v for the odd constraints q,qbar. Every ghost has ghost number $+1$. Its derivative has number $-1$ and the same parity as that ghost. The precise polarization is

$$\begin{align}
\mathcal C_{\rm abs}\otimes\mathbb C[u,v]\otimes\Lambda(c,z), \tag{5.1}
\end{align}$$

with left odd derivatives; $\mathcal C_{\rm abs}$ is the matter finite-eigenmode core. Matter odd operators anticommute with c,z and commute with u,v. Neither the commuting ghost polynomials nor intermediate products are truncated. No negative-ghost-number state sectors, nonminimal antighost coordinates or ghost Hilbert measure are included in this particular complex.

The normally ordered odd operator is

$$\begin{align}
\Omega=ch+uq+v\bar q +z\big(J-u\partial_u+v\partial_v\big)-2uv\partial_c^L. \tag{5.2}
\end{align}$$

Each term has ghost number one and odd parity. To prove nilpotence, separate $\Omega_0=ch+uq+v\bar q+zJ$ from the remaining ghost terms. The algebra gives

$$\begin{align}
\Omega_0^2=2uvh+zuq-zv\bar q. \tag{5.3}
\end{align}$$

The anticommutator with the ghost part is its negative. The ghost part squares to zero because $z^2=0$, $\partial_c^2=0$ and $-u\partial_u+v\partial_v$ annihilates uv. Thus $\Omega^2=0$ on the stated polynomial core, with no matter equation imposed.

Dropping $-2uv\partial_c$ leaves $2uvh$; dropping the J ghost-charge correction leaves the corresponding nonzero z-charge terms. These are consequential failures, not optional changes of notation. Exact graded checks used 80 input monomials through commuting ghost degree three, retained every intermediate polynomial and checked parity and ghost number as well as nilpotence. The algebraic expansion above supplies the all-degree proof.

In this polarization ghost number zero means a ghost-independent matter state. Independence of c,u,v,z implies

$$\begin{align}
\Omega\Psi=0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad h\Psi=q\Psi=\bar q\Psi=J\Psi=0. \tag{5.4}
\end{align}$$

There is no ghost-number-minus-one state sector, so no degree-zero exact quotient to take in this selected complex. It follows that its degree-zero cohomology is the joint Dirac kernel. In particular the absolute representative is (4.6). Its norm is the matter Hodge norm already defined, not a norm automatically produced by BRST ghosts. Allowing other ghost polarizations or distributions, introducing nonminimal pairs, constructing a proper BFV resolution and proving gauge-fixing independence are additional tasks. Nilpotence alone has not solved the irregular classical quotient or defined a BRST path-integral projector.

## 6. Heat Traces and a Cutoff That Creates a False Physical State

For absolute boundary conditions take the normalized modes

$$\begin{align}
e_0=L^{-1/2},\quad e_n=\sqrt{2/L}\cos(n\pi x/L),\quad o_n=\sqrt{2/L}\sin(n\pi x/L)dx,\quad n\ge1. \tag{6.1}
\end{align}$$

They obey

$$\begin{align}
\hat Qe_n=i\hbar k_no_n,\quad \hat{\bar Q}o_n=-i\hbar k_ne_n,\quad E_n=\frac{\hbar^2k_n^2}{2},\qquad k_n=n\pi/L. \tag{6.2}
\end{align}$$

All nonzero eigenvalues are even/odd paired. With $\beta>0$ of inverse-H units, the trace-class heat operators have

$$\begin{align}
Z(\beta)=\operatorname{Tr}e^{-\beta H} =1+2\sum_{n\ge1}e^{-\beta E_n},\qquad \mathcal I(\beta)=\operatorname{Tr}((-1)^{N_f}e^{-\beta H})=1. \tag{6.3}
\end{align}$$

Relative boundary conditions give the same ordinary heat trace and graded index $-1$. At $L=\pi$, $\hbar=1$, $\beta=3/5$, the ordinary trace is about $3.23604318759$, not the index one. The 100-mode sum has a tail bounded by $e^{-3000}/30$. These are auxiliary heat/thermal traces before imposing the joint Dirac constraints, including degree projection, not a physical gravitational partition function; beta is not a relational clock or a temperature assigned to a constrained spacetime.

An energy-paired cutoff keeps $e_0,e_1,\ldots,e_K,o_1,\ldots,o_K$. The subspace is invariant under Q and Qbar, retains the exact algebra and has index one. It is not a finite representation of the full canonical position–momentum commutator.

A seemingly balanced alternative keeps $K+1$ even and $K+1$ odd modes by adding $o_{K+1}$ without $e_{K+1}$. Let P be that projection and $Q_P=PQP$. If $H_P=PHP$ is the compressed true Hamiltonian, direct multiplication gives

$$\begin{align}
\{Q_P,Q_P^\dagger\}-2H_P =-2E_{K+1}|o_{K+1}\rangle\langle o_{K+1}|. \tag{6.4}
\end{align}$$

It has rank one; BRST squaring acquires the same defect times $uv/P_*^2$. Keeping the true energy breaks the constraint algebra. Instead defining $H_{\rm susy}=\{Q_P,Q_P^\dagger\}/2$ restores that finite algebra but assigns zero energy to the top odd mode. It then has two zero modes and index zero. Under the explicitly different degree-one constraint $J_1$, this becomes a spurious physical class where the continuum absolute model has none. Its L-two norm is one, but the true Qbar sends it to a nonzero omitted even mode; positive norm alone does not make it physical.

The two balanced graded traces are

$$\begin{align}
\mathcal I_{H_{\rm susy}}=0,\qquad \mathcal I_{H_P}=1-e^{-\beta E_{K+1}}. \tag{6.5}
\end{align}$$

The latter tends to one at fixed positive beta as K grows, but its limits do not commute: taking $K\to\infty$ before $\beta\to0^+$ gives one, while the reversed order gives zero. The finite matrices at $K=1,2,3,4$ exhibit exactly the defect, false degree-one kernel and trace formulas. The displayed all-K result follows directly from the missing pair in (6.2), not from extrapolating those four matrices. A nilpotent finite BRST charge obtained by redefining the Hamiltonian is therefore not enough to establish the correct continuum physical states or index.

## 7. Coordinate Covariance Is an Ordering Test

Use a nonlinear coordinate y with

$$\begin{align}
x=L_0(y+y^3/3),\quad 0\le y\le1,\quad a(y)=L_0(1+y^2)>0,\quad L=4L_0/3. \tag{7.1}
\end{align}$$

The target metric is $a^2dy^2$ and its volume is $a\,dy$. The same Hilbert norm and differential expressions become

$$\begin{align}
\|(f,g\,dy)\|^2=\int_0^1\left(a|f|^2+\frac{|g|^2}{a}\right)dy, \quad \delta(g\,dy)=-\frac1a\partial_y(g/a), \quad \Delta f=-\frac1a\partial_y(a^{-1}\partial_y f). \tag{7.2}
\end{align}$$

The normalized scalar is still $1/\sqrt L$, but the relative harmonic one-form is $a(y)dy/\sqrt L$, not a constant dy coefficient. Both norms remain one and its codifferential is zero.

The actual connection is $\Gamma^y{}_{yy}=a'/a$. It was computed in xAct as the spectator-independent y block of a positive two-dimensional product metric $\operatorname{diag}(a^2,1)$. That computational embedding is distinct from the primary one-dimensional target and from physical Lorentzian spacetime. Its scalar covariant Hessian contraction agrees with (7.2).

Naively replacing H by $-\hbar^2\partial_y^2/(2a^2)$ omits the drift $+\hbar^2a'\partial_y/(2a^3)$. The domain-admissible mode $\cos(\pi x(y)/L)$ satisfies the correct eigenvalue equation and endpoint normal derivatives, but not that naive equation. For the linear coordinate function the correct local expression is $Hy=\hbar^2y/[L_0^2(1+y^2)^3]$, while the naive expression vanishes. That linear function is not asserted to obey the absolute Hamiltonian boundary conditions; it is only a local ordering control, not an extra physical zero mode.

## 8. What a Four-Dimensional Construction Still Has to Supply

The complete action chosen and quantized here is (2.2). No four-dimensional supergravity action or its local constraint algebra has been derived. A four-dimensional assertion must first fix such an action, its boundary terms and its on/off-shell framework. For example old-minimal off-shell N=1 data include a tetrad, gravitino, complex scalar and real-vector auxiliary fields; removing auxiliaries changes which closure statements require field equations. Listing those fields does not specify their transformation coefficients or prove their action invariant.

After a selected four-dimensional action has supplied its fermionic second-class matrix, a canonical derivation must produce the actual Dirac brackets and smeared Lorentz, spatial, Hamiltonian and supersymmetry constraints. Their closure can involve field-dependent structure functions and derivatives of smearing parameters, not the constant coefficients of (2.7). A formal finite CAR identity does not calculate those functions, their operator domains or their possible quantum anomalies.

An indefinite Lorentzian spinor pairing is likewise not a positive physical Hilbert norm. The latter requires a selected constrained-state construction and observables. Our interval Hodge kernel supplies such a norm only for its explicitly solved auxiliary model. No gravitational Hamiltonian projector, EPRL/refinement equivalence, area-spectrum completion or canonical–covariant supergravity identity follows from it. These limits preserve the distinctions already required in [EC-07](<EC-07.md#L549-L555>) and [EC-17](<EC-17.md#L313-L319>).

The worked example establishes a closed graded constraint algebra, a specified ordering and domain, an algebraically nilpotent ghost operator, an explicit normalized physical state and a concrete regulator failure. Those are meaningful canonical quantization results. Extending them to four-dimensional local supersymmetry requires the additional action, gauge and analytic work just identified, not a relabelling of tensor or spinor indices.
