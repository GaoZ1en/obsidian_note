# Free Fields in Twistor and Momentum-Spinor Variables

DRAFT — bounded review recorded.

Twistor variables give a useful construction of massless fields on an appropriate open complex domain. Two independent momentum spinors instead factor a future timelike momentum and furnish a massive little-group frame. These statements concern different data. We connect them explicitly to normalized Weyl, Maxwell and massive Dirac solutions on flat spacetime, rather than treating a list of familiar fields as an exhaustive fundamental spectrum. The curved torsion problem assigned to EC-14 is a separate study; no arbitrary Einstein–Cartan propagation law is inferred below.

## 1. Physical and Spinor Conventions

Work on oriented, time-oriented Minkowski spacetime with coordinates $x^\mu=(t,x,y,z)$, metric $g=\operatorname{diag}(-1,1,1,1)$, orientation $\epsilon_{0123}=+1$, and $c=\hbar=1$. Coordinates have length units; momentum and mass have inverse-length units. Write a future momentum as $p^\mu=(E,\mathbf p)$, $E>0$, so $p_\mu=(-E,\mathbf p)$ and

$$\begin{align}
e^{ip_\mu x^\mu}=e^{-iEt+i\mathbf p\cdot\mathbf x},\qquad p^2=-E^2+|\mathbf p|^2. \tag{1}
\end{align}$$

All polarization spinors in the calculations are commuting complex columns. Fermionic field coefficients and their statistics are extra field-theory data, not consequences of a two-component determinant.

Use the unnormalized Pauli soldering map of [AST-13](<AST-13.md>):

$$\begin{align}
P=X(p)=EI+\mathbf p\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma, \qquad Q=\operatorname{adj}P=EI-\mathbf p\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma, \qquad PQ=(E^2-|\mathbf p|^2)I. \tag{2}
\end{align}$$

Here $\sigma_i\sigma_j=\delta_{ij}I+i\epsilon_{ijk}\sigma_k$. The inverse map gives $E=\operatorname{tr}P/2$ and $p_i=\operatorname{tr}(\sigma_iP)/2$. In particular $\det P=-p^2$; no $1/\sqrt2$ has been inserted into the soldering matrices. Under $A\in SL(2,\mathbb C)$, $P\mapsto APA^\dagger$.

Our alternating matrices and their actual inverses are

$$
\epsilon_{AB}=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\-1&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 \epsilon^{AB}=\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\1&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 \lambda_A=\epsilon_{AB}\lambda^B.
 \tag{3}
$$

Thus $(a,b)^T$ lowers to $(b,-a)^T$. The identity $A^T\epsilon A=\epsilon$ implies

$$\begin{align}
\pi=\epsilon\lambda\quad\Longrightarrow\quad \pi\mapsto A^{-T}\pi\quad\text{when}\quad\lambda\mapsto A\lambda. \tag{4}
\end{align}$$

This is the required map between a fundamental momentum spinor and the **dual unprimed covector** used in [AST-14](<AST-14.md>). It is not complex conjugation.

Retain the physical Dirac dictionary of [AST-07](<AST-07.md>) and [EC-02](<EC-02.md>):

$$
\gamma^0=\begin{pmatrix}0&I\\I&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 \gamma^i=\begin{pmatrix}0&\sigma_i\\-\sigma_i&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 \{\gamma^a,\gamma^b\}=-2g^{ab}I_4,
 \tag{5}
$$

$$\begin{align}
\bar\Psi=\Psi^\dagger\gamma^0,\qquad \gamma_5=i\gamma^0\gamma^1\gamma^2\gamma^3 =\operatorname{diag}(-I,+I),\qquad \rho_D(A)=\operatorname{diag}((A^\dagger)^{-1},A). \tag{6}
\end{align}$$

The lower positive-$\gamma_5$ block transforms by the fundamental A. This fixes the chirality language used here. For a metric spin connection the shared convention remains $\Omega=-\omega_{ab}\gamma^{ab}/4$, with derivative-first affine slots, $T^\rho{}_{\mu\nu}=\Gamma^\rho{}_{\mu\nu}-\Gamma^\rho{}_{\nu\mu}$ and $Q_{\lambda\mu\nu}=-\nabla_\lambda g_{\mu\nu}$. Torsion and nonmetricity vanish in the worked flat frame; unindexed Q below denotes only the momentum adjugate (2).

## 2. Null Momentum, Phase Charts and Helicity

If $p^2=0$ and $p\ne0$ is future directed, P has eigenvalues $2E,0$. Hence

$$\begin{align}
P=\lambda\lambda^\dagger,\qquad \lambda^\dagger\lambda=2E, \qquad \lambda\sim e^{i\alpha}\lambda. \tag{7}
\end{align}$$

Changing the modulus of lambda changes E; only its phase is redundant at fixed momentum. The zero momentum is a separate rank-zero case. For $\mathbf p=E\mathbf n$ with spherical angles $\theta,\phi$, two normalized direction frames are

$$
\xi_N=\begin{pmatrix}\cos(\theta/2)\\ e^{i\phi}\sin(\theta/2)\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 \xi_S=\begin{pmatrix}e^{-i\phi}\cos(\theta/2)\\\sin(\theta/2)\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad \lambda_{N/S}=\sqrt{2E}\,\xi_{N/S}.
 \tag{8}
$$

The sphere is an auxiliary space of momentum directions, not a two-dimensional spacetime. N excludes the south pole and S excludes the north pole. They obey $\xi_N=e^{i\phi}\xi_S$, $\xi^\dagger\xi=1$ and $(\mathbf n\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma)\xi=\xi$. This exhibits the momentum sphere without pretending there is a global smooth phase choice.

Indeed the anti-Hermitian frame connections $a_{N/S}=\xi_{N/S}^\dagger d\xi_{N/S}$ give

$$\begin{align}
a_N=\frac i2(1-\cos\theta)d\phi,\qquad a_S=-\frac i2(1+\cos\theta)d\phi,\qquad \frac i{2\pi}\int_{S^2}da=-1. \tag{9}
\end{align}$$

The orientation is $\sin\theta\,d\theta\wedge d\phi$. The nonzero first Chern number of this eigenline, in the [unitary Chern convention](<AST-08.md>), obstructs a global normalized eigenvector. Here the displayed local columns are frames, with frame transition $e^{i\phi}$; a section's coefficients transform by its inverse. This distinction avoids a sign error when comparing phase bundles.

Define helicity h by the active phase $e^{-ih\vartheta}$ for a rotation through $\vartheta$ about the momentum. A rotation $A=\exp[-i\vartheta\mathbf n\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma/2]$ acts on lambda by $e^{-i\vartheta/2}$. Thus a finite-helicity mode with $2h\in\mathbb Z$ and spinor-frame weight $e^{2ih\alpha}$ has that helicity. Its mode-basis line has Chern number $-2h$, with coefficient functions transforming inversely. The full null little group also contains null rotations. Ordinary finite-helicity particle representations assume its translation part acts trivially; gauge potentials can realize that part by gauge changes. This assumption is not a classification of every possible massless Poincare representation.

## 3. An Actual Open-Twistor Field and Its Normalization

### 3.1 The Forward Contour Construction

Keep the dual incidence dictionary

$$
Z=\begin{pmatrix}\omega^{A'}\\\pi_A\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 \omega=iY\pi,\qquad
 Y=X(x)^T/L_0,\qquad L_0>0,
 \tag{10}
$$

where $X(x)=tI+x^i\sigma_i$ and the reference length makes Y dimensionless. The transformation is $\omega\mapsto\overline A\omega$, $\pi\mapsto A^{-T}\pi$. In particular pi is not the opposite Dirac chirality. Momentum-normalized lambda has units length$^{-1/2}$, and (4) has the same units; projective pi keeps only its direction. The chosen dimensionless incidence makes omega and pi carry the same units if a representative scale is supplied.

Use $\mathbb{PT}^{\circ}=\{[\omega,\pi]\in\mathbb{CP}^3:\pi\ne0\}$, with patches $V_0=\{\pi_0\ne0\}$ and $V_1=\{\pi_1\ne0\}$. For $n=0,1,2$, take the overlap representative

$$\begin{align}
f_n(Z)=\frac{\exp(-\nu\omega_0/\pi_0)}{\pi_0^{n+1}\pi_1},\qquad \nu>0. \tag{11}
\end{align}$$

It is holomorphic on the overlap and homogeneous of degree $-n-2$. Nonextension to either omitted divisor is permitted for an overlap cocycle. The flat forward Penrose formula is

$$\begin{align}
\phi_{A_1\cdots A_n}(x) =\frac1{2\pi i}\oint_{\Gamma_x}\pi_{A_1}\cdots\pi_{A_n} f_n(iY\pi,\pi)\,D\pi, \qquad D\pi=\pi_0d\pi_1-\pi_1d\pi_0. \tag{12}
\end{align}$$

The weights are $n+(-n-2)+2=0$. A patch coboundary integrates to zero when its pieces extend to the two sides of the contour. Holomorphicity, contour transport without singularity crossings, and that boundary extension condition are part of the construction, not optional shorthand.

Set $\pi=(1,\zeta)^T$ and choose a positively oriented finite circle around zero. Incidence gives

$$\begin{align}
f_n|_{L_Y}= \frac{\exp[-i\nu(t+z+(x+iy)\zeta)/L_0]}{\zeta}, \qquad D\pi=d\zeta.
\end{align}$$

There is no moving pole. Evaluating the residue gives

$$
\phi=e^{-i\kappa(t+z)},\qquad
 \phi_A=\begin{pmatrix}e^{-i\kappa(t+z)}\\0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 \phi_{AB}=e^{-i\kappa(t+z)}\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&0\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad \kappa=\nu/L_0.
 \tag{13}
$$

These are nonzero overlap classes, since a coboundary would have zero contour. They are entire flat-spacetime profiles. The standard compact-projective cohomology fact $H^1(\mathbb{CP}^3,\mathcal O(k))=0$ is why this construction must retain its open domain; the wave classes are not put into that compact group.

With the actual inverse-soldering normalization,

$$\begin{align}
\partial^{AA'}=\frac12(I\partial_t-\sigma_i\partial_i)^{AA'},
\end{align}$$

(13) satisfies $\Box\phi=0$ and $\partial^{AA'}\phi_{AA_2\cdots A_n}=0$ for $n=1,2$. More generally differentiating incidence in (12) produces a pi factor; its raised contraction with the field's pi factor vanishes by antisymmetry. This is a flat forward wave construction, not a global inverse-transform or Hilbert-space-completeness theorem.

### 3.2 The Physical Helicities and Unit Modes

The phase in (13) has $p=(\kappa,0,0,-\kappa)$. Its normalized fundamental momentum spinor is $\lambda=\sqrt{2\kappa}(0,1)^T$, with $\epsilon\lambda=\sqrt{2\kappa}(1,0)^T$. The projective pi pole in the contour and the physical momentum therefore agree through the explicit epsilon map. Raising the field index gives

$$
\eta=\epsilon^{-1}\phi_A
 =e^{-i\kappa(t+z)}\begin{pmatrix}0\\1\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 (E-\mathbf p\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma)\eta=0.
 \tag{14}
$$

Placed in the lower Dirac block, this is positive chirality. Rotation about $-\widehat z$ acts by $e^{-i\vartheta/2}$, giving helicity $+1/2$. The Maxwell profile in (13), reconstructed with the fixed [AST-13](<AST-13.md>) normalization, is

$$\begin{align}
F=e^{-i\kappa(t+z)}(dt+dz)\wedge(dx-i\,dy),\qquad *F=+iF. \tag{15}
\end{align}$$

Its spatial circular polarization is $\boldsymbol\epsilon_+=(1,-i,0)/\sqrt2$. Rotation about the same momentum gives $e^{-i\vartheta}$, so the helicity is $+1$. Direct calculation gives $dF=0$ and $\partial_\mu F^{\mu\nu}=0$. Its complex conjugate supplies the opposite Hodge/frequency partner when a real electromagnetic field is wanted. Reality does not mean that the holomorphic single-helicity representative itself is real.

The contour profiles have not yet been normalized as physical modes. To evaluate that step, use a periodic spatial box of side $\ell$, volume $V=\ell^3$, with $\kappa\ell=2\pi N$, $N$ a positive integer. This is an explicit infrared convention, not a claim that the regulator retains all Minkowski symmetries. Use the periodic spin structure and omit the Maxwell harmonic zero-momentum sector. Then

$$
u_0=\frac{e^{-i\kappa(t+z)}}{\sqrt{2\kappa V}},\qquad
 \eta_+=\frac{e^{-i\kappa(t+z)}}{\sqrt V}\begin{pmatrix}0\\1\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 A_\mu^{(+)}=\epsilon_\mu^{(+)}u_0,
 \quad \epsilon_\mu^{(+)}=\frac{(0,1,-i,0)}{\sqrt2}.
 \tag{16}
$$

The scalar Klein–Gordon, Weyl current, and radiation-gauge Maxwell norms are respectively

$$\begin{align}
i\int_V(u_0^*\dot u_0-\dot u_0^*u_0)d^3x=1,\qquad \int_V\eta_+^\dagger\eta_+d^3x=1,\qquad i\int_V\overline A_i^{(+)}\overleftrightarrow{\partial_t}A_i^{(+)}d^3x=1. \tag{17}
\end{align}$$

The first and third integrands are $1/V$, because the time derivative contributes $2\kappa$ and $\epsilon_+^*\cdot\epsilon_+=1$. The middle one is also $1/V$. Periodicity removes side flux. In particular the Weyl mode does not need an extra scalar factor $1/\sqrt{2\kappa}$ after its two-component polarization is already unit normalized.

The opposite circular polarization is $\epsilon_-^i=\overline{\epsilon_+^i}$, and

$$\begin{align}
\sum_{h=\pm1}\epsilon_h^i\overline{\epsilon_h^j} =\delta^{ij}-\widehat p^i\widehat p^j. \tag{18}
\end{align}$$

The conjugation is essential: omitting it gives the wrong sign in the second transverse direction. The normalized potential's field strength is $-i\kappa u_0(dt+dz)\wedge(dx-i\,dy)/\sqrt2$. Thus physical scalar, Weyl and Maxwell-potential amplitudes have units length$^{-1}$, length$^{-3/2}$ and length$^{-1}$, while Maxwell components have length$^{-2}$. The free action and conserved inner product, not twistor homogeneity, set these normalization conventions; see [EC-03](<EC-03.md>).

## 4. Timelike Momentum from Two Spinors

For $m>0$, the future mass shell is

$$\begin{align}
E=\sqrt{|\mathbf p|^2+m^2},\qquad P>0,\qquad \det P=m^2.
\end{align}$$

Every such positive Hermitian matrix has a positive square root. An explicit one is

$$\begin{align}
\Lambda_0=\sqrt P =\frac{(E+m)I+\mathbf p\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma}{\sqrt{2(E+m)}}, \qquad \Lambda_0\Lambda_0^\dagger=P,\qquad \det\Lambda_0=m. \tag{19}
\end{align}$$

Squaring the numerator uses $(\mathbf p\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma)^2=|\mathbf p|^2I$ and $E^2-|\mathbf p|^2=m^2$. Its eigenvalues are positive since $E+m>|\mathbf p|$. This proves it is the positive root, not merely some matrix whose square is P.

Writing any determinant-fixed factor as $\Lambda=(\lambda_1,\lambda_2)$ gives

$$\begin{align}
P=\lambda_1\lambda_1^\dagger+\lambda_2\lambda_2^\dagger, \qquad \det P=|\lambda_1^T\epsilon\lambda_2|^2=m^2. \tag{20}
\end{align}$$

Each column yields a future null momentum $k_I^0=\lambda_I^\dagger\lambda_I/2$ and $k_I^i=\lambda_I^\dagger\sigma_i\lambda_I/2$, with $p=k_1+k_2$. The columns must be independent for $m>0$. This is a useful decomposition of one momentum; it does not assert that a massive particle consists of two massless particles. The chosen normalization makes $p^2=-m^2$ with no extra factor two. The condition $\det\Lambda=m$ fixes a phase; it does not dynamically predict m.

### The Massive Little Group Is Not an Internal Gauge Group

All determinant-fixed factors of the same P are $\Lambda=\Lambda_0 U$ with $U\in SU(2)$. Indeed $U=\Lambda_0^{-1}\Lambda$ obeys $UU^\dagger=I$ and $\det U=1$. At rest $P=mI$, this is exactly the stabilizer inside $SL(2,\mathbb C)$. For general momentum put $S(p)=\Lambda_0/\sqrt m$. If $P'=APA^\dagger$, then

$$\begin{align}
W(A,p)=S(p')^{-1}A S(p)\in SU(2). \tag{21}
\end{align}$$

Unitarity follows by inserting $P'=APA^\dagger$, and its determinant is one. This is the momentum-dependent Wigner rotation relating canonical spin frames.

A right change of the two column labels preserves the same momentum. To describe a particle one additionally chooses how its state or polarization transforms under this stabilizer. Choosing spin $j$ gives the usual $(2j+1)$-dimensional finite-dimensional little-group representation; that representation theorem is an input [1]. It does not select j. For the Dirac example we choose $j=1/2$, with $S_i=\sigma_i/2$, $[S_i,S_j]=i\epsilon_{ijk}S_k$ and $\sum S_i^2=3I/4$. The components do not all commute; a chosen spin projection can be diagonalized with momentum and mass, not all three spin projections simultaneously.

This SU(2) is a spacetime-momentum stabilizer and spin-frame freedom. It is not an independently derived Yang–Mills group or a spatial gauge connection. No local internal symmetry, matter charge assignment or interaction follows just from having two spinor columns. Massive spin and its frame-dependent helicity projections should also not be confused with the invariant massless helicity label.

## 5. A Massive Dirac Field, Not Just a Mass-Shell Count

### 5.1 Define the Actual Plane-Wave Matrix

On the phase (1), $i\partial_\mu$ acts as $-p_\mu$. Thus define

$$
M(p):=E\gamma^0-p_i\gamma^i=-c_g(p)
 =\begin{pmatrix}0&Q\\P&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 M(p)^2=m^2I_4.
 \tag{22}
$$

Here $c_g(p)=p_\mu\gamma^\mu$ uses the negative-time spacetime metric. Calling this Clifford contraction the plane-wave matrix without its minus sign would change the equation. The free equation is $(i\gamma^\mu\partial_\mu-m)\Psi=0$, so positive-frequency amplitudes obey $(M-m)u=0$. Each component also obeys $(\Box-m^2)\Psi=0$ in the physical signature.

Let $e_I$ be the standard orthonormal basis of $\mathbb C^2$. The momentum factor gives explicit solutions

$$
u_I(p)=\begin{pmatrix}m(\Lambda^\dagger)^{-1}e_I\\\Lambda e_I\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 v_I(p)=\begin{pmatrix}m(\Lambda^\dagger)^{-1}e_I\\-\Lambda e_I\end{pmatrix}.
 \tag{23}
$$

For example $P\,m(\Lambda^\dagger)^{-1}=m\Lambda$ and $Q\Lambda=m^2(\Lambda^\dagger)^{-1}$, which prove $(M-m)u_I=0$ and $(M+m)v_I=0$. The latter amplitude multiplies $e^{+iEt-i\mathbf p\cdot\mathbf x}$, where the first-order differential symbol is $-M$, so it solves the same massive field equation.

For $\Lambda_0=\sqrt P$, the upper block is $\sqrt Q\,e_I$ and the lower block is $\sqrt P\,e_I$. Since $\sqrt P\sqrt Q=mI$ and $P+Q=2EI$, the evaluated normalizations and spin sums are

$$\begin{align}
\bar u_Iu_J=2m\delta_{IJ},\qquad \bar v_Iv_J=-2m\delta_{IJ},\qquad \bar u_Iv_J=0, \tag{24}
\end{align}$$

$$\begin{align}
u_I^\dagger u_J=v_I^\dagger v_J=2E\delta_{IJ},\qquad \sum_Iu_I\bar u_I=M+mI_4,\qquad \sum_Iv_I\bar v_I=M-mI_4. \tag{25}
\end{align}$$

A different determinant-fixed factor $\Lambda_0U$ rotates the two columns of each solution by U, leaving the sums unchanged. This makes the little-group frame dependence explicit.

The Hermitian Hamiltonian is $H(\mathbf p)=\alpha^i p_i+\beta m$, with $\alpha^i=\gamma^0\gamma^i$ and $\beta=\gamma^0$. It obeys $H^2=E^2I$ and

$$\begin{align}
H(\mathbf p)u_I(p)=Eu_I(p),\qquad H(-\mathbf p)v_I(p)=-Ev_I(p).
\end{align}$$

At fixed spatial Fourier wavevector $\mathbf p$, its orthogonal energy projectors are therefore

$$\begin{align}
\Pi_+(\mathbf p)=\frac1{2E}\sum_Iu_I(p)u_I(p)^\dagger =\frac{EI+H(\mathbf p)}{2E},
\end{align}$$

$$\begin{align}
\Pi_-(\mathbf p)=\frac1{2E}\sum_Iv_I(E,-\mathbf p)v_I(E,-\mathbf p)^\dagger =\frac{EI-H(\mathbf p)}{2E}. \tag{26}
\end{align}$$

The reversed momentum in the second line is necessary. These Hermitian projectors are distinct from the covariant symbol projectors built from M; M is not generally Hermitian for the positive Cauchy inner product.

In a periodic box, the modes $u_Ie^{-iEt+i\mathbf p\cdot\mathbf x}/\sqrt{2EV}$ and $v_Ie^{+iEt-i\mathbf p\cdot\mathbf x}/\sqrt{2EV}$ each have unit conserved positive Dirac norm $\int\Psi^\dagger\Psi\,d^3x$. Covariant bar normalization $2m$ is not that positive norm. Fourier completeness and (26) give the complete four-component Cauchy data space. The spatial Hamiltonian is self-adjoint on periodic $H^1$ spinors; on $\mathbb R^3$ its Fourier realization has domain $H^1(\mathbb R^3,\mathbb C^4)$. The identity $H(\mathbf p)^\dagger H(\mathbf p)=(|\mathbf p|^2+m^2)I$ makes its graph norm equivalent to the $H^1$ norm. These statements use ordinary Fourier completeness, not a completeness theorem inferred from incidence.

### 5.2 Fully Evaluated Timelike Example

In a fixed inverse-length unit take $m=3$, $\mathbf p=(0,0,4)$ and $E=5$. Then

$$\begin{align}
P=\operatorname{diag}(9,1),\qquad Q=\operatorname{diag}(1,9),\qquad \Lambda_0=\operatorname{diag}(3,1),
\end{align}$$

$$
u_1=\begin{pmatrix}1\\0\\3\\0\end{pmatrix},\quad
 u_2=\begin{pmatrix}0\\3\\0\\1\end{pmatrix},\quad
 v_1=\begin{pmatrix}1\\0\\-3\\0\end{pmatrix},\quad
 v_2=\begin{pmatrix}0\\3\\0\\-1\end{pmatrix}.
 \tag{27}
$$

The two null constituent momenta have $(E,k_z)=(9/2,9/2)$ and $(1/2,-1/2)$; their sum has $p^2=-9$. The u columns have bar norm 6 and positive norm 10, while the v columns have bar norm minus 6 and positive norm 10. The actual positive spin sum is

$$
\sum_Iu_I\bar u_I=
 \begin{pmatrix}3&0&1&0\\0&3&0&9\\9&0&3&0\\0&1&0&3\end{pmatrix}=M+3I_4.
 \tag{28}
$$

For an allowed box momentum, for instance $\ell=\pi/2$ in the corresponding length unit, the field $u_1e^{-i5t+i4z}/\sqrt{10V}$ has unit integrated probability density and solves the first-order Dirac equation. Using the scalar normalization with an already unit-normalized spinor, or substituting $c_g(p)$ for M, would fail these explicit tests.

A neutral Majorana condition is a further choice, not another mass-shell factorization. With $J=i\gamma^2$ followed by conjugation, $J^2=1$ and J maps a positive-frequency solution to its negative-frequency partner. In the present basis $i\gamma^2\overline{u(p,\chi)}=v(p,\epsilon\overline\chi)$. A real solution can be formed as $U^{(+)}+JU^{(+)}$; imposing J-reality on one fixed positive-frequency amplitude alone would miss this frequency exchange. Statistics and any internal-charge compatibility still require their own field-theory input.

## 6. What a Pair of Incident Twistors Does and Does Not Specify

Use the massive momentum factor to form $\Pi=\epsilon\Lambda$ and choose a common real spacetime point Y. Define

$$
\Omega=iY\Pi,\qquad
 \mathsf Z=\begin{pmatrix}\Omega\\\Pi\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 \mathcal H=\begin{pmatrix}0&I\\I&0\end{pmatrix}.
 \tag{29}
$$

These are chosen scale-retaining representatives; separate projectivization would discard the momentum normalization in (20). Since $Y^\dagger=Y$, $\mathsf Z^\dagger\mathcal H\mathsf Z=0$: each column is a null twistor and the two columns are mutually null. Also $\det\Pi=\det\Lambda=m$, so

$$\begin{align}
Y=-i\Omega\Pi^{-1}. \tag{30}
\end{align}$$

Conversely, for invertible Pi the mutual-null Gram equation makes this recovered Y Hermitian. Thus the actual common-real-point condition is stronger than assigning two unrelated twistor labels. At zero determinant this reconstruction cannot be inverted; the rank-one geometry must be handled separately.

This is an incidence and momentum construction, not a complete canonical spinning-particle model. An action or symplectic form, constraints, spin variables or little-group equivariance, a reduction by redundant variables, and a polarization for quantization remain to be specified. Mixed derivatives of the incidence relation commuting do not establish canonical commutators between its entries. The phase convention of the massless momentum eigenline and the SU(2) freedom of a timelike factor are not themselves additional internal gauge theories.

A projective null twistor can encode a null ray with the real contact/CR structure described in [AST-14](<AST-14.md>). A spacetime twistor-spinor equation is a different background geometric equation. A Penrose overlap class constructs a massless spacetime field, while Lambda in (19) is a momentum-spin frame. Neither the common word spinor nor a shared matrix size identifies these objects with one another or supplies the missing particle dynamics.

## 7. Scope Beyond the Flat Examples

A fixed nonzero m breaks the massless conformal scaling: under $x\mapsto ax$, the wave operator scales as $a^{-2}$ but a fixed $m^2$ does not. The massive construction here is a positive-energy Fourier/Dirac construction using two spinors. It is not the unchanged massless Penrose transform with a mass parameter inserted into its cocycle, nor a proof of a general massive twistor-cohomology correspondence.

The separate EC-14 assignment concerns curved geometry and torsion; these flat results do not replace that problem by formal index substitution. Curved commutators contain curvature and, for tensor Hessians, torsion terms; the flat commuting distribution and contour proof need separate integrability hypotheses. Higher-spin field strengths or gauge potentials on a curved background require their own gauge, constraint and consistency analysis. No arbitrary Einstein–Cartan extension of the flat formulas is asserted.

Likewise a prescribed torsion potential can keep an equation linear, but eliminating an Einstein–Cartan connection in terms of spinor matter produces an interaction, not automatically the free problem above. [EC-02](<EC-02.md>) and [EC-03](<EC-03.md>) distinguish those choices. Global hyperbolicity, boundary flux conditions, a state and any positive-frequency splitting must be reconsidered on a different spacetime. Twistor incidence does not select them.

For the flat free theory a chosen action and the normalized mode families can support the usual bosonic CCR or fermionic CAR and a chosen Fock vacuum, as developed in [EC-03](<EC-03.md>). Those canonical structures, statistics, state and distributional completions are additional inputs. A contour residue is not by itself a functional-integral measure, a renormalized determinant, or a proof of equivalence of quantum constructions. The concrete results here are actual massless overlap representatives, their helicities and normalized modes, a future massive momentum factor, and explicit massive Dirac solutions and spin sums. They neither select a unique list of fundamental fields nor determine Standard Model or quantum-gravity data.
