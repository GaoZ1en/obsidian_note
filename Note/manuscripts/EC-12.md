# Microscopic Matter, Scalar Averaging, and an Inhomogeneous Dust Cosmos

DRAFT — bounded mathematical review recorded.

A macroscopic equation needs more than a list of microscopic fields. It needs a state, a coarse-graining map and enough correlation data to evaluate its nonlinear source terms. This chapter makes that requirement concrete. We derive scalar averaging equations and solve them on an exact inhomogeneous dust spacetime. Its expansion variance is positive, but the kinematical backreaction vanishes because shear cancels it. The averaged domain expands and decelerates; no bounce follows from variance alone.

The example is a controlled spinless Levi–Civita sector of metric-compatible Einstein–Cartan theory. It is not a derivation of a quantum continuum limit or of a complete cosmology from Standard Model fields.

## 1. What Information Does a Coarse-Graining Map Retain?

In a finite quantum system a state is a positive semidefinite matrix with unit trace. For a Hermitian observable $O$, its mean and variance are

$$\begin{align}
\varpi\ge0,\qquad \operatorname{Tr}\varpi=1,\qquad \langle O\rangle=\operatorname{Tr}(\varpi O),\qquad \operatorname{Var}(O)=\langle O^2\rangle-\langle O\rangle^2. \tag{1}
\end{align}$$

Knowing the mean does not determine the second moment. For a real positive $s$, take the following explicit three-level data:

$$\begin{align}
O=\operatorname{diag}(-s,0,s),\qquad \varpi_A=\operatorname{diag}(\tfrac12,0,\tfrac12),\qquad \varpi_B=\operatorname{diag}(0,1,0). \tag{2}
\end{align}$$

Both states are normalized and have zero mean, but

$$\begin{align}
\langle O\rangle_A=\langle O\rangle_B=0,\qquad \langle O^2\rangle_A=s^2,\qquad \langle O^2\rangle_B=0. \tag{3}
\end{align}$$

Thus the map retaining only the first moment is not sufficient for a quadratic interaction. This is an information-loss example, not a microscopic model of fermionic spin. In quantum field theory the state is more generally a positive functional on an observable algebra; local products also require a specified renormalization prescription. A global trace-class density matrix need not exist.

For example, eliminating an algebraic torsion field can produce a quadratic axial-current interaction. Evaluating its macroscopic effect then needs a renormalized current correlation, not merely the mean current. Schematically, in a specified frame/transport prescription,

$$\begin{align}
\langle J_5^aJ_5^b\rangle =\langle J_5^a\rangle\langle J_5^b\rangle+C^{ab}. \tag{4}
\end{align}$$

A zero mean does not set the connected tensor $C^{ab}$ to zero. Nor is a Lorentzian contraction of $C^{ab}$ a positive scalar variance. Its contribution to stress must be obtained by varying the chosen action and evaluating the resulting operator in the chosen state. An interaction coefficient is not by itself an equation of state or a cosmological energy density.

A spatial average is a different map from a quantum expectation. It requires a foliation, a volume measure, a domain and a rule for transporting that domain. We now specify all four rather than identify these two kinds of averaging by notation.

## 2. The Local Dust Sector and Its Assumptions

Use four-dimensional signature $(-,+,+,+)$, set $c=1$ and retain Newton's constant $G$. In the usual metric-compatible Einstein–Cartan model, a pointwise vanishing Cartan spin source gives zero torsion. We choose that sector, with a matter action that does not source the independent Lorentz connection. This is stronger than assuming that an averaged spin vanishes. The connection below is Levi–Civita, and the cosmological constant is zero.

The selected matter model is pressureless dust:

$$\begin{align}
G_{\mu\nu}=\kappa\rho u_\mu u_\nu,\qquad \kappa=8\pi G>0,\qquad \rho>0,\qquad u^\mu u_\mu=-1. \tag{5}
\end{align}$$

The density is rest-frame energy density. Dust is already a matter-model assumption; no Standard Model scattering calculation or spin-fluid closure is being hidden in it. Assume geodesic, irrotational flow, so proper-time slices orthogonal to $u$ exist on the patch considered.

Define the spatial projector, expansion tensor and shear by

$$
\begin{aligned}
h_{\mu\nu}&=g_{\mu\nu}+u_\mu u_\nu,\\
B_{\mu\nu}&=h_\mu{}^\alpha h_\nu{}^\beta\nabla_\alpha u_\beta
 =\tfrac13\theta h_{\mu\nu}+\sigma_{\mu\nu},\\
\theta&=\nabla_\mu u^\mu,\qquad
\sigma^2=\tfrac12\sigma_{\mu\nu}\sigma^{\mu\nu}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{6}
$$

The tensor $B_{\mu\nu}$ is symmetric because vorticity vanishes. Its trace is the local volume-expansion rate. The shear is spatial and trace-free, so its squared norm is nonnegative. A dot denotes differentiation along the dust flow.

The local mass conservation, Raychaudhuri equation and scalar Einstein constraint are

$$
\begin{aligned}
\dot\rho+\theta\rho&=0,\\
\dot\theta&=-\tfrac13\theta^2-2\sigma^2-\tfrac\kappa2\rho,\\
{}^{(3)}R+\tfrac23\theta^2-2\sigma^2&=2\kappa\rho.
\end{aligned}
\tag{7}
$$

Here the three-curvature belongs to the flow-orthogonal slice. These equations assume zero pressure, acceleration, vorticity and cosmological constant. Spinful or nonminimally coupled matter requires its own local equations before any averaging step.

## 3. Proper-Volume Averages Do Not Commute with Evolution

Let $D$ be a compact comoving domain: its boundary is transported by the dust. In synchronous comoving coordinates the time is dust proper time, the lapse is one and the shift is zero. Here $h=\det(h_{ij})$ and $\dot h_{ij}=2B_{ij}$, so the determinant identity $\partial_t\sqrt h=\frac12\sqrt h\,h^{ij}\dot h_{ij}$ gives the volume-expansion relation below. With no matter flux through this moving physical boundary, define

$$\begin{align}
V_D(t)=\int_{D(t)}dV,\qquad \langle S\rangle_D=\frac1{V_D}\int_{D(t)}S\,dV, \qquad \partial_t dV=\theta\,dV. \tag{8}
\end{align}$$

The domain is fixed in comoving labels, not in physical radius. For a fixed reference time with $V_D(t_{\rm ref})>0$, define a dimensionless volume scale factor and its expansion rate:

$$\begin{align}
a_D(t)=\left[\frac{V_D(t)}{V_D(t_{\rm ref})}\right]^{1/3}, \qquad H_D=\frac{\dot a_D}{a_D} =\frac{\dot V_D}{3V_D}=\frac13\langle\theta\rangle_D. \tag{9}
\end{align}$$

Differentiate the normalized integral using the product and quotient rules. The change in its measure gives the exact commutator

$$\begin{align}
\partial_t\langle S\rangle_D-\langle\dot S\rangle_D =\langle S\theta\rangle_D- \langle S\rangle_D\langle\theta\rangle_D. \tag{10}
\end{align}$$

In particular, for the expansion scalar the right side is its variance. Setting the measure equal to a coordinate volume, or forgetting the derivative of its normalization, would miss this term. A noncomoving domain adds flux terms; another lapse or foliation changes the averaging problem.

Introduce the averaged spatial curvature and kinematical backreaction

$$\begin{align}
\mathcal R_D=\langle{}^{(3)}R\rangle_D,\qquad Q_D=\frac23\bigl(\langle\theta^2\rangle_D -\langle\theta\rangle_D^2\bigr) -2\langle\sigma^2\rangle_D. \tag{11}
\end{align}$$

Averaging the local equations, and using the commutator rather than interchanging the operations, yields the dust Buchert equations:

$$
\begin{aligned}
3H_D^2&=\kappa\langle\rho\rangle_D
              -\tfrac12\mathcal R_D-\tfrac12Q_D,\\
3\frac{\ddot a_D}{a_D}&=-\tfrac\kappa2\langle\rho\rangle_D+Q_D,\\
\partial_t\langle\rho\rangle_D+3H_D\langle\rho\rangle_D&=0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{12}
$$

For example, differentiating the mean expansion gives its mean Raychaudhuri term plus the expansion variance. The combination entering the acceleration is consequently the variance minus the shear term in $Q_D$, not the variance alone. Differentiating the first averaged equation and using the other two gives

$$\begin{align}
\dot Q_D+6H_DQ_D+\dot{\mathcal R}_D+2H_D\mathcal R_D=0. \tag{13}
\end{align}$$

These relations are not a closed equation for the mean density alone. They still require information about inhomogeneous geometry or a justified closure. We will supply that information by solving an explicit spacetime, not by guessing $Q_D$.

## 4. An Expanding, Genuinely Inhomogeneous Dust Spacetime

On a regular patch with $1+2E>0$ and $R'>0$ away from the centre, a spherically symmetric comoving Lemaître–Tolman–Bondi metric is

$$\begin{align}
ds^2=-dt^2+\frac{R'(t,r)^2}{1+2E(r)}\,dr^2 +R(t,r)^2d\Omega_2^2. \tag{14}
\end{align}$$

The areal radius $R$ makes each symmetry sphere have area $4\pi R^2$. A prime is a radial-label derivative. The dimensionless function $E$ is a shell-energy datum; it is not a matter energy density. Define the geometrized shell mass by $1-2M/R=g^{\mu\nu}\partial_\mu R\partial_\nu R$. Dust Einstein equations make it constant along the comoving flow, so $M=M(r)$. Inserting the metric gives the radial evolution law, while the Einstein density equation gives the second relation below. For zero cosmological constant,

$$\begin{align}
\dot R^2=\frac{2M(r)}R+2E(r),\qquad \kappa\rho=\frac{2M'(r)}{R^2R'}. \tag{15}
\end{align}$$

Here $M$ is a geometrized Misner–Sharp mass with length units. The corresponding mass in ordinary mass/energy units is $M/G$. This convention fixes the factors of $G$ in all following averages.

Choose a dimensionless comoving radius and the data

$$\begin{align}
E(r)=0,\qquad M(r)=M_0r^3,\qquad t_B(r)=-br^2,\qquad M_0>0,\quad b\ge0,\quad t>0. \tag{16}
\end{align}$$

The function $t_B$ is the bang time of a shell, namely the integration constant at which its areal radius vanishes on the expanding solution. Both $t$ and $b$ have length/time units. Fix a finite domain $0\le r\le r_D$ with positive dimensionless outer label. No exterior matching or black-hole interpretation is imposed.

At fixed positive $r$, the expanding root integrates to $R^{3/2}=\frac32\sqrt{2M}\,(t-t_B)$. Substituting the chosen data, define

$$\begin{align}
A=\left(\frac{9M_0}{2}\right)^{1/3},\qquad \tau=t+br^2,\qquad \upsilon=t+\frac73br^2, \qquad R=Ar\tau^{2/3}. \tag{17}
\end{align}$$

The amplitude $A$ has length to the one-third power. The rates $\theta,H_D$ have inverse-length units, while $\sigma^2,Q_D,\kappa\rho$ have inverse-length-squared units. Direct differentiation gives

$$\begin{align}
R'=A\tau^{-1/3}\upsilon>0,\qquad \dot R=\frac23Ar\tau^{-1/3}>0\quad(r>0). \tag{18}
\end{align}$$

Thus this patch has neither shell crossings nor shell focusing at positive $t$. A shell crossing would require zero R-prime at nonzero mass gradient; the chosen signs exclude it. The formal past bang surfaces are not included in the smooth patch.

The transverse and radial expansion rates are respectively $\dot R/R$ and $\dot R'/R'$. Their eigenvalues give

$$\begin{align}
\kappa\rho=\frac4{3\tau\upsilon},\qquad \theta=\frac1\tau+\frac1\upsilon,\qquad \sigma^2=\frac13\left(\frac1\upsilon-\frac1\tau\right)^2 =\frac{16b^2r^4}{27\tau^2\upsilon^2}. \tag{19}
\end{align}$$

These expressions satisfy all three local scalar equations. The full Einstein tensor has only the dust source, and its four-dimensional Ricci scalar is kappa times rho. Since $R/r$ is smooth and even in $r$ at positive $t$, the polar centre extends regularly there. The limiting invariants and radial scale are:

$$\begin{align}
\lim_{r\to0}\frac Rr=At^{2/3},\qquad \lim_{r\to0}\kappa\rho=\frac4{3t^2},\qquad \lim_{r\to0}\theta=\frac2t,\qquad \lim_{r\to0}\sigma^2=0. \tag{20}
\end{align}$$

For positive $b$ the density and expansion decrease with radius, and shear is nonzero away from the centre. The mass-coordinate choice proportional to $r^3$ has not made the spacetime homogeneous: the nonconstant bang time remains physical data. At $b=0$ the solution reduces to flat Einstein–de Sitter dust.

Every fixed-time spatial slice is nevertheless intrinsically flat. Since $R'>0$, $R$ itself is a valid radial coordinate on that slice and its metric becomes $dR^2+R^2d\Omega_2^2$. Consequently

$$\begin{align}
{}^{(3)}R=0,\qquad {}^{(4)}R=\kappa\rho. \tag{21}
\end{align}$$

Flat spatial slices do not imply homogeneous spacetime or zero four-curvature.

## 5. Exact Averages and a Boundary Cancellation

On the full comoving ball the proper volume and total rest mass are

$$
\begin{aligned}
dV&=R^2R'\sin\vartheta\,dr\,d\vartheta\,d\varphi,\\
V_D&=4\pi\int_0^{r_D}R^2R'\,dr=\frac{4\pi}3R_D^3,\\
\int_D\rho\,dV&=\frac{M(r_D)}G,
\qquad R_D=R(t,r_D).
\end{aligned}
\tag{22}
$$

Positivity of R-prime allowed us to remove an absolute value in the volume element. Regularity at the centre supplies the zero lower boundary term. The mass identity here uses $E=0$; for nonzero $E$ the proper-volume weight includes an additional factor and cannot simply be dropped.

Writing $\tau_D=t+br_D^2$, direct integration gives

$$\begin{align}
\langle\theta\rangle_D=\frac2{\tau_D},\qquad \kappa\langle\rho\rangle_D=\frac4{3\tau_D^2}. \tag{23}
\end{align}$$

To compute $Q_D$ without separately integrating two complicated terms, define the second expansion invariant

$$\begin{align}
\mathrm{II}=\frac13\theta^2-\sigma^2 =H_\perp^2+2H_\perp H_\parallel, \qquad H_\perp=\frac{\dot R}R,\quad H_\parallel=\frac{\dot R'}{R'}. \tag{24}
\end{align}$$

Elementary product differentiation proves the two identities

$$\begin{align}
\theta R^2R'=\partial_r(R^2\dot R),\qquad \mathrm{II}\,R^2R'=\partial_r(R\dot R^2). \tag{25}
\end{align}$$

They hold for any smooth $R$ with the stated nondegeneracy, before inserting the dust equations. The angular integral and the regular-centre boundary condition therefore give

$$\begin{align}
\langle\theta\rangle_D=3\frac{\dot R_D}{R_D},\qquad \langle\mathrm{II}\rangle_D=3\frac{\dot R_D^2}{R_D^2}. \tag{26}
\end{align}$$

It follows, rather than being postulated, that

$$\begin{align}
Q_D=2\langle\mathrm{II}\rangle_D -\frac23\langle\theta\rangle_D^2=0. \tag{27}
\end{align}$$

The cancellation uses an $E=0$ spherical domain containing a regular centre, with its proper-volume measure and comoving foliation. It is not a theorem that every inhomogeneous spacetime has zero backreaction. Nonzero $E$, other domain shapes or other foliations require a new calculation.

In this model the resulting volume history is explicitly

$$\begin{align}
a_D(t)=\left(\frac{t+br_D^2}{t_{\rm ref}+br_D^2}\right)^{2/3}, \qquad H_D=\frac2{3\tau_D}>0,\qquad \frac{\ddot a_D}{a_D}=-\frac2{9\tau_D^2}<0. \tag{28}
\end{align}$$

It satisfies both averaged Einstein equations and mass conservation. It has no bounce in the chosen domain. A bounce would require a vanishing Hubble rate and positive acceleration; neither occurs here.

## 6. Positive Variance and a Fully Evaluated Commutator

The cancellation does not mean that averaging commutes with time evolution. For positive $b$ and a domain of positive radius, the expansion is not constant over the ball. Its variance and the mean shear are positive. In fact

$$\begin{align}
\langle\sigma^2\rangle_D =\frac{16b^2}{9r_D^3\tau_D^2} \int_0^{r_D}\frac{r^6\,dr}{\tau\upsilon},\qquad \operatorname{Var}_D(\theta)=3\langle\sigma^2\rangle_D>0. \tag{29}
\end{align}$$

The second relation follows from the already established $Q_D$ cancellation; it can also be checked by integrating both moments independently.

For a concrete nonhomogeneous point choose a reference length/time $L$ and set

$$\begin{align}
t=b=L,\qquad r_D=1,\qquad M_0=\frac{2L}{9},\qquad A^3=L. \tag{30}
\end{align}$$

Time derivatives are taken with $b$ fixed before evaluating this point. They are not derivatives along a parameter choice in which $b$ varies with time. The needed positive integral is elementary:

$$
\begin{aligned}
I_1&=\int_0^1\frac{y^6\,dy}{(1+y^2)(1+\tfrac73y^2)}\\
&=-\frac{23}{49}+\frac{3\pi}{16}
 -\frac{27}{196}\sqrt{\frac37}\arctan\sqrt{\frac73}
 \simeq0.03027649993.
\end{aligned}
\tag{31}
$$

The exact proper-volume averages are

$$
\begin{aligned}
\langle\theta\rangle_D&=L^{-1},\qquad
\langle\sigma^2\rangle_D=\frac{4I_1}{9L^2},\\
\operatorname{Var}_D(\theta)&=\frac{4I_1}{3L^2}
 \simeq\frac{0.04036866657}{L^2},\\
\langle\dot\theta\rangle_D&=-\frac1{2L^2}-\frac{4I_1}{3L^2},
\qquad \partial_t\langle\theta\rangle_D=-\frac1{2L^2}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{32}
$$

Thus there is a consequential, nonzero failure of commutation:

$$\begin{align}
\partial_t\langle\theta\rangle_D-\langle\dot\theta\rangle_D =\frac{4I_1}{3L^2}>0,\qquad Q_D=0. \tag{33}
\end{align}$$

If shear were incorrectly discarded, the purported backreaction would instead be

$$\begin{align}
Q_D^{\mathrm{variance\ only}}=\frac{8I_1}{9L^2} \simeq\frac{0.02691244438}{L^2}\ne0. \tag{34}
\end{align}$$

That number does not satisfy the definition or the averaged constraint for this exact spacetime. Positive variance alone therefore determines neither the actual backreaction nor a bounce.

## 7. What a Macroscopic Scale Factor Does Not Establish

The domain scale factor is defined from a volume, not by averaging metric components. Buchert's equations average scalars on a specified foliation. They do not define a tensor-transport prescription or a four-metric average. In general there is no identity equating the Einstein tensor of an averaged metric with the average of the original Einstein tensor. Even here, where the volume history obeys flat dust Friedmann equations, the original spacetime remains inhomogeneous and shearing.

Finite mean quantities also do not prove local regularity. For fixed positive $b$ and domain radius, the one-sided boundary limit gives

$$\begin{align}
\lim_{t\downarrow0}\kappa\langle\rho\rangle_D =\frac4{3b^2r_D^4}<\infty, \qquad \kappa\rho(t,0)=\frac4{3t^2}\longrightarrow\infty. \tag{35}
\end{align}$$

The latter is the four-dimensional Ricci scalar at the centre, not a removable coordinate effect. The smooth solution was restricted to positive $t$; a finite averaged density has not constructed an extension across its singular boundary.

Indeed a higher moment exposes what the first moment misses:

$$\begin{align}
\langle(\kappa\rho)^2\rangle_D =\frac{16}{3r_D^3\tau_D^2} \int_0^{r_D}\frac{r^2\,dr} {(t+br^2)(t+\tfrac73br^2)}. \tag{36}
\end{align}$$

As $t$ decreases to zero the positive integrand approaches a nonintegrable multiple of inverse r-squared near the centre. Its exact leading behavior is

$$\begin{align}
\langle(\kappa\rho)^2\rangle_D \sim \frac{2\pi(1-\sqrt{3/7})}{b^{7/2}r_D^7\sqrt t} \longrightarrow\infty\qquad(b>0). \tag{37}
\end{align}$$

This is a classical curvature-moment statement, not a quantum renormalization prescription. It shows why a successful equation for one averaged quantity need not control fluctuations, singularities or all observables.

A microscopic-to-macroscopic derivation would therefore have to supply the actual microscopic action and observables; a state and renormalized composite stress/spin operators; a coarse-graining map with its scale, domain, transport and gauge choices; the retained correlations and a justified closure; constraint-compatible effective dynamics; and quantitative control of any discrete-to-continuum limit. A finite graph count or a representation dimension does not establish a four-dimensional continuum field theory or a conformal fixed point. Nor does an averaged turning-point ansatz establish an exterior matching, a black-hole interior, a nonsingular extension or the creation of another universe.

What has been achieved here is narrower and explicit: a local dust solution, its proper-volume averaging map, a nonzero evolution commutator, an exact shear–variance cancellation and an averaged expanding history with known limits. Spin correlations or a different geometry can change the problem, but must enter through specified data and newly checked equations.
