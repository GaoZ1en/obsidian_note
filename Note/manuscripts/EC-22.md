# Free Gauge Superfields and a Massive Vector Multiplet

DRAFT — bounded review recorded.

We construct two flat four-dimensional N=1 theories: an Abelian vector multiplet and a supersymmetric Stückelberg massive vector. The second action has a propagating real scalar and two Weyl fermions in addition to its massive vector. Its physical mass is derived from the component action, not identified with a parameter by name. These are concrete free theories, not an exhaustive list of fundamental fields. Off-shell supersymmetry will follow from actual odd operators and gauge constraints, not from matching component counts.

## 1. Superspace with the Fixed Physical Signs

Use Minkowski metric $\eta=\operatorname{diag}(-1,1,1,1)$, $c=\hbar=1$, and length-valued coordinates. The Pauli systems and alternating matrices are

$$\begin{align}
s^\mu=(I,\sigma_i),\qquad t^\mu=(I,-\sigma_i),\qquad s^\mu t^\nu+s^\nu t^\mu=-2\eta^{\mu\nu}I,
\end{align}$$

$$
\epsilon_{AB}=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\-1&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 \epsilon^{AB}=\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}.
 \tag{1}
$$

An upper unprimed column transforms by $A\in SL(2,\mathbb C)$. In the physical Dirac dictionary,

$$
\rho(A)=\operatorname{diag}((A^\dagger)^{-1},A),\quad
 \gamma^0=\begin{pmatrix}0&I\\I&0\end{pmatrix},\quad
 \gamma^i=\begin{pmatrix}0&\sigma_i\\-\sigma_i&0\end{pmatrix},\quad
 \gamma_5=\operatorname{diag}(-I,+I).
 \tag{2}
$$

Thus the unprimed fundamental space is the positive-gamma-five block. We keep $\{\gamma^a,\gamma^b\}=-2\eta^{ab}$ and $\bar\Psi=\Psi^\dagger\gamma^0$ throughout; no opposite Clifford convention is silently imported.

The odd coordinates are covectors $\theta_A$ and $\overline\theta_{A'}$, transformed by $A^{-T}$ and $\overline A^{-T}$. Their real structure obeys $\theta_A^\dagger=\overline\theta_{A'}$ and reverses product order. In a complex calculation the four coordinate generators are independent; the real superspace has dimension $4|4$. These are four real formal odd directions, not four extra ordinary positions. Fermionic component fields anticommute with them and with each other.

All odd derivatives are left derivatives, so

$$\begin{align}
\partial^L_{\xi}(fg)=(\partial^L_{\xi}f)g+(-1)^{|f|}f\partial^L_{\xi}g.
\end{align}$$

In particular $\partial^L_{\theta_1}(\theta_0\theta_1)=-\theta_0$. Fix the normalized monomials and measures by

$$\begin{align}
\theta^2=2\theta_0\theta_1,\qquad \overline\theta^2=-2\overline\theta_{0'}\overline\theta_{1'},\qquad \int d^2\theta\,\theta^2=\int d^2\overline\theta\,\overline\theta^2=1. \tag{3}
\end{align}$$

Consequently $\int d^4\theta$ extracts minus one quarter of the ordered coefficient of $\theta_0\theta_1\overline\theta_{0'}\overline\theta_{1'}$. It is not evaluation at zero odd coordinates. Conjugation of an odd contraction has a sign: $(\theta_A\lambda^A)^\dagger=-\overline\theta_{A'}\overline\lambda^{A'}$.

Write $T^{AA'}=(t^\mu)^{AA'}\partial_\mu$. The [AST-15](<AST-15.md>) operators are

$$
\begin{aligned}
 Q^A&=i\partial^L_{\theta_A}-\overline\theta_{A'}T^{AA'},&
 \overline Q^{A'}&=-i\partial^L_{\overline\theta_{A'}}+\theta_AT^{AA'},\\
 D^A&=i\partial^L_{\theta_A}+\overline\theta_{A'}T^{AA'},&
 \overline D^{A'}&=-i\partial^L_{\overline\theta_{A'}}-\theta_AT^{AA'}.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{4}
$$

Their actual graded algebra is

$$\begin{align}
\{Q^A,\overline Q^{A'}\}=2iT^{AA'},\qquad \{D^A,\overline D^{A'}\}=-2iT^{AA'},\qquad \{Q,D\}=\{Q,\overline D\}=\{\overline Q,D\}=\{\overline Q,\overline D\}=0, \tag{5}
\end{align}$$

with same-type brackets zero. These identities hold on all superfunctions, without a field equation. The book's real even variation is $\delta_\varepsilon=-i(\varepsilon_AQ^A-\overline\varepsilon_{A'}\overline Q^{A'})$, with odd constant conjugate parameters. Let $D_A=\epsilon_{AB}D^B$ and similarly for barred indices. Then

$$\begin{align}
D^2=D^AD_A=2D^0D^1,\qquad \overline D^2=2\overline D^{0'}\overline D^{1'},\qquad D^2\theta^2|_0=4,\quad \overline D^2\overline\theta^2|_0=-4. \tag{6}
\end{align}$$

The differing last signs are needed below.

On $e^{-iEt+i\mathbf p\cdot\mathbf x}$ the charge bracket becomes $2X(p)$, where $X(p)=EI+\mathbf p\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma$. A positive Hilbert realization with barred charge the adjoint requires $E\ge|\mathbf p|$. This condition is not imposed on formal off-shell superfunctions. For example E=0,pz=1 gives the indefinite matrix diag(1,−1), while the formal sixteen-dimensional exterior-coordinate matrices still satisfy (5). Formal closure and physical positivity are different assertions.

Coordinates theta have units length$^{1/2}$, and Q,D length$^{-1/2}$. We take V dimensionless, A with units length$^{-1}$, lambda length$^{-3/2}$ and its real auxiliary d length$^{-2}$. A canonical chiral superfield S has units length$^{-1}$; its components s,chi,F have units length$^{-1}$, length$^{-3/2}$ and length$^{-2}$. Both mass parameters introduced below have inverse-length units. With these choices each component Lagrangian has units length$^{-4}$.

## 2. A Real Vector Superfield and Its Actual Field Strength

Let $V^\dagger=V$. The Abelian supergauge transformation is

$$\begin{align}
V\longmapsto V+\Lambda+\Lambda^\dagger,\qquad \overline D^{A'}\Lambda=0. \tag{7}
\end{align}$$

A chiral parameter is a superfield, not only an ordinary scalar gauge function. Its independent components remove the extra lowest, single-odd and pure-two-odd components of V. In a local Wess–Zumino gauge choose

$$
\begin{aligned}
 B^\mu&=\theta_At^{\mu AA'}\overline\theta_{A'},\\
 V_{\rm WZ}&=B^\mu A_\mu
 +i\theta^2\overline\theta_{A'}\overline\lambda^{A'}
 +i\overline\theta^2\theta_A\lambda^A
 +\frac12\theta^2\overline\theta^2d.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{8}
$$

A and d are real. Both displayed fermionic terms have coefficient plus i in this dictionary. Their conjugation exchanges them, including order reversal of the odd field coefficients. The familiar expression from a different derivative/index convention cannot be copied without those signs.

The residual parameter is $\Lambda=i\alpha(y)/2$ for a real body function alpha, with

$$\begin{align}
y^\mu=x^\mu-iB^\mu,\qquad \delta A_\mu=\partial_\mu\alpha,\qquad \delta\lambda=\delta d=0. \tag{9}
\end{align}$$

Here the chiral function is Taylor-expanded at y. Indeed the B coefficient in $\Lambda+\Lambda^\dagger$ is $\partial_\mu\alpha$. Supersymmetry need not preserve the gauge representative (8) alone: a compensating supergauge transformation restores it. This does not require a field equation.

Define the strengths with the book phases,

$$\begin{align}
W^A=\frac14\overline D^2D^AV,\qquad \overline W^{A'}=\frac14D^2\overline D^{A'}V. \tag{10}
\end{align}$$

They satisfy

$$\begin{align}
\overline D^{A'}W^A=0,\qquad D^A\overline W^{A'}=0,\qquad \delta_\Lambda W=0,\qquad D_AW^A=\overline D_{A'}\overline W^{A'}. \tag{11}
\end{align}$$

Chirality follows from three equal-type odd derivatives vanishing. For a chiral gauge parameter the remaining derivative bracket produces an ordinary derivative of Lambda, killed by the next barred derivative; for its antichiral conjugate D annihilates it. The last identity is the real vector-strength Bianchi identity, not an equation of motion. Thus an arbitrary unconstrained pair of chiral spinors would not be the same field content.

The actual component projection of (10), in chiral coordinates, is

$$\begin{align}
W^A(y,\theta)=\lambda^A(y)+\theta_B\mathcal M^{AB}(y)+\theta^2\mathcal N^A(y),\qquad \mathcal N=i\,t^\mu\epsilon\,\partial_\mu\overline\lambda. \tag{12}
\end{align}$$

The last expression uses ordinary two-column matrix multiplication and the lower epsilon in (1). Define $F_{\mu\nu}=\partial_\mu A_\nu-\partial_\nu A_\mu$. With row A and column B ordered0,1,

$$
\mathcal M=
 \begin{pmatrix}
 F_{01}-iF_{02}+F_{13}-iF_{23}&-F_{03}+iF_{12}-id\\
 -F_{03}+iF_{12}+id&-F_{01}-iF_{02}+F_{13}+iF_{23}
 \end{pmatrix}.
 \tag{13}
$$

In particular $W|_0=\lambda$, and the auxiliary part of (12) is $i\theta^A d$, not theta times d with a borrowed phase. Its antisymmetric part and the symmetric field-strength part are both retained. Direct graded differentiation gives (12)–(13); no mass shell was used.

## 3. First Action: a Free Abelian Vector Multiplet

For an odd spinor define the conjugate bilinears in their stated order:

$$\begin{align}
W^2=W^AW_A=2W^0W^1,\qquad \overline W^2=(W^2)^\dagger =\overline W_{A'}\overline W^{A'}=-2\overline W^{0'}\overline W^{1'}.
\end{align}$$

The action in these conventions is

$$\begin{align}
I_V=-\frac14\int d^4x\left[ \int d^2\theta\,W^2+\int d^2\overline\theta\,\overline W^2\right]. \tag{14}
\end{align}$$

This sign is tied to (3), (4) and (10). For example the auxiliary part gives $W^2|_{\theta^2}=-d^2$; the two chiral integrals and the coefficient in (14) therefore give plus one half d squared. The field-strength matrix (13) gives the Maxwell contraction. The lambda–N terms supply the fermion derivative, with the odd coefficients kept in order. The complete result, up to a spacetime divergence, is

$$\begin{align}
\mathcal L_V=-\frac14F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu} +i\overline\lambda s^\mu\partial_\mu\lambda+\frac12d^2. \tag{15}
\end{align}$$

The asymmetric-looking fermion derivative is equivalent to its symmetrized Hermitian form after integration by parts. We assume compactly supported variations and vanishing surface flux, or explicit periodic conditions. These assumptions remove a spacetime divergence, not a nonzero local density by declaration.

Varying d gives d=0. The remaining equations are Maxwell's equation and $is^\mu\partial_\mu\lambda=0$. The auxiliary has no kinetic term or particle mode. For the radiative Maxwell sector use $A_0=0$ and $\partial_iA_i=0$, quotient the allowed boundary-trivial gauge transformations, and exclude harmonic zero-momentum modes. The two transverse photon families and the Weyl gaugino then have positive canonical norms, as in [EC-03](<EC-03.md>). Boundary gauge charges and global flux sectors are not removed by that local statement.

The action is supersymmetric off shell before auxiliary elimination. Charges anticommute with the chirality derivatives, so they preserve (11) and the gauge equivalence. A full superspace variation is an odd derivative plus a spacetime divergence. A chiral F-term has the same integrated property: at barred theta zero, the unbarred charge is an odd derivative and the barred charge reduces on chiral functions to $2\theta T$. Berezin integration removes the odd derivative; the boundary hypothesis removes the ordinary derivative. This proves invariance without imposing the field equations. After choosing (8), closure is understood modulo the compensating gauge transformation. Equal numbers of components are not the proof.

## 4. Second Action: a Stückelberg Massive Vector

Introduce an independent chiral superfield

$$\begin{align}
S(y,\theta)=s(y)+\sqrt2\theta_A\chi^A(y)+\theta^2F_S(y),\qquad s=(c+ia)/\sqrt2, \tag{16}
\end{align}$$

where c and a are real. Fix a real parameter $\mu>0$ and extend (7) by

$$\begin{align}
S\longmapsto S-\mu\Lambda,\qquad \Sigma=S+S^\dagger+\mu V,\qquad \delta\Sigma=0. \tag{17}
\end{align}$$

The new free action is

$$\begin{align}
I_\mu=I_V+\frac12\int d^4x\,d^4\theta\,\Sigma^2. \tag{18}
\end{align}$$

The term involving only S reduces to the positive chiral kinetic action: half of $(S+S^\dagger)^2$ contains $S^\dagger S$, while the integrated pure-chiral and antichiral terms are boundary divergences. This is an integrated identity, not pointwise deletion of their x-dependent top components. Off-shell invariance follows from the real D-term measure and the same operator algebra, not from the equations of S.

### Derive the Physical Mass

From the Pauli/exterior algebra,

$$\begin{align}
B^\mu B^\nu=-\frac12\eta^{\mu\nu}\theta^2\overline\theta^2, \qquad \int d^4\theta\,V_{\rm WZ}^2=-\frac12A_\mu A^\mu.
\end{align}$$

Consequently the pure-vector mass contribution in (18) is $-\mu^2A^2/4$. Comparing with the canonical Proca coefficient fixes

$$\begin{align}
-\frac{\mu^2}{4}A^2=-\frac{M^2}{2}A^2, \qquad M^2=\frac{\mu^2}{2},\quad M=\frac\mu{\sqrt2}. \tag{19}
\end{align}$$

The parameter mu is not the physical mass M with this normalization of V and its action.

The bottom component of Sigma is $\sqrt2c$, its B coefficient is $\sqrt2\partial_\mu a+\mu A_\mu$, and its top bosonic coefficient includes $\sqrt2\Box c/4+\mu d/2$. Their products give the scalar kinetic term, covariant axion derivative and c–d coupling. Including the actual odd products gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
 \mathcal L_\mu={}&\mathcal L_V
 -\frac12\partial_\mu c\,\partial^\mu c
 -\frac12(\partial_\mu a+MA_\mu)(\partial^\mu a+MA^\mu)\\
 &+i\overline\chi s^\mu\partial_\mu\chi+F_S^*F_S
 +Mc\,d-iM\lambda\chi+iM\overline\lambda\,\overline\chi.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{20}
$$

Here $\lambda\chi=\lambda^0\chi^1-\lambda^1\chi^0$, while its conjugate is $\overline\lambda\,\overline\chi=-(\overline\lambda^{0'}\overline\chi^{1'}-\overline\lambda^{1'}\overline\chi^{0'})$. The i in the mixed mass is a computed phase. All coefficients in (20) follow from the same Berezin calculation as (15), including the independent fermionic component coefficients.

For the residual ordinary gauge transformation,

$$\begin{align}
\delta A_\mu=\partial_\mu\alpha,\qquad \delta a=-M\alpha, \qquad \delta c=0,\qquad B_\mu^{\rm inv}=A_\mu+\partial_\mu a/M. \tag{21}
\end{align}$$

This B with a lower spacetime index is the gauge-invariant vector, not the odd bilinear $B^\mu$ in (8). The combination in (20) is exactly $M B_\mu^{\rm inv}$.

### Do Not Fix the Same Gauge Freedom Twice

After choosing Wess–Zumino gauge, the remaining real alpha can set a=0 locally. It does not also remove c or chi. Alternatively a full chiral supergauge parameter $\Lambda=S/\mu$ sets S to zero and produces

$$\begin{align}
V_{\rm unit}=V+(S+S^\dagger)/\mu=\Sigma/\mu. \tag{22}
\end{align}$$

This is a general real superfield, not a Wess–Zumino representative. Its bottom and single-theta components include $\sqrt2c/\mu$ and $\sqrt2\chi/\mu$. They have not disappeared. Imposing S=0 and then also declaring those components of V zero would discard physical modes rather than make another gauge choice. Global winding, patching and allowed boundary gauge transformations need additional analysis; the gauges here are local or in the trivial sector.

## 5. Eliminate Auxiliaries, Then Analyze Physical Dynamics

Vary the algebraic fields before substituting:

$$\begin{align}
d=-Mc,\qquad F_S=0,\qquad \frac12d^2+Mc\,d =\frac12(d+Mc)^2-\frac12M^2c^2. \tag{23}
\end{align}$$

In particular d is not zero in the second model. Its elimination gives the stable real-scalar mass, equal to the vector mass already derived in (19).

Rephase $\chi'=i\chi$ and define the four-component field with its explicit epsilon map,

$$
\Psi=\begin{pmatrix}-\epsilon\overline{\chi'}\\\lambda\end{pmatrix}.
 \tag{24}
$$

The upper column transforms by $(A^\dagger)^{-1}$, not by A. The fermionic terms in (20) are, modulo a divergence, $i\bar\Psi\gamma^\mu\partial_\mu\Psi-M\bar\Psi\Psi$. This statement includes the mass sign and conjugation order; a full graded Fourier-pair calculation verifies it. The two-by-two Weyl mass matrix before rephasing has off-diagonal entries iM, whose squared singular masses are both $M^2$.

In the local gauge a=0 the reduced Lagrangian is therefore

$$\begin{align}
\mathcal L_{\rm red}=-\frac14F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu} -\frac12M^2B_\mu^{\rm inv}B^{{\rm inv}\mu} -\frac12\partial_\mu c\partial^\mu c-\frac12M^2c^2 +i\bar\Psi\gamma^\mu\partial_\mu\Psi-M\bar\Psi\Psi. \tag{25}
\end{align}$$

Its vector equation implies $\partial_\mu B^{{\rm inv}\mu}=0$ because M is nonzero. The scalar obeys $(\Box-M^2)c=0$. The fermion obeys the shared Dirac equation. On a positive-frequency phase, its matrix is

$$\begin{align}
\mathcal P(p)=E\gamma^0-p_i\gamma^i=-p_\mu\gamma^\mu,\qquad (\mathcal P-M)u=0, \qquad p^2=-M^2, \tag{26}
\end{align}$$

not the unchanged Clifford contraction with the opposite sign. This agrees with [EC-15](<EC-15.md>).

For positivity, use $\Pi_i=F_{0i}$ and integrate the spatial constraint term by parts. Gauss's equation fixes $B_0^{\rm inv}=-\partial_i\Pi_i/M^2$. The bosonic Hamiltonian on the reduced constraint space is

$$
\begin{aligned}
 H_{\rm bos}=\frac12\int d^3x\bigg[&\boldsymbol\Pi^2+
 (\boldsymbol\nabla\times\mathbf B^{\rm inv})^2+
 M^2(\mathbf B^{\rm inv})^2+\frac{(\boldsymbol\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol\Pi)^2}{M^2}\\
 &+\pi_c^2+(\boldsymbol\nabla c)^2+M^2c^2\bigg].
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{27}
$$

Every term is nonnegative. The time component was a constraint variable, not a fourth negative-norm vector oscillator. The fermionic Cauchy inner product is $\int\Psi^\dagger\Psi$, with the usual positive/negative-frequency mode interpretation of [EC-03](<EC-03.md>). This derives the healthy constrained free dynamics; positivity was not inferred from a component count.

At mu=0 the superspace action separates into a massless vector and a free chiral multiplet. Formula (21), the massive constraint elimination and the Proca inverse cannot be used by dividing by M at that point. The action limit and those particular gauge-fixed parametrizations have different domains.

## 6. Physical Modes and Ordinary Propagators

The mode wavefunctions in this section have commuting complex entries; fermionic mode coefficients carry the CAR. Their positive norm is not a positivity assertion about Grassmann numbers. For $p^\mu=(E,0,0,q)$, $E=\sqrt{q^2+M^2}$, take

$$\begin{align}
\varepsilon_1=(0,1,0,0),\quad \varepsilon_2=(0,0,1,0),\quad \varepsilon_L=(q/M,0,0,E/M). \tag{28}
\end{align}$$

They satisfy $p\cdot\varepsilon=0$, $\varepsilon_\lambda^*\cdot\varepsilon_{\lambda'}=\delta_{\lambda\lambda'}$, and

$$\begin{align}
\sum_\lambda\varepsilon_\lambda^\mu\varepsilon_\lambda^{\nu*} =\eta^{\mu\nu}+p^\mu p^\nu/M^2. \tag{29}
\end{align}$$

A positive Proca norm uses the field strength, not just the spatial polarization length:

$$\begin{align}
i\int_V(B_i^*F_{0i}-F_{0i}^*B_i)d^3x=1
\end{align}$$

for $B^\mu=\varepsilon_\lambda^\mu e^{-iEt+iqz}/\sqrt{2EV}$. The actual coefficient identity is

$$\begin{align}
\frac1E\sum_i\varepsilon_{\lambda i} (E\varepsilon_{\lambda'i}^*-p_i\varepsilon_{\lambda'}^{0*}) =\delta_{\lambda\lambda'}. \tag{30}
\end{align}$$

The nonzero time component of the longitudinal mode is essential. Its spatial polarization sum alone is not the identity.

For a fully evaluated example, use a periodic box of side $\ell=\pi/2$ in a fixed length unit, V=ell cubed, and periodic spin structure. Set $\mu=3\sqrt2$, hence M=3. With E=5 and q=4, the scalar mode $e^{-i5t+i4z}/\sqrt{10V}$, the longitudinal vector with $\varepsilon_L=(4/3,0,0,5/3)$, and the Dirac mode $(1,0,3,0)^Te^{-i5t+i4z}/\sqrt{10V}$ each have unit positive canonical norm. Direct differentiation also verifies the scalar, Proca and Dirac equations. Treating mu itself as the physical mass would give $E^2-q^2-\mu^2=-9$, not zero.

The first model has, for example,

$$
A_\mu^{(+)}=\frac{(0,1,-i,0)}{\sqrt2}
 \frac{e^{-i4(t+z)}}{\sqrt{8V}},\qquad
 \lambda^{(+)}=\begin{pmatrix}0\\1\end{pmatrix}
 \frac{e^{-i4(t+z)}}{\sqrt V}.
 \tag{31}
$$

The transverse Maxwell norm and the Weyl current norm are each one. These periodic modes are an infrared normalization convention, not a Lorentz-invariant finite-volume theorem. Conjugate-frequency partners implement the appropriate real/fermionic field reality; a single complex plane wave is not declared a real electromagnetic field.

The propagator kernels give a further off-shell check. Write $k^2=\eta_{\mu\nu}k^\mu k^\nu$. For the gauge-invariant massive vector after local gauge reduction,

$$\begin{align}
K^\mu{}_{\nu}=-(k^2+M^2)\delta^\mu{}_{\nu}+k^\mu k_\nu,\qquad (K^{-1})^\mu{}_{\nu}=-\frac{\delta^\mu{}_{\nu}+k^\mu k_\nu/M^2}{k^2+M^2}. \tag{32}
\end{align}$$

Direct multiplication gives the identity away from the poles. Its determinant is $M^2(k^2+M^2)^3$; the direction proportional to k has eigenvalue $-M^2$, not an additional propagating pole. Before gauge fixing, use the coupled vector and Stückelberg kernel

$$
\mathbb K=\begin{pmatrix}K^\mu{}_{\nu}&-iMk^\mu\\ iMk_\nu&-k^2\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 \mathbb K\begin{pmatrix}ik^\nu\\-M\end{pmatrix}=0.
$$

It acts on the contravariant vector components and a; the displayed null vector is exactly their gauge variation. This matrix must not be inverted as a nonsingular five-field operator.

The scalar inverse is $-1/(k^2+M^2)$. With $\mathcal P(k)=-k_\mu\gamma^\mu$, the fermion inverse is

$$\begin{align}
(\mathcal P-M)^{-1}=\frac{\mathcal P+M}{-k^2-M^2},\qquad \det(\mathcal P-M)=(k^2+M^2)^2. \tag{33}
\end{align}$$

The usual Minkowski-vacuum Feynman kernels are i times these inverses, with $k^2+M^2$ replaced by $k^2+M^2-i0$. This specifies that free prescription, not an arbitrary-background state or functional measure. Pole multiplicities alone do not prove a physical polarization count, a CPT theorem or off-shell closure; the constraint analysis, positive modes and operator identities were given separately.

A finite positive rest-frame check is also useful. Let $c_0=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&1\\0&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)$ and $z=\operatorname{diag}(1,-1)$. On an auxiliary spin-half vacuum factor times a two-oscillator Fock space, take

$$\begin{align}
a_1=I_2\otimes c_0\otimes I_2,\quad a_2=I_2\otimes z\otimes c_0,\qquad Q_a=\sqrt{2M}\,a_a,\quad \overline Q_a=Q_a^\dagger. \tag{34}
\end{align}$$

The actual CAR matrices satisfy $\{a_a,a_b^\dagger\}=\delta_{ab}I_8$ and $\{a_a,a_b\}=0$. Choosing the spin-half Clifford vacuum odd gives the usual even spin0 plus spin1 and two odd spin-half sectors. The little-group product is checked exactly, but that representation input and positive finite inner product are additional data. This is an eight-state rest fibre, not eight-dimensional spacetime, and it does not replace the off-shell field derivation or establish a fundamental field inventory.

## 7. What These Constructions Do Not Extend Automatically

Flat superspace already has a nonzero odd-index bracket (5), while its ordinary Minkowski body has zero spacetime torsion. Those are not the same torsion tensor. A curved Einstein–Cartan connection on the body does not by itself supply a consistent curved superspace, chiral projectors, Berezinian/chiral measures or supersymmetry generators. A curved superspace or supergravity formulation must specify its supertorsion constraints, graded Bianchi identities, compensators and admissible background supersymmetries. Replacing every partial derivative above by an arbitrary Gamma is not such a construction.

The two models have no interacting charged matter, gravity multiplet, non-Abelian dynamics or higher-spin gauge system. Such extensions need their own gauge/constraint and background-consistency analysis. Nor is an on-shell twistor helicity package an off-shell superfield with independent auxiliaries, supergauge freedom and the Berezin measure used here. A commuting derivative tower can organize solutions without establishing the odd super-Poincare algebra or these actions. No assertion of exhaustive eleven-field content, automatic quantum equivalence, or full quantum gravity follows from the present free examples.
