# Lorentzian Twistors, Shape Matching and Regulated Spin-Foam Amplitudes

**EC-17 — DRAFT; bounded review recorded.**

A twistor parametrization, a quantum simplicity prescription and a covariant amplitude are three different constructions. We give each explicitly. Two oriented canonical twistors describe a regular semisimple cotangent sector of $SL(2,\mathbb C)$, with a discrete branch identification. Equal face areas do not impose equal face shapes. A chosen principal-series convention then determines the exact weak simplicity label, including its ordering dependence. Finally we define a regulated Lorentzian vertex and evaluate a separate compact BF recoupling/gluing control. None of these steps proves equivalence of complete canonical and covariant quantum gravity.

The physical signature remains $(-,+,+,+)$ in four dimensions. Assume an oriented, time-oriented spin spacetime when interpreting the variables as gravitational boundary data; $w_2(TM)=0$, a chosen spin lift and local future frames are input. $SL(2,\mathbb C)=\operatorname{Spin}^+(1,3)$ acts on Hermitian matrices $X=x^0I+x^i\sigma_i$ by $X\mapsto gXg^\dagger$, with $\det X=-g(x,x)$. The stabilizer of the future unit normal $n_0=(1,0,0,0)$ is the compact SU(2), not the whole Lorentz group.

Use $c=1$, explicit $\hbar$, $\kappa=8\pi G>0$ and real $\gamma\ne0$. The area scale is $a_0=\kappa|\gamma|\hbar=8\pi|\gamma|\ell_P^2$, where $\ell_P^2=G\hbar$. Spinors and group variables below are auxiliary coordinates, not extra spacetime dimensions.

## 1. Canonical Boundary Twistors Are Not Incidence Coordinates

At the source of an oriented link choose $\omega\in\mathbb C^2$ and a dual row $\pi\in(\mathbb C^2)^*$. The target has independent $\widetilde\omega,\widetilde\pi$. Bars are complex conjugates on the real phase space; no relation between pi and omega is imposed. Both may be assigned square-root action units. The Lorentz action is $\omega\mapsto g\omega$, $\pi\mapsto\pi g^{-1}$ at each end. Choose the real potential and symplectic sign

$$\begin{align}
\Theta=2\operatorname{Re}(\pi_A d\omega^A- \widetilde\pi_A d\widetilde\omega^A),\qquad\Omega=-d\Theta.
\end{align}$$

It gives the holomorphic brackets

$$\begin{align}
\{\omega^A,\pi_B\}=\delta^A_B,\qquad \{\widetilde\omega^A,\widetilde\pi_B\}=-\delta^A_B,
\end{align}$$

the conjugate brackets, and zero mixed brackets. For example $\omega=(q+iQ)/\sqrt2$, $\pi=(p-iP)/\sqrt2$ turns a source contribution into $p\,dq+P\,dQ$. The minus sign at the target is an orientation choice that will be tested by the cotangent potential, not inferred from a dimension count.

These eight-real-dimensional endpoint variables can be packaged as canonical Lorentz twistors $(\omega^A,i\bar\pi_{A'})$. We do **not** impose flat spacetime incidence, a null-twistor condition or particle-helicity reduction. In particular [AST-14](<AST-14.md#L21-L51>) uses the dual incidence slots $(\omega^{A'},\pi_A)$ with a stated transpose in its incidence matrix. Its chirality, projectivization and contour-field labels cannot be transferred to this phase space without translating the spaces and imposing additional conditions.

## 2. Complex Matching and the Actual Cotangent Map

Set $E_s=\pi\omega$, $E_t=\widetilde\pi\widetilde\omega$ and impose the complex matching constraint

$$\begin{align}
\mathcal C=E_s-E_t=0.
\end{align}$$

This means two real equations. The real Hamiltonian $2\operatorname{Re}(\lambda\mathcal C)$, for complex lambda, generates the simultaneous transformations

$$\begin{align}
(\omega,\pi;\widetilde\omega,\widetilde\pi) \mapsto(z\omega,z^{-1}\pi; z\widetilde\omega,z^{-1}\widetilde\pi), \qquad z\in\mathbb C^*.
\end{align}$$

This is not ordinary common rescaling of both canonical slots. On $E_s=E_t\ne0$ the action is free. The regular constraint and quotient remove four of the original sixteen real dimensions, leaving twelve, the dimension of $T^*SL(2,\mathbb C)$.

Fix the numerical alternating matrix and two frames by

$$
\varepsilon=\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\1&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
S=(\omega,\varepsilon\pi^{\mathsf T}),\qquad
\widetilde S=(\widetilde\omega,\varepsilon\widetilde\pi^{\mathsf T}).
$$

Then $\det S=E_s$, $\det\widetilde S=E_t$. On the nonzero domain define

$$\begin{align}
h=\widetilde S S^{-1},\qquad J_s=\omega\pi-\frac{E_s}2I,\qquad J_t=\widetilde\omega\widetilde\pi-\frac{E_t}2I.
\end{align}$$

Thus $\det h=E_t/E_s$, becoming one on matching. The map sends both columns of S to the corresponding target columns and is invariant under the complex matching action. Under endpoint Lorentz changes $S\mapsto g_sS$, $\widetilde S\mapsto g_t\widetilde S$, it gives $h\mapsto g_t h g_s^{-1}$.

On matching, the **outward** target momentum is

$$\begin{align}
J_t^{\rm out}=-J_t=-hJ_sh^{-1}.
\end{align}$$

The potential is also determined, not guessed. If $\vartheta_s=\pi d\omega$, $\vartheta_t=\widetilde\pi d\widetilde\omega$, direct frame differentiation gives

$$\begin{align}
\vartheta_s-\vartheta_t+\operatorname{tr}(J_sh^{-1}dh) =\frac12d\mathcal C+ \frac{\mathcal C}{E_t}\left(\vartheta_t-\frac12dE_t\right).
\end{align}$$

Both terms on the right vanish after pullback to the matching surface. Therefore the reduced real potential is

$$\begin{align}
\Theta_{\rm red}=-2\operatorname{Re}\operatorname{tr}(J_sh^{-1}dh).
\end{align}$$

This identifies the local reduced symplectic structure with the real cotangent structure. For traceless matrices X,Y, let $Q_X=2\operatorname{Re}\operatorname{tr}(J_sX)$ and $\widetilde Q_X=-2\operatorname{Re}\operatorname{tr}(J_tX)$. Actual brackets give

$$\begin{aligned}
\{Q_X,Q_Y\}&=Q_{[X,Y]},&\{Q_X,h\}&=hX,\\
\{\widetilde Q_X,\widetilde Q_Y\}&=\widetilde Q_{[X,Y]}, &\{\widetilde Q_X,h\}&=-Xh,\\
\{Q_X,\widetilde Q_Y\}&=0,&\{h_{AB},h_{CD}\}_{\rm red}&=0.
\end{aligned}$$

The h-entry assertion uses matching; it is not an unrestricted identity for arbitrary unmatched spinors. The canonical sign is the one in [EC-07](<EC-07.md#L190-L205>), and the outward insertion pattern agrees with [AST-20](<AST-20.md#L55-L100>), although the present coordinates and group are Lorentzian.

### The Branch and Excluded Strata

The traceless momentum obeys $2\operatorname{tr}J_s^2=E_s^2$. Reconstructing spinors from a semisimple J therefore chooses one of its two nonzero eigenvalues. After choosing E, pick omega in the $+E/2$ eigenline and pi so that $\omega\pi=J+EI/2$; the residual scale is precisely $\mathbb C^*$. The target follows from h.

There is still a simultaneous discrete operation at both ends:

$$\begin{align}
\omega'=\varepsilon\pi^{\mathsf T},\qquad \pi'=(\omega_1,-\omega_0).
\end{align}$$

It swaps the columns of S, sends $E\mapsto-E$, but leaves J and h unchanged. It is not a matching rescaling. Consequently the regular matching quotient is a double cover of the nonzero-Casimir cotangent sector before this $\mathbb Z_2$ identification. Use either a local eigenvalue branch or the discrete quotient. There is no global single-valued square root on the full punctured complex Casimir plane. This eigenline ambiguity is not the central sign $h\mapsto-h$ of the spin cover.

The condition $E\ne0$ excludes more than zero momentum. For $\omega=(1,0)^{\mathsf T}$, $\pi=(0,1)$, the nonzero matrix $J=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&1\\0&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)$ is nilpotent with E=0, and the frame is singular. Neither these null-Casimir data nor the zero section is covered by the displayed inverse. No global identification of all twistor strata with the full cotangent bundle is claimed.

## 3. Lorentz Charges and a Real Shape-Matching Failure

Use $\tau_i=-i\sigma_i/2$ for rotations and $b_i=\sigma_i/2$ for boosts. Their real Lie brackets are $[\tau_i,\tau_j]=\epsilon_{ij}{}^k\tau_k$, $[\tau_i,b_j]=\epsilon_{ij}{}^kb_k$ and $[b_i,b_j]=-\epsilon_{ij}{}^k\tau_k$. Define action-valued charges

$$\begin{align}
\mathcal L_i=2\operatorname{Re}\operatorname{tr}(J\tau_i),\qquad \mathcal K_i=2\operatorname{Re}\operatorname{tr}(Jb_i).
\end{align}$$

They inherit the Lorentz Poisson algebra. If $v_i=\operatorname{tr}(J\sigma_i)$, then $v=\mathcal K+i\mathcal L$ and

$$\begin{align}
E^2=\mathcal K^2-\mathcal L^2+2i\mathcal K\cdot\mathcal L.
\end{align}$$

With a chosen timelike normal, a gravitational linear-simplicity convention is $\mathcal K=\gamma\mathcal L$. On a nonzero simple sector this gives $E=\pm(\gamma+i)|\mathcal L|$. In the selected time-gauge normalization take the spatial area flux as $\mathbf N=\kappa\gamma\mathcal L$. Matching then implies equal areas $\kappa|\gamma||\mathcal L|$ at the two ends. Without simplicity/normal data, equal complex Casimirs alone are not a normal-independent equality of rotation norms.

Equal area is still not equal shape. In an auxiliary Euclidean spatial frame, measure lengths in $\ell>0$. Consider the two tetrahedra with vertices

$$\begin{aligned}
T_1/\ell&=\{(0,0,0),(1,0,0),(0,1,0),(0,0,1)\},\\
T_2/\ell&=\{(0,0,0),(2,0,0),(0,\tfrac12,0),(0,0,-1)\}.
\end{aligned}$$

Their outward face-area vectors, divided by $\ell^2$, are respectively

$$\begin{aligned}
&\{(\tfrac12,\tfrac12,\tfrac12),(-\tfrac12,0,0), (0,-\tfrac12,0),(0,0,-\tfrac12)\},\\
&\{(\tfrac14,1,-\tfrac12),(-\tfrac14,0,0), (0,-1,0),(0,0,\tfrac12)\}.
\end{aligned}$$

Both sums vanish, both normal sets span three dimensions and both volumes are $\ell^3/6$. The base faces have area $\ell^2/2$ with opposite normals, satisfying the outward flux matching with identity transport. Yet their two-edge Gram matrices are

$$
G_1=\ell^2\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad
G_2=\ell^2\begin{pmatrix}4&0\\0&1/4\end{pmatrix}.
$$

Their determinants agree but traces and unordered edge lengths do not. The triangles cannot be glued isometrically by relabelling or rotation. Closure and a common face area/normal thus allow twisted, non-Regge data. Shape matching is additional information, not a deduction from the link phase-space dimension or a forced microscopic torsion defect.

## 4. Linear Simplicity Is Not a First-Class Quantum Projector

Write dimensionless quantum generators L,K; physical charges are $\hbar L,\hbar K$. On their common representation domain,

$$\begin{align}
[L_i,L_j]=i\epsilon_{ij}{}^kL_k,\quad [L_i,K_j]=i\epsilon_{ij}{}^kK_k,\quad [K_i,K_j]=-i\epsilon_{ij}{}^kL_k.
\end{align}$$

For $C_i=K_i-\gamma L_i$ this gives

$$\begin{align}
[C_i,C_j]=-i\epsilon_{ij}{}^k \bigl((1+\gamma^2)L_k+2\gamma C_k\bigr).
\end{align}$$

Classically the same expression without i is the Poisson bracket. On C=0 its matrix has rank two when L is nonzero. Thus the simplicity set contains second-class directions; the three conditions do not form a first-class gauge algebra there. Three antisymmetric brackets cannot all constitute a nondegenerate three-by-three second-class matrix either.

If all $C_i\psi=0$ strongly on a domain where these commutators apply, real gamma forces $L_i\psi=0$, and then $K_i\psi=0$. A nonzero solution must be a Lorentz-invariant vector, absent from a nontrivial unitary principal-series representation. Merely asking for zero matrix elements in a selected finite SU(2) subspace is a different prescription.

### A Fixed Principal-Series Convention

Take the standard unitary representation $\mathcal H_{\rho,k}=\widehat\bigoplus_{l=k}^{\infty}V_l$, where $\rho\in\mathbb R$, $k\ge0$ is a half-integer and l increases in integer steps. Its orthonormal basis is $|l,m\rangle$. This representation and its common smooth-vector domain are inputs, not consequences of a coordinate count. Rotations act by the usual angular-momentum matrices. Fix the boost sign by

$$\begin{aligned}
K_3|l,m\rangle={}&i a_l\sqrt{l^2-m^2}|l-1,m\rangle +\beta_l m|l,m\rangle\\
&-i a_{l+1}\sqrt{(l+1)^2-m^2}|l+1,m\rangle,\\
a_l^2={}&\frac{(l^2-k^2)(l^2+\rho^2)}{l^2(4l^2-1)}, \qquad \beta_l=\frac{k\rho}{l(l+1)}.
\end{aligned}$$

Use positive $a_l$ for l>k and set $a_k=0$ before evaluating its potentially singular formula. At l=k=0 set $\beta_0=0$. Define $K_+=-[L_+,K_3]$, $K_-=[L_-,K_3]$. The opposite imaginary off-diagonal signs give $K_3^\dagger=K_3$, and the standard representation fixes self-adjoint generators and unitary $D^{(\rho,k)}(g)$. Our Casimir sign is $\mathbf L\cdot\mathbf K=k\rho I$; reversing the boost convention would reverse the matching sign below.

Rotational covariance implies $P_l\mathbf KP_l=\beta_l\mathbf L$ on the unique spin-l block. Taking its scalar product with L and using the Casimir gives exactly the displayed beta. With the minimum spin l=k=j>0, the isometric inclusion $Y_j:V_j\to\mathcal H_{\rho,j}$ therefore obeys

$$\begin{align}
Y_j^\dagger\mathbf K Y_j=\frac\rho{j+1}\mathbf L, \qquad Y_j^\dagger(\mathbf K-\gamma\mathbf L)Y_j=0 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\rho=\gamma(j+1).
\end{align}$$

This is exact weak linear simplicity in the chosen convention. At j=0 it does not fix rho; we choose $(\rho,k)=(0,0)$ for that boundary label. Its minimum-spin vector is not a Lorentz-invariant vector.

The often-used alternative $\rho=\gamma j$ must not be asserted to satisfy this same exact equation. Define the spectral spin operator $\widehat\jmath=(\sqrt{I+4\mathbf L^2}-I)/2$. The two prescriptions

$$\begin{align}
\mathcal D_1=\mathbf L\cdot\mathbf K-\gamma\mathbf L^2, \qquad \mathcal D_0=\mathbf L\cdot\mathbf K-\gamma\widehat\jmath^{\,2}
\end{align}$$

differ by $\gamma\widehat\jmath$. On the minimum j block they give respectively $j\rho-\gamma j(j+1)$ and $j\rho-\gamma j^2$. Their roots are $\gamma(j+1)$ and $\gamma j$. These are different spectral/ordering choices, agreeing only in their leading large-j relation. Under our unshifted linear condition the alternative leaves the coefficient $-\gamma/(j+1)$ multiplying L. The label choice is not a unique EPRL theorem. Nor are Lorentzian principal-series labels a pair of finite Spin(4) spins: the Euclidean two-spin construction belongs to a different group/signature problem.

### Weak Constraints and Finite Boosts Have Real Defects

For k=1/2, rho=3/2 and gamma=1, the minimum block satisfies weak C=0, but $C_3|\tfrac12,\tfrac12\rangle=-i|\tfrac32,\tfrac12\rangle$ has norm one. It is not strongly annihilated. In that block $P[C_1,C_2]P=-2iPL_3P\ne0$, despite $PC_iP=0$. Compression does not preserve operator products.

Likewise a cutoff l at most lmax preserves rotations but removes boost excursions through lmax+1. If $K_i^{\rm cut}=P K_iP$, then

$$\begin{align}
[K_i^{\rm cut},K_j^{\rm cut}]+i\epsilon_{ij}{}^kPL_kP =-PK_i(I-P)K_jP+PK_j(I-P)K_iP.
\end{align}$$

The right side is supported at the highest retained block and is generally nonzero. Exact finite calculations below that boundary do not give a finite-dimensional unitary Lorentz representation. In particular one cannot use a cutoff's false full algebra to prove a physical constraint projector.

The standard compact boundary area prescription remains $a_0\sqrt{j(j+1)}$. Neither the weak Y map nor that kinematical spectrum supplies the gravitational Hamiltonian constraint or the physical Hilbert-space norm.

## 5. A Specified Gauge-Fixed Lorentzian Vertex

Consider the boundary graph of a four-simplex, with five nodes a=1,...,5. For a<b orient the propagator from b to a. Choose admissible spins $j_{ab}$ and normalized SU(2) invariant tensors at each node, using the dual spaces on incoming/outgoing legs. These are boundary spin-network data; arbitrary such data need not reconstruct a shape-matched Lorentzian simplex.

Choose spacelike boundary tetrahedra when making a geometric interpretation. Each has a local future unit timelike normal fixed to $n_0$; outward time orientation is separate sign data, not the assertion that all outward normals are future. Use the exact weak-label prescription above and write

$$\begin{align}
B_{ab}(g_a,g_b)=Y_{j_{ab}}^\dagger D^{(\rho_{ab},j_{ab})}(g_a^{-1}g_b)Y_{j_{ab}}.
\end{align}$$

This maps the b spin space to the a spin space. At node a the contracting tensor lies in the invariant part of the tensor product of dual V legs for b>a and V legs for b<a. Let $\mathcal B_i(\{B_{ab}\})$ mean the explicit tensor-network evaluation: multiply all ten matrices and sum each boundary magnetic index against its chosen invariant tensor, pairing each oriented V leg with its dual. This fixes the boundary contraction and its normalization; it is a finite sum, not an unspecified functional measure.

Fix one Haar measure dg on $SL(2,\mathbb C)$; its overall constant is a convention, and its total volume is infinite. The relative matrices are invariant under common left multiplication of all five g's. Remove that redundant volume by setting $g_5=I$. With

$$\begin{align}
\mathcal C_\Lambda=\{g:\operatorname{tr}(g^\dagger g)\le2\cosh\Lambda\},\qquad\Lambda>0,
\end{align}$$

define the regulated vertex

$$\begin{align}
A_{v,\Lambda}(j,i)= \int_{\mathcal C_\Lambda^4}\prod_{a=1}^4dg_a\, \mathcal B_i(\{B_{ab}(g_a,g_b)\}).
\end{align}$$

The cutoff set is compact. Unitarity of D and isometry of Y bound each matrix entry by one; finite boundary contraction bounds the integrand by a finite constant depending on the chosen data. Thus the fixed-label regulated integral exists. It uses a chosen root frame; regulator-independent Lorentz covariance and the removal of Lambda require separate arguments. No unrestricted convergence theorem is inferred from compact normalization.

For a fixed finite two-complex one may now **choose** face weights $w_f=2j_f+1$, edge weights one in the normalized intertwiner basis, a finite half-integer spin cutoff J with $0\le j\le J$,  and contract the corresponding vertex amplitudes with fixed boundary tensors. This is a finite regulated amplitude prescription. Different face/edge weights, measures or spectral simplicity orderings define different prescriptions. No continuum Einstein–Cartan path-integral derivation is claimed by writing this product.

Y is an SU(2) intertwiner, not a Lorentz intertwiner onto an invariant subspace. Accordingly $Y^\dagger D(g_1)Y\,Y^\dagger D(g_2)Y$ need not equal $Y^\dagger D(g_1g_2)Y$: an intermediate projector has been inserted. The explicit boost leakage above already shows why. A vertex amplitude is therefore not automatically an idempotent physical projector, a transfer unitary or a proof of canonical/covariant equivalence.

## 6. An Evaluated Covariant recoupling/gluing Control

A smaller model lets us test an actual covariant sum rather than merely name one. Use **auxiliary three-dimensional SU(2) BF theory**, not Lorentzian four-dimensional gravity. On a finite dual complex choose normalized compact Haar measures, ordered face holonomies $G_f$, fixed boundary spin-network polynomial $\Psi_\partial$ and the regulator

$$\begin{align}
Z_{J,\Delta}[\Psi_\partial] =\int\prod_{e^*}dg_{e^*}\,\Psi_\partial \prod_{f^*}\delta_J(G_{f^*}),\qquad \delta_J(g)=\sum_{j\le J}(2j+1)\chi_j(g).
\end{align}$$

This finite integral is defined by its displayed measure/weights; it is the regulated flatness amplitude associated with the continuum BF constraint F=0. It is not a positive statistical distribution or a regulated four-dimensional EC action. No normalization of a noncompact Haar measure is hidden here.

Character expansion and compact Haar invariant-tensor integration give a tetrahedral Racah 6j contraction. Fix its standard real Racah phase and normalized boundary recoupling states. With four exterior spins all one, the intermediate channels are a,b=0,1,2. Put

$$
F_{ab}=\sqrt{(2a+1)(2b+1)}
 \begin{Bmatrix}1&1&a\\1&1&b\end{Bmatrix}.
$$

The all-six-spin-one normalized tetrahedral contraction has the Racah vertex coefficient shown below. The same factorial sum gives the genuinely evaluated recoupling matrix

$$
F=\begin{pmatrix}
1/3&-\sqrt3/3&\sqrt5/3\\
-\sqrt3/3&1/2&\sqrt{15}/6\\
\sqrt5/3&\sqrt{15}/6&1/6
\end{pmatrix},\qquad
\begin{Bmatrix}1&1&1\\1&1&1\end{Bmatrix}=\frac16.
$$

The same numbers test gluing with its indispensable dimension weight:

$$
FF^{\mathsf T}=I_3,\qquad
\sum_{x=0}^2(2x+1)
 \begin{Bmatrix}1&1&a\\1&1&x\end{Bmatrix}
 \begin{Bmatrix}1&1&b\\1&1&x\end{Bmatrix}
 =\frac{\delta_{ab}}{2a+1}.
$$

At J at least two, this local control retains every admissible channel. If x=2 is omitted, the defect of the normalized identity is $F_{\cdot2}F_{\cdot2}^{\mathsf T}$, of rank one, with its 00 entry equal to 5/9. Gluing is not recovered by ignoring the missing channel or dimension weights. These are exact evaluated recoupling data, not an unevaluated spin-foam symbol.

A general finite J can still break identities requiring omitted channels, and redundant flatness constraints can diverge as the regulator is removed. This local calculation proves neither full refinement invariance nor convergence of all bulk sums. It tests compact BF contraction and boundary normalization only; it does not evaluate the Lorentzian four-simplex integral or establish its gravitational meaning.

## 7. What the Constructions Do and Do Not Connect

The canonical step supplied a real symplectic potential, actual Lorentz moment maps, a regular cotangent map and its necessary discrete/stratum qualifications. The shape example showed that area and closure leave genuine metric-matching data unfixed. The representation step derived weak simplicity labels from a stated boost coefficient and spectral prescription, with explicit nonzero leakage and cutoff controls. The covariant step defined a regulated Lorentzian amplitude and separately evaluated an auxiliary BF gluing calculation.

These are consequential constructions, but not a complete EC quantum theory. The torsion/matter action, fermionic canonical reduction and measure, scalar/diffeomorphism constraints, a physical inner product and the relation between boundary amplitudes and those constraints remain additional work. The [EC-08 clock model](<EC-08.md#L144-L178>) illustrates why a physical norm and clock branch require their own construction; neither an isometric Y nor a coordinate change supplies them.

No generic curved-spacetime Penrose incidence, universal Immirzi value, fermion non-doubling result, four-dimensional semiclassical Regge limit, continuum/refinement convergence or canonical/covariant physical equivalence is derived here. In particular a quantum Gauss invariant is not forced to have a residual torsion defect merely because classical area ratios can be irrational. Quantization, constraints and geometric interpretation must retain their separate hypotheses.
