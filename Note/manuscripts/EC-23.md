# Standard Model Superfields and a Controlled Supersymmetric Extension

DRAFT — a chirality-explicit N=1 MSSM construction, component actions, anomaly and vacuum calibrations. Bounded independent review recorded.

A supersymmetric extension of the Standard Model is not a change of notation for its measured particle spectrum. We construct a specified flat-space minimal supersymmetric Standard Model (MSSM): the Standard Model gauge pattern is an input, while scalar partners, gauginos, two Higgs multiplets and Higgsinos are genuine additional fields. Its couplings and symmetry-breaking data are not fixed by twistor coordinates. The constructive results below are an actual graded component action, a complete charge-consistency calculation with a one-Higgsino countermodel, and an evaluated Higgs-sector mass/vacuum example.

## 1. Physical Chirality and the Multiplets Being Chosen

Work on oriented Minkowski space with $\eta=\operatorname{diag}(-1,1,1,1)$, $\varepsilon_{0123}=+1$ and $c=\hbar=1$. Coordinates have length units. The fixed Dirac dictionary is

$$\begin{align}
\{\gamma^a,\gamma^b\}=-2\eta^{ab},\qquad \gamma_5=\operatorname{diag}(-I,+I),\qquad \rho(A)=\operatorname{diag}((A^\dagger)^{-1},A). \tag{1.1}
\end{align}$$

Thus the fundamental unprimed upper space $S_+$ is **positive** chirality. The physical all-left fermions in the Standard Model table occupy the negative block $S_-$, not an unannounced renaming of $S_+$. We choose book-chiral multiplets with $S_+$ fermions and **conjugate internal representations**. This implements the same physical chiral gauge pattern after conjugation, not an extra mirror family.

For the weak doublet define an internal numerical matrix $J_w=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&1\\-1&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)$. It obeys $J_w\overline U=UJ_w$ for $U\in SU(2)$. Let $\epsilon_L$ have the same numerical entries on Lorentz spin labels; it is a different tensor acting on a different factor. Lorentz raising uses $(\epsilon_L^{AB})=\epsilon_L^{-1}=-\epsilon_L$, and $\mathcal W_A=(\epsilon_L)_{AB}\mathcal W^B$. A definite physical-component readout is

$$
\begin{aligned}
\chi_{\rm phys}&=i\epsilon_L\psi_{\rm book}^*,&
\varphi_{\rm phys}&=\varphi_{\rm book}^*
&&\text{for weak singlets},\\
\chi_{\rm phys}&=(i\epsilon_L\otimes J_w)\psi_{\rm book}^*,&
\varphi_{\rm phys}&=J_w\varphi_{\rm book}^*
&&\text{for weak doublets}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{1.2}
$$

Color indices are conjugated in these formulas. The identity $\epsilon_L\overline A=(A^\dagger)^{-1}\epsilon_L$ gives the negative-block Lorentz transformation. The factor i fixes a fermion-bilinear phase: with $\psi\xi=\psi^0\xi^1-\psi^1\xi^0$, the corresponding negative-block product, with internal indices held fixed, satisfies $\chi_i\chi_j=(\psi_i\psi_j)^\dagger$. This is a stated phase change from the free Dirac packaging $\chi=-\epsilon_L\overline\psi$ in [AST-15](<AST-15.md>); its mass parameter must not be imported without that phase translation.

Use $G=SU(3)_c\times SU(2)_w\times U(1)$ as the chosen global product. Display conventional $Y$, but take the compact Abelian character to be the integer $q=6Y$ with coupling $g_Y/6$. A quotient by a common center is additional global input.

| Physical all-left component representation | Book-chiral multiplet and representation |
|---|---|
| $Q:(\mathbf3,\mathbf2,1/6)$ | $\widehat Q:(\overline{\mathbf3},\mathbf2,-1/6)$ |
| $U^c:(\overline{\mathbf3},\mathbf1,-2/3)$ | $\widehat U:(\mathbf3,\mathbf1,2/3)$ |
| $D^c:(\overline{\mathbf3},\mathbf1,1/3)$ | $\widehat D:(\mathbf3,\mathbf1,-1/3)$ |
| $L:(\mathbf1,\mathbf2,-1/2)$ | $\widehat L:(\mathbf1,\mathbf2,1/2)$ |
| $E^c:(\mathbf1,\mathbf1,1)$ | $\widehat E:(\mathbf1,\mathbf1,-1)$ |
| $H_u:(\mathbf1,\mathbf2,1/2)$ | $\widehat H_u:(\mathbf1,\mathbf2,-1/2)$ |
| $H_d:(\mathbf1,\mathbf2,-1/2)$ | $\widehat H_d:(\mathbf1,\mathbf2,1/2)$ |

The weak entries in the second column have already used $J_w:\overline{\mathbf2}\to\mathbf2$. There are three chosen matter generations $I=1,2,3$ and one of each Higgs multiplet. $U^c,D^c,E^c$ mean left-handed conjugates of right-handed physical singlets, not extra singlet species. The two Higgs multiplets are independent: $\widehat H_u^\dagger$ is antichiral, so it is not $\widehat H_d$ merely because their hypercharges are opposite.

Every even chiral multiplet has a complex scalar $\varphi$, a two-component odd $\psi\in S_+$ and a complex auxiliary $F$. Thus matter has squark/slepton partners and the Higgs fields have Higgsino partners. Each gauge factor has a real vector superfield with $(A_\mu^a,\lambda^{aA},D^a)$: adjoint gauginos and real auxiliary D fields accompany the gauge bosons. The adjoints are real and have $Y=0$. Conjugate fields are not counted a second time. F and D have no independent kinetic term below; they are not additional asymptotic particles. Matching component counts would not prove any of these interactions or a CPT theorem.

## 2. Genuine Graded Superspace and the Gauge Action

The real flat superspace has dimension $4|4$. In its complexified calculation $\theta_A,\overline\theta_{A'}$ are four independent odd generators; the real structure relates the barred pair by order-reversing dagger. Fermionic component fields anticommute with them. All odd derivatives are left derivatives. Adopt

$$\begin{align}
\theta^2=2\theta_0\theta_1,\qquad \overline\theta^2=-2\overline\theta_{0'}\overline\theta_{1'},\qquad \int d^2\theta\,\theta^2= \int d^2\overline\theta\,\overline\theta^2=1. \tag{2.1}
\end{align}$$

Here $[\theta]=L^{1/2}$. The full measure extracts $-1/4$ times the ordered $\theta_0\theta_1\overline\theta_{0'}\overline\theta_{1'}$ coefficient; the integral of a constant is zero. This is not evaluation at zero of commuting coordinates.

Put $s^\mu=(I,\sigma_i)$ and $t^\mu=(I,-\sigma_i)$. With $T^{AA'}=(t^\mu)^{AA'}\partial_\mu$, the actual operators are

$$
\begin{aligned}
Q^A&=i\partial^L_{\theta_A}-\overline\theta_{A'}T^{AA'},&
\overline Q^{A'}&=-i\partial^L_{\overline\theta_{A'}}+\theta_AT^{AA'},\\
D^A&=i\partial^L_{\theta_A}+\overline\theta_{A'}T^{AA'},&
\overline D^{A'}&=-i\partial^L_{\overline\theta_{A'}}-\theta_AT^{AA'}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{2.2}
$$

They give $\{Q^A,\overline Q^{A'}\}=2iT^{AA'}=-2(t^\mu)^{AA'}P_\mu$ for $P_\mu=-i\partial_\mu$, and $\{D^A,\overline D^{A'}\}=-2iT^{AA'}$. Same-type brackets vanish, translations commute with the charges, and charges anticommute with chirality derivatives. N=1 counts one minimal four-real-charge structure; it does not count directions in a two-dimensional spinor space or generations of matter.

The chiral constraint and its component solution are

$$\begin{align}
\overline D^{A'}\widehat\Phi=0,\qquad \widehat\Phi(y,\theta)=\varphi(y)+\sqrt2\theta_A\psi^A(y)+\theta^2F(y), \qquad y^\mu=x^\mu-i\theta t^\mu\overline\theta. \tag{2.3}
\end{align}$$

To make (1.2) fully explicit, introduce $\zeta=i\epsilon_L\overline\theta$. Then $\zeta^2=\overline\theta^2$ and, before the weak intertwiner, with components evaluated at $\overline y^\mu=x^\mu+i\theta t^\mu\overline\theta$,

$$\begin{align}
\widehat\Phi^\dagger =\varphi^*+\sqrt2\zeta^T\chi+\zeta^2F^*,\qquad \chi=i\epsilon_L\psi^*. \tag{2.4}
\end{align}$$

Here $\zeta\mapsto\overline A\zeta$ and $\chi\mapsto\overline A^{-T}\chi$, so the displayed coordinate–field pairing is Lorentz invariant. These physical all-left variables are **antichiral in the book convention**; zeta is not another independent supersymmetry. Apply the weak intertwiner to the entire doublet multiplet, including F. A complex representation and its Hermitian conjugate describe one field and its adjoint, not two independent chiral multiplets.

For Hermitian gauge generators use $\operatorname{tr}(T_aT_b)=\delta_{ab}/2$ in the non-Abelian fundamentals and

$$\begin{align}
\mathcal D_\mu=\partial_\mu-i\sum_r g_r A^a_{\mu r}T_{ar},\qquad \mathcal F_{\mu\nu r}=\partial_\mu A_{\nu r}-\partial_\nu A_{\mu r} -ig_r[A_{\mu r},A_{\nu r}]. \tag{2.5}
\end{align}$$

The sum runs over color, weak and Abelian factors with $g_r=(g_s,g,g_Y)$ and the appropriate Y generator. Before an intertwiner is applied, conjugate-representation generators are $-T_{a,\rm physical}^*$. For the weak fundamentals already identified by $J_w$, they instead read $J_w(-T_i^*)J_w^{-1}=T_i$. The color conjugates in the table are not so identified. These gauge indices never replace Lorentz indices.

A useful Wess–Zumino gauge convention is, with $B_\theta^\mu=\theta t^\mu\overline\theta$,

$$\begin{align}
V^a=-B_\theta^\mu A^a_\mu +\theta^2\overline\theta_{A'}\overline\lambda^{aA'} -\overline\theta^2\theta_A\lambda^{aA} +\tfrac12\theta^2\overline\theta^2D^a. \tag{2.6}
\end{align}$$

It is Hermitian under the stated order reversal. The gaugino phase in this formula is part of the convention. Supersymmetry in this gauge includes a compensating gauge transformation; the ungauge-fixed superfield description avoids confusing that compensation with a failure of the algebra.

For $g_r\ne0$ define the normalized chiral field strength by

$$\begin{align}
\mathcal W_r^A=-\frac{i}{8g_r}\overline D^2 \left(e^{-2g_rV_r}D^A e^{2g_rV_r}\right),\qquad \overline D^2=\overline D^{0'}\overline D^{1'}- \overline D^{1'}\overline D^{0'}. \tag{2.7}
\end{align}$$

Its component expression is polynomial and extends to the free-coupling limit. In the Abelian case $\mathcal W^A=i(-\overline D^2/4)D^AV$. For a chiral parameter $\Lambda$, take $\Phi\mapsto e^{-i\Lambda}\Phi$ and $e^{2gV}\mapsto e^{-i\Lambda^\dagger}e^{2gV}e^{i\Lambda}$; the strength transforms adjointly. These are the ordinary constrained flat gauge-superspace identities, not an arbitrary curved Einstein–Cartan construction.

The specified canonical action is

$$\begin{align}
S_{\rm susy}=\int d^4x\left[ \int d^4\theta\sum_f\widehat\Phi_f^\dagger e^{2\sum_r g_rV_r^aT_{ar}}\widehat\Phi_f +\left\{\int d^2\theta\left( \mathscr W_B+\frac14\sum_{r,a}\mathcal W_r^{aA}\mathcal W^a_{rA} \right)+\text{h.c.}\right\}\right]. \tag{2.8}
\end{align}$$

The Abelian kinetic field strength has unit generator here; Y occurs in the matter exponential. Non-Abelian matrix notation gives the equivalent coefficient $\tfrac12\operatorname{tr}(\mathcal W^A\mathcal W_A)$. We choose constant real gauge-kinetic coefficients, no Fayet–Iliopoulos term and no theta-angle term. More general choices are additional models. The full spacetime integral is invariant under rigid supersymmetry when boundary fluxes vanish; a Berezin integral at a fixed x need not itself have zero variation. We use sufficient falloff and compactly supported variations, or retain the corresponding boundary fluxes.

## 3. Holomorphic Interactions and Their Actual Components

For weak columns write $X\cdot Y=X^TJ_wY$. Because chiral superfields are even, $X\cdot Y=-Y\cdot X$. Color contracts the conjugate fundamental with the fundamental. Define physical-reference Yukawa matrices $Y_u,Y_d,Y_e$ and mass $\mu$ as free parameters. In the book variables choose

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathscr W_B={}&
\widehat U_I^a(Y_u^*)_{IJ}(\widehat Q_{Ja}\cdot\widehat H_u)
-\widehat D_I^a(Y_d^*)_{IJ}(\widehat Q_{Ja}\cdot\widehat H_d)\\
&-\widehat E_I(Y_e^*)_{IJ}(\widehat L_J\cdot\widehat H_d)
+\mu^*\widehat H_u\cdot\widehat H_d.
\end{aligned}
\tag{3.1}
$$

A conjugated constant does not spoil holomorphy; a conjugated field would. Under (1.2) and (2.4), its adjoint is the conventional physical-component polynomial

$$\begin{align}
\mathscr W_{\rm phys} = u_I^c(Y_u)_{IJ}(q_J\cdot h_u) -d_I^c(Y_d)_{IJ}(q_J\cdot h_d) -e_I^c(Y_e)_{IJ}(\ell_J\cdot h_d)+\mu h_u\cdot h_d. \tag{3.2}
\end{align}$$

The fields in (3.2) are the physical scalar components, not newly declared book-chiral fields of negative chirality. The equality follows from the two weak intertwiners in every term: $(J_w\overline X)\cdot(J_w\overline Y)=\overline{X\cdot Y}$. Their inverse maps have two compensating minus signs. The fermionic phase in (1.2) fixes the same adjoint relation for Yukawa bilinears.

Each term is gauge invariant. For example $1/6+1/2-2/3=0$, $1/6-1/2+1/3=0$ and $-1/2-1/2+1=0$ in physical variables; all signs reverse in book variables. Representation products $\mathbf3\otimes\overline{\mathbf3}=\mathbf1\oplus\mathbf8$ and $\mathbf2\otimes\mathbf2=\mathbf1\oplus\mathbf3$ exhibit the contracted singlets. The gauge contractions are intertwiners, while Yukawa matrices are flavor tensor coefficients, not invariant matrices for all independent flavor groups or Lorentz matrices. With flavor columns $u^c\mapsto U_Uu^c$, $q\mapsto U_Qq$, their basis law is

$$\begin{align}
Y_u\mapsto U_U^{-T}Y_uU_Q^{-1}, \tag{3.3}
\end{align}$$

and similarly for down quarks and leptons. Canonical kinetic terms allow unitary flavor basis changes, but fixed generic Yukawa matrices break the corresponding interaction symmetries. Singular values and relative mixing data remain parameters, not predictions of the representation dimensions.

For ordinary chiral component labels i,j, including internal and flavor components, actual ordered Grassmann multiplication gives

$$\begin{align}
\int d^2\theta\,\mathscr W_B(\Phi) =W_iF_i-\frac12W_{ij}\psi_i\psi_j,\qquad W_i=\frac{\partial\mathscr W_B}{\partial\varphi_i},\quad W_{ij}=\frac{\partial^2\mathscr W_B}{\partial\varphi_i\partial\varphi_j}. \tag{3.4}
\end{align}$$

Here $\psi_i\psi_j=\psi_i^0\psi_j^1-\psi_i^1\psi_j^0$ is symmetric in i,j because the components are odd. For a cubic monomial $yABC$, (3.4) is explicitly

$$\begin{align}
y\left(F_A B C+A F_B C+A B F_C -C\psi_A\psi_B-B\psi_A\psi_C-A\psi_B\psi_C\right).
\tag{3.5}
\end{align}$$

This calculation, not an equal boson/fermion count, produces the interaction coefficients. The gauge D term similarly produces $F_i^*F_i$, $g_rD_r^a\varphi^\dagger T_{ar}\varphi$ and, in the gaugino phase of (2.6), $+\sqrt2g_r\varphi^\dagger T_{ar}(\lambda_r^a\psi)+\text{h.c.}$.

For completeness the actual Abelian vector calibration in chiral coordinates is

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal W^A&=\lambda^A+
\left[D I+i\boldsymbol\sigma\cdot(\mathbf E-i\mathbf B)\right]^A{}_B\theta^B
+\theta^2\rho^A,\\
\rho&=i\epsilon_L^{-1}(s^\mu)^T\partial_\mu\overline\lambda,
\qquad E_i=\mathcal F_{0i},\quad B_i=\tfrac12\varepsilon_{ijk}\mathcal F_{jk}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{3.6}
$$

The raised theta is $\theta^A=\epsilon_L^{AB}\theta_B$. In the matrix expression for rho, barred components are a column. Independent first jets in all four directions verify this formula without fermion equations of motion. Its chiral integral plus adjoint is

$$\begin{align}
-\frac14\mathcal F_{\mu\nu}\mathcal F^{\mu\nu} +\frac i2\left[\lambda^\dagger s^\mu\partial_\mu\lambda -(\partial_\mu\lambda^\dagger)s^\mu\lambda\right] +\frac12D^2.
\tag{3.7}
\end{align}$$

The non-Abelian prepotential supplies the adjoint covariant derivative and commutator curvature. A separate noncommuting constant weak-gauge calibration verifies both that curvature and the trace normalization. Lorentz Pauli matrices and weak-generator Pauli matrices in that calculation act on distinct tensor factors.

Combining these terms gives, up to the stated spacetime divergences, canonical scalar kinetic terms $-(\mathcal D_\mu\varphi)^\dagger\mathcal D^\mu\varphi$, the corresponding symmetrized $S_+$ fermion kinetic terms with $s^\mu$, gauge kinetic terms (3.7), the gaugino vertices, the Yukawa term $-W_{ij}\psi_i\psi_j/2+\text{h.c.}$ from (3.4), and

$$\begin{align}
\mathscr L_{\rm aux}= \sum_i\left(F_i^*F_i+W_iF_i+\overline W_iF_i^*\right) +\frac12\sum_{r,a}(D_r^a)^2+\sum_{r,a}D_r^aJ_r^a, \qquad J_r^a=g_r\sum_f\varphi_f^\dagger T_{ar}\varphi_f. \tag{3.8}
\end{align}$$

Vary F and its conjugate independently before imposing the real structure. Completing squares gives

$$\begin{align}
F_i=-\overline W_i,\qquad D_r^a=-J_r^a,\qquad V=V_F+V_D=\sum_i|W_i|^2+\frac12\sum_{r,a}(J_r^a)^2. \tag{3.9}
\end{align}$$

This nonnegative potential statement assumes the chosen global canonical Kähler metric and unbroken rigid action; it is not a general supergravity potential formula. In length units $[\varphi]=L^{-1}$, $[\psi]=[\lambda]=L^{-3/2}$, $[F]=[D]=L^{-2}$ and $[\mathscr W]=L^{-3}$. The measures have units $[d^2\theta]=L^{-1}$ and $[d^4\theta]=L^{-2}$, so every component density has units $L^{-4}$.

## 4. Charge Consistency and the Need for Two Higgs Multiplets

Holomorphy already distinguishes the model from the nonsupersymmetric Standard Model. With only physical $H_u$ of $Y=1/2$, the charge sums for $D^cQH_u$ and $E^cLH_u$ are both one, not zero. In book variables the corresponding wrong-Higgs sums are $-1$. Replacing $\widehat H_u$ by its weak-intertwined adjoint repairs the charge, but that adjoint is generically antichiral and cannot enter $\mathscr W_B$. A constant vacuum value does not change the chirality requirement on fluctuations. An independent book-chiral $\widehat H_d$ permits both renormalizable holomorphic down/lepton couplings. This is not permission to count $H_u$ and its adjoint as two independent Higgsinos.

The anomaly calculation supplies a second, independent consistency test. Use the standard four-dimensional chiral anomaly formulas, with $A(\mathbf3)=1$, $A(\overline{\mathbf3})=-1$ and $T(\mathbf3)=T(\mathbf2)=1/2$. For one physical all-left species in $(R_c,R_w,Y)$ its coefficient vector is

$$\begin{align}
\mathcal A_f= \left(d_w A_c,\ d_wT_cY,\ d_cT_wY,\ d_cd_wY^3,\ d_cd_wY\right), \tag{4.1}
\end{align}$$

in the order $SU(3)^3,SU(3)^2Y,SU(2)^2Y,Y^3,\mathrm{grav}^2Y$. The color dimension must not be multiplied into the already normalized cubic color index. A book $S_+$ fermion contributes the **negative** of (4.1) evaluated on its book representation. Conjugating the representation reverses the relevant indices/charges, giving the same physical coefficient species by species. Merely finding a zero total would not test this chirality dictionary.

For a matter generation the five sums are

$$
\begin{aligned}
&2-1-1=0,\\
&2(1/6)(1/2)+(-2/3)(1/2)+(1/3)(1/2)=0,\\
&3(1/6)(1/2)+(-1/2)(1/2)=0,\\
&6(1/6)^3+3(-2/3)^3+3(1/3)^3+2(-1/2)^3+1=0,\\
&6(1/6)+3(-2/3)+3(1/3)+2(-1/2)+1=0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4.2}
$$

The local cubic weak anomaly vanishes because the doublet is pseudoreal; triangles with one traceless non-Abelian generator vanish as well. Real adjoint gauginos have zero cubic indices and zero Y. Scalars and auxiliary fields are not added to these fermionic sums.

The Higgsino pair contributes opposite local vectors and cancels. Keeping only the physical $H_u$ Higgsino would leave

$$\begin{align}
\mathcal A_{\widetilde H_u}=(0,0,1/4,1/4,1), \tag{4.3}
\end{align}$$

so it is an actual anomalous countermodel, not an alternative minimal supersymmetric completion with the same quantum consistency.

For the separate standard $SU(2)$ global-anomaly criterion on spin four-manifolds, color supplies three Q doublets and L one per generation. There are $12+2=14$ fundamental Weyl doublets in this MSSM. The weak adjoint gaugino contributes $2T(\mathbf3)=4$, even. With only one Higgsino there would be thirteen fundamental doublets, odd, so that countermodel fails the global test too. The bosonic Higgs of the ordinary one-Higgs Standard Model has no such fermionic anomaly.

These cancellations already hold per matter generation. They do not select three generations or determine the gauge group. They use the standard anomaly-index formulas and global criterion; they do not classify every global quotient/bundle or prove ultraviolet completeness, supersymmetry Ward identities at every regulator, or quantum gravity.

## 5. A Derived Higgs Potential and a Checked Vacuum

We now use the **physical scalar components**

$$\begin{align}
h_u=(h_u^+,h_u^0)^T,\qquad h_d=(h_d^0,h_d^-)^T. \tag{5.1}
\end{align}$$

They are related by $h=J_w\varphi_{\rm book}^*$, not a relabelling of the book hypercharges. For real neutral vacuum values this means $\varphi_{\widehat H_u}=(-v_u/\sqrt2,0)^T$ and $\varphi_{\widehat H_d}=(0,v_d/\sqrt2)^T$.

Set other scalar components to zero. Derivatives of the mu term and the Pauli completeness identity give

$$\begin{align}
V_F=|\mu|^2(|h_u|^2+|h_d|^2),\qquad V_D=\frac{g^2+g_Y^2}{8}(|h_u|^2-|h_d|^2)^2 +\frac{g^2}{2}|h_u^\dagger h_d|^2. \tag{5.2}
\end{align}$$

For example $\sum_i(h_u^\dagger\sigma_i h_u+h_d^\dagger\sigma_i h_d)^2=(|h_u|^2-|h_d|^2)^2+4|h_u^\dagger h_d|^2$. Thus the Higgs quartic is fixed by the gauge couplings in this particular canonical model, not an arbitrary Standard Model quartic. For nonzero mu the supersymmetric Higgs-only minimum is the origin; no measured electroweak scale has been derived.

To obtain a phenomenological breaking sector, add **extra soft inputs**. The usual allowed choices include adjoint gaugino Majorana masses, Hermitian scalar mass-squared matrices and gauge-invariant scalar trilinears with the Yukawa tensor structure, but not necessarily Yukawa alignment. Their values and a supersymmetry-breaking mechanism are not supplied by the graded coordinates. For the Higgs example take

$$\begin{align}
V_{\rm soft}=m_{u,\rm soft}^2|h_u|^2+m_{d,\rm soft}^2|h_d|^2 +(b\,h_u\cdot h_d+\text{h.c.}),\qquad m_1^2=|\mu|^2+m_{d,\rm soft}^2,\quad m_2^2=|\mu|^2+m_{u,\rm soft}^2. \tag{5.3}
\end{align}$$

Mu is a supersymmetric mass, whereas b and the displayed soft masses are breaking parameters. Choose a CP-conserving neutral Higgs sector with real $b>0$, $v_d,v_u>0$ and $\tan\beta=v_u/v_d$. The neutral potential is

$$\begin{align}
V_0=\frac12m_1^2v_d^2+\frac12m_2^2v_u^2-bv_uv_d +\frac{g_Z^2}{32}(v_u^2-v_d^2)^2,\qquad g_Z^2=g^2+g_Y^2. \tag{5.4}
\end{align}$$

Stationarity, with $v^2=v_u^2+v_d^2$ and $m_Z^2=g_Z^2v^2/4$, gives

$$\begin{align}
m_1^2=b\tan\beta-\tfrac12m_Z^2\cos2\beta,\qquad m_2^2=b\cot\beta+\tfrac12m_Z^2\cos2\beta,\qquad m_A^2=\frac{2b}{\sin2\beta}. \tag{5.5}
\end{align}$$

Write $h_d^0=(v_d+s_d+ia_d)/\sqrt2$ and $h_u^0=(v_u+s_u+ia_u)/\sqrt2$. The actual second derivative in $(s_d,s_u)$ is

$$
M_{\rm even}^2=\begin{pmatrix}
m_A^2\sin^2\beta+m_Z^2\cos^2\beta&-(m_A^2+m_Z^2)\sin\beta\cos\beta\\
-(m_A^2+m_Z^2)\sin\beta\cos\beta&m_A^2\cos^2\beta+m_Z^2\sin^2\beta
\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{5.6}
$$

Its trace is $m_A^2+m_Z^2$ and determinant $m_A^2m_Z^2\cos^22\beta$. The odd neutral matrix is $m_A^2\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\sin^2\beta&\sin\beta\cos\beta\\\sin\beta\cos\beta&\cos^2\beta\end{smallmatrix}\right)$, with one neutral Goldstone and eigenvalue $m_A^2$. The charged sector has two real Goldstone directions and $m_{H^\pm}^2=m_A^2+m_W^2$, where $m_W^2=g^2v^2/4$.

The smaller even eigenvalue is

$$\begin{align}
m_h^2=\frac12\left[m_A^2+m_Z^2- \sqrt{(m_A^2+m_Z^2)^2-4m_A^2m_Z^2\cos^22\beta}\right]. \tag{5.7}
\end{align}$$

A short proof of the tree bound uses the normalized direction $(\cos\beta,\sin\beta)$: its Rayleigh value in (5.6) is $m_Z^2\cos^22\beta$, because the $m_A^2$ contribution cancels. The smallest-eigenvalue variational principle therefore gives

$$\begin{align}
m_h^2\le m_Z^2\cos^22\beta\le m_Z^2. \tag{5.8}
\end{align}$$

This is a **tree-level MSSM** bound, not a 125 GeV prediction. Relating the model to the observed Higgs mass requires radiative corrections and additional measured/coupling/soft-spectrum data, such as the stop sector. No loop spectrum, fit or unique geometric mass selection is computed here.

An exact nonzero calibration makes the vacuum statement concrete. In an arbitrary mass unit $M_0>0$ take $g^2=g_Y^2=1$, $(v_d,v_u)=(1,2)M_0$, $b=2M_0^2$, $\mu=M_0$. Equations (5.5) give $(m_1^2,m_2^2)=(19/4,1/4)M_0^2$, so $(m_{d,\rm soft}^2,m_{u,\rm soft}^2)=(15/4,-3/4)M_0^2$. The full eight-real-field Hessian has spectrum

$$\begin{align}
M_0^2\left\{0,0,0,\frac{15-3\sqrt{17}}4, \frac{15+3\sqrt{17}}4,5,\frac{25}4,\frac{25}4\right\}.
\tag{5.9}
\end{align}$$

Its three zero vectors are actual infinitesimal broken gauge orbits, checked by applying the Hessian, not inferred from a count. All five remaining eigenvalues are positive. The Higgs D-flat boundedness inequality $m_1^2+m_2^2>2|b|$ and origin instability $m_1^2m_2^2<|b|^2$ hold at this point. This establishes a Higgs-sector local minimum modulo gauge, not stability against every squark/slepton direction or a unique global MSSM vacuum.

\mathcal M_u=\frac{v_u}{\sqrt2}Y_u,\qquad \mathcal M_d=\frac{v_d}{\sqrt2}Y_d,\qquad \mathcal M_e=\frac{v_d}{\sqrt2}Y_e. \tag{5.10}

## 6. Matter Parity Is a Choice, and Vacua Need Not Be Unique

The displayed renormalizable superpotential is selected by an extra matter parity

$$\begin{align}
M=(-1)^{3(B-L)}:\qquad M(Q,U^c,D^c,L,E^c)=-1,\quad M(H_u,H_d,V_r)=+1. \tag{6.1}
\end{align}$$

Conjugating B and L does not change this sign in the book variables. Matter parity acts on the entire multiplet while leaving theta fixed. Component R parity is $R_p=M(-1)^{2s}$, equivalently combining this transformation with $\theta\mapsto-\theta$: a Standard Model fermion is Grassmann-odd but $R_p=+1$, whereas its scalar partner is Grassmann-even but $R_p=-1$. Higgsinos and gauginos also have $R_p=-1$. Thus neither parity is simply Grassmann degree or a consequence of the supersymmetry algebra.

Without the extra selection, gauge invariance permits additional holomorphic book terms whose physical-reference polynomials are

$$\begin{align}
\tfrac12\lambda_{IJK}L_I\cdot L_J E_K^c, \quad\lambda'_{IJK}L_I\cdot Q_J D_K^c, \quad\tfrac12\lambda''_{IJK}\epsilon^{abc}U^c_{Ia}D^c_{Jb}D^c_{Kc}, \quad\mu_I L_I\cdot H_u.
\tag{6.2}
\end{align}$$

This is physical-reference notation, related to the book by the same conjugation dictionary. The first flavor tensor is antisymmetric in I,J and the third in J,K, since the even chiral multiplets commute but the contracted weak/color tensors are antisymmetric. These operators are matter-parity odd, so our model excludes them. A particular exact discrete-gauge origin and its anomaly classification have not been derived.

Matter parity does **not** imply complete baryon conservation. Dimension-five superpotential operators

$$\begin{align}
\frac{c_{IJKN}}{M_*}\epsilon_{abc}(Q_I^a\cdot Q_J^b)(Q_K^c\cdot L_N),\qquad \frac{\widetilde c_{IJKN}}{M_*}\epsilon^{abc}U^c_{Ia}U^c_{Jb}D^c_{Kc}E_N^c
\tag{6.3}
\end{align}$$

can be gauge invariant and matter-parity even while violating B and L by one unit. Suitable distinct flavors make them nonzero; only flavor projections selected by the displayed weak/color contractions contribute. Their Wilson coefficients and ultraviolet scale are further inputs; setting them to zero was part of the renormalizable model, not a theorem of R parity.

There is also an explicit vacuum-nonuniqueness calibration in the exact renormalizable supersymmetric limit, with all soft and higher-dimensional terms absent. Take a common scalar value v in $q_1^{1,\uparrow}$, $q_2^{2,\downarrow}$, $q_3^{3,\uparrow}$ and $\ell_1^{\downarrow}$, and set all other scalars to zero. Color densities sum to $|v|^2I_3$, so every traceless color D moment vanishes. There are two up and two down weak entries, and $3(1/6)-1/2=0$ makes the Y moment vanish. Every one of the 49 derivatives of (3.2) is zero: Higgs and singlet matter factors vanish in each derivative, including the mu terms. Yet

$$\begin{align}
\epsilon_{abc}(q_1^a\cdot q_2^b)(q_3^c\cdot\ell_1)=v^4. \tag{6.4}
\end{align}$$

Changing its magnitude changes a gauge-invariant value, so these zero-energy configurations are not all one vacuum orbit. They are not a phenomenologically selected vacuum; gauge symmetry can be broken in an unacceptable way. Soft and higher operators can lift them. This explicit example rules out a claim that superspace kinematics alone selects the Higgs vacuum or its scale.

## 7. Why a Generic Einstein–Cartan Background Needs More Structure

The action above was constructed on flat rigid superspace. Adjoining odd coordinates does not by itself make an Einstein–Cartan-coupled matter action supersymmetric. Replacing flat derivatives by an arbitrary Einstein–Cartan connection need not preserve the flat algebra. Ordinary odd-frame superspace torsion, already nonzero in the derivative bracket (2.2), is not the same tensor as torsion of the ordinary spacetime body.

A new off-shell geometric example makes the obstruction nonzero. Keep a flat metric/coframe, let a be a nonzero real inverse-length constant and choose the metric-compatible connection matrices

$$\begin{align}
\mathsf A_x=aB_{01},\qquad \mathsf A_y=aB_{02},\qquad (B_{0i})^0{}_i=(B_{0i})^i{}_0=1, \tag{7.1}
\end{align}$$

with other entries/connection directions zero and $\omega^a{}_b=(\mathsf A_\mu)^a{}_b dx^\mu$. Use $T^a=de^a+\omega^a{}_b\wedge e^b$, $\mathcal R^a{}_b=d\omega^a{}_b+\omega^a{}_c\wedge\omega^c{}_b$ and nonmetricity $\mathcal Q_{\rho\mu\nu}=-\nabla_\rho g_{\mu\nu}$, with $e^a=dx^a$. The derivative slot is $\nabla_\mu v^a=\partial_\mu v^a+(\mathsf A_\mu)^a{}_b v^b$. Actual component covariant differentiation gives

$$\begin{align}
\mathcal Q=0,\qquad T^1=-a\,dt\wedge dx,\quad T^2=-a\,dt\wedge dy, \qquad\mathcal R^1{}_2=a^2dx\wedge dy,\quad \mathcal R^2{}_1=-a^2dx\wedge dy,\quad R=2a^2. \tag{7.2}
\end{align}$$

For the fixed Clifford-minus lift $\Omega=-\omega_{ab}\gamma^{ab}/4$, $\gamma^{ab}=[\gamma^a,\gamma^b]/2$, the coordinate spin-bundle operators satisfy

$$\begin{align}
[\nabla_x,\nabla_y]\psi=F^S_{xy}\psi, \qquad F^S_{xy}=-\frac{a^2}{2}\gamma^{12},\qquad \det F^S_{xy}=\frac{a^8}{16}\ne0. \tag{7.3}
\end{align}$$

Hence there is no nonzero ordinary parallel spinor parameter for this connection, and the naive covariant operators $P'_\mu=-i\nabla_\mu$ do not commute. This does not deny commutativity of ordinary coordinate partial derivatives. This is a prescribed off-shell calibration, not an Einstein–Cartan or supergravity solution. It does not rule out modified Killing-spinor equations with additional background fields. As in [EC-04](<EC-04.md>), a typed second covariant Hessian also differentiates its direction slot and has a torsion term; that is not the coordinate bundle-operator commutator in (7.3). Internal gauge curvature still uses $dA-igA\wedge A$, not an uncorrected torsionful antisymmetrized Hessian.

A consistent local supersymmetric coupling requires a supergravity multiplet and its constraints: for example old-minimal off-shell N=1 supergravity uses a tetrad, spin-three-halves gravitino, complex scalar and real vector auxiliaries. Its superspace supertorsion constraints, Bianchi identities, gauge symmetries and auxiliary equations must be compatible with the matter Kähler potential, superpotential and gauge kinetic terms. An arbitrary independent EC connection is not automatically such a constrained superconnection. Nor does appending a minimal EC four-fermion contact to the flat action prove local supersymmetry. The gravitational/matter auxiliary reduction and potential must be derived for the chosen completion.

No such general supergravity completion, curved twistor correspondence or quantum-gravitational measure is constructed here. The controlled positive model is the specified N=1 supersymmetric Standard Model extension; gauge group, three generations, Yukawa data, mu, soft terms and vacuum selection remain inputs.
