# Joint Free-Field and Geometry Quantization: Modes, Interfaces, and Limits

**EC-18 — DRAFT bounded review recorded.**

A geometry-dependent field frequency does not specify how a change of geometry acts on the field's quantum state. We make that identification explicitly for a neutral real scalar and two gauge-invariant shape states. The resulting Hamiltonian has a common canonical Hilbert space, calculable geometry-changing matrix elements and controlled transition probabilities for seven actual spatial field modes. Relative vacuum-energy subtractions change those probabilities. Extending the mode family also gives a precise distinction between finite-volume Fock equivalence and an infinite-volume obstruction. The construction is a selected joint model, not a classification of all fields or a quantization of the full gravitational constraints.

We use physical Lorentzian signature $(-,+,+,+)$, $c=1$ and explicit $\hbar$. A free field means a field with a specified quadratic action when the geometry label is held fixed; its coupling to a quantum geometry register makes the combined system interacting. Twistor or Lorentz representation labels do not by themselves select that action, its positive-frequency subspace or a positive quantum inner product. We use the scalar sector and canonical conventions of [EC-03](<EC-03.md#L19-L159>), rather than treating an indefinite covariant component pairing as a probability norm.

## 1. A Gauge-Reduced Geometry Register

Take the four outward spin-one-half legs and normalized invariant states of [EC-07](<EC-07.md#L399-L440>) and [AST-20](<AST-20.md#L247-L295>). In terms of a normalized pair singlet s and triplets t, they are

$$\begin{align}
|0\rangle=|s\rangle_{12}|s\rangle_{34},\qquad |1\rangle=\frac{|t_+t_-\rangle-|t_0t_0\rangle+|t_-t_+\rangle}{\sqrt3}. \tag{1.1}
\end{align}$$

They span $\mathcal G=\operatorname{Inv}(V_{1/2}^{\otimes4})\simeq\mathbb C^2$, and $\sum_f\mathbf J_f$ kills both. The pair-coupling operator distinguishes them:

$$
\mathsf D_{12}=\mathbf J_1\cdot\mathbf J_2
=\frac12\left[(\mathbf J_1+\mathbf J_2)^2-\frac32I\right]
=\begin{pmatrix}-3/4&0\\0&1/4\end{pmatrix}_{(0,1)}.
\tag{1.2}
$$

The pair spin is zero in the first state and one in the second. Both complete vertex states have total spin zero; these intermediate labels are not the scalar field's Poincaré spin. Thus

$$\begin{align}
\mathsf P_1=\mathsf D_{12}+\frac34I=|1\rangle\langle1|, \qquad \mathsf P_0=\frac14I-\mathsf D_{12}. \tag{1.3}
\end{align}$$

These are shape-sector projectors, not different face-area eigenvalues. With $\kappa=8\pi G>0$ and constant real $\gamma\ne0$, the inherited flux/area scale is

$$\begin{align}
\widehat{\mathbf N}_f=\kappa\gamma\hbar\mathbf J_f, \qquad a_0=\kappa|\gamma|\hbar, \qquad A_f=a_0\frac{\sqrt3}{2} \quad\text{in both states}. \tag{1.4}
\end{align}$$

The signed flux and positive area are distinct. No spatial volume or field-mode separation follows from this one area. These are invariant graph states, not a unique reconstructed classical metric.

Choose two positive inverse Compton lengths $m_0,m_1$. Define the additional coupling datum

$$\begin{align}
\widehat m^2=m_0^2\mathsf P_0+m_1^2\mathsf P_1. \tag{1.5}
\end{align}$$

The physical rest energy in branch g is $\hbar m_g$. This mass dependence is chosen, not derived from the area spectrum. A chosen shape-changing operator is

$$\begin{align}
\mathsf X=|0\rangle\langle1|+|1\rangle\langle0|, \qquad\mathsf X^2=I,\quad\|\mathsf X\|=1. \tag{1.6}
\end{align}$$

It belongs to the actual invariant shape algebra. Using the [other pairing matrix in AST-20](<AST-20.md#L328-L337>),

$$
\mathsf D_{13}=\begin{pmatrix}0&-\sqrt3/4\\-\sqrt3/4&-1/2\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
\mathsf X=-\frac4{\sqrt3}
\left(\mathsf D_{13}+\frac12\mathsf D_{12}+\frac38I\right).
\tag{1.7}
$$

Both pairings commute with total spin; their restriction is an invariant subspace, not a compression with discarded Gauss leakage. The new mass and mixing operators preserve this space. Our matter is a rotation/gauge singlet, so its Gauss generator is zero and the simultaneous Gauss action is already accounted for. Spinorial or charged matter would instead contribute its own charge and require joint intertwiners; a tensor product alone would not establish that reduction.

## 2. A Periodic Field and Its Actual Canonical Modes

Use a fixed reference spacetime $\mathbb R_T\times\mathbb T_L^3$ with metric $-dT^2+d\mathbf x^2$, spatial period $L>0$ and no timelike boundary. The box length is independent model data, not $\sqrt{A_f}$. In branch g the scalar action is

$$\begin{align}
I_g=\frac12\int dT\int_{[0,L]^3}d^3x \left[(\partial_T\phi)^2-|\nabla\phi|^2-m_g^2\phi^2\right]. \tag{2.1}
\end{align}$$

It has the shared covariant minus sign and equation $(\bar\Box-m_g^2)\phi=0$. The canonical momentum is $\pi=\partial_T\phi$. Periodicity removes spatial boundary flux; no independent torsion variable enters this scalar action.

Let $V=L^3$ and $\mathbf k_{\mathbf n}=2\pi\mathbf n/L$. Choose one representative from every nonzero pair $\{\mathbf n,-\mathbf n\}$. An orthonormal real basis consists of

$$\begin{align}
f_0=V^{-1/2},\qquad f_{\mathbf n,c}=\sqrt{2/V}\cos(\mathbf k_{\mathbf n}\cdot\mathbf x),\qquad f_{\mathbf n,s}=\sqrt{2/V}\sin(\mathbf k_{\mathbf n}\cdot\mathbf x). \tag{2.2}
\end{align}$$

Retain all lattice vectors with $|\mathbf k|\le\Lambda$, symmetrically under sign, and the corresponding real modes r. There are $M_\Lambda=1+2\#\{\text{retained nonzero pairs}\}$ real modes, equal to the number of retained lattice vectors including zero. In particular the zero mode is not doubled.

Expand

$$\begin{align}
\phi_\Lambda=\sum_rQ_rf_r,\qquad \pi_\Lambda=\sum_rP_rf_r, \qquad \int f_rf_s\,d^3x=\delta_{rs},\quad -\nabla^2f_r=k_r^2f_r. \tag{2.3}
\end{align}$$

Substitution into the action gives the actual reduced Lagrangian and momentum,

$$\begin{align}
L_g=\frac12\sum_r(\dot Q_r^2-\omega_{g,r}^2Q_r^2), \qquad P_r=\dot Q_r,\qquad \omega_{g,r}=\sqrt{k_r^2+m_g^2}>0. \tag{2.4}
\end{align}$$

The strictly positive masses keep the spatial zero mode an oscillator. A massless zero mode would be a free particle and needs a different vacuum treatment.

In the regular Schrödinger representation,

$$\begin{align}
\mathcal H_{f,\Lambda}=L^2(\mathbb R^{M_\Lambda},dQ),\qquad \widehat Q_r=Q_r,\quad\widehat P_r=-i\hbar\partial_{Q_r},\quad [\widehat Q_r,\widehat P_s]=i\hbar\delta_{rs}. \tag{2.5}
\end{align}$$

Multiplication and weak-derivative operators take their maximal self-adjoint domains; Schwartz functions form a common core. A finite number of spatial modes still has infinitely many oscillator occupations. No finite matrices are being assigned an exact canonical commutator.

The regulated spacetime-field relation is

$$\begin{align}
[\hat\phi_\Lambda(\mathbf x),\hat\pi_\Lambda(\mathbf y)] =i\hbar\delta_\Lambda(\mathbf x,\mathbf y),\qquad \delta_\Lambda=\sum_rf_r(\mathbf x)f_r(\mathbf y), \tag{2.6}
\end{align}$$

an orthogonal-projection kernel, not already the full periodic delta distribution. The latter is recovered distributionally by Fourier completeness when the cutoff is removed.

The action has units of action when $[\phi]=\sqrt\hbar\,\mathrm{length}^{-1}$. Hence $[Q_r]=\sqrt\hbar\,\mathrm{length}^{1/2}$, $[P_r]=\sqrt\hbar\,\mathrm{length}^{-1/2}$ and $[m_g]=[\omega_{g,r}]=\mathrm{length}^{-1}$. Frequencies are not energies until multiplied by $\hbar$.

## 3. A Self-Adjoint Joint Hamiltonian

The common canonical space is

$$\begin{align}
\mathcal H_\Lambda=\mathcal G\otimes\mathcal H_{f,\Lambda}. \tag{3.1}
\end{align}$$

For real offsets $\mathcal E_g^\Lambda$ and a real mixing energy $\varepsilon_G$, specify

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathsf h_g&=\mathcal E_g^\Lambda+
\frac12\sum_r(P_r^2+\omega_{g,r}^2Q_r^2),\\
H_d&=\sum_{g=0,1}\mathsf P_g\otimes\mathsf h_g,\qquad
H_\Lambda=H_d+\varepsilon_G\mathsf X\otimes I.
\end{aligned}
\tag{3.2}
$$

The offsets may include counterterms; their relative value will matter. The reference length, masses, offsets and mixing are prescribed, not coefficients determined by one face area or by a gravitational Hamiltonian constraint.

The normalized Hermite basis in branch g is $|\mathbf n;g\rangle=\bigotimes_r|n_r;\omega_{g,r}\rangle$. The oscillator normalization/eigenvalue formula is the one in [AST-22](<AST-22.md#L213-L243>) with the kinetic normalization of (2.4). Thus $|g;\mathbf n\rangle=|g\rangle\otimes|\mathbf n;g\rangle$ is a complete orthonormal joint basis for $H_d$, with

$$\begin{align}
E_{g,\mathbf n}=\mathcal E_g^\Lambda+ \hbar\sum_r\omega_{g,r}(n_r+\tfrac12). \tag{3.3}
\end{align}$$

Orthogonality between different g comes from the geometry factor even when their field wavefunctions overlap. Define the diagonal operator on

$$\begin{align}
D(H_d)=\left\{\sum c_{g,\mathbf n}|g;\mathbf n\rangle:
\sum E_{g,\mathbf n}^2|c_{g,\mathbf n}|^2<\infty\right\}. \tag{3.4}
\end{align}$$

It is self-adjoint. The mixing has norm $|\varepsilon_G|$, so the bounded-perturbation theorem makes $H_\Lambda$ self-adjoint on the same domain, and

$$\begin{align}
H_\Lambda\ge E_{\min}-|\varepsilon_G|, \qquad E_{\min}=\min_g\left(\mathcal E_g^\Lambda+ \frac\hbar2\sum_r\omega_{g,r}\right). \tag{3.5}
\end{align}$$

Consequently $e^{-iTH_\Lambda/\hbar}$ is unitary on the whole positive Hilbert space. The space $\mathcal G\otimes\mathcal S(\mathbb R^{M_\Lambda})$ is a common core for the displayed polynomial operators and is preserved by the mixing. Positivity of frequencies establishes branch stability; strict positivity of the total Hamiltonian additionally requires a suitable offset or $E_{\min}>|\varepsilon_G|$.

### Coupled Operator Motion

Let $\mathsf Z=\mathsf P_0-\mathsf P_1$ and $\mathsf Y=-i|0\rangle\langle1|+i|1\rangle\langle0|$. The commutator equations on the common core include

$$\begin{align}
\dot Q_r=P_r,\qquad \dot P_r=-(k_r^2I+\widehat m^2)Q_r, \tag{3.6}
\end{align}$$

and

$$
\begin{aligned}
\dot{\mathsf Z}&=\frac{2\varepsilon_G}{\hbar}\mathsf Y,\\
\dot{\mathsf X}&=-\frac1\hbar\mathsf Y\,\Delta\mathsf h,\\
\dot{\mathsf Y}&=\frac1\hbar\mathsf X\,\Delta\mathsf h
-\frac{2\varepsilon_G}{\hbar}\mathsf Z,\qquad
\Delta\mathsf h=\mathsf h_0-\mathsf h_1
=\mathcal E_0^\Lambda-\mathcal E_1^\Lambda+
\frac{m_0^2-m_1^2}{2}\sum_rQ_r^2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{3.7}
$$

Products use the embedded equal-time Heisenberg operators, with identity factors and time arguments suppressed. Their mutual commutators remain zero by common unitary conjugation; this does not assert locality in the original tensor decomposition after interacting evolution. The geometry motion depends on a field quadratic observable, while the field has an operator-valued frequency. These are the two directions of the chosen interaction. Expectation-value derivatives require the corresponding operator domains; the commutator identities are not assertions about every unbounded moment in every Hilbert vector.

## 4. Conditional Fock Bases and a Geometry-Changing Interface

All branches use the same $Q_r,P_r$ at a reference time. Define their annihilators by

$$\begin{align}
a_{g,r}=\sqrt{\frac{\omega_{g,r}}{2\hbar}}Q_r+ \frac{iP_r}{\sqrt{2\hbar\omega_{g,r}}}. \tag{4.1}
\end{align}$$

They determine different positive-frequency/ground-state choices. They are not two mode families solving the same mass equation at all times; their identification is through the explicitly fixed equal-time canonical data. Direct substitution gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
a_{1,r}&=\alpha_ra_{0,r}+\beta_ra_{0,r}^\dagger,\\
\alpha_r&=\frac12\left(\sqrt{\omega_{1,r}/\omega_{0,r}}+
\sqrt{\omega_{0,r}/\omega_{1,r}}\right),\\
\beta_r&=\frac12\left(\sqrt{\omega_{1,r}/\omega_{0,r}}-
\sqrt{\omega_{0,r}/\omega_{1,r}}\right),\qquad
\alpha_r^2-\beta_r^2=1.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4.2}
$$

The sign of beta is fixed by this annihilator relation, rather than borrowed from a different mode-expansion convention. For $N_1=\sum_ra_{1,r}^\dagger a_{1,r}$, in particular

$$\begin{align}
\langle\mathrm{vac}_0|N_1|\mathrm{vac}_0\rangle=\sum_r\beta_r^2. \tag{4.3}
\end{align}$$

This is an expectation, not a number eigenvalue. With positive real position-space phases, the normalized branch vacua are

$$\begin{align}
\psi_g(Q)=\prod_r\left(\frac{\omega_{g,r}}{\pi\hbar}\right)^{1/4} \exp\left[-\frac1{2\hbar}\sum_r\omega_{g,r}Q_r^2\right]. \tag{4.4}
\end{align}$$

Their Gaussian integral factorizes, giving the vacuum interface

$$\begin{align}
\mathcal O_\Lambda=\langle\mathrm{vac}_1|\mathrm{vac}_0\rangle =\prod_r\left(\frac{2\sqrt{\omega_{0,r}\omega_{1,r}}} {\omega_{0,r}+\omega_{1,r}}\right)^{1/2} =\prod_r\alpha_r^{-1/2}. \tag{4.5}
\end{align}$$

It is strictly positive for finite $M_\Lambda$. The elementary integral is the same normalized Gaussian building block used in [AST-22](<AST-22.md#L269-L311>); the field application requires the product over the actual spatial modes.

The mixing in (3.2) therefore has matrix elements

$$\begin{align}
\langle1;\mathbf n|\varepsilon_G\mathsf X\otimes I|0;\mathbf m\rangle =\varepsilon_G\langle\mathbf n;1|\mathbf m;0\rangle. \tag{4.6}
\end{align}$$

For one two-quantum excitation r in the final branch, Hermite orthogonality and a quadratic Gaussian moment give

$$\begin{align}
\langle 2_r;1|\mathrm{vac}_0\rangle =\mathcal O_\Lambda\frac{\beta_r}{\sqrt2\alpha_r}, \qquad \langle1_r;1|\mathrm{vac}_0\rangle=0. \tag{4.7}
\end{align}$$

Indeed the normalized second Hermite polynomial contributes $[2\omega_{1,r}Q_r^2/\hbar-1]/\sqrt2$; the cross-Gaussian variance is $\hbar/(\omega_{0,r}+\omega_{1,r})$. This yields $(\omega_{1,r}-\omega_{0,r})/[\sqrt2(\omega_{1,r}+\omega_{0,r})]=\beta_r/(\sqrt2\alpha_r)$. Odd parity gives the second result.

The bare flip $\mathsf X\otimes I$ acts on geometry alone and does not itself entangle an instantaneous product in the common Q representation. Equations (4.6)–(4.7) are its matrix elements in different conditional number bases. Joint dynamics comes from combining it with the branch-dependent field evolution.

### Why an Identification of Fock Labels Changes the Operator

For finite mode number, the product of the normalized dilations in [AST-22](<AST-22.md#L292-L313>) gives unitary $S_g$ with $S_g|\mathbf n;0\rangle=|\mathbf n;g\rangle$, taking $S_0=I$. Set $W=\sum_g\mathsf P_g\otimes S_g$. In the common reference-number basis,

$$\begin{align}
W^\dagger(\mathsf X\otimes I)W =|1\rangle\langle0|\otimes S_1^\dagger S_0 +|0\rangle\langle1|\otimes S_0^\dagger S_1. \tag{4.8}
\end{align}$$

The interface unitaries cannot be replaced by identity while claiming the same model. Such replacement would identify instantaneous number labels instead of the original canonical fields. Nor does this finite product of dilations prove implementability for infinitely many field modes.

## 5. An Evaluated Field Transition and Relative Vacuum Energies

Choose a length $\ell>0$, period $L=2\pi\ell$, cutoff $|\mathbf n|^2\le1$, and

$$\begin{align}
m_0=1/\ell,\qquad m_1=2/\ell. \tag{5.1}
\end{align}$$

There are seven real modes: the constant mode and a cosine/sine pair along each coordinate axis. Their frequencies are

$$\begin{align}
\boldsymbol\omega_0=\ell^{-1}(1,\sqrt2,\sqrt2,\sqrt2,\sqrt2,\sqrt2,\sqrt2), \qquad \boldsymbol\omega_1=\ell^{-1}(2,\sqrt5,\sqrt5,\sqrt5,\sqrt5,\sqrt5,\sqrt5). \tag{5.2}
\end{align}$$

For example the actual commutator projection kernel is

$$\begin{align}
\delta_7(\mathbf x,\mathbf y)=\frac1{L^3} \left[1+2\sum_{i=1}^3\cos\frac{2\pi(x_i-y_i)}L\right], \qquad\delta_7(\mathbf x,\mathbf x)=7/L^3. \tag{5.3}
\end{align}$$

The evaluated vacuum product and number expectation are

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal O_7&=\sqrt{\frac{2\sqrt2}{3}}
\left(\frac{2\,10^{1/4}}{\sqrt2+\sqrt5}\right)^3
=0.8980960163\ldots,\\
\sum_r\beta_r^2&=-\frac{23}{8}+\frac{21}{2\sqrt{10}}
=0.4453915432\ldots.
\end{aligned}
\tag{5.4}
$$

The zero-mode pair channel is $\mathcal O_7/(3\sqrt2)$, which is nonzero. Thus the subspace of just the two branch vacua is not invariant under the mixing. Diagonalizing its two-by-two compression would not give the spectrum or exact evolution of the full field model.

### A Controlled First-Order Observable

Prepare $|0;\mathrm{vac}_0\rangle$ and measure the final projection onto $|1;\mathrm{vac}_1\rangle$. Write

$$\begin{align}
E_g^{\rm vac}=\mathcal E_g^\Lambda+\frac\hbar2\sum_r\omega_{g,r}. \tag{5.5}
\end{align}$$

The first-order Duhamel term obeys the actual projected inhomogeneous equation

$$\begin{align}
i\hbar\dot A^{(1)}=E_1^{\rm vac}A^{(1)}+ \varepsilon_G\mathcal O_\Lambda e^{-iE_0^{\rm vac}T/\hbar}, \qquad A^{(1)}(0)=0. \tag{5.6}
\end{align}$$

Only its source is restricted to the initial vacuum; the full Hamiltonian still contains all other field levels. Integration gives

$$\begin{align}
A^{(1)}(T)=\varepsilon_G\mathcal O_\Lambda \frac{e^{-iE_0^{\rm vac}T/\hbar}-e^{-iE_1^{\rm vac}T/\hbar}} {E_0^{\rm vac}-E_1^{\rm vac}}. \tag{5.7}
\end{align}$$

At equal branch vacuum energies E the removable limit is $-i\varepsilon_G\mathcal O_\Lambda(T/\hbar)e^{-iET/\hbar}$.

This is a controlled approximation to the actual amplitude. Since $\mathsf Z$ commutes with $H_d$ and anticommutes with the mixing, only odd Dyson orders can connect the two geometry labels. In the interaction picture the mixing norm is $|\varepsilon_G|$. The ordered n-time simplex has volume $|T|^n/n!$, so

$$\begin{align}
|A(T)-A^{(1)}(T)|\le R(x):=\sum_{n=3,5,\ldots}\frac{x^n}{n!} =\sinh x-x,\qquad x=|\varepsilon_G T|/\hbar. \tag{5.8}
\end{align}$$

The estimate uses all field states and is independent of mode number. It is not an estimate obtained by discarding excited channels. In particular the exact probability satisfies

$$\begin{align}
\big[\max(0,|A^{(1)}|-R)\big]^2\le P(T) \le(|A^{(1)}|+R)^2. \tag{5.9}
\end{align}$$

For the seven-mode model take $\mathcal E_0^\Lambda=\mathcal E_1^\Lambda=0$, $T=\ell$ and $\varepsilon_G=\hbar/(10\ell)$. The detuning is

$$\begin{align}
E_1^{\rm vac}-E_0^{\rm vac} =\frac\hbar{2\ell}\left[1+6(\sqrt5-\sqrt2)\right] =2.9655632454\ldots\,\hbar/\ell. \tag{5.10}
\end{align}$$

Then $|A^{(1)}|=0.06033387909\ldots$ and $R=0.00016675002\ldots$. The exact expression (5.9) gives, with decimal endpoints rounded outwards,

$$\begin{align}
0.0036200\le P(\ell)\le0.0036604. \tag{5.11}
\end{align}$$

### Normal Ordering Is a Relative Branch Choice

A common constant added to both branch energies produces only a global phase in (5.7). By contrast, separately removing each branch's zero-point sum means choosing

$$\begin{align}
\mathcal E_g^\Lambda=\overline{\mathcal E}_g^\Lambda-\frac\hbar2\sum_r\omega_{g,r}. \tag{5.12}
\end{align}$$

This is a geometry-dependent counterterm, not one common energy origin. If $\overline{\mathcal E}_0^\Lambda=\overline{\mathcal E}_1^\Lambda=0$, both branch vacua have zero energy. At the same T and mixing, the first-order modulus is now $0.08980960163\ldots$, and the same rigorous remainder gives

$$\begin{align}
0.0080358\le P(\ell)\le0.0080958. \tag{5.13}
\end{align}$$

These disjoint probability ranges compare two specified Hamiltonians. They do not select a preferred renormalization prescription; they show that relative branch energies or renormalization conditions must be supplied. If desired a common constant can make the normal-ordered total Hamiltonian nonnegative without changing these probabilities. A vacuum-energy convention harmless in a superselected branch is not automatically harmless when geometry can change.

## 6. The Ultraviolet Limit at Fixed Spatial Volume

Now retain the whole periodic mode family, not just seven modes. Keep the canonical identification (2.3) fixed while comparing the two masses. For spatial dimension d, used here to diagnose convergence, let $k=|\mathbf k|$. For a nontrivial mass change,

$$\begin{align}
\beta(k)=\frac{m_1^2-m_0^2} {2\sqrt{\omega_0(k)\omega_1(k)}[\omega_0(k)+\omega_1(k)]} =\frac{m_1^2-m_0^2}{4k^2}+O(k^{-4}). \tag{6.1}
\end{align}$$

The masses are positive, so the few low modes are finite and the frequency ratios remain bounded. The bosonic Shale criterion [1,2] says that this symplectic comparison is unitarily implementable in Fock space precisely when its antilinear beta map is Hilbert–Schmidt. In this discrete diagonal real-mode basis the condition is

$$\begin{align}
\sum_r|\beta_r|^2<\infty. \tag{6.2}
\end{align}$$

The field mode multiplicity must be counted. Lattice points in a dyadic annulus of radius $2^j$ number on the order of $2^{jd}$; (6.1) makes their beta-squared contribution on the order of $2^{j(d-4)}$. Comparison with that geometric series gives

$$\begin{align}
\text{fixed finite volume, }m_1\ne m_0: \qquad\text{implementable}\ \Longleftrightarrow\ d<4. \tag{6.3}
\end{align}$$

At d=4 the sum grows logarithmically and above it by a power. This uses the actual lattice growth, equivalently the mode-counting setting of [AST-17](<AST-17.md#L106-L121>), not a finite matrix or a single-oscillator result. The physical three-dimensional spatial box therefore admits this Fock comparison. Equal masses are the trivial beta-zero exception in every dimension.

Moreover

$$\begin{align}
\log\mathcal O_\Lambda=-\frac14\sum_r\log(1+\beta_r^2). \tag{6.4}
\end{align}$$

Since the logarithm is asymptotic to beta squared at high k, the vacuum product has a nonzero ultraviolet limit at fixed finite volume in d=3. This does not identify the two Hamiltonian spectra or choose a preferred field vacuum on arbitrary geometry.

### Energy and Zero-Point Control Are Stronger Questions

After removing the branch1 field zero point, its excitation-energy expectation in the branch0 vacuum is

$$\begin{align}
\langle\mathrm{vac}_0|\hbar\sum_r\omega_{1,r}a_{1,r}^\dagger a_{1,r}|\mathrm{vac}_0\rangle =\hbar\sum_r\omega_{1,r}\beta_r^2, \qquad \omega_1\beta^2\sim\frac{(m_1^2-m_0^2)^2}{16k^3}. \tag{6.5}
\end{align}$$

The corresponding tail is $\int^\infty k^{d-4}dk$: it is finite only for d<3 and logarithmically divergent in physical d=3. Thus representation equivalence does not imply that this instantaneous vacuum comparison has uniformly finite energy. It is not, by itself, a convergence proof for the chosen cutoff Hamiltonians or their geometry-changing term.

The relative zero-point sum also needs its own prescription. Per mode,

$$\begin{align}
\omega_1-\omega_0= \frac{m_1^2-m_0^2}{2k} -\frac{m_1^4-m_0^4}{8k^3}+O(k^{-5}). \tag{6.6}
\end{align}$$

Its leading sum diverges quadratically in three spatial dimensions. A regulator and relative branch counterterms are required; the finite parts are not selected by the canonical commutator or by (6.3). Neither finite-mode unitarity nor the Shale condition settles all continuum energy/domain questions.

A different chosen model with $\omega_g(k)\sim c_gk$, at the same canonical Q,P, instead has

$$\begin{align}
\beta(k)\longrightarrow\frac{c_1-c_0}{2\sqrt{c_0c_1}}.
\tag{6.7}
\end{align}$$

For unequal positive asymptotic speeds its ultraviolet sum fails in every positive spatial dimension. This is not the mass-change model. A change of physical box length, field normalization or kinetic coefficient likewise requires a new explicit canonical identification before invoking a Fock criterion.

## 7. Infinite Volume: a Density Is Not a Hilbert–Schmidt Norm

Assume $m_1\ne m_0$ throughout this section. Converting real cosine/sine pairs to plane-wave labels gives

$$\begin{align}
a_{\mathbf k}=\frac{a_c-ia_s}{\sqrt2},\qquad a_{-\mathbf k}=\frac{a_c+ia_s}{\sqrt2},\qquad a_{1,\mathbf k}=\alpha_k a_{0,\mathbf k} +\beta_k a_{0,-\mathbf k}^\dagger. \tag{7.1}
\end{align}$$

Thus the same sum uses all lattice vectors once, not a further factor of two. In infinite volume the one-particle Hilbert space is $L^2(\mathbb R^3,d^3k)$ with the [continuum delta normalization of EC-03](<EC-03.md#L136-L150>). The antilinear comparison is

$$\begin{align}
(Bf)(\mathbf k)=\beta(k)\overline{f(-\mathbf k)}. \tag{7.2}
\end{align}$$

It is multiplication followed by reflection/conjugation, not an integral operator with an ordinary square-integrable kernel. Let $N_{\Lambda,L}=\sum_r\beta_r^2$ be the comparison-number expectation. Taking the volume limit first at a fixed ultraviolet band gives a mode Riemann integral; removing that band afterwards yields the finite density

$$\begin{align}
\lim_{\Lambda\to\infty}\lim_{L\to\infty}\frac{N_{\Lambda,L}}V =\frac1{(2\pi)^3}\int d^3k\,\beta(k)^2 \tag{7.3}
\end{align}$$

This convergent density in d=3 is not $\|B\|_{\rm HS}^2$. For $m_0=1/\ell,m_1=2/\ell$, the integral gives $0.01471055958\ldots\,\ell^{-3}$.

### An Explicit Infinite Orthonormal Obstruction

Choose $K_*>0$ and $E=[0,K_*]^3$. For integers n define

$$\begin{align}
f_n(\mathbf k)=K_*^{-3/2}\,\mathbf1_E(\mathbf k) e^{2\pi i n k_1/K_*}. \tag{7.4}
\end{align}$$

They are orthonormal: the first coordinate integral vanishes for distinct integers, and the remaining volume factors cancel the normalization. The nonzero mass beta is continuous and bounded away from zero on this compact cube. Consequently

$$\begin{align}
\|Bf_n\|^2=K_*^{-3}\int_E\beta(k)^2d^3k\ge b_*^2>0 \qquad\text{for every }n. \tag{7.5}
\end{align}$$

For example, with $m_0=K_*,m_1=2K_*$, beta squared decreases radially because

$$\begin{align}
\frac{d\beta^2}{dk}=-k\beta^2 \left(\frac1{\omega_0}+\frac1{\omega_1}\right)^2. \tag{7.6}
\end{align}$$

On E its minimum occurs at $k=\sqrt3K_*$. One may take

$$\begin{align}
b_*^2=\frac{11-4\sqrt7}{8\sqrt7}=0.01970115039\ldots>0. \tag{7.7}
\end{align}$$

The infinite sum of the squared norms in (7.5) diverges, so B is not Hilbert–Schmidt. This is an infinite-sequence proof, not an extrapolation from finitely many modes. It already works in a nonzero bounded momentum band. Hence the stated translation-invariant mass comparison has no global Fock implementer in infinite volume, despite its finite density. Local algebra/state comparisons are separate questions.

The same volume effect appears in the finite-box vacuum product. At fixed positive ultraviolet band, ordinary mode Riemann sums give

$$\begin{align}
-\frac{\log\mathcal O_{\Lambda,L}}V\longrightarrow \frac1{4(2\pi)^3}\int_{|\mathbf k|\le\Lambda}
\log(1+\beta(k)^2)d^3k>0. \tag{7.8}
\end{align}$$

The overlap therefore tends to zero exponentially with volume. This describes this matrix element and limit; it is not a theorem that every geometry transition vanishes in every continuum dynamics. Abstract separable Hilbert spaces may be isomorphic even when no unitary intertwines the chosen canonical field map. In that situation “identity on matter” between geometry-dependent Fock fibres is not a canonical prescription for a limiting joint Hamiltonian. Additional inter-fibre or algebraic dynamics is required.

## 8. A Clock and a History Representation of the Cutoff Model

An external-time Hamiltonian alone has not imposed a gravitational constraint. To illustrate a genuine constraint/norm choice for this model, add an ideal clock pair on $L^2(\mathbb R_T,dT)$, with $p_T=-i\hbar\partial_T$, and choose

$$\begin{align}
\widehat C=\widehat p_T+H_\Lambda. \tag{8.1}
\end{align}$$

The clock momentum and $H_\Lambda$ act on separate factors and strongly commute, so their spectral sum defines a self-adjoint constraint. This is one chosen deparametrized constraint, not the inhomogeneous gravitational constraint algebra. T is not assumed to be the massive scalar zero mode, which would oscillate rather than provide an automatic global clock.

Distributional solutions satisfy

$$\begin{align}
i\hbar\partial_T\Psi=H_\Lambda\Psi,\qquad \Psi(T)=e^{-i(T-T_0)H_\Lambda/\hbar}\psi, \qquad \langle\Psi_1,\Psi_2\rangle_{\rm phys} :=\langle\Psi_1(T_0),\Psi_2(T_0)\rangle_{\mathcal H_\Lambda}. \tag{8.2}
\end{align}$$

This norm is positive and independent of the clock slice. Nonzero solutions are not square integrable over all T in the kinematical clock norm. The sign in (8.1) fixes $p_T=-H_\Lambda$ and the phase in (8.2); a different scalar-momentum sector such as [EC-08](<EC-08.md#L132-L196>) must be translated, not silently identified. The kinematical T operator is not a Dirac observable canonically conjugate to a semibounded physical Hamiltonian.

### Actual Time-Slice Kernels

For positive Euclidean duration tau and fixed finite mode cutoff, the branch kernel is

$$\begin{align}
K_g(Q',Q;\tau)=e^{-\tau\mathcal E_g^\Lambda/\hbar} \prod_r K_{\omega_{g,r}}(Q'_r,Q_r;\tau), \tag{8.3}
\end{align}$$

where the normalized Mehler factor is

$$\begin{align}
K_\omega(q',q;\tau)= \sqrt{\frac\omega{2\pi\hbar\sinh(\omega\tau)}} \exp\left[-\frac{\omega[(q'^2+q^2)\cosh(\omega\tau)-2q'q]} {2\hbar\sinh(\omega\tau)}\right]. \tag{8.4}
\end{align}$$

Here $h_\omega=-\hbar^2\partial_{q'}^2/2+\omega^2q'^2/2$ acts on the first endpoint. It satisfies $\hbar\partial_\tau K_\omega+h_\omega K_\omega=0$. The register factor is explicit too:

$$\begin{align}
\mathcal M(\delta\tau)=e^{-\delta\tau\varepsilon_G\mathsf X/\hbar} =\cosh(\delta\tau\varepsilon_G/\hbar)I -\sinh(\delta\tau\varepsilon_G/\hbar)\mathsf X. \tag{8.5}
\end{align}$$

At one geometry interface the field integration really uses two different frequencies. For one mode, set

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal A&=\omega_1\coth(\omega_1 a)+\omega_0\coth(\omega_0 b)>0,\\
\mathcal B&=\omega_1\operatorname{csch}(\omega_1a)q_f+
\omega_0\operatorname{csch}(\omega_0b)q_i,\\
\mathcal C&=\omega_1\coth(\omega_1a)q_f^2+
\omega_0\coth(\omega_0b)q_i^2,\qquad a,b>0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{8.6}
$$

Completing the square gives the evaluated mixed factor

$$\begin{align}
\int dq\,K_{\omega_1}(q_f,q;a)K_{\omega_0}(q,q_i;b) =\sqrt{\frac{\omega_0\omega_1} {2\pi\hbar\mathcal A\sinh(\omega_1a)\sinh(\omega_0b)}} \exp\left[-\frac{\mathcal C-\mathcal B^2/\mathcal A}{2\hbar}\right]. \tag{8.7}
\end{align}$$

After completing the square, integration of the centered q Gaussian with $\mathcal A>0$ contributes $\sqrt{2\pi\hbar/\mathcal A}$. For equal frequencies the result reduces to $K_\omega(q_f,q_i;a+b)$. Unequal branch frequencies are not replaced by an arbitrary common Gaussian.

Since the mixing is bounded, the Trotter product theorem applies to the actual self-adjoint operators constructed above:

$$\begin{align}
e^{-\tau H_\Lambda/\hbar} =\operatorname*{s-lim}_{N\to\infty} \left(e^{-\delta\tau H_d/\hbar} e^{-\delta\tau\varepsilon_G\mathsf X/\hbar}\right)^N, \qquad\delta\tau=\tau/N. \tag{8.8}
\end{align}$$

For endpoints $g_0,Q_0$ and $g_N,Q_N$, the product kernel is concretely

$$\begin{align}
\sum_{g_1,\ldots,g_{N-1}}\int\prod_{j=1}^{N-1}dQ_j
\prod_{j=1}^{N}\left[ K_{g_j}(Q_j,Q_{j-1};\delta\tau) \mathcal M(\delta\tau)_{g_jg_{j-1}}\right]. \tag{8.9}
\end{align}$$

Its limit is asserted in the strong-operator sense on states, not as an unqualified pointwise formula for every path. This removes the time slicing at fixed field cutoff; it does not remove the ultraviolet or spatial-volume regulators. For positive $\varepsilon_G$ the off-diagonal entries of (8.5) are negative, so individual history coefficients are not probabilities. Positivity of the Hilbert norm is a different statement.

The spectral unitary evolution and this history representation describe the same chosen Hamiltonian. They do not establish an EPRL projection, a unique gravitational path-integral measure or a full canonical/covariant equivalence for four-dimensional gravity. Extending beyond the present neutral, Gauss-reduced, finite-field model requires an action and regulator for the additional geometry/matter degrees of freedom, their simultaneous constraints, a physical clock/norm prescription and controlled continuum domains and counterterms.
