# Classical Matter on Riemann–Cartan Spacetime

The first question in coupling a matter field to geometry is not which partial derivatives can be replaced by covariant derivatives. It is which fields the action depends on. A scalar gradient, the exterior derivative of a one-form, and the covariant derivative of a spinor are different constructions. They need not involve the same connection.

This chapter develops three examples: scalar fields, Maxwell and Proca fields, and spinors. The geometry is initially prescribed; the optional Einstein–Cartan calculation in Section 6 eliminates its algebraic torsion. No electromagnetic coupling of the scalar or spinor is assumed. A global phase symmetry can nevertheless have a conserved Noether charge; the absence of an electromagnetic coupling does not abolish global charges.

The prerequisites are tensor calculus and elementary variational calculus. The [shared conventions](<../CONVENTIONS.md>) and the compatible-connection construction in [EC-01](<EC-01.md>) are used throughout: signature $(-,+,+,+)$, derivative-first connection slots, $c=\hbar=1$, and the stated curvature sign. Spinor conventions are fixed explicitly in Section 5 rather than inferred from the metric sign.

## 1. Geometry and Integration by Parts

Let the metric-compatible connection be $\Gamma=\bar\Gamma+K$, where the bar denotes the Levi-Civita connection. Define

$$\begin{align}
\nabla_\mu V^\rho=\partial_\mu V^\rho+\Gamma^\rho{}_{\mu\sigma}V^\sigma,\qquad T^\rho{}_{\mu\nu}=\Gamma^\rho{}_{\mu\nu}-\Gamma^\rho{}_{\nu\mu},\qquad t_\mu=T^\rho{}_{\mu\rho}. \tag{1.1}
\end{align}$$

The trace definition matters: $T^\rho{}_{\rho\mu}=-t_\mu$. With $K_{abc}=g_{a\rho}K^\rho{}_{bc}$, compatibility and the torsion definition give

$$\begin{align}
K_{abc}=-K_{cba},\qquad K_{abc}=\tfrac12(T_{abc}-T_{bca}+T_{cab}).
\end{align}$$

Two useful contractions follow by inserting this formula and relabeling summed indices:

$$\begin{align}
K^\rho{}_{\rho\mu}=-t_\mu,\qquad g^{\mu\nu}K^\rho{}_{\mu\nu}=t^\rho. \tag{1.2}
\end{align}$$

Let $e=\sqrt{-\det g}$. Metric compatibility also implies $\Gamma^\rho{}_{\mu\rho}=\partial_\mu\log e$. Consequently,

$$\begin{align}
\nabla_\mu V^\mu=\bar\nabla_\mu V^\mu-t_\mu V^\mu,\qquad \frac1e\partial_\mu(eV^\mu)=(\nabla_\mu+t_\mu)V^\mu. \tag{1.3}
\end{align}$$

The volume-form divergence is therefore not the uncorrected torsionful divergence. For a scalar f and vector V, integration by parts reads

$$\begin{align}
\int_M eV^\mu\partial_\mu f\,d^4x =-\int_M ef(\nabla_\mu+t_\mu)V^\mu\,d^4x +\int_{\partial M}fV^\mu d\Sigma_\mu. \tag{1.4}
\end{align}$$

Below, variations may be taken compactly supported, so this last term vanishes. If a finite boundary is part of the problem, its boundary conditions must instead be supplied. This convention avoids silently assuming that every total covariant divergence integrates to zero.

## 2. Scalar Fields: Let the Action Determine the Wave Operator

### Minimal Action and Stress-Energy

A real scalar has the action

$$\begin{align}
I_\phi=-\int_M e\left[\frac12g^{\mu\nu}\partial_\mu\phi\partial_\nu\phi+V(\phi)\right]d^4x. \tag{2.1}
\end{align}$$

A scalar's first covariant derivative is just its differential, $\nabla_\mu\phi=\partial_\mu\phi$. Thus this action depends on the metric but not the independent connection. Its first variation is

$$\begin{align}
\delta I_\phi =\int_M e\left[\frac1e\partial_\mu(eg^{\mu\nu}\partial_\nu\phi)-V'(\phi)\right]\delta\phi\,d^4x -\int_{\partial M}\delta\phi\partial^\mu\phi\,d\Sigma_\mu.
\end{align}$$

The Euler equation is therefore

$$\begin{align}
\bar\Box\phi-V'(\phi)=0,\qquad \bar\Box\phi:=\frac1e\partial_\mu(eg^{\mu\nu}\partial_\nu\phi). \tag{2.2}
\end{align}$$

For $V=m^2\phi^2/2$, this is $(\bar\Box-m^2)\phi=0$. In Minkowski coordinates $\bar\Box=-\partial_t^2+\nabla^2$. A mode $e^{-i\omega t+i\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf x}$ has $\omega^2=|\mathbf k|^2+m^2$, or $p_\mu p^\mu=-m^2$.

A second torsionful derivative is different:

$$\begin{align}
g^{\mu\nu}\nabla_\mu\nabla_\nu\phi =\bar\Box\phi-t^\rho\partial_\rho\phi. \tag{2.3}
\end{align}$$

Replacing the wave operator in (2.2) by this expression without the compensating trace term changes the equation. Equivalently, the action-derived equation can be written $(\nabla_\mu+t_\mu)\nabla^\mu\phi-V'=0$. There is no torsion force in the specified minimal action on a fixed metric background. This is a statement about this action, not a prohibition on curvature- or torsion-dependent scalar interactions.

Varying the metric with $T_{\mu\nu}=-2e^{-1}\delta I_\phi/\delta g^{\mu\nu}$ gives

$$\begin{align}
T_{\mu\nu}=\partial_\mu\phi\partial_\nu\phi -g_{\mu\nu}\left[\tfrac12(\partial\phi)^2+V\right],\qquad \bar\nabla^\mu T_{\mu\nu}=(\bar\Box\phi-V')\partial_\nu\phi. \tag{2.4}
\end{align}$$

In the spatially flat FLRW examples, choose comoving spatial coordinates with units of length and a dimensionless scale factor. Cosmic proper time t and $H=\dot a/a$ are defined by $ds^2=-dt^2+a(t)^2d\mathbf x^2$. A homogeneous field obeys

$$\begin{align}
\ddot\phi+3H\dot\phi+V'=0,\quad \varepsilon=\tfrac12\dot\phi^2+V,\quad p=\tfrac12\dot\phi^2-V.
\end{align}$$

For a massless homogeneous field, $a^3\dot\phi$ is constant. Its kinetic energy then scales as $a^{-6}$ and has $p=\varepsilon$. This conclusion concerns that homogeneous kinetic state; it is not the equation of state of every massless scalar configuration.

### A Conformal Example

Choose a conformally flat metric $g_{\mu\nu}=a(\eta)^2\eta_{\mu\nu}$, with $a>0$ and conformal time $\eta$. A nonminimal but connection-independent scalar action adds $-\xi\bar R\phi^2/2$ to its Lagrangian. Here $\bar R$ is explicitly Levi-Civita curvature, not $R(\Gamma)$. The equation is

$$\begin{align}
(\bar\Box-m^2-\xi\bar R)\phi=0.
\end{align}$$

For the declared curvature sign, $\bar R=6a''/a^3$. Put $\phi=\chi/a$. Direct differentiation gives

$$\begin{align}
\bar\Box(\chi/a) =a^{-3}\Box_\eta\chi+\frac{a''}{a^4}\chi,\qquad (\bar\Box-\bar R/6)(\chi/a)=a^{-3}\Box_\eta\chi. \tag{2.5}
\end{align}$$

More generally, the full rescaled equation is

$$\begin{align}
\left[\Box_\eta-m^2a^2+(1-6\xi)\frac{a''}{a}\right]\chi=0,\qquad \Box_\eta=-\partial_\eta^2+\Delta_{\mathbf x}.
\end{align}$$

Thus the massless equation at $\xi=1/6$ reduces to the flat wave equation. The minimally coupled equation instead retains the $a''$ term, and a fixed mass breaks this conformal reduction. The stress tensor (2.4) applies to the minimal action; varying the curvature-coupled action requires additional terms.

### A De Sitter Example with Distinct Mass Regimes

For a homogeneous minimally coupled field with $a(t)=e^{Ht}$ and constant $H>0$, write $\phi=e^{-3Ht/2}u$. The equation becomes

$$\begin{align}
\ddot u+\left(m^2-\frac94H^2\right)u=0. \tag{2.6}
\end{align}$$

If $m>3H/2$, u oscillates at $\nu=\sqrt{m^2-9H^2/4}$, and the oscillation envelope is exactly $a^{-3/2}$. At $m=3H/2$, $u=C_1+C_2t$. For $m<3H/2$, the two independent solutions are

$$\begin{align}
\phi=C_+e^{(-3H/2+\sqrt{9H^2/4-m^2})t} +C_-e^{(-3H/2-\sqrt{9H^2/4-m^2})t}.
\end{align}$$

In particular, $m=0$ admits a constant mode and a mode proportional to $a^{-3}$. There is no universal $a^{-3/2}$ envelope in this light-field regime. For a rapidly oscillating massive field, $m\gg H$, averaging over many oscillations but little expansion gives approximately equal mean kinetic and potential energies and $\langle p\rangle\simeq0$.

### Complex Scalars

For $\Phi=(\phi_1+i\phi_2)/\sqrt2$, the free action

$$\begin{align}
I_\Phi=-\int e\left(\partial_\mu\Phi^*\partial^\mu\Phi+m^2|\Phi|^2\right)d^4x
\end{align}$$

is the sum of two real-scalar actions. The global transformation $\Phi\mapsto e^{-i\alpha}\Phi$ has current

$$\begin{align}
j^\mu=-i(\Phi^*\partial^\mu\Phi-\Phi\partial^\mu\Phi^*),\qquad \bar\nabla_\mu j^\mu=0
\end{align}$$

on the equations of motion. This is a global Noether current, not an automatically positive local probability density or an electromagnetic source. Interactions can couple the two real fields; their independence here follows from the specified quadratic action.

## 3. Maxwell Theory: the Field Strength Is an Exterior Derivative

Let $A=A_\mu dx^\mu$ be a real one-form. Its gauge-invariant field strength is

$$\begin{align}
F=dA,\qquad F_{\mu\nu}=\partial_\mu A_\nu-\partial_\nu A_\mu. \tag{3.1}
\end{align}$$

This definition gives $dF=0$ and $F\mapsto F$ under $A\mapsto A+d\lambda$. It does not use an affine connection. With torsion,

$$\begin{align}
\nabla_\mu A_\nu-\nabla_\nu A_\mu =F_{\mu\nu}-T^\rho{}_{\mu\nu}A_\rho. \tag{3.2}
\end{align}$$

The right-hand extra term is precisely why antisymmetrizing a torsionful covariant derivative is not a substitute for the exterior derivative.

For an explicit test, choose a compatible connection whose torsion contains $T^0{}_{12}=\tau$ in a local Minkowski-coordinate patch. The pure-gauge potential $A=d t$ has $F=0$. The expression $\nabla_1A_2-\nabla_2A_1$ is nevertheless $-\tau$. A gauge transformation from zero potential has created a nonzero value of that alternative object. It cannot represent the gauge-invariant Maxwell field strength in this theory.

The Maxwell action and its variation are

$$\begin{align}
I_M=-\frac14\int eF_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu}d^4x,\qquad \delta I_M=\int e(\bar\nabla_\mu F^{\mu\nu})\delta A_\nu d^4x -\int_{\partial M}F^{\mu\nu}\delta A_\nu d\Sigma_\mu.
\end{align}$$

Thus, for compact variations or appropriate fixed boundary data,

$$\begin{align}
\bar\nabla_\mu F^{\mu\nu} =\frac1e\partial_\mu(eF^{\mu\nu})=0. \tag{3.3}
\end{align}$$

The equality uses the antisymmetry of F and the symmetry of the Levi-Civita connection. Holding the metric fixed, neither the action nor this equation involves the independent torsion. The field still has electromagnetic polarizations and stress-energy; absence of dependence on an independent connection does not mean absence of photon helicity.

The Hilbert stress is

$$\begin{align}
T_{\mu\nu}=F_{\mu\rho}F_\nu{}^\rho-\frac14g_{\mu\nu}F_{\alpha\beta}F^{\alpha\beta}. \tag{3.4}
\end{align}$$

It is traceless in four dimensions. In an orthonormal frame its energy density is $(|\mathbf E|^2+|\mathbf B|^2)/2$. An isotropic ensemble has radiation pressure $p=\varepsilon/3$; an individual electromagnetic configuration need not have isotropic stress.

### Potentials, Gauge Fixing and Curvature

For any one-form A, commuting Levi-Civita derivatives gives

$$\begin{align}
\bar\nabla^\mu F_{\mu\nu} =\bar\Box A_\nu-\bar\nabla_\nu(\bar\nabla_\mu A^\mu) -\bar R_\nu{}^\rho A_\rho. \tag{3.5}
\end{align}$$

The Lorenz gauge is $\bar\nabla_\mu A^\mu=0$. It gives

$$\begin{align}
\bar\Box A_\nu-\bar R_\nu{}^\rho A_\rho=0.
\end{align}$$

To impose it, a gauge parameter must solve $\bar\Box\lambda=-\bar\nabla_\mu A^\mu$, with admissible initial and boundary data. A local calculation does not guarantee a chosen global gauge on every topology or boundary problem. Residual transformations satisfy $\bar\Box\lambda=0$.

The displayed Ricci term is a derivative-commutator identity for the potential. It is not the sole cause of gravitational optics: light is also deflected in Ricci-flat geometries. In geometric optics, inserting a rapidly varying phase with $k_\mu=\partial_\mu\theta$ gives $k^2=0$ and transverse leading amplitude. Since k is a gradient,

$$\begin{align}
k^\nu\bar\nabla_\nu k_\mu=\tfrac12\partial_\mu(k^2)=0.
\end{align}$$

The rays are therefore metric-null geodesics at this leading order. This is an approximation with wavelength small compared with the geometric variation scale, not a statement about every exact field configuration.

## 4. Proca Theory: a Constraint Rather than a Gauge Choice

For a real massive one-form, choose the exterior-derivative action

$$\begin{align}
I_P=\int e\left[-\frac14F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu}-\frac12m^2A_\mu A^\mu\right]d^4x,\qquad m>0. \tag{4.1}
\end{align}$$

The mass term breaks the Maxwell gauge symmetry. The choice $F=dA$ remains a definite, connection-independent model choice; without gauge symmetry it is not forced by gauge invariance. Adding torsion-dependent terms would define another theory.

Variation gives

$$\begin{align}
\bar\nabla_\mu F^{\mu\nu}-m^2A^\nu=0. \tag{4.2}
\end{align}$$

For an antisymmetric tensor F,

$$\begin{align}
\bar\nabla_\nu\bar\nabla_\mu F^{\mu\nu} =\tfrac12[\bar\nabla_\nu,\bar\nabla_\mu]F^{\mu\nu}=0.
\end{align}$$

The commutator reduces to contractions of F with the symmetric Ricci tensor, which vanish. Taking the divergence of (4.2) therefore yields

$$\begin{align}
\bar\nabla_\mu A^\mu=0,\qquad (\bar\Box-m^2)A_\nu-\bar R_\nu{}^\rho A_\rho=0. \tag{4.3}
\end{align}$$

Here the Lorenz condition is a consequence, not a gauge fixing. Its hypotheses include constant nonzero mass and the stated equation. With an external source defined by $\bar\nabla_\mu F^{\mu\nu}-m^2A^\nu=J^\nu$, it becomes $-m^2\bar\nabla_\mu A^\mu=\bar\nabla_\mu J^\mu$. If $m=m(x)$, the constrained quantity is $\bar\nabla_\mu(m^2A^\mu)$ instead. None of these statements licenses replacing the bar divergence by an uncorrected torsionful divergence.

In Minkowski spacetime take $A^\mu=\epsilon^\mu e^{-i\omega t+ikz}$ and $p^\mu=(\omega,0,0,k)$. The equations imply $p_\mu\epsilon^\mu=0$ and $\omega^2=k^2+m^2$. Three polarization vectors are

$$\begin{align}
\epsilon_{(1)}=(0,1,0,0),\quad \epsilon_{(2)}=(0,0,1,0),\quad \epsilon_{(L)}=(k/m,0,0,\omega/m).
\end{align}$$

The longitudinal vector is orthogonal to p and has unit positive norm. There is no residual Maxwell gauge transformation that removes it. In a constrained initial-value description, the time component of (4.2) is a Gauss-type constraint, not a fourth freely propagating polarization.

The Proca stress adds $m^2(A_\mu A_\nu-g_{\mu\nu}A^2/2)$ to (3.4). Its trace is $-m^2A^2$ and its orthonormal energy density is

$$\begin{align}
T_{00}=\tfrac12(E^2+B^2)+\tfrac12m^2(A_0^2+|\mathbf A|^2).
\end{align}$$

Complex Maxwell or Proca fields can be defined as two real copies. For $Z=(A+iB)/\sqrt2$, the normalization $\mathscr L=-F_Z^*F_Z/2-m^2Z^*Z$ gives exactly the sum of the two real Proca Lagrangians, with the mass term absent for Maxwell. A global rotation mixing the two copies is not an electromagnetic minimal coupling. In the Maxwell case, potential-based Noether currents also require care with gauge dependence; they should not be promoted to a gauge-invariant local photon-number density.

## 5. Spinors: Specify the Clifford Representation with the Equation

### Frames and the Spin Connection

Spinors are defined using local orthonormal frames and a chosen spin structure; they are not ordinary four-component coordinate vectors. Assume the oriented, time-oriented Lorentzian manifold admits such a structure. A local coframe $e^a=e^a{}_{\mu}dx^\mu$ and its inverse obey

$$\begin{align}
g_{\mu\nu}=\eta_{ab}e^a{}_{\mu}e^b{}_{\nu},\qquad \eta_{ab}=\mathrm{diag}(-1,1,1,1),\qquad e_a{}^\mu e^a{}_{\nu}=\delta^\mu_\nu.
\end{align}$$

Frame indices a,b run from 0 to 3. The metric-compatible frame connection is defined by the tetrad postulate

$$\begin{align}
\partial_\mu e^a{}_{\nu}-\Gamma^\rho{}_{\mu\nu}e^a{}_{\rho} +\omega^a{}_{b\mu}e^b{}_{\nu}=0,\qquad \omega_{ab\mu}=-\omega_{ba\mu}. \tag{5.1}
\end{align}$$

Its torsion two-form is $T^a=de^a+\omega^a{}_b\wedge e^b$. Different local frames are related by Lorentz transformations, with the corresponding spin lifts acting on spinors.

### A Consistent Matrix Dictionary

We use four-by-four matrices satisfying the deliberately explicit relation

$$\begin{align}
\{\gamma^a,\gamma^b\}=-2\eta^{ab}\mathbf1,\qquad (\gamma^0)^\dagger=\gamma^0,\qquad (\gamma^i)^\dagger=-\gamma^i. \tag{5.2}
\end{align}$$

Thus the matrices have the usual particle-physics squares, while the spacetime metric remains negative-time. One concrete representation is

$$
\gamma^0=\begin{pmatrix}0&I_2\\ I_2&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
\gamma^i=\begin{pmatrix}0&\sigma^i\\-\sigma^i&0\end{pmatrix},
$$

where the Pauli matrices obey $\sigma^i\sigma^j=\delta^{ij}I_2+i\epsilon^{ijk}\sigma^k$. Define

$$\begin{align}
\bar\psi=\psi^\dagger\gamma^0,\quad \gamma^{ab}=\tfrac12[\gamma^a,\gamma^b],\quad \gamma_5=i\gamma^0\gamma^1\gamma^2\gamma^3 =\mathrm{diag}(-I_2,I_2),\quad \gamma^\mu=e_a{}^\mu\gamma^a.
\end{align}$$

These choices fix the spinor connection and derivatives:

$$\begin{align}
\Omega_\mu=-\tfrac14\omega_{ab\mu}\gamma^{ab},\qquad D_\mu\psi=\partial_\mu\psi+\Omega_\mu\psi,\qquad D_\mu\bar\psi=\partial_\mu\bar\psi-\bar\psi\Omega_\mu. \tag{5.3}
\end{align}$$

The minus sign in $\Omega$ belongs with the Clifford sign in (5.2). In fact,

$$\begin{align}
[\Omega_\mu,\gamma^a]=-\omega^a{}_{b\mu}\gamma^b,\qquad \Omega_\mu^\dagger\gamma^0+\gamma^0\Omega_\mu=0.
\end{align}$$

Together with (5.1), the first identity gives covariant constancy of the curved gamma matrices. The second makes the two definitions of the derivative of $\bar\psi$ compatible. It would be inconsistent to change the Clifford sign while keeping the connection representation unchanged.

Curvature is encoded by the frame-connection two-form $\mathcal R^a{}_b=d\omega^a{}_b+\omega^a{}_c\wedge\omega^c{}_b$. Commuting the derivatives in the tetrad postulate gives

$$\begin{align}
\mathcal R^a{}_{b\mu\nu}=e^a{}_{\rho}e_b{}^\sigma R^\rho{}_{\sigma\mu\nu},\qquad [\partial_\mu+\Omega_\mu,\partial_\nu+\Omega_\nu]\psi =-\tfrac14\mathcal R_{ab\mu\nu}\gamma^{ab}\psi.
\end{align}$$

The second formula treats these as operators in a coordinate spinor trivialization. If the derivative label on $D_\nu\psi$ is also differentiated as a covector index, the full tensor-Hessian commutator additionally contains $-T^\rho{}_{\mu\nu}D_\rho\psi$. The two operations should not be confused.

### Flat Equation, Mass Shell and Current

The flat equation is $(i\gamma^\mu\partial_\mu-m)\psi=0$. Multiplication by the operator with the opposite mass sign gives

$$\begin{align}
(i\gamma^\mu\partial_\mu+m)(i\gamma^\nu\partial_\nu-m)\psi =(\Box_\eta-m^2)\psi=0. \tag{5.4}
\end{align}$$

For a plane wave, the first-order matrix is $P-m$ with $P=\omega\gamma^0-k_i\gamma^i$. Since $P^2=(\omega^2-|\mathbf k|^2)I_4$,

$$\begin{align}
\det(P-mI_4)=(\omega^2-|\mathbf k|^2-m^2)^2.
\end{align}$$

The mass shell is therefore $p^2=-m^2$, consistently with the scalar and Proca fields. The factor i in the first-order equation is consistent because the Clifford relation is $-2g$, not $+2g$.

For a commuting spinor wavefunction, the current is

$$\begin{align}
j^\mu=\bar\psi\gamma^\mu\psi,\qquad j^0=\psi^\dagger\psi,\qquad j^i=\psi^\dagger\alpha^i\psi,\quad \alpha^i=\gamma^0\gamma^i.
\end{align}$$

The $\alpha^i$ and $\beta=\gamma^0$ are Hermitian. The Dirac equation and its adjoint yield $\partial_\mu j^\mu=0$. The nonnegative density statement refers to commuting wavefunctions, not to an ordering prescription for quantum field operators.

## 6. The Hermitian Dirac Action and Axial Torsion

### Variation and the Trace Term

Use the symmetrized action

$$\begin{align}
I_D=\int e\left[\frac i2\left(\bar\psi\gamma^\mu D_\mu\psi-(D_\mu\bar\psi)\gamma^\mu\psi\right)-m\bar\psi\psi\right]d^4x. \tag{6.1}
\end{align}$$

For the moment use independent compact variations of $\psi$ and $\bar\psi$. The key integration-by-parts identity is

$$\begin{align}
\frac1e\partial_\mu(e\bar\chi\gamma^\mu\psi) =(D_\mu\bar\chi)\gamma^\mu\psi+\bar\chi\gamma^\mu D_\mu\psi +t_\mu\bar\chi\gamma^\mu\psi.
\end{align}$$

Applying it to the variation gives

$$\begin{align}
i\gamma^\mu D_\mu\psi+\frac i2t_\mu\gamma^\mu\psi-m\psi=0, \tag{6.2}
\end{align}$$

and the adjoint equation

$$\begin{align}
i(D_\mu\bar\psi)\gamma^\mu+\frac i2t_\mu\bar\psi\gamma^\mu+m\bar\psi=0.
\end{align}$$

Multiplying by $\bar\psi$ or $\psi$ and adding shows $\nabla_\mu j^\mu=-t_\mu j^\mu$, hence

$$\begin{align}
\bar\nabla_\mu j^\mu=\frac1e\partial_\mu(ej^\mu)=0. \tag{6.3}
\end{align}$$

For a local phase variation $\delta\psi=-i\alpha(x)\psi$, the action density varies by $ej^\mu\partial_\mu\alpha$. Integration by parts therefore gives precisely this volume-form Noether conservation law. On a region between spacelike slices, its integral gives the same flux through both slices if the side-boundary flux vanishes. A pointwise time derivative of the density alone need not vanish: spatial transport and the volume factor matter.

### Why Only Axial Torsion Remains in the Minimal Action

Let $\mathcal C_{ab\mu}=\omega_{ab\mu}-\bar\omega_{ab\mu}$, so

$$\begin{align}
\mathcal C_{ab\mu}=e_{a\rho}K^\rho{}_{\mu\nu}e_b{}^\nu,\qquad \mathcal C_{abc}=K_{acb}
\end{align}$$

in an orthonormal frame. Its last slot is the derivative slot; it is antisymmetric in a,b. Inserting $\Omega-\bar\Omega=-\mathcal C_{ab\mu}\gamma^{ab}/4$ into (6.1) gives the actual connection-dependent term

$$\begin{align}
\mathscr L_K=-\frac i8\mathcal C_{abc}\bar\psi\{\gamma^c,\gamma^{ab}\}\psi. \tag{6.4}
\end{align}$$

The anticommutator is twice the completely antisymmetrized product $\gamma^{cab}$. Therefore only $\mathcal C_{[abc]}$ survives. The trace and the remaining non-axial tensor components do not couple in this minimal Hermitian action. In the equation (6.2), their apparent contributions through $\gamma^\mu\Omega_\mu$ are canceled by the displayed trace term. Omitting that term before varying a general torsionful action would lose this cancellation.

### Eliminating Torsion in Minimal Einstein–Cartan Theory

Now add $I_g=(2\kappa)^{-1}\int eR(\Gamma)d^4x$, with $\kappa=8\pi G>0$. Variations of the connection are compactly supported. The derivative-contorsion part of its scalar curvature is a boundary term. The Cartan equation is algebraic; the fully antisymmetric spin source of (6.4) gives a fully antisymmetric torsion, with other components zero. Parametrize it by

$$\begin{align}
T_{abc}=\epsilon_{abcd}v^d,\qquad \epsilon_{0123}=+1,\qquad K_{abc}=T_{abc}/2,
\end{align}$$

and define $a_5^a=\bar\psi\gamma^a\gamma_5\psi$. The two terms depending on v reduce to

$$\begin{align}
R(\Gamma)-\bar R=-\tfrac14T_{abc}T^{abc}=\tfrac32v_av^a,\qquad \mathscr L_K=\tfrac34v_a a_5^a.
\end{align}$$

The resulting square completion exposes the coefficient without substituting a matter equation into the action:

$$\begin{align}
\mathscr L_{\mathrm{torsion}} =\frac{3}{4\kappa}v^2+\frac34v\cdot a_5 =\frac{3}{4\kappa}(v+\kappa a_5/2)^2-\frac{3\kappa}{16}a_5^2. \tag{6.5}
\end{align}$$

Varying v gives $v^a=-\kappa a_5^a/2$. Equivalently,

$$\begin{align}
T_{abc}=-\frac{\kappa}{2}\epsilon_{abcd}a_5^d,\qquad \mathscr L_{\mathrm{eff}}=\mathscr L_D(\bar D)-\frac{3\kappa}{16}a_{5a}a_5^a. \tag{6.6}
\end{align}$$

The effective Dirac equation is

$$\begin{align}
i\gamma^\mu\bar D_\mu\psi-m\psi -\frac{3\kappa}{8}a_{5\mu}\gamma^\mu\gamma_5\psi=0. \tag{6.7}
\end{align}$$

The factor two arises by varying the product of the two axial bilinears. Signs and coefficients in these equations belong to the action, orientation and Clifford dictionary just specified. The contact interaction preserves the global phase symmetry and its conserved volume-form current. This local action calculation does not supply a cosmological fluid average or a nonsingularity theorem.

## 7. A Worked Curved-Spinor Example

Take a torsion-free, spatially flat FLRW metric in conformal time and the coframe

$$\begin{align}
e^0=a(\eta)d\eta,\qquad e^i=a(\eta)dx^i.
\end{align}$$

Solving $de^a+\bar\omega^a{}_b\wedge e^b=0$ gives

$$\begin{align}
\bar\omega^i{}_0=\bar\omega^0{}_i=\frac{a'}{a}dx^i,\qquad \bar\omega_{0i}=-\frac{a'}{a}dx^i.
\end{align}$$

With (5.3), $\bar\Omega_\eta=0$ and $\bar\Omega_i=(a'/2a)\gamma^0\gamma^i$. Each spatial index contributes $(a'/2a)\gamma^0$ to $\gamma^i\bar\Omega_i$. Thus the free equation is

$$\begin{align}
i\gamma^0\left(\partial_\eta+\frac{3a'}{2a}\right)\psi +i\gamma^i\partial_i\psi-am\psi=0. \tag{7.1}
\end{align}$$

The substitution $\psi=a^{-3/2}\chi$ removes the connection term:

$$\begin{align}
(i\gamma^a\partial_a-am)\chi=0. \tag{7.2}
\end{align}$$

For a free massless spinor this is the flat equation in conformal coordinates. For a massive spinor it has a time-dependent conformal mass. This example sets torsion to zero and does not include the dimensionful contact interaction (6.6); the full self-interacting Einstein–Cartan theory should not be declared conformally invariant from (7.2).

In the massless case $j^\eta=a^{-4}\chi^\dagger\chi$. The conserved slice integral is $\int a^4j^\eta d^3x$, which reduces to $\int\chi^\dagger\chi d^3x$ when there is no boundary flux. A comoving Fourier wavevector $\mathbf k$ corresponds to physical momentum $|\mathbf k|/a$. Differentiating with respect to cosmic proper time gives $\dot p=-Hp$.

## 8. Chirality, Reality and the Statistics of Classical Spinors

A classical commuting spinor wavefunction and a Grassmann-valued classical fermion are different mathematical objects. The latter's components anticommute. This distinction changes which quadratic bilinears vanish and is indispensable when imposing a Majorana condition.

### Weyl Restriction

Since $\gamma_5^2=1$, the projectors $P_L=(1-\gamma_5)/2$ and $P_R=(1+\gamma_5)/2$ select two-component spinors. The massless free equation preserves each chirality; the Dirac mass couples the two. For a left-handed spinor,

$$\begin{align}
\gamma_5\psi_L=-\psi_L,\qquad a_5^\mu=-j^\mu.
\end{align}$$

In our matrix representation $\psi_L=(\xi,0)$ with two-component $\xi$, so $j^0=\xi^\dagger\xi$ and $j^i=-\xi^\dagger\sigma^i\xi$. For commuting components, direct two-component multiplication gives

$$\begin{align}
j_\mu j^\mu=0,\qquad j_\mu\gamma^\mu\psi_L=0.
\end{align}$$

Consequently the isolated single-spinor self-interaction in (6.7) vanishes on that commuting Weyl restriction. This does not imply a vanishing spin current or a vanishing algebraic torsion field, nor does it remove interactions with another species. The identities also cannot be transferred to Grassmann products by commuting their factors silently.

### Majorana Restriction

Define charge conjugation in the chosen representation by

$$\begin{align}
\psi^c=B\psi^*,\qquad B=i\gamma^2,\qquad BB^*=1,\qquad B(\gamma^a)^*B^{-1}=-\gamma^a.
\end{align}$$

These relations make the free Dirac equation invariant under conjugation. A Majorana spinor satisfies $\psi^c=\psi$. Equivalently $\bar\psi=\psi^TC$, where $C=B\gamma^0$ and $C^T=-C$. The relevant matrix symmetries are

$$\begin{align}
(C\gamma^a)^T=C\gamma^a,\qquad (C\gamma^a\gamma_5)^T=-C\gamma^a\gamma_5. \tag{8.1}
\end{align}$$

For commuting components, a bilinear $\psi^TM\psi$ selects the symmetric part of M. It follows that a commuting Majorana wavefunction has zero axial bilinear and zero scalar bilinear, while its vector bilinear can be nonzero. The usual symmetrized Majorana Dirac Lagrangian is then degenerate: its kinetic terms cancel because $C\gamma^a$ is symmetric, and its mass term vanishes because C is antisymmetric. Imposing a real condition on solutions of a linear wave equation is not thereby a nondegenerate physical Majorana matter action.

For Grassmann components, the same product selects the antisymmetric part. The vector bilinear of a single Majorana field then vanishes, while scalar and axial bilinears are allowed. A classical fermionic Majorana action uses this graded algebra, with the usual factor one-half for the reality constraint. Its axial torsion response is not excluded by a supposed real-versus-complex criterion. No quantization or operator-product prescription is supplied by these algebraic statements.

## 9. What the Calculations Establish

For the minimal scalar action and the chosen exterior-derivative vector actions, the independent connection does not enter. Their field equations involve Levi-Civita derivatives, even when the ambient connection has torsion. The Hermitian Dirac action does depend on the spin connection, but its minimal torsion coupling is purely axial after the trace bookkeeping is done. In minimal Einstein–Cartan theory this produces the contact term derived in (6.6).

Flat-space mass shells and characteristic cones must also be distinguished. All the free massive examples have $p^2=-m^2$ and subluminal finite-momentum group velocity. Their wavefront characteristics are governed by highest-derivative terms, which are metric-null in these models; mass is a lower-order term. Maxwell theory first requires handling its gauge redundancy. Algebraic axial interactions in the specified Dirac equation do not change its principal differential operator. These facts are not a theorem about every possible nonminimal torsion coupling.

Finally, a stress tensor, a microscopic spinor bilinear and an isotropic spin fluid are different objects. Passing from the contact action to cosmological pressure or a proposed bounce requires a state, an averaging prescription and the gravitational field equations. Those additional assumptions must be stated rather than read off from a spin label or a flat-space mass shell.
