# Thermal Fields on a Conformal Cylinder

**EC-20 — Finite-temperature twistor formulations. DRAFT; bounded review recorded.**

A twistor description of classical massless solutions does not by itself select a thermal state. A usable finite-temperature formulation must specify a time generator, a positive-frequency splitting, a field Hilbert space, constraints and a trace. We construct a field-theory reference with those data: scalar, Weyl and Maxwell fields on a round Einstein cylinder; a scalar thermal Green function evaluated in three representations; and an exactly projected two-field mode ensemble. These provide quantities that a proposed thermal twistor formulation would have to reproduce.

Units are $c=\hbar=k_B=1$, with physical signature $(-,+,+,+)$. Gravity is not dynamical in this reference. The Levi-Civita metric and free matter actions are prescribed; no auxiliary-torsion interaction or area-dependent dispersion is included. This is a test-field model choice, not an inference that a vanishing mean axial current removes spin correlations.

## 1. Spacetime, Flow and the Auxiliary Thermal Circle

Take the universal-cover Einstein cylinder

$$\begin{align}
ds^2=-dt^2+R^2\bigl[d\chi^2+\sin^2\chi (d\theta^2+\sin^2\theta\,d\varphi^2)\bigr],\qquad t\in\mathbb R,\quad R>0. \tag{1}
\end{align}$$

The spatial volume and four-dimensional scalar curvature are

$$\begin{align}
V=2\pi^2R^3,\qquad \mathcal R=\frac6{R^2}. \tag{2}
\end{align}$$

The flow is $K=\partial_t$, with $K^2=-1$. Its generator $H$ measures the energy of these static observers, so $\beta=1/T$ is their inverse temperature without an additional lapse factor. The compact spatial geometry is physical background data. By contrast $\tau\sim\tau+\beta$ in the Euclidean thermal construction is an auxiliary imaginary-time circle, not a periodic identification of Lorentzian time.

The connection with conformal massless descriptions can be made explicit. On the connected diamond $|t|/R+\chi<\pi$, put $D=\cos(t/R)+\cos\chi>0$ and define Minkowski coordinates

$$\begin{align}
t_M=\frac{R\sin(t/R)}D,\qquad r_M=\frac{R\sin\chi}D. \tag{3}
\end{align}$$

Their metric is $ds_M^2=D^{-2}ds^2$. The cylinder time flow pushes forward to

$$\begin{align}
K=\frac{R^2+t_M^2+r_M^2}{2R^2}\partial_{t_M} +\frac{t_Mr_M}{R^2}\partial_{r_M}. \tag{4}
\end{align}$$

It is not Minkowski time translation. A classical conformal solution map therefore does not identify a cylinder Gibbs state with a homogeneous Minkowski Gibbs state. A twistor description must carry the chosen flow and quantum inner product as well as classical field data. Projective homogeneity can label a field representation; it does not set the independent parameter $\beta$ below.

We use smooth fields on the closed spatial sphere, completed in their natural $L^2$ and Sobolev domains. The spin structure of $S^3$ is unique. The Euclidean time circle has two spin structures: antiperiodic fermions implement the ordinary thermal trace; periodic fermions implement a parity insertion under the conditions discussed in Section 4. A flat gauge twist is further boundary data, not a third spin structure. The spectral/domain distinctions follow [AST-17](<AST-17.md#1-the-operator-includes-its-domain>); specifying a stationary state and flow follows [AST-23](<AST-23.md#L388-L405>).

## 2. Actual Field Operators and Their Thermal Spectra

### 2.1 The Conformal Scalar and Its Gap

For one real scalar use

$$\begin{align}
S_E=\frac12\int_0^\beta d\tau\int_{S^3}dV \left[(\partial_\tau\phi)^2+|\nabla\phi|^2 +\frac{\mathcal R}{6}\phi^2\right]. \tag{5}
\end{align}$$

The coupling $\xi=1/6$ is the four-dimensional conformal choice. It gives the operator

$$\begin{align}
P_E=-\partial_\tau^2-\Delta_{S^3}+\frac1{R^2}. \tag{6}
\end{align}$$

Scalar harmonics of degree $\ell\ge0$ obey $-R^2\Delta_{S^3}=\ell(\ell+2)$ and have multiplicity $(\ell+1)^2$. Consequently

$$\begin{align}
\omega_\ell=\frac{\ell+1}{R},\qquad \lambda_{m\ell}(P_E)=\left(\frac{2\pi m}{\beta}\right)^2 +\frac{(\ell+1)^2}{R^2}. \tag{7}
\end{align}$$

Even the spatially constant mode is gapped. This is a curvature contribution to a specified operator, present at zero temperature, not an interaction-generated thermal mass.

The contrast with minimal massless coupling is consequential. With $\xi=0$, the spatial constant mode has zero frequency and the $m=0,\ell=0$ Euclidean eigenvalue vanishes. For a noncompact real field, its normalized coordinate $Q_0=\sqrt V\,\phi_0$ has $H_0=P_0^2/2$. Its full heat trace diverges because $Q_0$ ranges over the real line. Per unit $Q_0$ length the diagonal heat trace is

$$\begin{align}
\frac{\operatorname{Tr}e^{-\beta P_0^2/2}}{\int dQ_0} =\int\frac{dp}{2\pi}e^{-\beta p^2/2} =\frac1{\sqrt{2\pi\beta}}. \tag{8}
\end{align}$$

Per unit uniform field shift $\phi_0$, the answer instead contains $\sqrt V$. Adding a small mass makes this mode an oscillator with partition factor $[2\sinh(\beta m/2)]^{-1}\sim(\beta m)^{-1}$. Dividing out its coordinate volume, lifting it with a mass, and using the conformal curvature gap are distinct prescriptions. Finite temperature alone does not fix this zero-mode problem.

### 2.2 Deriving the Spinor and Transverse-Vector Branches

The sphere is also the group $SU(2)$. Choose an oriented global orthonormal frame with $[e_i,e_j]=2\epsilon_{ijk}e_k/R$ and set $L_i=iRe_i/2$, so $[L_i,L_j]=i\epsilon_{ijk}L_k$. Peter–Weyl decomposition gives orbital spin $j=0,1/2,\ldots$ and an independent multiplicity $2j+1$ from the other group factor. For scalar functions, $-R^2\Delta=4\mathbf L^2$, reproducing (7) with $\ell=2j$.

For spinors use the skew Riemannian Clifford matrices $c_i=i\sigma_i$, as in [AST-17](<AST-17.md#1-the-operator-includes-its-domain>), and $\mathbf S_{1/2}=\boldsymbol\sigma/2$. The Levi-Civita spin connection contributes $\sum\epsilon_{ijk}c_ic_jc_k/(4R)=3/(2R)$, hence

$$\begin{align}
RD_3=4\mathbf L\cdot\mathbf S_{1/2}+\frac32. \tag{9}
\end{align}$$

Using $\mathbf J=\mathbf L+\mathbf S$ and $2\mathbf L\cdot\mathbf S=\mathbf J^2-\mathbf L^2-\mathbf S^2$, the $J=j+1/2$ branch has eigenvalue $2j+3/2$ and multiplicity $(2j+2)(2j+1)$. The $J=j-1/2$ branch, present for $j\ge1/2$, has eigenvalue $-(2j+1/2)$ and multiplicity $2j(2j+1)$. Relabelling the two branches gives

$$\begin{align}
\operatorname{spec}D_3: \quad \pm\frac{\ell+3/2}{R},\qquad d_\ell=(\ell+1)(\ell+2)\quad\text{for each sign},\qquad\ell\ge0. \tag{10}
\end{align}$$

A single complex Weyl field uses positive-energy particles and the conjugate antiparticles from these branches. The thermal multiplicity is $2d_\ell$, not $4d_\ell$. In the flat large-volume counting this is one particle helicity and one antiparticle helicity, not a Dirac field's four states.

For Maxwell theory impose Gauss' constraint and retain coexact one-forms, rather than four unconstrained vector components. For vector components use $(S_i)_{jk}=-i\epsilon_{ijk}$. In the same frame, $(\operatorname{curl}A)_i=\epsilon_{ijk}e_jA_k-2A_i/R$, so

$$\begin{align}
R\operatorname{curl}=-2(\mathbf L\cdot\mathbf S_1+1). \tag{11}
\end{align}$$

Coupling spin $j$ to spin one gives $J=j+1,j,j-1$, when allowed, with curl eigenvalues $-2(j+1),0,2j$. The middle branch is the gradient sector: divergence maps it to scalar harmonics, whereas the other irreducible branches are transverse. After removing gradients, the two nonzero branches give frequencies $n/R$, $n\ge2$, each with multiplicity $n^2-1$. There is no harmonic one-form on $S^3$. The nondynamical temporal component is not an extra photon oscillator; temporal gauge holonomy in a charged theory must be treated separately.

The mode data entering the trace are therefore

| Field | Positive frequency | Thermal multiplicity |
|---|---|---|
| One real conformal scalar | $n/R$, $n\ge1$ | $n^2$ |
| One complex Weyl field, including antiparticles | $(\ell+3/2)/R$, $\ell\ge0$ | $2(\ell+1)(\ell+2)$ |
| One Maxwell field | $n/R$, $n\ge2$ | $2(n^2-1)$ |

### 2.3 Letters, Fock Traces and an Explicit Vacuum Scheme

Put $b=\beta/R$ and $q=e^{-b}\in(0,1)$. A letter function is the one-particle trace, not the full field partition function. The spectral sums give

$$
\begin{aligned}
 z_S(q)&=\sum_{n\ge1}n^2q^n=\frac{q(1+q)}{(1-q)^3},\\
 z_W(q)&=2\sum_{\ell\ge0}(\ell+1)(\ell+2)q^{\ell+3/2}
        =\frac{4q^{3/2}}{(1-q)^3},\\
 z_V(q)&=2\sum_{n\ge2}(n^2-1)q^n
        =\frac{2q^2(3-q)}{(1-q)^3}.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{12}
$$

On the particle/antiparticle Fock space define $H=E_0+\sum_\lambda\omega_\lambda N_\lambda$, with $\omega_\lambda>0$, bosonic $N_\lambda\in\mathbb N_0$ and fermionic $N_\lambda=0,1$. Its trace is

$$\begin{align}
\log Z=-\beta E_0+\sum_{k=1}^\infty\frac1k \left[z_B(q^k)+(-1)^{k+1}z_F(q^k)\right]. \tag{13}
\end{align}$$

This follows by taking logarithms of $\prod(1-e^{-\beta\omega})^{-1}$ and $\prod(1+e^{-\beta\omega})$. The spherical degeneracies grow polynomially while the Boltzmann factors decay exponentially. The thermal products and their derivatives converge normally on positive-temperature compact intervals. Gauge-coloured versions of (13) are still kinematic traces until their constraints have been imposed.

Vacuum energy is different from this convergent thermal sum. To give an explicit reference, analytically continue the dimensionless energy zeta functions from their convergence half-plane and set $RE_0^\zeta=\tfrac12[\zeta_B(-1)-\zeta_F(-1)]$. For the three fields,

$$
\begin{aligned}
 RE_{0,S}^\zeta&=\tfrac12\zeta_{\mathrm R}(-3)=\frac1{240},\\
 RE_{0,W}^\zeta&=-\left[\zeta_{\mathrm H}(-3,3/2)
                      -\tfrac14\zeta_{\mathrm H}(-1,3/2)\right]=\frac{17}{960},\\
 RE_{0,V}^\zeta&=\zeta_{\mathrm R}(-3)-\zeta_{\mathrm R}(-1)=\frac{11}{120}.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{14}
$$

Here $\zeta_{\mathrm R}$ and $\zeta_{\mathrm H}$ denote the Riemann and Hurwitz zeta functions. The fermionic minus sign multiplies half the particle-plus-antiparticle multiplicity. Equation (14) is an analytic prescription, not evaluation of a convergent sum of vacuum frequencies.

Nor is it a universal absolute energy. For example an additional finite Euclidean counterterm changes the effective action by

$$\begin{align}
\delta W=c_{R^2}\int_0^\beta d\tau\int dV\,\mathcal R^2 =72\pi^2c_{R^2}\frac\beta R, \qquad \delta E_0=\frac{72\pi^2c_{R^2}}R. \tag{15}
\end{align}$$

The displayed zeta prescription is used without this additional finite term; other gravitational counterterms also require a declared renormalization convention. A temperature-independent shift changes $F$ and $U$, but not $S=\log Z+\beta U$ or the heat capacity at fixed radius. The distinction between analytic continuation and a physical effective action follows [AST-17](<AST-17.md#5-zeta-functions-and-determinants-with-units>).

For a finite-volume numerical reference, the thermal products at $b=1$ give

| Field | $\log Z_{\mathrm{th}}$ | $R U_{\mathrm{th}}$ |
|---|---:|---:|
| Scalar | $2.138365$ | $6.489773$ |
| Weyl | $3.394606$ | $10.935453$ |
| Maxwell | $2.908072$ | $10.606344$ |

The energies are obtained by differentiating the same spectral products, or by summing $d\omega n_{B/F}(\omega)$. Adding (14) uses the stated vacuum scheme. These are free fields on a finite sphere, not an interacting plasma equation of state.

## 3. A Full Scalar Thermal Green Function

Let $\gamma\in(0,\pi)$ be the spatial geodesic angle. Normalize the harmonics by $\int dV\,\overline Y_{\ell A}Y_{\ell' B}=\delta_{\ell\ell'}\delta_{AB}$. The addition formula is

$$\begin{align}
\sum_A Y_{\ell A}(\Omega)\overline Y_{\ell A}(\Omega') =\frac{\ell+1}{2\pi^2R^3} \frac{\sin[(\ell+1)\gamma]}{\sin\gamma}. \tag{16}
\end{align}$$

Its coincident angular limit is the multiplicity divided by $V$. Using the oscillator covariance already fixed in [EC-05](<EC-05.md#L296-L314>), the field inverse of (6) is, for $0<\tau<\beta$,

$$\begin{align}
G_\beta(\tau,\gamma)=\frac1{4\pi^2R^2\sin\gamma}
 \sum_{n=1}^\infty\sin(n\gamma) \frac{e^{-n\tau/R}+e^{-n(\beta-\tau)/R}}{1-e^{-n\beta/R}}. \tag{17}
\end{align}$$

The gap and positive separation make this spatial-mode sum convergent. At zero temperature the geometric sine sum gives

$$\begin{align}
G_\infty(\tau,\gamma)= \frac1{8\pi^2R^2[\cosh(\tau/R)-\cos\gamma]}. \tag{18}
\end{align}$$

For small physical separation it approaches $[4\pi^2(\tau^2+R^2\gamma^2)]^{-1}$, the four-dimensional scalar normalization of (5), not the logarithmic two-dimensional scalar covariance. Periodizing (18) supplies a second representation,

$$\begin{align}
G_\beta(\tau,\gamma)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb Z} G_\infty(\tau+k\beta,\gamma). \tag{19}
\end{align}$$

It is periodic and has the correct local source. Uniqueness follows because (6) is strictly positive on the closed Euclidean cylinder.

A third representation resolves spatial propagation at a fixed Matsubara frequency. Write $\nu_m=2\pi m/\beta$ and $a=|\nu_m|R$. The zonal inverse of $-\Delta_{S^3}+R^{-2}+\nu_m^2$ is

$$\begin{align}
\mathcal G_a(\gamma)=\frac1{4\pi R\sin\gamma} \frac{\sinh[a(\pi-\gamma)]}{\sinh(\pi a)},\qquad a>0. \tag{20}
\end{align}$$

To derive it, write $y(\gamma)=\sin\gamma\,\mathcal G_a(\gamma)$. Away from the source the radial equation becomes $y''=a^2y$. Regularity at the antipode requires $y(\pi)=0$, while the near-source form $\mathcal G_a\sim1/(4\pi R\gamma)$ fixes its coefficient. Equivalently the outward delta-source flux is $\lim_{\gamma\to0}[-4\pi R\gamma^2\partial_\gamma\mathcal G_a]=1$.

The zero-frequency term is not zero and is not a zero eigenmode of (6):

$$\begin{align}
\mathcal G_0(\gamma)=\frac{\pi-\gamma}{4\pi^2R\sin\gamma}. \tag{21}
\end{align}$$

The Matsubara reconstruction is consequently

$$\begin{align}
G_\beta(\tau,\gamma)=\frac1\beta \left[\mathcal G_0(\gamma)+2\sum_{m\ge1} \cos(\nu_m\tau)\mathcal G_{|\nu_m|R}(\gamma)\right]. \tag{22}
\end{align}$$

For $\gamma>0$ its frequency terms decay exponentially. The raw double frequency/harmonic series is a distributional inverse; exchanging its sums without a regulator is not justified by these separated formulas. Heat or Abel damping supplies an appropriate intermediate prescription. No finite coincident-point variance or renormalized stress tensor is extracted from (17)–(22).

For example choose $R=3/2$, $\beta=7/5$, $\tau=2/5$, $\gamma=\pi/3$ in one reference length unit. The image sum, spatial-mode sum and frequency sum give respectively

$$
\begin{array}{c|ccc}
 (K,N,M)&\text{images }|k|\le K&\text{harmonics }n\le N&\text{frequencies }m\le M\\ \hline
 (20,120,4)&0.0291538854401&0.0291538855583&0.0291538855583\\
 (40,240,8)&0.0291538855583&0.0291538855583&0.0291538855583\\
 (80,480,16)&0.0291538855583&0.0291538855583&0.0291538855583
 \end{array}
 \tag{23}
$$

These are cutoff-refinement comparisons, not uniform error bounds. Deleting the zero-frequency term would change the answer by $-0.0291708410276$ at this point. The field gap makes that term invertible; it does not authorize omitting it.

## 4. Weyl Modes and the Two Temporal Spin Structures

The spatial operator (9) supplies the Weyl mode projectors and energies $E=\pm(\ell+3/2)/R$. For the Grassmann covariance choose the explicit convention $C=(\partial_\tau+E)^{-1}$. With antiperiodic boundary conditions it is

$$\begin{align}
C_E^{\mathrm A}(\tau)=\frac{e^{-E\tau}}{1+e^{-\beta E}}, \qquad0<\tau<\beta, \qquad C_E^{\mathrm A}(\tau+\beta)=-C_E^{\mathrm A}(\tau). \tag{24}
\end{align}$$

The homogeneous equation holds away from zero and $C_E^{\mathrm A}(0^+)+C_E^{\mathrm A}(\beta^-)=1$ fixes the delta jump. Multiplying by the spatial eigenspinor projectors and summing constructs the field covariance; both spectral signs are needed. This inverse is not a trace of an unbounded negative-energy one-particle Hamiltonian: the thermal Fock trace uses the positive particle/antiparticle energies and the separately prescribed vacuum energy.

The Fourier coefficient is

$$\begin{align}
\int_0^\beta d\tau\,e^{i\omega_m\tau}C_E^{\mathrm A}(\tau) =\frac1{E-i\omega_m},\qquad \omega_m=\frac{(2m+1)\pi}{\beta}. \tag{25}
\end{align}$$

Thus the ordinary time-ordered Green function of [EC-05](<EC-05.md#L265-L294>) is $G=-C^{\mathrm A}$ and has coefficient $(i\omega_m-E)^{-1}$. There is no extra minus sign in trace cyclicity; the fermionic time ordering and the convention for $G$ must be retained.

Periodic Grassmann boundary conditions instead give, on $0<\tau<\beta$,

$$\begin{align}
C_E^{\mathrm P}(\tau)=\frac{e^{-E\tau}}{1-e^{-\beta E}}, \qquad \omega_m^{\mathrm P}=\frac{2\pi m}{\beta}, \qquad C_E^{\mathrm P}(0^+)-C_E^{\mathrm P}(\beta^-)=1. \tag{26}
\end{align}$$

Here $E\ne0$ is required. On this sphere the spatial Dirac gap is $3/(2R)$, so the periodic temporal zero frequency does not create a zero of the full operator. The positive adjoint product is $(\partial_\tau+E)^\dagger(\partial_\tau+E)=-\partial_\tau^2+E^2$, with eigenvalues $\omega_m^2+E^2$; this is not the ordinary square of the first-order operator. The mode inverse is well-defined for either temporal spin structure. This does not determine a general chiral determinant's phase. Our partition functions are defined by the stated Fock trace, not by an unspecified square root of a squared Dirac determinant.

For a parity-preserving Hamiltonian, periodic coherent-state fermions compute $\operatorname{Tr}[(-1)^F e^{-\beta H}]$, rather than the Gibbs partition function. For the Weyl field its excitation factors are $(1-e^{-\beta\omega_\ell})^{2d_\ell}$ instead of $(1+e^{-\beta\omega_\ell})^{2d_\ell}$. The graded functional is not generally positive: one positive-energy occupation mode has graded expectation $-1/(e^{\beta E}-1)<0$ for its positive number operator. Periodicity alone therefore supplies neither a thermal state nor a supersymmetric index. An index would need an appropriate conserved supercharge, pairing, insertion and convergence assumptions, none supplied by changing this boundary condition.

## 5. An Evaluated Gauge Projection of Actual Field Modes

Unprojected coloured Fock states are not the physical singlet thermal ensemble on a closed space. To evaluate a constraint rather than merely write a formal group integral, retain the spatially constant harmonics of **two real adjoint scalar fields** of an auxiliary $SU(2)$ model. Each retained mode has frequency $1/R$ from (7), with creation operators $a_c^\dagger,b_c^\dagger$, $c=1,2,3$. The normalized harmonic is $Y_0=V^{-1/2}$; for example $\phi_A^c=Y_0(a_c+a_c^\dagger)/\sqrt{2/R}$ within this regulator.

Normal-order the truncated Hamiltonian and impose

$$\begin{align}
H_{\mathrm{mode}}=\frac{N_A+N_B}{R},\qquad G^a=-i\epsilon^{abc}(a_b^\dagger a_c+b_b^\dagger b_c),\qquad \mathcal H_G=\bigcap_{a=1}^3\ker G^a. \tag{27}
\end{align}$$

This retains infinitely many bosonic occupations of specified field modes. It is an explicitly chosen global constraint sector of a mode regulator, not a solution of every local Yang–Mills Gauss constraint or an interacting Standard Model. Omitted modes and interactions cannot be restored by replacing their degeneracy with a colour count. Any common vacuum-energy offset can be added separately to both traces below.

Use normalized Haar measure and $P_G=\int dU\,\mathcal U(U)$. Haar invariance and inversion give $P_G^2=P_G=P_G^\dagger$; it commutes with (27). Refine the occupations by $0<x,y<1$ before setting both to $q$. Then

$$\begin{align}
Z_G(x,y)=\operatorname{Tr}_{\mathcal F}(P_Gx^{N_A}y^{N_B}) =\frac2\pi\int_0^\pi \frac{\sin^2\theta\,d\theta} {(1-x)(1-2x\cos2\theta+x^2) (1-y)(1-2y\cos2\theta+y^2)}. \tag{28}
\end{align}$$

The denominators are the three adjoint weights for each field. The integral is evaluated as follows. With $z=e^{2i\theta}$, the character product $f(z)$ is invariant under $z\mapsto z^{-1}$. Haar integration is its constant term with $1-z$. After including the contour measure the integrand is

$$\begin{align}
I(z)=\frac{(1-z)z} {(1-x)(1-y)(1-xz)(1-yz)(z-x)(z-y)}. \tag{29}
\end{align}$$

For distinct $x,y$ the poles inside the unit circle are $x,y$; adding their residues gives

$$\begin{align}
Z_G(x,y)=\frac1{(1-x^2)(1-xy)(1-y^2)}. \tag{30}
\end{align}$$

The equal-energy limit is removable. This result can also be read from actual representation multiplicities: $\operatorname{Sym}^mV_1=V_m\oplus V_{m-2}\oplus\cdots$ for spin one. A singlet in the two occupations requires a common spin, giving $\lfloor\min(m,n)/2\rfloor+1$ when $m,n$ have the same parity and zero otherwise. These are exactly the coefficients of (30). The three invariant polynomial generators are $a^\dagger\cdot a^\dagger$, $a^\dagger\cdot b^\dagger$, $b^\dagger\cdot b^\dagger$; their factorized counting does not assert canonical oscillator commutators for these composite operators.

For instance $x=1/4,y=1/3$ gives $Z_G=72/55$, whereas the unprojected trace is $(1-x)^{-3}(1-y)^{-3}=8$. At the physical equal-mode choice,

$$\begin{align}
Z_G^{\mathrm{th}}=(1-q^2)^{-3},\qquad RU_G=\frac6{e^{2b}-1},\qquad C_G=\frac{12b^2e^{2b}}{(e^{2b}-1)^2},\qquad S_G=\log Z_G^{\mathrm{th}}+bRU_G. \tag{31}
\end{align}$$

At $b=1$ these are $Z_G=1.546881$, $RU_G=0.939106$, $S_G=1.375346$ and $C_G=2.172185$. The unprojected values are $Z_{\mathrm{kin}}=15.674746$ and $RU_{\mathrm{kin}}=3.491860$. Projection has changed the actual ensemble, not just its notation.

If represented through a Euclidean temporal gauge variable, the group element in (28) is an integrated holonomy and twists the field boundary conditions. It is not an arbitrary real chemical potential for a colour charge. A gauge charge annihilates singlet states; one chosen colour multiplier cannot replace normalized Haar projection. Full local Gauss constraints and a consistent interacting gauge-field action require more than this regulator.

## 6. An Ideal-Content Comparison and the Limits of the Construction

For comparison with the empirical minimal Standard Model, adopt an auxiliary massless, free, unbroken-phase content with a conformally coupled Higgs reference. There are four real scalar components, 12 gauge generators and 45 independent Weyl components. The unprojected one-particle functions are

$$\begin{align}
z_B^{\mathrm{kin}}=4z_S+12z_V,\qquad z_F^{\mathrm{kin}}=45z_W. \tag{32}
\end{align}$$

One Weyl field in (12) already includes its antiparticle. Thus the flat high-temperature counts are $g_B=4+2\cdot12=28$ and $g_F=2\cdot45=90$, exactly the convention of [EC-10](<EC-10.md#6-a-precisely-scoped-standard-model-thermal-baseline>), not 90 independent Weyl fields.

The small-$b$ letter coefficients are $z_S\sim2/b^3$, $z_W\sim4/b^3$ and $z_V\sim4/b^3$. Inserting them in (13) gives the leading kinetic free-gas limit

$$\begin{align}
\log Z_{\mathrm{th}}^{\mathrm{kin}} \sim\frac{\pi^4}{45}\left(28+\frac78\,90\right)b^{-3},\qquad \frac{U_{\mathrm{th}}^{\mathrm{kin}}}V \sim\frac{\pi^2}{30}\frac{427}{4}T^4. \tag{33}
\end{align}$$

After multiplying (13) by $b^3$, its terms have a summable $C/k^4$ bound because $u^3z(e^{-u})$ is bounded for each letter function. The alternating fermionic sum produces $\sum(-1)^{k+1}k^{-4}=(7/8)\zeta_{\mathrm R}(4)$. This reference does not turn an unprojected coloured Fock trace into the physical gauge-singlet partition function of the Standard Model. The Higgs curvature coupling, masses, gauge projection, interactions and renormalized couplings remain separate inputs. No electroweak or QCD thermal transition is predicted by (32)–(33).

If a true conserved global charge is added, a complex conformal scalar's particle/antiparticle energies become $\omega_\ell\mp\mu$. Its free grand trace requires $|\mu|<1/R$; equality makes the lowest bosonic factor diverge. A real neutral scalar has no such charge merely because its free occupation number can be counted. Fermionic exclusion permits any finite real $\mu$ for a specified conserved global charge in this discrete free spectrum, since only finitely many levels can have negative $\omega-\mu$. This is not authorization for a colour or a nonconserved axial chemical potential. The variables $x,y$ in (28) are initially counting refinements; their physical thermal specialization was explicitly made in (31).

At fixed finite $R$ and fixed field multiplicities the stated free traces converge for every real $\beta>0$ and their logarithms are real analytic there. Polynomial spectral growth and exponential weights give locally uniform convergence with derivatives. A commuting projection cannot increase a positive real thermal trace. Complex partition functions may have zeros; real positivity alone does not establish a globally single-valued logarithm on the complex half-plane. The rational projected example (30) is explicitly analytic for the stated fugacities.

The limits $R\to\infty$, $\beta\to0$ and increasing gauge rank are different operations. Equation (33) concerns the leading free energy per volume as $\beta/R\to0$; the infinite-volume global partition function itself is not finite. No large-rank limit has been taken in the $SU(2)$ example. A nonanalytic large-rank or volume limit would require its own control, not a pole at $q=1$ relabelled as a finite-temperature transition.

The finite-volume spectra, Green functions and projected trace have all used explicit spacetime and Hilbert-space data. Transporting them to twistor variables would require an actual solution/quantization map that carries the flow, inner product, mode decomposition, spin structure and constraints. Neither a projective coordinate degree nor an auxiliary circle supplies that map, the Gibbs state or gravitational dynamics.
