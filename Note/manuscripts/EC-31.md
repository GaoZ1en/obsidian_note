# Loop Amplitudes, Pairings and the Double Copy

**EC-31 — DRAFT study chapter; bounded review recorded.**

We evaluate three constructions: a helicity-dependent four-point gauge/gravity relation with an actual factorization pole; a dimensionally regulated one-loop integral and its physical two-particle cut; and a descendant pairing with both an invertible sewing kernel and a genuine null state. They use different spaces and different pairings. Their relation is methodological: normalization, intermediate states and limiting operations must be specified before an amplitude can be assembled.

Physical spacetime is flat four-dimensional Minkowski space with signature $(-,+,+,+)$ and $c=\hbar=1$. The two-dimensional conformal module below is auxiliary. Dimensional regularization analytically continues an integration dimension; it does not replace the physical spacetime by another target. The positive-energy and spinor conventions are those of [EC-21](<EC-21.md>); the flat in/out and Feynman prescription agrees with [EC-16](<EC-16.md#L450-L470>).

## 1. Signature, Helicity and the Normalization of a Vertex

For a possibly complex momentum write

$$\begin{align}
X(p)=p^0I+p^i\sigma_i,\qquad \det X(p)=-p^2. \tag{1.1}
\end{align}$$

A nonzero complex null matrix factors as $X_i=\lambda_i\widetilde\lambda_i^T$. Both columns commute; they are momentum coordinates, not fermionic field coefficients. On a future real null ray one may choose $\widetilde\lambda_i=\overline{\lambda_i}$ and $\lambda_i^\dagger\lambda_i=2E_i$. On the complexification the two columns are independent. The rescaling $(\lambda_i,\widetilde\lambda_i)\mapsto(t_i\lambda_i,t_i^{-1}\widetilde\lambda_i)$ leaves momentum fixed.

Keep $\epsilon_{01}=1$ and its ordinary inverse. Define the contraction orders explicitly:

$$\begin{align}
\langle ij\rangle=\det(\lambda_i,\lambda_j),\qquad [ij]=(\epsilon\widetilde\lambda_i)^T\widetilde\lambda_j =-\det(\widetilde\lambda_i,\widetilde\lambda_j). \tag{1.2}
\end{align}$$

The minus sign in the second coordinate formula is not a change of metric or of primed epsilon: the lowered spinor is contracted first. Expanding the determinant of $X_i+X_j$ now derives

$$\begin{align}
s_{ij}:=-(p_i+p_j)^2=\langle ij\rangle[ji]. \tag{1.3}
\end{align}$$

In particular this is nonnegative for two future real null momenta. Four signed null momenta with sum zero obey $s+t+u=0$, where $s=s_{12},t=s_{23},u=s_{13}$. In the analytic examples the momenta need not lie on a real slice. Physical incoming/outgoing assignments require crossing, not a new metric sign.

Take the canonical gauge action $\mathscr L=-F^a_{\mu\nu}F^{a\mu\nu}/4$, with $F^a_{\mu\nu}=\partial_\mu A^a_\nu-\partial_\nu A^a_\mu+g f^{abc}A^b_\mu A^c_\nu$. At vertices use Fourier coefficients $e^{ip\cdot x}$ and signed momenta summing to zero. Differentiating its cubic term gives $-g f^{abc}V_3$, with

$$\begin{align}
V_3=(\varepsilon_1\!\cdot\!\varepsilon_2) (p_1-p_2)\!\cdot\!\varepsilon_3+\text{two cyclic terms}. \tag{1.4}
\end{align}$$

There is no missing factor of i: the derivative contributes i and expansion of $e^{iS}$ contributes another i. Normalize the polarization matrices by

$$\begin{align}
X(\varepsilon^+)=\frac{i\sqrt2\,r\widetilde\lambda^T}{\langle r\lambda\rangle},\qquad X(\varepsilon^-)=-\frac{i\sqrt2\,\lambda\widetilde r^T}{[\widetilde\lambda\,\widetilde r]}. \tag{1.5}
\end{align}$$

These are external amplitude polarization factors; their signs label the duals of outgoing particle states. They are transverse, and changing a reference adds a multiple of p. For conjugate real-future data $\varepsilon^-=(\varepsilon^+)^*$ and $\varepsilon^+\cdot\varepsilon^-=1$. For a future +z ray, $\varepsilon^+$ is proportional to $(0,1,-i,0)$ and rotates by $e^{+i\alpha}$; its conjugate outgoing wave rotates by $e^{-i\alpha}$ and has physical helicity +1. Thus amplitude weight $t^{-2h}$, with $t=e^{-i\alpha/2}$, is not the active phase of the unconjugated wave profile. Incoming/outgoing use still requires crossing and the appropriate conjugation. With the ordered colour tensor $C_3^{abc}=\sqrt2 f^{abc}$, the canonical coupling has the stripped three-point normalization

$$\begin{align}
\mathcal A_3=i g a_3,\qquad a_3(1^-,2^-,3^+)=\frac{\langle12\rangle^3}{\langle23\rangle\langle31\rangle},\qquad a_3(1^+,2^+,3^-)=\frac{[12]^3}{[23][31]}. \tag{1.6}
\end{align}$$

For the factorization momenta below, direct contraction gives $V_3=-i\sqrt2 a_3$ on both the MHV and anti-MHV branches, hence $-g f V_3=(ig)C_3a_3$. The opposite unit phases in (1.5) preserve conjugacy and fix this common two-branch normalization; calibrating only one branch would not suffice. Other Fourier, crossing or trace conventions must be converted together. The symbols $\mathcal A$ and $\mathcal G$ below denote Feynman amplitudes including their overall i.

## 2. A Four-Point KLT Calculation with an Evaluated Factorization Pole

Use the Parke–Taylor tree formula as a Yang–Mills input [1]. For negative-helicity labels1,2 and any cyclic order $\alpha$,

$$\begin{align}
\mathcal A_4(\alpha)=i g^2 a_4(\alpha),\qquad a_4(\alpha)=\frac{\langle12\rangle^4} {\langle\alpha_1\alpha_2\rangle\langle\alpha_2\alpha_3\rangle \langle\alpha_3\alpha_4\rangle\langle\alpha_4\alpha_1\rangle}. \tag{2.1}
\end{align}$$

The weights are $t_1^2t_2^2t_3^{-2}t_4^{-2}$, the amplitude convention $t_i^{-2h_i}$ for helicities $(-1,-1,+1,+1)$. This does not rename the physical chirality of the book's fundamental spinor space.

The four-point BCJ relation is

$$\begin{align}
s_{12}a_4(1234)=s_{13}a_4(1324). \tag{2.2}
\end{align}$$

Here is a direct proof on the open set of nonzero displayed denominators. The ratio of the two Parke–Taylor functions is $-\langle12\rangle\langle34\rangle/(\langle13\rangle\langle24\rangle)$. Momentum conservation gives $\langle42\rangle[21]+\langle43\rangle[31]=0$, so that ratio equals $s_{12}/s_{13}$. The identity extends as a rational identity across removable chart boundaries, not by assigning a finite value to a genuine physical pole.

Distinguish the book's $\kappa=8\pi G$ from the perturbative gravitational normalization $\kappa_g=2\sqrt\kappa$. Set $g_G=\kappa_g/2=\sqrt\kappa$, of length dimension one. The Einstein action has coefficient $1/(2\kappa)=2/\kappa_g^2$. Its chosen three-graviton tree normalization is $\mathcal G_3=i g_G a_3\widetilde a_3$; this is an Einstein-action input, not a determination of G from g.

In these phases the field-theory four-point KLT relation [2,3] is

$$\begin{align}
\mathcal G_4=-i g_G^2 s_{12} \mathcal A_4^{(0)}(1234)\widetilde{\mathcal A}_4^{(0)}(1243) =i g_G^2 m_4,\qquad m_4=s_{12}a_4(1234)\widetilde a_4(1243), \tag{2.3}
\end{align}$$

where $\mathcal A_4^{(0)}=\mathcal A_4/g^2=i a_4$ and the two gauge couplings are stripped separately. The second copy is another amplitude, **not a complex conjugate**. Taking identical copies gives helicities $(-2,-2,+2,+2)$ and weights $t_1^4t_2^4t_3^{-4}t_4^{-4}$. Stripping the i's differently would change the printed KLT sign. Dimensional analysis also prevents extracting the dimensionful $g_G$ from a dimensionless gauge coupling without additional scale/normalization data.

A conserving family makes both the kernel and its pole cancellation explicit. Display spinors in units of $\sqrt{E_*}$, with fixed $E_*>0$:

$$
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
 i&\lambda_i/\sqrt{E_*}&\widetilde\lambda_i/\sqrt{E_*}\\\hline
 1&(1,0)&(-1,-1)\\
 2&(0,1)&(-1,-z)\\
 3&(1,1)&(1,0)\\
 4&(1,z)&(0,1)
 \end{array}
 \tag{2.4}
$$

Every matrix is null and their sum is zero for every complex z. These are analytic scattering data, not four real positive-energy momenta. The evaluated results are

$$\begin{align}
\frac{(s,t,u)}{E_*^2}=(z-1,-z,1),\qquad a_4(1234)=\frac1{z(z-1)},\quad a_4(1243)=-\frac1{z-1},\quad \frac{m_4}{E_*^2}=-\frac1{z(z-1)}. \tag{2.5}
\end{align}$$

The two apparent poles in the product at z=1 are reduced to one by the kernel. Crossed constructions using $s_{13}a_4(1324)a_4(1342)$ and $s_{23}a_4(1423)a_4(1432)$ give the same rational gravity function, not merely the same number at one point.

To check the residue, temporarily set $E_*=1$. At z=1 the internal momentum entering the left vertex has spinors $\lambda_P=(1,1),\widetilde\lambda_P=(1,1)$. At the right vertex use $\lambda_{-P}=\lambda_P,\widetilde\lambda_{-P}=-\widetilde\lambda_P$. Both three-point momentum sums vanish. The MHV left factor and anti-MHV right factor are respectively $a_L=1,a_R=-1$. Because $P^2=-s$, the book propagator is $-i/(P^2-i0)=i/(s+i0)$. Hence

$$\begin{align}
\mathop{\rm Res}_{s=0}\mathcal A_4 =(ig a_L)i(ig a_R)=ig^2,\qquad \mathop{\rm Res}_{s=0}\mathcal G_4 =(ig_G a_L^2)i(ig_G a_R^2)=-ig_G^2. \tag{2.6}
\end{align}$$

These are exactly the residues of (2.5). Restoring $E_*$ multiplies the two residues by $E_*^2$ and $E_*^4$, respectively. The nonzero test checks the three-point branch, crossing sign, coupling phase and cancellation of a spurious double pole. It is not an all-point or loop proof of KLT.

## 3. Which States Are Being Doubled?

Two positive-norm four-dimensional gluon helicity spaces have tensor states $|++\rangle,|+-\rangle,|-+\rangle,|--\rangle$. The first and last are graviton helicities. The orthonormal combinations

$$\begin{align}
|\phi\rangle=\frac{|+-\rangle+|-+\rangle}{\sqrt2},\qquad |B\rangle=\frac{|+-\rangle-|-+\rangle}{\sqrt2} \tag{3.1}
\end{align}$$

have helicity zero. In the usual unrestricted Yang–Mills double-copy theory they supply the dilaton and antisymmetric two-form sector; in four dimensions a massless two-form has one physical polarization. They are not negative-norm ghosts.

The same count in target dimension D follows from the transverse vector representation of dimension $n=D-2$:

$$\begin{align}
n^2=\left[\frac{n(n+1)}2-1\right]+\frac{n(n-1)}2+1 =\frac{D(D-3)}2+\frac{(D-2)(D-3)}2+1. \tag{3.2}
\end{align}$$

This counts the symmetric traceless, antisymmetric and trace components under the stated transverse assumptions; the dynamical double-copy identification is additional amplitude-theory input. At D=4 it is $4=2+1+1$. The graviton-only projector has rank2, not the rank4 identity of the tensor-product cut space.

Selecting external gravitons in (2.3) does not remove internal scalar/two-form pairs from loop sums. Pure-Einstein loops require an explicit consistent state projection or extra-state subtraction and verified cuts. At loop level one must additionally construct regulated cubic-graph numerators, their signed Jacobi relations, contact terms and momentum routing. A four-point relation among ordered trees supplies none of those all-loop data. Four-dimensional helicity cuts alone can also miss regulator-dimensional states or rational terms. The following loop calculation therefore tests a different, fully declared matter theory; it is not presented as a gravity-loop double copy.

## 4. An Evaluated Loop and Its Physical Cut

Choose a real massive scalar with

$$\begin{align}
\mathscr L=-\frac12\partial_\mu\varphi\partial^\mu\varphi -\frac12m^2\varphi^2-\frac{\lambda}{4!}\varphi^4, \qquad m>0,\quad\lambda>0. \tag{4.1}
\end{align}$$

Use the flat in/out vacuum, $e^{iS}$ and states normalized by $2E(2\pi)^3\delta^3(\mathbf p-\mathbf q)$. The vertex is $-i\lambda$ and the scalar propagator is $-i/(\ell^2+m^2-i0)$. This is a separate real-scalar model, not the charged Compton example or a physical Higgs process.

For $p^2=-s$ define the bubble with both propagators included:

$$\begin{align}
I_F(s)=\mu_R^{2\varepsilon}\int\frac{d^{\mathsf d}\ell}{(2\pi)^{\mathsf d}} \frac{(-i)^2}{(\ell^2+m^2-i0)((\ell+p)^2+m^2-i0)}, \qquad\mathsf d=4-2\varepsilon. \tag{4.2}
\end{align}$$

Write the bare coupling as $\lambda_B=\mu_R^{2\varepsilon}(\lambda+\delta\lambda)$. Strip the common $\mu_R^{2\varepsilon}$ factor from the four-point amplitude; $I_F$ contains the remaining loop scale. Combine the denominators with a Feynman parameter and shift the loop momentum. Wick rotation contributes i while the two propagators contribute minus one. For positive Euclidean $\Delta$ the actual radial/proper-time integrations give

$$\begin{align}
\int\frac{d^{\mathsf d}k_E}{(2\pi)^{\mathsf d}} \frac1{(k_E^2+\Delta)^2} =\frac{\Gamma(2-\mathsf d/2)}{(4\pi)^{\mathsf d/2}}
 \Delta^{\mathsf d/2-2},\qquad0<\mathsf d<4. \tag{4.3}
\end{align}$$

For example the radial Gaussian is $\Gamma(\mathsf d/2)/(2t^{\mathsf d/2})$; integrating $t^{1-\mathsf d/2}e^{-t\Delta}$ and including the sphere area proves (4.3). Continuing from this convergent domain with the Feynman prescription yields

$$\begin{align}
I_F(s)=-\frac{i\mu_R^{2\varepsilon}\Gamma(\varepsilon)}{(4\pi)^{2-\varepsilon}} \int_0^1dx\,[m^2-sx(1-x)-i0]^{-\varepsilon}. \tag{4.4}
\end{align}$$

Thus the UV pole and finite part are

$$\begin{align}
I_F(s)=-\frac{i}{16\pi^2}\left[\frac1{\bar\varepsilon}+B_R(s)+O(\varepsilon)\right],\quad \frac1{\bar\varepsilon}=\frac1\varepsilon-\gamma_E+\log4\pi,\quad B_R(s)=-\int_0^1\log\frac{m^2-sx(1-x)-i0}{\mu_R^2}\,dx. \tag{4.5}
\end{align}$$

We subtract the displayed pole in the $\overline{\rm MS}$ convention. The positive mass removes soft/collinear IR problems in these kinematics; it does not remove the UV divergence. The timelike branch cut is not an IR subtraction. No interchange with a massless or threshold limit is assumed.

At $\mu_R=m$ two fully evaluated values are

$$\begin{align}
B_R(2m^2)=2-\frac\pi2,\qquad B_R(8m^2)=2-\sqrt2\log(1+\sqrt2)+\frac{i\pi}{\sqrt2}. \tag{4.6}
\end{align}$$

The first lies below threshold and is an analytic-function calibration, not a physical two-massive-particle scattering point. For the second, the two roots are $x_\pm=(1\pm1/\sqrt2)/2$. Splitting the real logarithm at those roots gives the printed real part. Between them, $\log(-a-i0)=\log a-i\pi$ for $a>0$; the minus sign in (4.5) gives the positive imaginary part.

In the four-dimensional limit after this subtraction, for $s>4m^2$ put $\beta=\sqrt{1-4m^2/s}$. The negative-log interval has length beta. Therefore

$$\begin{align}
\operatorname{Disc}B_R=2\pi i\beta,\qquad \operatorname{Disc}I_F=\frac{\beta}{8\pi}. \tag{4.7}
\end{align}$$

Disc is the difference of the two boundary values in s. The factor minus i in $I_F$ matters: its discontinuity is real, not $2i\operatorname{Im}I_F$. The physical amplitude below instead has a real multiple of $B_R$.

In the centre-of-mass frame the ordered two-body phase space is

$$\begin{align}
\int d\Phi_2=\int(2\pi)^4\delta^4(p-k_1-k_2)
 \prod_{j=1}^2\frac{d^3k_j}{(2\pi)^3\,2E_j} =\frac{\beta}{8\pi}. \tag{4.8}
\end{align}$$

Indeed $|\mathbf k|=\sqrt{s/4-m^2}$, $E_k=\sqrt s/2$ and the energy delta function supplies the Jacobian $E_k/(2|\mathbf k|)$. The angular integral is $4\pi$. This fixes every $2\pi$ and energy factor.

The s-channel graph has an identical internal-pair symmetry factor one half. After the real UV subtraction,

$$\begin{align}
i\mathcal M_s^{(1)}=\frac{(-i\lambda)^2}{2}I_{F,R} =\frac{i\lambda^2}{32\pi^2}B_R(s),\qquad \mathcal M^{(0)}=-\lambda. \tag{4.9}
\end{align}$$

Consequently its physical optical relation is

$$\begin{align}
2\operatorname{Im}\mathcal M_s^{(1)} =\frac{\lambda^2\beta}{16\pi} =\frac12\int d\Phi_2\,|\mathcal M^{(0)}|^2. \tag{4.10}
\end{align}$$

For forward scattering at $s=8m^2$, one may take incoming momenta $(\sqrt2m,0,0,\pm m)$. Then t=0 and $u=-4m^2$, so the crossed bubbles have no absorptive cut. Real counterterms do not alter (4.10). Omitting the identical-state half would fail this equality by a factor of two. This is an actual regulated one-loop unitarity calibration [4], not all-loop unitarity or a UV completion. It has not evaluated a gravitational loop amplitude.

## 5. A BPZ Pairing with a Nontrivial Descendant Kernel

A physical Hilbert inner product is conjugate-linear in one argument and positive on physical states. A BPZ form is instead a complex bilinear conformal pairing. For the inversion $z\mapsto-1/z$ its Virasoro anti-involution is $\operatorname{bpz}(L_n)=(-1)^nL_{-n}$. Choose a highest-weight module paired with its contragredient copy, normalize the primary pairing to one and fix the inversion branch accordingly. After an identification of the two copies,

$$\begin{align}
\mathsf B(L_{-n}u,v)=(-1)^n\mathsf B(u,L_nv). \tag{5.1}
\end{align}$$

This identification must not erase charge-selection rules in an actual CFT correlator. In a free-boson realization the dual primary may have opposite charge.

Use the Virasoro algebra and highest-weight conditions

$$\begin{align}
[L_m,L_n]=(m-n)L_{m+n}+\frac{c_V}{12}m(m^2-1)\delta_{m+n,0},\qquad L_{n>0}|h\rangle=0,\quad L_0|h\rangle=h|h\rangle. \tag{5.2}
\end{align}$$

Here h is a conformal weight, not a spacetime helicity. In a unitary realization the Hermitian adjoint is $L_n^\dagger=L_{-n}$. At fixed level N the real descendant matrices of (5.1) and this Hermitian form differ by $(-1)^N$; their coefficient conjugations also differ. For example at $c_V=1,h=1/4$, the level-one Hermitian norm is $1/2$ but the normalized BPZ value is $-1/2$. Moreover $\mathsf B(iu,iu)=-\mathsf B(u,u)$ while a Hilbert norm is unchanged. A BPZ sign is therefore not by itself a physical ghost diagnosis.

In the level-two basis $(L_{-2}|h\rangle,L_{-1}^2|h\rangle)$ the commutators derive

$$
G_2=\begin{pmatrix}4h+c_V/2&6h\\6h&4h(2h+1)\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 \det G_2=4h\left[8h^2+(c_V-5)h+c_V/2\right].
 \tag{5.3}
$$

For instance $L_2L_{-2}|h\rangle=(4h+c_V/2)|h\rangle$ and $L_2L_{-1}^2|h\rangle=6h|h\rangle$ give its first row; applying $L_1$ twice gives the last entry. The BPZ sign at this even level is plus.

At $c_V=1,h=1$ this is an actual non-diagonal sewing kernel:

$$
G_2=\begin{pmatrix}9/2&6\\6&12\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 G_2^{-1}=\begin{pmatrix}2/3&-1/3\\-1/3&1/4\end{pmatrix},\qquad \det G_2=18.
 \tag{5.4}
$$

An insertion of a complete basis on this finite level requires the inverse Gram matrix, not the identity matrix. Its off-diagonal entries are consequential.

At $c_V=1,h=1/4$ the answer changes:

$$
G_2=\frac32\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\1&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 |n\rangle=(L_{-2}-L_{-1}^2)|h\rangle,\qquad G_2(1,-1)^T=0.
 \tag{5.5}
$$

Directly $L_1|n\rangle=L_2|n\rangle=0$; higher positive modes vanish by the level. At level three, $L_{-1}|n\rangle$ has coefficients $(1,1,-1)$ in the basis $(L_{-3},L_{-2}L_{-1},L_{-1}^3)|h\rangle$ and is again null. The level-three Gram rank is2. This is a genuine singular-vector chain, not a determinant rounded to zero.

There is also a positive oscillator realization. For one auxiliary chiral boson let $[a_m,a_n]=m\delta_{m+n,0}$, with $a_n^\dagger=a_{-n}$, and choose primary momentum $\alpha=1/\sqrt2$, so $h=\alpha^2/2=1/4$. Then

$$\begin{align}
L_{-2}|\alpha\rangle=\alpha a_{-2}|\alpha\rangle+\tfrac12a_{-1}^2|\alpha\rangle,\qquad L_{-1}^2|\alpha\rangle=\alpha a_{-2}|\alpha\rangle+\alpha^2a_{-1}^2|\alpha\rangle. \tag{5.6}
\end{align}$$

The vectors coincide exactly and have norm-squared $3/2$; $a_{-2}|\alpha\rangle$ and $a_{-1}^2|\alpha\rangle$ each have norm-squared2. This Hilbert calculation does not assert a nonzero same-charge two-point BPZ correlator. The latter uses the appropriate dual/opposite-charge primary.

One must quotient the radical before inverting (5.5). Its one-dimensional quotient Gram value is $3/2$, hence its inverse is $2/3$. If two coupling functionals have components $(j,j)$ and $(k,k)$, they annihilate the null vector and their finite sewing factor is $2jk/3$. Equivalently the matrix $G_2^+=\frac16\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\1&1\end{pmatrix}$ gives the same contraction. Its associated rank-one matrix $G_2^+G_2$ is idempotent. A functional such as $(1,0)$ does not annihilate the radical and has no well-defined value on this quotient. Assigning it an arbitrary pseudoinverse contraction would not fix that problem.

Even exact finite sewing does not supply the full Virasoro representation. Project onto levels0,1 at $h=1/4$; in the ordered basis $(|h\rangle,L_{-1}|h\rangle)$,

$$\begin{align}
[P L_1P,P L_{-1}P]-2P L_0P =\operatorname{diag}(0,-3). \tag{5.7}
\end{align}$$

The interior identity survives while the top misses a channel. This is a counterexample for this projection, not a theorem that every finite quotient or regulator is inconsistent.

## 6. Ghosts, BRST Cohomology and Completed Sewing

The matter example is not a full string field space. For illustration, the reparametrization ghost system has odd modes with $\{b_m,c_n\}=\delta_{m+n,0}$ and weights $(2,-1)$. In the usual bosonic-string normalization its BRST charge has the schematic normal-ordered mode expression

$$\begin{align}
Q_B=\sum_n c_{-n}L_n^{m} -\frac12\sum_{m,n}(m-n):c_{-m}c_{-n}b_{m+n}: -a c_0. \tag{6.1}
\end{align}$$

The infinite module, normal ordering and intercept are part of this definition. Nilpotence for the standard critical model requires $c_m=26,a=1$ and the ghost contribution $c_{gh}=-26$; its physical-state/no-ghost statement is additional framework input, as in [AST-16](<AST-16.md#L151-L195>) [5,6]. Our $c_V=1$ descendant module is not that complete critical matter system. Central-charge balance alone neither constructs a stable vacuum nor fixes a four-dimensional particle spectrum.

A sphere chiral ghost normalization already shows why an ordinary positive matrix cannot replace the ghost pairing:

$$\begin{align}
\int dc\;1=0,\qquad \int dc\;c_{-1}c_0c_1=1,\qquad \int dc\;(c_{-1}+c_0)(c_0+c_1)(c_1+c_{-1})=2. \tag{6.2}
\end{align}$$

Here the notation means extraction of the ordered top coefficient in a three-generator exterior algebra; reversing all three factors gives minus one. This finite functional calibrates saturation and orientation, not the full ghost Fock module. The open/chiral BPZ selection rule has total ghost number3. Closed-string sewing also needs the other chiral sector and the appropriate antighost/moduli insertions; (6.2) is not an all-genus zero-mode formula.

Given a genuinely nilpotent, domain-defined Q, physical BRST classes are $\ker Q/\operatorname{im}Q$ at the chosen ghost number. For a pairing to descend one needs, with its total grading fixed,

$$\begin{align}
\mathsf B(Qu,v)+(-1)^{|u|}\mathsf B(u,Qv)=0. \tag{6.3}
\end{align}$$

Then exact states pair to zero with closed states. Positivity of a physical Hermitian form on this quotient still requires its own no-ghost/state-space argument. A null Virasoro descendant is not automatically identified with a BRST-exact state merely by its zero Gram value. The positive-complex theorem of [AST-09](<AST-09.md#L78-L149>) also cannot be transferred to an arbitrary indefinite or complex bilinear form.

For one module, formal descendant sewing contains terms of the form

$$\begin{align}
\sum_{N,I,J}|N,I\rangle\,q^{h+N}(G_N^{-1})^{IJ} \mathsf B(|N,J\rangle,\,\cdot\,),\qquad |q|<1, \tag{6.4}
\end{align}$$

after handling radicals and choosing compatible dual bases. A full theory must specify all primary sectors, ghosts, operator domains, completion, measures and boundary limits. Trace-class or convergence properties of the inserted operators and control of degenerating moduli are not consequences of an inverse at level2. A level regulator can be tested, and a Q-preserving regulator needs compatibility with Q; even that would not prove the omitted-state sum or the infinite limit. The finite idempotent above is not a four-dimensional gravitational physical projector, just as the regulated vertex of [EC-17](<EC-17.md#L235-L266>) is not automatically one.

The physical cut (4.10), the KLT bilinear (2.3) and BPZ sewing (6.4) thus use three different operations: a positive-state Hermitian sum, a product of two ordered amplitudes, and a conformal bilinear with ghost/quotient data. Their explicit small constructions are useful precisely because they expose those extra inputs. None derives Standard Model gauge groups or three families, a universal Newton coupling, a UV completion, a nonperturbative partition function or all-loop quantum gravity. The analytic Ward bundle construction in [AST-24](<AST-24.md#L19-L61>) has its own chirality/reality hypotheses and does not remove these dynamical requirements.
