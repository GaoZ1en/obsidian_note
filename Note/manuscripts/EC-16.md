# Interacting Matter and Twistor Variables: Actions and Quantization

**EC-16 — DRAFT study chapter; bounded review recorded.**

An interacting field theory requires a choice of fields, representations and action. Quantization adds a state prescription, gauge fixing, a regulator and integration measures or operator domains. Twistor variables can organize particular geometric and field-theoretic structures within those choices. We develop this relation through a chiral Yang–Mills action with explicit normalization, explicit Standard Model interaction data, a finite auxiliary-field integral, a nonlinear BRST system and an evaluated Compton amplitude.

Physical spacetime has signature $(-,+,+,+)$, orientation $\varepsilon_{0123}=+1$ in an oriented orthonormal frame, and $c=\hbar=1$. The algebraic chiral reformulation holds on a prescribed oriented Lorentzian metric. The explicit quantum scattering calculations use flat Minkowski spacetime. A positive Euclidean mode integral will be introduced separately, with its own measure.

## 1. Spacetime Spinors, Internal Fields and Incidence Data

Assume a smooth oriented, time-oriented Lorentzian spin four-manifold with a chosen spin structure. A metric-compatible Einstein–Cartan connection has a Lorentz spin lift. We use the [fixed Clifford dictionary](<AST-07.md#L168-L307>),

$$\begin{align}
\{\gamma^a,\gamma^b\}=-2\eta^{ab}I, \qquad \Omega_\mu=-\frac14\omega_{ab\mu}\gamma^{ab}, \qquad \gamma^{ab}=\frac12[\gamma^a,\gamma^b]. \tag{1.1}
\end{align}$$

The [two-spinor representation](<AST-13.md#L219-L250>) fixes the physical chirality names:

$$\begin{align}
\rho(L)=\operatorname{diag}((L^\dagger)^{-1},L), \quad \gamma_5=\operatorname{diag}(-I_2,+I_2), \quad \Psi=\binom{\chi_{A'}}{\psi^A}, \quad P_L=\frac{I-\gamma_5}{2},\quad P_R=\frac{I+\gamma_5}{2}. \tag{1.2}
\end{align}$$

Here $L\in SL(2,\mathbb C)$. Unprimed upper spinors form the fundamental, positive-$\gamma_5$ space: the physical right block in this representation. Physical left fields are in the upper, negative-$\gamma_5$ conjugate-dual primed block. Raising or lowering spinor indices uses the explicitly chosen alternating form, not a relabelling of physical chirality:

$$
\epsilon_{AB}=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\-1&0\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad
 \epsilon^{AB}=\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}.
 \tag{1.3}
$$

An internal representation bundle $W_R$ is independent of these Lorentz modules. A left fermion is a section of $S_L\otimes W_R$; a Higgs scalar is a section of its internal bundle alone. The compatible derivative is the [tensor-sum connection](<AST-21.md#L43-L98>),

$$\begin{align}
D_\mu=\partial_\mu+\Omega_\mu\otimes I+I\otimes\mathcal A_\mu. \tag{1.4}
\end{align}$$

The spin and internal matrices commute on their separate tensor factors, although a matter action couples both to the same field. No ordinary Clifford-compatible lift of arbitrary nonmetricity is assumed. Global fields also need the actual spin and gauge transition data.

The geometric spinors used to label null directions have commuting complex components. Fermion fields have Grassmann-odd coefficients in a functional integral and operator-valued distributions after quantization. Their products are consequently different even when the linear Lorentz matrices agree.

### 1.1 The Dual Twistor Convention Used Here

For $X(x)=tI+x^i\sigma_i$, the [incidence construction](<AST-14.md#L9-L107>) uses a reference length $L_0>0$ and the dual convention

$$\begin{align}
Y=X^T/L_0,\qquad Z=\binom{\omega^{A'}}{\pi_A},\qquad \omega=iY\pi,\qquad [Z]\in\mathbb{CP}^3,\quad \pi\ne0. \tag{1.5}
\end{align}$$

Under the Lorentz matrix $L$, $\pi\mapsto L^{-T}\pi$, $\omega\mapsto\overline L\omega$ and $Y\mapsto\overline LYL^T$, so incidence is preserved. The slots are primed upper and unprimed lower; they have not been exchanged with the opposite twistor convention. At fixed $Y$, varying $[\pi]$ gives a projective line $L_Y\simeq\mathbb{CP}^1$ in twistor space. At fixed $Z$, the solutions of $Y\pi=-i\omega$ form a two-complex-dimensional affine incidence plane in complexified spacetime. These are different fibres of the correspondence. Neither is a matter field or a quantum state.

In particular the homogeneous coordinates in (1.5) are not a Dirac wavefunction. Their scale is projective; a matter field has its own mass dimension, statistics, action and normalization. We will use the incidence planes to identify one gauge-curvature sector and use commuting momentum spinors for actual scattering kinematics.

## 2. The Standard Model Interaction Data

Take the [matter action](<EC-04.md>) as specified field-theory input. For global charge bookkeeping choose the compact product

$$\begin{align}
G_0=SU(3)_c\times SU(2)_w\times U(1)_q, \qquad q=6Y_{\rm hyp}\in\mathbb Z. \tag{2.1}
\end{align}$$

The last circle has period $2\pi$, with character $e^{iq\beta}$. Its coupling is $g_1/6$, so the conventional hypercharge derivative is unchanged. The possible central quotients and their bundle implications are [separate global choices](<EC-09.md#L18-L60>). We use $G_0$ in this reference construction.

For each chosen generation $i=1,2,3$, the independent left fields and the Higgs are

| Field | Color | Weak | $q$ | Statistics |
|---|---|---|---:|---|
| $q_i$ | $\mathbf3$ | $\mathbf2$ | $1$ | odd |
| $u_i^c$ | $\overline{\mathbf3}$ | $\mathbf1$ | $-4$ | odd |
| $d_i^c$ | $\overline{\mathbf3}$ | $\mathbf1$ | $2$ | odd |
| $\ell_i$ | $\mathbf1$ | $\mathbf2$ | $-3$ | odd |
| $e_i^c$ | $\mathbf1$ | $\mathbf1$ | $6$ | odd |
| $H$ | $\mathbf1$ | $\mathbf2$ | $3$ | even |

The superscript $c$ denotes the left-handed charge conjugate of a physical right-handed field, not another independent copy. Counting internal components, the table has fifteen independent two-component Weyl fields per generation. The chosen three generations, representations and coupling matrices are physical input. Neutrino-mass extensions require additional fields or operators, as described in the matter chapter.

Choose real $g_s,g_2,g_1>0$ and arbitrary complex flavor matrices $Y_f$. With Hermitian internal generators $t_A,t_I$, use

$$\begin{align}
D_\mu\chi=\left(\partial_\mu+\Omega_\mu -ig_sG^A_\mu t_A-ig_2W^I_\mu t_I -i\frac{g_1}{6}qB_\mu\right)\chi. \tag{2.2}
\end{align}$$

Absent factors act trivially in the corresponding representation. On $H$, the spin and color terms are absent. Gauge curvature is defined by the exterior connection formula $F=d\mathcal A+\mathcal A\wedge\mathcal A$; it is not obtained by inserting torsion into an antisymmetrized affine derivative of a gauge potential.

Let $e=\sqrt{-g}$ and $dV_g=e\,d^4x$. The reference action is $S_{\rm SM}=\int dV_g(\mathscr L_g+\mathscr L_H+\mathscr L_f+\mathscr L_Y)$, with

$$
\begin{aligned}
 \mathscr L_g&=-\frac14G^A_{\mu\nu}G^{A\mu\nu}
 -\frac14W^I_{\mu\nu}W^{I\mu\nu}
 -\frac14B_{\mu\nu}B^{\mu\nu},\\
 \mathscr L_H&=-(D_\mu H)^\dagger D^\mu H-V_H(H),
 \qquad V_H=-\mu_H^2H^\dagger H+\lambda_H(H^\dagger H)^2,\\
 \mathscr L_f&=\frac i2\sum_\chi
 \big[\bar\chi\gamma^\mu D_\mu\chi
 -(D_\mu\bar\chi)\gamma^\mu\chi\big].
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{2.3}
$$

Each sum includes its internal and generation indices. In four dimensions the physical scalar and gauge potentials have mass dimension one, fermions dimension $3/2$, and $g_s,g_2,g_1,\lambda_H,Y_f$ are dimensionless. The volume is the tetrad density $|\det(e^a{}_\mu)|$. A background connection, rather than a quantum gravitational measure, is being supplied.

### 2.1 Gauge-Invariant Yukawa Tensors

For left columns define the spinor contraction $\chi\xi=\chi^TC\xi$, with $C=i\gamma^2\gamma^0$. In the chosen component basis its physical left block has the same numerical alternating matrix as (1.3), with its primed-dual slots understood; the bilinears below retain the $C$ convention. The odd coefficients make this mass bilinear symmetric under exchange of the two fields. Color indices are $r=1,2,3$, weak indices $p,q=1,2$, and the weak alternating tensor has $\epsilon_{12}=1$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
 \mathscr L_Y=-\big[&
 (Y_u)_{ij}\epsilon_{pq}(q_i^{rp}H^q)u^c_{jr}
 +(Y_d)_{ij}(H^\dagger_p\,q_i^{rp})d^c_{jr}\\
 &+(Y_e)_{ij}(H^\dagger_p\,\ell_i^p)e^c_j
 +\mathrm{h.c.}\big].
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{2.4}
$$

The color singlet pairs $\mathbf3$ with $\overline{\mathbf3}$. The up term uses the weak alternating tensor, whereas down and charged-lepton terms use the Hermitian dual doublet. Their integer charge sums are respectively $1+3-4=0$, $1-3+2=0$ and $-3-3+6=0$. These contractions explain gauge invariance; their existence does not fix the entries of $Y_f$.

For a flat constant broken-phase reference with $\mu_H^2>0$ and $\lambda_H>0$, take $v>0$ and write

$$\begin{align}
H_0=\frac1{\sqrt2}\binom{0}{v},\qquad v^2=\frac{\mu_H^2}{\lambda_H},\qquad H=\frac1{\sqrt2}\binom{0}{v+h}. \tag{2.5}
\end{align}$$

The tree fermion mass matrices are $m_f=vY_f/\sqrt2$. These are coefficients of the all-left bilinears. Their diagonalization therefore obeys

$$\begin{align}
U_{fL}^Tm_fU_{fc}=D_f,\qquad f_L=U_{fL}f'_L,\quad f^c=U_{fc}f'^c,\qquad V_{\rm CKM}=U_{uL}^\dagger U_{dL}. \tag{2.6}
\end{align}$$

The transpose follows from the mass bilinear, while the charged current contains an adjoint from its kinetic pairing. For example its quark term has coefficient $(g_2/\sqrt2)\bar u'_L\gamma^\mu V_{\rm CKM}d'_LW^+_\mu+\mathrm{h.c.}$, with $W^+=(W^1-iW^2)/\sqrt2$.

### 2.2 The Electroweak Quadratic Form

Write $V_\mu=(W^1_\mu,W^2_\mu,W^3_\mu,B_\mu)^T$. Acting on $H_0$ with the four matrices $g_2\sigma_I/2$ and $g_1I/2$ gives vectors $u_a=-i\mathsf g_aH_0$. Since $-(DH_0)^\dagger DH_0=-V_\mu^TM^2V^\mu/2$, the real symmetric coefficient is $M^2_{ab}=2\operatorname{Re}(u_a^\dagger u_b)$:

$$
M^2=\frac{v^2}{4}
 \begin{pmatrix}
 g_2^2&0&0&0\\
 0&g_2^2&0&0\\
 0&0&g_2^2&-g_2g_1\\
 0&0&-g_2g_1&g_1^2
 \end{pmatrix}.
 \tag{2.7}
$$

The neutral null and massive directions are $(g_1,g_2)$ and $(g_2,-g_1)$, each divided by $\sqrt{g_2^2+g_1^2}$. Consequently

$$\begin{align}
A_\mu=\frac{g_1W^3_\mu+g_2B_\mu}{\sqrt{g_2^2+g_1^2}},\quad Z_\mu=\frac{g_2W^3_\mu-g_1B_\mu}{\sqrt{g_2^2+g_1^2}},\quad m_W^2=\frac{g_2^2v^2}{4},\quad m_Z^2=\frac{(g_2^2+g_1^2)v^2}{4}. \tag{2.8}
\end{align}$$

The potential Hessian has three zero tangent directions to the classical gauge orbit and radial value $m_h^2=2\lambda_Hv^2$. The unbroken generator is

$$\begin{align}
Q_{\rm em}=\frac{\sigma_3}{2}+\frac I2 =\operatorname{diag}(1,0),\qquad Q_{\rm em}H_0=0. \tag{2.9}
\end{align}$$

Thus the physical radial Higgs $h$ is neutral. The charged scalar used in §7 will be a separate scalar-QED model, not this physical Higgs. All these tree parameters are selected couplings and vacuum data; physical pole masses and a gauge-fixed broken-phase quantum vacuum require further calculation.

## 3. An Evaluated Flavor Interaction

A nontrivial allowed choice of Yukawa data illustrates what the charged current actually measures. Use dimensionless Yukawas and define the Hermitian operator on the weak-left generation space by

$$\begin{align}
\mathcal H_f=\overline{Y_f}Y_f^T. \tag{3.1}
\end{align}$$

Indeed $Y_f\mapsto U_L^TY_fU_c$ implies $\mathcal H_f\mapsto U_L^\dagger\mathcal H_fU_L$. This is the left-space operator for the all-left mass convention (2.6). The right-column unitary matrices cancel.

Choose, as an illustrative parameter point rather than a fit to observed quark data,

$$
\zeta=\frac{-1+i\sqrt3}{2},\quad
 V=\frac1{\sqrt3}
 \begin{pmatrix}1&1&1\\1&\zeta&\zeta^2\\1&\zeta^2&\zeta\end{pmatrix},
 \quad Y_u=\operatorname{diag}(1,2,3),\quad
 Y_d=\overline V\operatorname{diag}(\sqrt2,\sqrt5,\sqrt{11}).
 \tag{3.2}
$$

The identity $1+\zeta+\zeta^2=0$ gives $V^\dagger V=I$. Moreover $V^TY_d=\operatorname{diag}(\sqrt2,\sqrt5,\sqrt{11})$. Hence $U_{uL}=I$, $U_{dL}=V$, $U_{uc}=U_{dc}=I$ solve the mass problem and $V_{\rm CKM}=V$.

For $b=-2+i\sqrt3$, the two Hermitian operators are

$$
\mathcal H_u=\operatorname{diag}(1,4,9),\qquad
 \mathcal H_d=\begin{pmatrix}6&b&\bar b\\\bar b&6&b\\b&\bar b&6\end{pmatrix}.
 \tag{3.3}
$$

The rephasing-invariant quartet is $J=\operatorname{Im}(V_{11}V_{22}V_{12}^*V_{21}^*)=1/(6\sqrt3)$. One can see the associated basis-invariant obstruction directly. Let $C_f=[\mathcal H_u,\mathcal H_d]$. Its diagonal vanishes, it is anti-Hermitian, and

$$\begin{align}
(C_f)_{12}(C_f)_{23}(C_f)_{31} =(-3)(-5)(8)b^3=120(10+9i\sqrt3). \tag{3.4}
\end{align}$$

There are three cyclic terms in each orientation in $\operatorname{tr}C_f^3$. Anti-Hermiticity makes the reversed product minus the conjugate, so

$$\begin{align}
\operatorname{tr}C_f^3=6i\operatorname{Im} [(C_f)_{12}(C_f)_{23}(C_f)_{31}] =6480i\sqrt3\ne0. \tag{3.5}
\end{align}$$

A common weak-left basis change conjugates $C_f$ and leaves its trace invariant. Complex conjugation of the Yukawa couplings conjugates this purely imaginary trace and reverses its sign. A CP symmetry, even after allowed flavor basis changes, would therefore require it to vanish. The example supplies a nonzero weak-Yukawa CP witness [1]. A strong-interaction theta parameter is separate data.

If the first two entries of $\mathcal H_u$ are instead equal, $(C_f)_{12}=0$ and this witness vanishes. A complex-looking mixing matrix alone is consequently not the invariant test. The numerical masses, mixing and CP violation in this example were put into the Yukawa tensors; the calculation extracts their consequences.

## 4. A Chiral Action That Retains Full Yang–Mills Theory

For one gauge factor choose an invariant bilinear quadratic form, denoted $\operatorname{tr}$, and anti-Hermitian generators with $\operatorname{tr}(T_aT_b)=-\delta_{ab}/2$ and extend this invariant bilinear trace complex linearly. Write its connection as $\mathcal A=g_{\rm YM}a^aT_a$. Then $F=d\mathcal A+\mathcal A\wedge\mathcal A=g_{\rm YM}f^aT_a$, and the physical Lorentzian functional is

$$\begin{align}
I_{\rm YM}=\frac1{g_{\rm YM}^2}\int_M\operatorname{tr}(F\wedge *_LF) =-\frac14\int_M f^a_{\mu\nu}f^{a\mu\nu}dV_g. \tag{4.1}
\end{align}$$

The plus sign in the form expression and the minus trace normalization are both needed for the component sign. For the integer Abelian generator in (2.1), matching this quadratic normalization uses $g_{\rm YM}=g_1/6$; for the canonical non-Abelian factors it uses $g_s$ or $g_2$. This quadratic form is not an unweighted trace over all matter multiplets. The $\mathcal A\wedge\mathcal A$ term supplies the non-Abelian cubic and quartic vertices when the action is expanded. The following reformulation acts on that nonlinear curvature, not just on an Abelian free approximation.

On Lorentzian two-forms $*_L^2=-1$. Define

$$\begin{align}
F_\pm=\frac12(F\mp i*_LF),\qquad *_LF_\pm=\pm iF_\pm. \tag{4.2}
\end{align}$$

The wedge pairing is complex bilinear, not a positive Hermitian norm. The identities $*_LF\wedge *_LF=-F\wedge F$ under the trace and symmetry of the two-form pairing give

$$\begin{align}
\operatorname{tr}(F_+\wedge F_+) =\frac12\operatorname{tr}(F\wedge F-iF\wedge *_LF), \qquad \operatorname{tr}(F_+\wedge F_-)=0. \tag{4.3}
\end{align}$$

Introduce an independent complex self-dual adjoint two-form $B_+$, with $*_LB_+=iB_+$. At fixed metric choose

$$\begin{align}
I_1[\mathcal A,B_+]= \frac{4i}{g_{\rm YM}^2}\int_M\operatorname{tr} \left(B_+\wedge F_+-\frac12B_+\wedge B_+\right) -\frac{i}{g_{\rm YM}^2}\int_M\operatorname{tr}(F\wedge F). \tag{4.4}
\end{align}$$

Completing the square and using (4.3) gives the exact identity

$$\begin{align}
I_1=I_{\rm YM} -\frac{2i}{g_{\rm YM}^2}\int_M\operatorname{tr} [(B_+-F_+)\wedge(B_+-F_+)]. \tag{4.5}
\end{align}$$

The self-dual bilinear is nondegenerate. Variation over the three complex components of $B_+$ for each adjoint generator therefore gives $B_+=F_+$, and substitution gives exactly (4.1). The term $-i\int\operatorname{tr}(F\wedge F)/g_{\rm YM}^2$ has been retained. Without it, the reduced action would be $I_{\rm YM}+i\int\operatorname{tr}(F\wedge F)/g_{\rm YM}^2$, not the chosen action.

Matter terms in §2 are independent of $B_+$. Adding them to (4.4) and eliminating $B_+$ consequently reproduces the chosen interacting gauge–matter action, factor by factor with the corresponding couplings. This is a classical fixed-background statement. It does not select a contour for the complex auxiliary field.

The Chern–Weil variation of $\int\operatorname{tr}(F\wedge F)$ is a boundary term for a fixed bundle. Local bulk equations may thus agree under compactly supported variations or a specified fixed boundary connection. Actions and quantum weights still need the global bundle sector and boundary data. An independently chosen real theta coupling is additional input; the fixed complex subtraction in (4.4) is required by the algebraic equality, not a prediction for that coupling.

### 4.1 The Precise Ward Subsector

In the dual incidence convention (1.5), the [analytic Ward construction](<AST-24.md#L19-L63>) differentiates along $\pi^AD_{AA'}$. For

$$\begin{align}
F_{AA'BB'}=\varphi_{AB}\epsilon_{A'B'} +\widetilde\varphi_{A'B'}\epsilon_{AB}, \tag{4.6}
\end{align}$$

flat base derivatives commute and the gauge commutator is

$$\begin{align}
[\pi^AD_{AA'},\pi^BD_{BB'}] =\epsilon_{A'B'}\pi^A\pi^B\varphi_{AB}. \tag{4.7}
\end{align}$$

It vanishes for every projective direction precisely when $\varphi_{AB}=0$, the $F_+=0$ condition in our Hodge dictionary. Dropping the $B_+^2$ term from a BF-type action would impose just this constraint through $B_+$; retaining it in (4.4) instead gives $F_+=B_+$. The quadratic auxiliary term is therefore consequential for the full gauge dynamics [2].

The Ward theorem also needs a holomorphic bundle $\mathcal E$, with the chosen complexified gauge-group structure, over the appropriate open twistor domain with $\mathcal E|_{L_Y}\simeq\mathcal O^{\oplus r}$ and a holomorphic splitting along those lines. This means holomorphic triviality, not only zero first Chern class. Appropriate reality data select a real gauge theory. The non-Abelian example in the companion chapter carries out this splitting on its stated domain and obtains a unit-charge instanton on a separately chosen positive Euclidean slice. Its real structure and action are not a Lorentzian real-slice assertion.

Indeed a compact-real Lorentzian curvature has real components in an anti-Hermitian basis. If $*_LF=-iF$, conjugating its coefficients in that fixed real basis also gives $*_LF=+iF$, hence $F=0$. Nonzero one-sector fields here must be complex or use another real slice. Complex helicity wavefunctions used in scattering are compatible with this fact: a real classical field combines a positive-frequency mode with its conjugate.

A metric-compatible EC spin lift by itself supplies neither an integrable complex incidence distribution nor the required holomorphic bundle splitting. Curved Penrose/Ward statements retain their stated analytic, torsion-free and Weyl-chirality hypotheses. The local spinor rewriting in §1 remains usable without asserting a global Penrose transform for generic interacting or torsionful fields.

## 5. Quantizing a Finite Auxiliary Sector with Its Contour

The distinction between eliminating an auxiliary field and integrating it can be calculated. Take one color block in a regulated Lorentzian cell of oriented volume $\mathcal V>0$, with constant curvature and auxiliary values in the cell. In this section $I_1,I_{\rm YM}$ mean those cell contributions. The following displayed components are coefficients of that fixed normalized generator. Parametrize its self-dual form by complex components $a_j$,

$$\begin{align}
(B_+)_{0j}=a_j,\qquad (B_+)_{jk}=i\epsilon_{jk\ell}a_\ell,\qquad j,k,\ell=1,2,3. \tag{5.1}
\end{align}$$

The same parametrization of $F_+$ has $a_{\rm cl}=(\mathbf E-i\mathbf B_{\rm mag})/2$, where $E_j=F_{0j}$ and $F_{jk}=\epsilon_{jk\ell}B_{\rm mag,\ell}$. The trace of the wedge square of a fluctuation is $-i\sum_j(a_j-a_{{\rm cl},j})^2dV_g$. Thus (4.5) becomes

$$\begin{align}
I_1-I_{\rm YM}=-\frac{2\mathcal V}{g_{\rm YM}^2}
 \sum_{j=1}^3(a_j-a_{{\rm cl},j})^2. \tag{5.2}
\end{align}$$

Fix the middle-dimensional cycle and its oriented holomorphic measure by

$$\begin{align}
a=a_{\rm cl}+e^{-i\pi/4}x,\qquad x\in\mathbb R^3, \qquad d^3a=e^{-3i\pi/4}d^3x. \tag{5.3}
\end{align}$$

This cycle has three real directions in the three-complex-dimensional auxiliary space; $d^3a=da_1\wedge da_2\wedge da_3$. For $g_{\rm YM}>0$, the exponent $i(I_1-I_{\rm YM})$ is $-2\mathcal V|x|^2/g_{\rm YM}^2$ along this cycle. The integral is absolutely convergent and evaluates to

$$\begin{align}
\mathcal N(\mathcal V,g_{\rm YM}) :=\int_{\mathcal C_F}d^3a\,e^{i(I_1-I_{\rm YM})} =e^{-3i\pi/4} \left(\frac{\pi g_{\rm YM}^2}{2\mathcal V}\right)^{3/2}. \tag{5.4}
\end{align}$$

The opposite rotation gives a growing real exponent and does not define this Gaussian. With precisely the chosen cycle and normalized measure, the finite equality is

$$\begin{align}
\mathcal N^{-1}\int_{\mathcal C_F}d^3a\,e^{iI_1} =e^{iI_{\rm YM}}. \tag{5.5}
\end{align}$$

The phase and volume power are part of the result. Since $[a_j]=\mathrm{length}^{-2}$, $\mathcal N$ has the units of $d^3a$, namely $\mathrm{length}^{-6}$. On a fixed background one may choose a corresponding normalization for this auxiliary Gaussian. If geometry is integrated, its volume dependence cannot be declared a geometry-independent constant. A continuum or joint gravity integral must supply a compatible global cycle, regulator, gauge measure and determinant prescription; (5.5) is the explicit finite-cell result, not a choice-independent functional-integral equivalence.

## 6. Gauge Fixing, Ghosts and a Normalized Reference Mode

For a canonical $SU(2)$ connection $a_\mu^a$ use $T_a=-i\sigma_a/2$, $[T_a,T_b]=\epsilon_{abc}T_c$. Introduce odd adjoint ghosts $c^a,\bar c^a$ and an even auxiliary field $b^a$. The classical BRST operation $s$ is an odd derivation, $s(XY)=(sX)Y+(-1)^{|X|}X(sY)$, with

$$
\begin{aligned}
 sa_\mu^a&=\partial_\mu c^a+g_2\epsilon_{abc}a_\mu^bc^c,
 &sc^a&=-\frac{g_2}{2}\epsilon_{abc}c^bc^c,\\
 sH&=-g_2c^aT_aH,
 &s\bar c^a&=b^a,\qquad sb^a=0.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{6.1}
$$

These ghosts are anticommuting scalar fields, not physical spin-$1/2$ matter. The antighost is an independent Grassmann variable. Jacobi gives $s^2c=0$. In $s^2a_\mu$, the derivative terms cancel in pairs because $s$ commutes with differentiation; the remaining $g_2^2acc$ terms form the Jacobi combination. For the doublet, the cancellation is explicit:

$$\begin{align}
s^2H=\frac{g_2^2}{2}c^ac^b
 \big(\epsilon_{abc}T_c-[T_a,T_b]\big)H=0. \tag{6.2}
\end{align}$$

Omitting $sc$ would leave a nonzero polynomial $-g_2^2c^ac^b[T_a,T_b]H/2$. The ghost self-interaction is essential to the non-Abelian differential. An explicit finite algebra realizes these rules using independent $c^a$ and $\partial_\mu c^a$, even connection components and the even Higgs doublet; it implements graded products rather than commuting ghost symbols.

On flat Minkowski space choose $\mathcal G^a(a)=\partial^\mu a_\mu^a$ and the gauge fermion

$$\begin{align}
\Psi_{\rm gf}=\int d^4x\,\bar c^a(\mathcal G^a+\xi b^a/2), \qquad \xi>0. \tag{6.3}
\end{align}$$

Applying the derivation gives

$$\begin{align}
S_{\rm gf+gh}=s\Psi_{\rm gf} =\int d^4x\left[b^a\mathcal G^a+\frac{\xi}{2}b^ab^a +\bar c^a(M_{\rm FP})^{ab}c^b\right], \quad M_{\rm FP}=-\partial^\mu D_\mu^{\rm adj}. \tag{6.4}
\end{align}$$

The sign of the ghost term comes from the odd antighost. The algebraic stationary equation is $b^a=-\mathcal G^a/\xi$; substitution gives $-(\mathcal G^a)^2/(2\xi)$. Integrating over $b$ additionally needs its measure and Lorentzian phase, as in §5. Near the trivial connection, after excluding residual gauge zero modes, this is the usual perturbative covariant gauge. Away from that patch $M_{\rm FP}$ depends on the connection and can have zero modes. Classical $s^2=0$ does not by itself establish invariance of a regulated quantum measure or absence of anomalies [3].

### 6.1 The Actual Gaussian and Berezin Normalizations

For a finite independent reference, now choose a positive Euclidean nonzero real Fourier-mode block with $K=|k_E|^2>0$. The modal coefficients below are measured in fixed reference units. Choose an orthonormal basis with first vector $k_E/\sqrt K$. Then $P_\parallel=\operatorname{diag}(1,0,0,0)$ and $P_\perp=I-P_\parallel$ project onto longitudinal and transverse coefficients. The vector Hessian is

$$\begin{align}
M_K=K(P_\perp+P_\parallel/\xi),\quad M_K^{-1}=\frac1K(P_\perp+\xi P_\parallel),\quad \det M_K=\frac{K^4}{\xi}. \tag{6.5}
\end{align}$$

Take four real bosonic coefficients with ordinary measure $d^4a$. The ghost Hessian for this gauge mode is the scalar $K$, not the entire vector matrix $M_K$. Fix the Berezin orientation by $\int d\bar c\,dc\,c\bar c=1$. Nilpotency then gives

$$\begin{align}
\int d\bar c\,dc\,e^{-\bar cKc}=K, \qquad \int d\bar c\,dc\,e^{i\bar cKc}=-iK. \tag{6.6}
\end{align}$$

Only the coefficient of the top Grassmann monomial contributes. With several pairs, antisymmetrizing the column choices gives the determinant; Lorentzian exponential conventions retain the corresponding phase. This algebraic finite-dimensional identity is not a definition of an unregularized functional determinant.

The positive bosonic integral and the normalized Gaussian average over the gauge condition give

$$
\begin{aligned}
 Z_a&=\int_{\mathbb R^4}d^4a\,e^{-a^TM_Ka/2}
 =(2\pi)^2\frac{\sqrt\xi}{K^2},\\
 Z_{\rm block}&=(2\pi\xi)^{-1/2}Z_a K
 =\frac{(2\pi)^{3/2}}K.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{6.7}
$$

Thus $\xi$ cancels with these declared measures. The $K$ dependence changes if the ghost determinant is omitted; a spurious $\sqrt\xi$ remains if the gauge-average normalization is omitted. This mode calculation fixes normalization data. It is neither a full interacting path integral nor a physical-state counting theorem, and it does not continue every complex $B_+$ cycle to a Euclidean one.

## 7. A Quantified Tree Interaction: Scalar Compton Scattering

Choose a separate complex scalar $\phi$ of mass $m>0$ and real electric coupling $e_{\rm toy}$. On flat Minkowski space take

$$\begin{align}
\mathscr L_{\rm toy}=-\frac14f_{\mu\nu}f^{\mu\nu} -(D_\mu\phi)^*D^\mu\phi-m^2|\phi|^2, \qquad D_\mu=\partial_\mu+ie_{\rm toy}A_\mu. \tag{7.1}
\end{align}$$

This is a massive charged scalar-QED model. It is not the neutral physical Higgs of the minimal Standard Model. The independent toy convention has $\phi\mapsto e^{-ie_{\rm toy}\alpha}\phi$ for $A_\mu\mapsto A_\mu+\partial_\mu\alpha$. Expanding the covariant derivative gives

$$\begin{align}
\mathscr L_{\rm int}=ie_{\rm toy}A_\mu [\phi^*\partial^\mu\phi-(\partial^\mu\phi^*)\phi] -e_{\rm toy}^2A_\mu A^\mu|\phi|^2. \tag{7.2}
\end{align}$$

Use the flat in/out vacuum, $e^{iS}$, plane waves $e^{ip\cdot x}$ for incoming positive-frequency fields, and relativistic external-state normalization $2E(2\pi)^3\delta^3(\mathbf p-\mathbf q)$. The scalar Feynman propagator is $-i/(p^2+m^2-i0)$. The one-photon scalar vertex is $-ie_{\rm toy}(p+p')^\mu$, and the two-photon contact vertex is $-2ie_{\rm toy}^2\eta^{\nu\mu}$. These choices fix the overall amplitude convention [4].

For $\phi(p)+\gamma(k)\to\phi(p')+\gamma(k')$, impose

$$\begin{align}
p+k=p'+k',\qquad p^2=p'^2=-m^2,\qquad k^2=k'^2=0, \quad D_s=(p+k)^2+m^2,\quad D_u=(p-k')^2+m^2. \tag{7.3}
\end{align}$$

Away from the internal poles, the two exchange graphs and the contact graph give $i\mathcal M=i e_{\rm toy}^2\epsilon_\nu^{\prime *}T^{\nu\mu}\epsilon_\mu$, where

$$\begin{align}
T^{\nu\mu}= \frac{(2p'+k')^\nu(2p+k)^\mu}{D_s} +\frac{(2p-k')^\nu(2p'-k)^\mu}{D_u} -2\eta^{\nu\mu}. \tag{7.4}
\end{align}$$

This tensor has two distinct Ward tests. The mass shells and momentum conservation imply

$$\begin{align}
k\cdot(2p+k)=D_s,\quad k\cdot(2p'-k)=-D_u,\quad k'\cdot(2p'+k')=D_s,\quad k'\cdot(2p-k')=-D_u. \tag{7.5}
\end{align}$$

Consequently

$$
\begin{aligned}
 T^{\nu\mu}k_\mu&=(2p'+k')^\nu-(2p-k')^\nu-2k^\nu=0,\\
 k'_\nu T^{\nu\mu}&=(2p+k)^\mu-(2p'-k)^\mu-2k'^\mu=0.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{7.6}
$$

Leaving out the contact graph instead gives $2k^\nu$ in the first test. The covariant-derivative interaction fixes the relative coefficient needed for gauge invariance; transversality of selected external vectors is not a substitute for both Ward identities.

### 7.1 Actual Momenta, Momentum Spinors and Helicities

In the initial scalar rest frame choose

$$
\begin{aligned}
 p&=(m,0,0,0),&k&=E(1,0,0,1),\\
 k'&=E'(1,\sin\theta,0,\cos\theta),&
 E'&=\frac{mE}{m+E(1-\cos\theta)},\qquad p'=p+k-k'.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{7.7}
$$

Here $m,E>0$ and $\theta$ is real. Solving $p'^2=-m^2$ gives the displayed recoil energy. It is positive, $D_s=-2mE$ and $D_u=2mE'$ are nonzero, and all external mass shells hold.

The unnormalized Hermitian momentum map $X(p)=p^0I+p^i\sigma_i$ has $\det X=-p^2$. The photons have the explicitly normalized commuting factors

$$\begin{align}
\lambda_k=\sqrt{2E}\binom{1}{0},\qquad \lambda_{k'}=\sqrt{2E'}\binom{\cos(\theta/2)}{\sin(\theta/2)}, \qquad X(k)=\lambda_k\lambda_k^\dagger, \quad X(k')=\lambda_{k'}\lambda_{k'}^\dagger. \tag{7.8}
\end{align}$$

Lowering lambda with (1.3) supplies a projective covector direction in (1.5); projectivization forgets the energy scale. The massive scalar rest momentum also has $X(p)=UU^\dagger$ with $U=\sqrt m I_2$ and $\det X=m^2$. This two-spinor factorization encodes the already selected mass shell. A scalar's momentum can have spinor factors without its field becoming a spinor or a pair of twistor operators.

Fix phases of the transverse photon vectors by

$$\begin{align}
\epsilon_h=\frac{(0,1,ih,0)}{\sqrt2},\qquad \epsilon'_{h'}=\frac{(0,\cos\theta,ih',-\sin\theta)}{\sqrt2}, \qquad h,h'\in\{+1,-1\}. \tag{7.9}
\end{align}$$

They have unit positive spatial norm and are transverse to their respective momenta. Since $p\cdot\epsilon_h=p\cdot\epsilon'_{h'}=0$ in this gauge, each exchange contribution to this projected rest-frame amplitude has a zero vertex factor. The total is then $-2e_{\rm toy}^2\epsilon_{h'}^{\prime *}\cdot\epsilon_h$. With rows $h'$ and columns $h$ each ordered $(+1,-1)$, this evaluates to

$$
(\mathcal M_{h'h})=e_{\rm toy}^2
 \begin{pmatrix}
 -1-\cos\theta&1-\cos\theta\\
 1-\cos\theta&-1-\cos\theta
 \end{pmatrix}.
 \tag{7.10}
$$

The exchange graphs are still needed in the covariant tensor and under gauge changes, as (7.6) shows. This is an interacting tree calculation with specified action, states, propagator prescription and polarizations. It illustrates how commuting spinor/helicity kinematics organizes a quantum amplitude once those field-theory data have been supplied.

## 8. What the Constructions Establish

The action data give an exact classical chiral reformulation of the chosen Yang–Mills factor coupled to the specified matter, a normalized finite auxiliary integral on a chosen cycle, a classically nilpotent gauge-fixing system, and the evaluated tree amplitude. The Ward construction concerns its restricted analytic curvature sector; the scattering calculation concerns a chosen flat-background quantum reference theory.

A full quantum Standard Model on a specified EC background additionally needs a regulator and counterterms, a global gauge and anomaly prescription, boundary/state data and compatible field measures. Integrating the geometry also requires the frame/connection measure and the physical gravitational constraints. Those are additional constructions, not supplied by the incidence coordinates, by classical BRST nilpotency or by the finite Ward tests above.
