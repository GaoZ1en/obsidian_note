# Supersymmetry, Charged Exteriors and a Collapse Shell

**EC-29 — Supersymmetric gravitational collapse. DRAFT; bounded review recorded.**

A supersymmetric exterior and a collapsing matter configuration answer different questions. We construct both in declared models: an extremal Einstein–Maxwell background with explicit Killing spinors in minimal ungauged N=2 supergravity, and a charged dust shell whose junction equations give a finite-proper-time collapse and exterior trapped spheres. The collapsing shell is not claimed to be a globally supersymmetric matter completion. Its static extremal limit also shows why a BPS mass–charge equality is not a formation mechanism.

## 1. Fix the Gravity Sector, Charges and Boundary Data

Use signature $(-,+,+,+)$, the derivative-first connection and curvature conventions of [classical matter](<EC-02.md>), and $c=1$. Write $\kappa=8\pi G>0$. These are classical constructions: $\hbar$ enters the action phase $e^{iS/\hbar}$ and $\ell_P^2=G\hbar$, not an assumed correction to a trajectory. No Barbero–Immirzi parameter $\gamma_{\mathrm{BI}}$, area-dependent dispersion or spin-fluid equation of state is part of this model. Clifford matrices $\gamma^a$ below are unrelated to $\gamma_{\mathrm{BI}}$.

Choose the standard on-shell, minimal, ungauged four-dimensional N=2 supergravity framework, with zero cosmological constant and no matter multiplets. Set both gravitino fields to zero. Their torsion source then vanishes pointwise, leaving the Levi–Civita connection. This is a classical field restriction, not an inference from a vanishing mean spin current. The gravity multiplet still contains gravitini; setting their background to zero does not remove their fluctuations or assert a no-superpartner theorem.

Its bosonic action in our graviphoton normalization is

$$\begin{align}
S_{\mathrm{bulk}}=\frac1{2\kappa}\int\mathrm d^4x\sqrt{-g} \left(\mathcal R-\mathcal F_{\mu\nu}\mathcal F^{\mu\nu}\right), \qquad \mathcal F=\mathrm d\mathcal A. \tag{1}
\end{align}$$

The standard local supersymmetry completion is framework input [1,2]; we specify its Killing-spinor convention in Section 3 rather than infer supersymmetry from (1) alone. If $F_{\mathrm{can}}$ has action $-\tfrac14\int\sqrt{-g}F_{\mathrm{can}}^2$, then

$$\begin{align}
\mathcal F=\sqrt{\kappa/2}\,F_{\mathrm{can}},\qquad m=GM_{\mathrm{ADM}},\qquad q=\frac1{4\pi}\int_{S^2_\infty}*\mathcal F =\sqrt{\frac G{4\pi}}\,e, \qquad e=\int_{S^2_\infty}*F_{\mathrm{can}}. \tag{2}
\end{align}$$

Thus $m,q$ are lengths. No charge lattice or elementary charge is derived here. Orientation is $\epsilon_{tr\theta\phi}=+\sqrt{-g}$; the Hodge star uses this orientation and the Lorentzian metric. In particular $*^2=-1$ on two-forms.

Volume-inclusive metric variation of (1), and variation of $\mathcal A$, give the source-free equations

$$\begin{align}
R_{\mu\nu}=2\left(\mathcal F_{\mu\rho}\mathcal F_\nu{}^\rho -\frac14g_{\mu\nu}\mathcal F^2\right), \qquad \mathrm d\mathcal F=0,\qquad \mathrm d*\mathcal F=0. \tag{3}
\end{align}$$

The trace is $\mathcal R=0$. The Maxwell boundary variation is

$$\begin{align}
\delta S_{\mathrm{EM}}= \frac2\kappa\int\sqrt{-g}\,\nabla_\mu\mathcal F^{\mu\nu}\delta\mathcal A_\nu\,\mathrm d^4x -\frac2\kappa\int_{\partial M}\mathrm d\Sigma_\mu\, \mathcal F^{\mu\nu}\delta\mathcal A_\nu. \tag{4}
\end{align}$$

Compact variations, or fixed tangential potential, remove the latter term. A fixed densitized normal-flux problem instead uses the Legendre term $+(2/\kappa)\int_{\partial M}\mathrm d\Sigma_\mu\,\mathcal A_\nu\mathcal F^{\mu\nu}$. These are different boundary data, not different bulk solutions. Metric Dirichlet data on smooth non-null boundaries require the GHY term with outward normal and its $n^2$ sign, as specified in [the action dictionary](<AST-19.md#L439-L474>). Null boundary/joint actions are not being computed. The bulk checks use compactly supported variations.

## 2. A Charged Electrovacuum with a Regular Extremal Horizon

For constant $m>0$ and real $q$, take

$$\begin{align}
\mathrm ds^2=-f(r)\mathrm dt^2+\frac{\mathrm dr^2}{f(r)}+r^2\mathrm d\Omega^2, \qquad f(r)=1-\frac{2m}r+\frac{q^2}{r^2},\qquad \mathcal A=-\frac qr\,\mathrm dt. \tag{5}
\end{align}$$

Here $r$ is areal radius, $\partial_t$ has unit norm at infinity and the potential tends to zero there. Initially use the outer static chart $r>r_+$, where $m\ge|q|$ and

$$\begin{align}
r_\pm=m\pm\sqrt{m^2-q^2}. \tag{6}
\end{align}$$

The inequality $m\ge|q|$ selects this black-hole comparison sector; the source-free equations alone do not impose it. Applying the supersymmetry bound below requires its separate positivity and boundary hypotheses.

Since $\mathcal F_{tr}=-q/r^2$, one obtains $(*\mathcal F)_{\theta\phi}=q\sin\theta$ and precisely the flux in (2). Direct tensor evaluation gives (3), with

$$\begin{align}
\mathcal F^2=-\frac{2q^2}{r^4},\qquad \mathcal R=0,\qquad \mathcal K:=R_{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}R^{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta} =\frac{48m^2}{r^6}-\frac{96mq^2}{r^7}+\frac{56q^4}{r^8}. \tag{7}
\end{align}$$

The metric is not flat merely because its Ricci scalar vanishes. For $q=sm$, $s=\pm1$, put $h=1-m/r>0$. Then $f=h^2$, the outer root is double and

$$\begin{align}
r_H=m,\qquad A_H=4\pi m^2,\qquad \varkappa_H=\frac12f'(r_H)=0,\qquad \mathcal K(r_H)=\frac8{m^4}. \tag{8}
\end{align}$$

The symbol $\varkappa_H$ is surface gravity, not $\kappa=8\pi G$. For the nonextremal roots its normalization is $\varkappa_H=(r_+-r_-)/(2r_+^2)$.

A static coordinate pole is not a curvature singularity. In an ingoing chart, $\mathrm dv=\mathrm dt+\mathrm dr/f$,

$$\begin{align}
\mathrm ds^2=-f\,\mathrm dv^2+2\,\mathrm dv\,\mathrm dr+r^2\mathrm d\Omega^2, \qquad \mathcal A_{\mathrm{reg}}=-\frac qr\,\mathrm dv. \tag{9}
\end{align}$$

The time–radius metric block has determinant $-1$, including at a zero of $f$; the field strength is smooth there for $r>0$. The static and ingoing potentials differ by a local gauge term in their overlap. This provides a regular future Killing-horizon extension, not a resolution of $r=0$: (7) still diverges there. Nor does a stationary exterior specify how infalling matter formed it.

## 3. Supersymmetry Is an Equation for a Spinor

Retain the physical Clifford convention

$$\begin{align}
\{\gamma^a,\gamma^b\}=-2\eta^{ab}I,\qquad \gamma^{ab}=\tfrac12[\gamma^a,\gamma^b],\qquad \Omega_\mu=-\tfrac14\omega_{ab\mu}\gamma^{ab},\qquad \gamma_\mu=e^a{}_\mu\gamma_a. \tag{10}
\end{align}$$

The unconstrained complex Dirac parameter packages the eight real supersymmetry parameters of the two-gravitino theory. In the chosen electric convention the gravitino Killing-spinor equation is

$$\begin{align}
\mathfrak D_\mu\epsilon= \left(\partial_\mu+\Omega_\mu +\frac14\mathcal F_{ab}\gamma^{ab}\gamma_\mu\right)\epsilon=0. \tag{11}
\end{align}$$

This is the standard ungauged equation translated to (10): setting $\Gamma^a=i\gamma^a$ gives $\{\Gamma^a,\Gamma^b\}=2\eta^{ab}$ and the term $i\mathcal F_{ab}\Gamma^{ab}\Gamma_\mu/4$. The relative factor in (11), the action normalization and the sign of $q$ are all fixed, not fitted after calling the metric BPS.

For $q=sm$ choose the polar orthonormal coframe

$$\begin{align}
e^0=h\,\mathrm dt,\qquad e^1=h^{-1}\mathrm dr,\qquad e^2=r\,\mathrm d\theta,\qquad e^3=r\sin\theta\,\mathrm d\phi. \tag{12}
\end{align}$$

The Levi–Civita connection, computed from the tetrad postulate, gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
 \Omega_t&=\tfrac12hh'\gamma^{01},&\Omega_r&=0,\\
 \Omega_\theta&=\tfrac h2\gamma^{12},&
 \Omega_\phi&=\tfrac12(h\sin\theta\,\gamma^{13}+\cos\theta\,\gamma^{23}),
 \end{aligned}
 \qquad \mathcal F_{01}=-s h',\quad h'=\frac m{r^2}.
 \tag{13}
$$

An explicit solution on the outer polar spin patch is

$$\begin{align}
\epsilon=\sqrt h\,S(\theta,\phi)\epsilon_0,\qquad S=e^{-\theta\gamma^{12}/2}e^{-\phi\gamma^{23}/2},\qquad \gamma^0\epsilon_0=-s\epsilon_0, \tag{14}
\end{align}$$

with constant $\epsilon_0$. The projector $P_s=(I-s\gamma^0)/2$ has complex rank two. Spatial rotations commute with $\gamma^0$, so the projection is preserved by $S$.

The cancellation can be followed without a supersymmetry label. The time equation is proportional to $\gamma^{01}(I+s\gamma^0)\epsilon$; the radial equation is

$$\begin{align}
\mathfrak D_r\epsilon=\frac{h'}{2h}(I+s\gamma^0)\epsilon=0. \tag{15}
\end{align}$$

Angular differentiation gives $\partial_\theta S=-\gamma^{12}S/2$ and $\partial_\phi S=-(\sin\theta\,\gamma^{13}+\cos\theta\,\gamma^{23})S/2$. The remaining angular coefficient vanishes because

$$\begin{align}
h-1+rh'=0. \tag{16}
\end{align}$$

Both charge signs satisfy all four equations. Direct evaluation of all six coordinate supercurvatures $[\mathfrak D_\mu,\mathfrak D_\nu]$ also annihilates (14). At a reference point $r/m=2$, $\theta=\pi/2$, their stacked matrix has rank two; together with the two independent solutions this gives precisely half the eight real supersymmetries on this connected exterior patch. Omitting the graviphoton term leaves a nonzero residual. The ordinary Levi–Civita spinor is not parallel.

The polar spin frame changes sign under $\phi\mapsto\phi+2\pi$, as does $S$; this is a spin-frame transition, not a multivalued physical spinor. Formula (14) uses a static frame singular at the horizon. Its vanishing static components as $h\to0$ must not be interpreted as a statement about supersymmetry in a regular horizon frame. No matching of this spinor to material shell fields is supplied.

The asymptotically flat minimal N=2 superalgebra, in a rest-frame sector with well-defined adjoint supercharges, appropriate falloff/inner-boundary data and positive physical norms, has the electric BPS bound

$$\begin{align}
m\ge|q|,\qquad M_{\mathrm{ADM}}\ge\frac{|e|}{\sqrt{4\pi G}}. \tag{17}
\end{align}$$

This standard framework statement concerns its allowed states and boundary charges [1,2]. Here saturation is accompanied by the exhibited Killing spinor and independently checked field equations. It is not a universal charge-to-proper-mass inequality for arbitrary phenomenological matter, nor a proof that charged states or collapse histories exist in every proposed completion.

## 4. A Separate Dynamical Model: a Charged Dust Shell

Now use ordinary Einstein–Maxwell theory with a phenomenological timelike charged layer. The interior is regular Minkowski space with no electromagnetic field, $f_-=1$. The exterior is (9), with constants $m,q$. There is no outgoing radiation flux in this spherical electrovacuum model. No supersymmetric matter multiplets or their fermionic junction conditions are being assumed.

Let $R(\tau)>0$ be the layer's areal radius and $\tau$ its proper time; dots differentiate $\tau$, while primes on $f$ differentiate areal radius. Its induced metric, stress and conserved proper rest mass are

$$\begin{align}
\mathrm ds_\Sigma^2=-\mathrm d\tau^2+R^2\mathrm d\Omega^2,\qquad S^i{}_j=\operatorname{diag}(-\sigma,0,0),\qquad M_0=4\pi R^2\sigma>0,\qquad \mu=GM_0. \tag{18}
\end{align}$$

The surface dust obeys the dominant energy condition. Its charge is a separate chosen datum, not fixed by that condition. For our flux sign a positive total charge $e$ couples as $+e\int A_{\mathrm{can}}$; the sourced equation is written $\nabla_\mu F_{\mathrm{can}}^{\nu\mu}=J_{\mathrm{can}}^\nu$. The surface current is $j^i=e u^i/(4\pi R^2)$.

Choose a single spacelike unit normal pointing from the regular interior to the exterior, and define $[X]=X_+-X_-$. Do not use each region's separate outward boundary normal in this jump. With the plus-sign convention of [submanifold geometry](<AST-10.md#L96-L105>),

$$\begin{align}
K_{ij}=h_i{}^\alpha h_j{}^\beta\nabla_\alpha n_\beta,\qquad [K_{ij}]-h_{ij}[K]=-\kappa S_{ij}. \tag{19}
\end{align}$$

The Israel equation is the standard distributional matching input [3,4], not a consequence of an averaged density or of a BPS label. The Maxwell jump is

$$\begin{align}
[n_\mu\mathcal F^{\nu\mu}]=\frac q{R^2}u^\nu =\sqrt{\kappa/2}\,\frac e{4\pi R^2}u^\nu. \tag{20}
\end{align}$$

The pullback potentials may be matched by patch gauges; the interior potential is pure gauge. Equation (20), rather than a discontinuous gauge choice, fixes the surface charge.

To retain orientation information put

$$\begin{align}
\beta_\pm=n_\pm^\alpha\partial_\alpha r =\varepsilon_\pm\sqrt{\dot R^2+f_\pm(R)},\qquad \varepsilon_\pm=\operatorname{sign}\beta_\pm. \tag{21}
\end{align}$$

The regular-center interior has $\varepsilon_-=+1$. In an ingoing exterior patch on the branch used below,

$$\begin{align}
u^\alpha=(\dot v,\dot R,0,0),\quad n_\alpha=(-\dot R,\dot v,0,0),\quad \dot v=\frac1{\beta_+-\dot R},\quad n^r=\beta_+. \tag{22}
\end{align}$$

Direct connection/pullback evaluation gives $u^2=-1$, $n^2=1$, $u\cdot n=0$ and

$$\begin{align}
K^\theta{}_{\theta\pm}=K^\phi{}_{\phi\pm}=\frac{\beta_\pm}R, \qquad K^\tau{}_{\tau\pm}=\frac{\ddot R+f'_\pm(R)/2}{\beta_\pm}. \tag{23}
\end{align}$$

These formulas are used only where the displayed $\beta_\pm$ are nonzero. The worked branch will keep both positive, including during outer-root crossing; it does not require $f_+>0$. The radial electric stress has $T_{\alpha\beta}u^\alpha n^\beta=0$, consistent with conservation of $M_0$ rather than a hidden rest-energy influx.

## 5. The Unsquared Energy Integral and Its Admissible Branch

The temporal Israel equation and the zero angular pressure are respectively

$$\begin{align}
\beta_--\beta_+=\frac\mu R,\qquad [K^\tau{}_{\tau}]+[K^\theta{}_{\theta}]=0. \tag{24}
\end{align}$$

Subtracting the squared definitions in (21) gives $\beta_-^2-\beta_+^2=2m/R-q^2/R^2$. Together with the first equation in (24), this yields the conserved ADM mass integral

$$\begin{align}
m=\mu\sqrt{1+\dot R^2}+\frac{q^2-\mu^2}{2R}. \tag{25}
\end{align}$$

The terms include electromagnetic self-energy and gravitational binding, not a universal equality between ADM mass and proper rest mass. When the shell lies outside $r_+$, its exterior electromagnetic Killing energy on a static slice is $q^2/(2GR)$, with the normalization (1)–(2). This slice interpretation is not continued through a region where $f<0$.

Define

$$\begin{align}
A(R)=\frac m\mu+\frac{\mu^2-q^2}{2\mu R}=\beta_-,\qquad B(R)=\frac m\mu-\frac{\mu^2+q^2}{2\mu R}=\beta_+. \tag{26}
\end{align}$$

The squared radial equation and the dust acceleration are

$$\begin{align}
\dot R^2=A^2-1,\qquad \ddot R=A A'=-\frac{A(\mu^2-q^2)}{2\mu R^2}. \tag{27}
\end{align}$$

The second expression follows from evolution and the pressure equation, not from selecting a sign in a graph of $A^2-1$. Direct substitution satisfies both equations in (24). Their use requires $R>0$, $A\ge1$, the chosen signs of $B$ and $\dot R$, and future $\dot v>0$. A zero of $B$ requires a different orientation analysis; it is not permission to retain only $B^2$.

For a simple wrong-branch control, when $q^2>\mu^2$ the positive radius

$$\begin{align}
R_{\mathrm{bad}}=\frac{q^2-\mu^2}{2(m+\mu)} \quad\hbox{has}\quad A(R_{\mathrm{bad}})=-1. \tag{28}
\end{align}$$

It is a turning root of the squared expression but violates the regular-center $\beta_->0$. It cannot be called a physical bounce of this shell. More generally neither overcharging nor cosmic censorship follows from an effective potential without its matter, branch, horizon and global hypotheses.

## 6. A Fully Evaluated Inward Trajectory

Choose the following reference length and initial data:

$$\begin{align}
\mu=L_0>0,\qquad q=\mu,\qquad m=\frac54\mu,\qquad R(0)=4\mu,\qquad \dot R(0)=-\frac34. \tag{29}
\end{align}$$

The relation $q=\mu$ is a selected specific charge, not a derived material BPS bound. Equations (26)–(27) now give

$$\begin{align}
A=\frac54,\qquad B=\frac54-\frac\mu R,\qquad R(\tau)=4\mu-\frac34\tau,\qquad \ddot R=0. \tag{30}
\end{align}$$

Fix the controlled interval $0\le\tau\le10\mu/3$, so $R\ge3\mu/2$. The two exterior roots are $r_+=2\mu$, $r_-=\mu/2$; the normal-sign zero is at $R=4\mu/5$, outside this interval. Throughout it, $7/12\le B\le1$, so the initially outward branch remains admissible. The surface density stays positive and the full pressure junction, not merely the squared radial equation, remains satisfied. Explicitly $K^\tau{}_{\tau-}=0$, $K^\tau{}_{\tau+}=\mu/R^2$ and $[K^\theta{}_{\theta}]=-\mu/R^2$ cancel in (24). Thus $\ddot R=0$ is not a claim of exterior geodesic motion.

The interior Minkowski clock has $\dot T_-=5/4$. An explicitly regular exterior clock is

$$\begin{align}
v(\tau)=v_0+\frac\tau2-\frac\mu3 \log\frac{2R(\tau)-\mu}{7\mu},\qquad \dot v=\frac R{2R-\mu}>0. \tag{31}
\end{align}$$

The shell reaches $R=r_+$ at $\tau_H=8\mu/3$, with $\dot v(\tau_H)=2/3$. The static $t$ coordinate diverges there, but the layer remains timelike in (9). This is actual proper-time evolution, not a sequence of equilibria. Its exterior is nonextremal, $m>|q|$, with $\varkappa_H=3/(16\mu)$; no Hawking state or emitted spectrum is being calculated.

Use the normalized future null pair of [the null-surface dictionary](<AST-10.md#L473-L500>),

$$\begin{align}
\ell=\partial_v+\frac f2\partial_r,\qquad k=-\partial_r, \qquad \ell\cdot k=-1,\qquad \theta_{(\ell)}=\frac fr,\quad\theta_{(k)}=-\frac2r. \tag{32}
\end{align}$$

Choose the time orientation with $\ell+k$ future timelike. Once $R(\tau)<r_+$, the actual retained exterior contains spheres with $R(\tau)<r<r_+$ and $r>r_-$, on which both expansions are negative. At the final retained time, $R=3\mu/2$, the exterior sphere $r=7\mu/4$ has the evaluated values

$$\begin{align}
\theta_{(\ell)}=-\frac{20}{343\mu},\qquad \theta_{(k)}=-\frac8{7\mu}. \tag{33}
\end{align}$$

Thus the model contains a controlled transition from an initially untrapped exterior to exterior future-trapped spheres. The outer-root sphere enters the electrovacuum domain when the shell crosses it. Calling a global boundary an event horizon additionally requires the future completion; a finite segment and a marginal sphere alone do not supply it, as emphasized in [spherical collapse](<EC-06.md#L231-L242>).

## 7. What the Extremal Limit Changes, and What Remains Open

Set $m=q=\mu$ instead. The same equations give $A=1$, $B=1-\mu/R$ and $\dot R=0$. Any fixed $R_0>\mu$ satisfies both dust junction conditions. Its exterior is extremal RN, but the formal root $r=\mu$ lies below the retained exterior $r\ge R_0$. With a regular flat center and the indefinitely static ordinary Einstein–Maxwell continuation, outgoing light can cross the layer and reach infinity; that solution is horizonless. An extremal exterior formula has not forced a black hole or a collapse.

The approach to this endpoint is also nonuniform in formation time. For $q=\mu$, $\mathsf E=m/\mu>1$, $w=\sqrt{\mathsf E^2-1}$ and a fixed initial radius $R_i>\mu$, the inward constant-speed branch has, when $R_i>r_+$,

$$\begin{align}
\tau_H=\frac{R_i-\mu(\mathsf E+w)}w \longrightarrow+\infty\qquad(\mathsf E\downarrow1). \tag{34}
\end{align}$$

The outside-initial-radius condition holds sufficiently close to this limit. It does not give finite-time formation of the static endpoint within this family.

The moving case (29) has instead supplied kinetic ADM energy above the electric BPS value. Its exterior data are non-BPS, and no preserved asymptotic supercharge is asserted for this moving solution. More importantly, the shell is phenomenological matter: no charged N=2 matter action, shell fermions or supersymmetry-preserving matching of gravitini and spinors was supplied. A supersymmetric exterior on one side never establishes those missing global matter conditions by itself.

The cutoff $R=3\mu/2$ is the boundary of the evaluated trajectory, not a predicted bounce or a quantum regulator. No inner-horizon, sign-flip or $r=0$ continuation is asserted. Taking $L_0\gg\ell_P$ keeps the bulk curvature weak on this interval, but does not give a finite-thickness or ultraviolet completion of a delta-function layer. The bulk invariant (7) is not a squared distributional curvature evaluated on the shell.

A realistic supersymmetric matter-collapse problem would additionally require its full matter action and charges, fermionic/connection constraints, regular initial and asymptotic data, surface or finite-thickness dynamics, radiation/transport, charge retention/discharge and energy conditions, perturbative and nonlinear stability, and a controlled ultraviolet prescription if large curvature is reached. The distinction between a material layer and a no-layer stellar surface is also essential [the compact-star matching discussion](<EC-13.md#L268-L291>). Spin correlations and averaging require their own state and closure [the averaging discussion](<EC-12.md#L9-L49>); they cannot be imported as an automatic negative-density component.

What has been achieved is positive but narrower: an explicitly supersymmetric charged electrovacuum, and a separately solved charged collapse segment with checked source jumps and trapped spheres. Neither a BPS label, a regular coordinate chart nor a root of a squared equation proves a bounce, cosmic censorship, a nonsingular global extension, a generated universe or a complete Standard Model supergravity.
