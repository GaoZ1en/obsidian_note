# Twistors in Riemann–Cartan Geometry

DRAFT — bounded mathematical review recorded.

A metric, a metric-compatible connection, a geometric twistor-spinor equation and a Penrose field transform are different data. They need not change together. This chapter constructs a flat-metric, flat-connection example with torsion whose connection-horizontal chiral null planes fail Frobenius. It also proves a positive special result: in four dimensions pure three-form torsion leaves the projectivized rank-two null-plane distribution equal to its Levi-Civita counterpart at fixed metric. Neither statement identifies the full spin connection, its homogeneous line transport or its Dirac operator with the torsion-free ones.

The metric/contact and flat incidence constructions build on [AST-12](<AST-12.md>) and [AST-14](<AST-14.md>). The spin dictionary is that of [AST-07](<AST-07.md>), [AST-13](<AST-13.md>) and [EC-02](<EC-02.md>). No generic complex Penrose threefold on every Einstein–Cartan background is assumed.

## 1. Geometric and Spinor Data

Let M be an oriented, time-oriented spin four-manifold with real Lorentzian metric of signature $(-,+,+,+)$. Choose a metric-compatible connection Gamma, with

$$\begin{aligned}
\nabla^\Gamma_\mu V^\rho&=\partial_\mu V^\rho+ \Gamma^\rho{}_{\mu\nu}V^\nu,\\
T^\rho{}_{\mu\nu}&=\Gamma^\rho{}_{\mu\nu}-\Gamma^\rho{}_{\nu\mu},\\
Q_{\mu\nu\rho}&=-\nabla^\Gamma_\mu g_{\nu\rho}=0.
\end{aligned}$$

We use $c=\hbar=1$. The middle slot of Gamma is the derivative slot. Curvature has the vector-operator sign $R(X,Y)V=\nabla_X\nabla_YV-\nabla_Y\nabla_XV-\nabla_{[X,Y]}V$. Write the Levi-Civita connection as a circle and set $\Gamma=\Gamma^\circ+K$. The shared trace is $t_\mu=T^\rho{}_{\mu\rho}$, not its negative $T^\rho{}_{\rho\mu}$. Frame gamma matrices obey

$$\begin{align}
\{\gamma^a,\gamma^b\}=-2\eta^{ab}I,\qquad \gamma^{ab}=\frac12[\gamma^a,\gamma^b],\qquad \Omega_\mu=-\frac14\omega_{ab\mu}\gamma^{ab}.
\end{align}$$

On the real Lorentzian slice take $(\gamma^0)^\dagger=\gamma^0$, $(\gamma^i)^\dagger=-\gamma^i$ and $\bar\psi=\psi^\dagger\gamma^0$.

The tetrad postulate is $\partial_\mu e^a{}_{\nu}-\Gamma^\rho{}_{\mu\nu}e^a{}_{\rho}+\omega^a{}_{b\mu}e^b{}_{\nu}=0$. It makes the curved gamma matrices covariantly constant. A genuine spin connection is used; no unspecified additional Abelian or spin-c gauge connection is included.

For chiral plane geometry use the unprimed fundamental bundle S fixed by the positive-gamma-five block in the shared dictionary. Its dual covector transforms as $\pi\mapsto A^{-T}\pi$. Using components in a local orthonormal frame, the soldering $X(v)=v^0I+v^i\sigma_i$ defines the plane associated with a nonzero dual pi by

$$\begin{align}
\Pi_{[\pi]}=\{v\in T_{\mathbb C}M:\pi_A X(v)^{AA'}=0\}.
\end{align}$$

Equivalently $\Pi_\ell=\ell\otimes S'$, where $\ell=\ker\pi\subset S$. These are the fixed-unprimed chiral planes of [AST-14](<AST-14.md>). We use their definition rather than an ambiguous self-dual/anti-self-dual name. Nullness and orthogonal complements here use the complex bilinear extension of g, not a Hermitian norm. Such a plane has complex dimension two and $\Pi^\perp=\Pi$.

Smooth real data suffice for the contact and differential-operator examples. Holomorphic twistor leaf spaces will instead require analytic data, a chosen local complexification and regular local quotients. Global spin structures, real structures, topology and Hausdorff questions are not supplied by a pointwise matrix identity.

## 2. The Metric Null-Ray Contact Space Does Not Require Gamma

On nonzero future null covectors define

$$\begin{align}
H_g(x,p)=\frac12g^{\mu\nu}p_\mu p_\nu=0,\qquad \theta=p_\mu dx^\mu,\qquad \iota_{X_H}d\theta=-dH_g.
\end{align}$$

This sign gives the usual Hamilton equations. The cone has dimension seven. On it $\theta(X_H)=2H_g=0$, and the Lie derivative of theta along the characteristic flow vanishes after restriction to the cone. The local quotient by that flow is a six-dimensional symplectic cone; quotienting positive momentum rescaling gives a five-dimensional contact manifold of unparametrized metric null geodesics. The construction is local unless those quotients are globally regular. Its geodesics are the metric/Levi-Civita ones, not an unspecified family of affine autoparallels.

A conformal change $\widehat g=e^{2f}g$ gives $H_{\widehat g}=e^{-2f}H_g$. On the null cone its Hamiltonian vector field has the same characteristic lines, with rescaled parameter. Thus the contact distribution depends on the conformal metric data, not on the independently chosen torsionful connection.

For Minkowski space every future null ray meets $t=0$ once. Write its data as $(\mathbf r,\mathbf n)\in\mathbb R^3\times S^2$, with $\mathbf x(t)=\mathbf r+t\mathbf n$. Choosing a positive energy section gives, up to a constant factor,

$$\begin{align}
\alpha=\mathbf n\cdot d\mathbf r.
\end{align}$$

On the angular chart $\mathbf n=(\sin\vartheta\cos\varphi,\sin\vartheta\sin\varphi,\cos\vartheta)$, direct exterior multiplication gives

$$\begin{align}
\alpha\wedge(d\alpha)^2=-2\sin\vartheta\, dr_x\wedge dr_y\wedge dr_z\wedge d\vartheta\wedge d\varphi.
\end{align}$$

It is nonzero for $0<\vartheta<\pi$; other angular charts cover the poles. The Reeb field of this chosen form is $\mathbf n\cdot\partial_{\mathbf r}$. Rays through a fixed event obey $\mathbf r=\mathbf x_0-t_0\mathbf n$, so the pullback of alpha is zero because $\mathbf n\cdot d\mathbf n=0$. Their two-dimensional sky is Legendrian. No affine connection entered these computations.

## 3. Geometric Twistor Spinors and Conformal Weights

For the chosen spin connection define the geometric Dirac contraction and its gamma-trace-free twistor operator by

$$\begin{align}
D^\Gamma=\gamma^\mu\nabla^\Gamma_\mu,\qquad P^\Gamma_\mu\psi=\nabla^\Gamma_\mu\psi+ \frac14\gamma_\mu D^\Gamma\psi.
\end{align}$$

The plus sign belongs with the negative Clifford anticommutator: $\gamma^\mu\gamma_\mu=-4I$, hence $\gamma^\mu P^\Gamma_\mu=0$. A geometric twistor spinor is a solution of $P^\Gamma\psi=0$. This definition does not assert the existence of a complex threefold.

In a Cartesian Levi-Civita spin frame on Minkowski space, every pair of constant Dirac spinors gives the explicit solution

$$\begin{align}
\psi(x)=\psi_0+x^\mu\gamma_\mu\chi_0,\qquad \partial_\mu\psi=\gamma_\mu\chi_0,\qquad D^\circ\psi=-4\chi_0.
\end{align}$$

Substitution makes $P^\circ\psi=0$. These examples are generally not massless Dirac solutions. Conversely, imposing both the geometric equation $D^\Gamma\psi=0$ and the twistor equation forces $\nabla^\Gamma\psi=0$; the Dirac equation alone does not force that stronger condition.

For Levi-Civita connections a conformal rescaling has a canonical local spin-bundle identification beta, taking the scaled orthonormal frames to each other. For the same base vector X it obeys $\widehat\gamma(X)\beta=e^f\beta\gamma(X)$. In that identification,

$$\begin{aligned}
\widehat\nabla_X(\beta\psi)&=\beta\left[ \nabla_X\psi-\frac12\gamma(X)\gamma(df)\psi -\frac12X(f)\psi\right],\\
\widehat D(\beta\psi)&=e^{-f}\beta\left[D\psi+\frac32\gamma(df)\psi\right].
\end{aligned}$$

These signs follow from the torsion-free Cartan equation and the displayed minus sign in Omega. Substituting $e^{f/2}\beta\psi$ cancels the gradient terms in P, giving the standard conformal twistor-spinor covariance

$$\begin{align}
\widehat P_X(e^{f/2}\beta\psi)=e^{f/2}\beta(P_X\psi).
\end{align}$$

The corresponding massless Dirac weight in four dimensions is instead $-3/2$. Neither covariance rule is a license to rescale an arbitrary metric-compatible connection without specifying how its torsion changes. Nor are geometric conformal weights a particle mass assignment.

### The Derivative Commutator Has Two Meanings

Coordinate operators $\partial_\mu+\Omega_\mu$ have spin-curvature commutator $\mathcal R^{\rm spin}_{\mu\nu}=-\mathcal R_{ab\mu\nu}\gamma^{ab}/4$. This is the Dirac representation; the unprimed restriction will be written $\mathcal R^S$ below. If the second derivative label is also differentiated as a covector index, the tensor-Hessian identity is

$$\begin{align}
(\nabla^\Gamma_\mu\nabla^\Gamma_\nu- \nabla^\Gamma_\nu\nabla^\Gamma_\mu)\psi =\mathcal R^{\rm spin}_{\mu\nu}\psi- T^\rho{}_{\mu\nu}\nabla^\Gamma_\rho\psi.
\end{align}$$

The distinction is explicit in [EC-02](<EC-02.md#L370-L378>). Inserting $\chi=D^\Gamma\psi$ and $\nabla_\mu\psi=-\gamma_\mu\chi/4$ gives the necessary relation

$$\begin{align}
4\mathcal R^{\rm spin}_{\mu\nu}\psi+T^\rho{}_{\mu\nu}\gamma_\rho\chi +\gamma_\nu\nabla_\mu\chi-\gamma_\mu\nabla_\nu\chi=0.
\end{align}$$

It is not a complete classification of torsionful twistor spinors. In particular an auxiliary chi does not automatically remove every torsion obstruction.

## 4. Deriving the Torsion Coefficient and the Physical Dirac Equation

Let $\mathcal C_{ab c}=(\omega^\Gamma-\omega^\circ)_{ab}(E_c)=K_{acb}$. Its first two slots are antisymmetric and its last slot is the derivative slot. Therefore

$$\begin{align}
T_{abc}=\mathcal C_{acb}-\mathcal C_{ab c},\qquad D^\Gamma-D^\circ=-\frac14\mathcal C_{ab c}\gamma^c\gamma^{ab}.
\end{align}$$

Define the fully antisymmetric part $H_{abc}=T_{[abc]}$ and $c(H)=H_{abc}\gamma^{abc}/3!$. Brackets are unit-weight antisymmetrization, with $\gamma^{abc}=\gamma^{[a}\gamma^b\gamma^{c]}$. Moving the gamma matrices into one-form and three-form normal order gives

$$\begin{align}
D^\Gamma-D^\circ=-\frac12t_\mu\gamma^\mu+\frac34c(H).
\end{align}$$

For example the identity used in this reduction is $\gamma^c\gamma^a\gamma^b=\gamma^{cab}-\eta^{ca}\gamma^b+\eta^{cb}\gamma^a-\eta^{ab}\gamma^c$. Its contractions with the antisymmetric connection difference give precisely the displayed trace and three-form terms. Thus the coefficient is derived from the shared Omega, not copied from a different Clifford convention.

For pure three-form torsion, $\mathcal C_{ab c}=-H_{abc}/2$ and the trace vanishes. Fix $(X\mathbin{\lrcorner}H)_{ab}=X^cH_{cab}$ and $c(X\mathbin{\lrcorner}H)=X^cH_{cab}\gamma^{ab}/2$. On spinors the resulting operator identities are

$$\begin{aligned}
\nabla^\Gamma_X-\nabla^\circ_X&=\frac14c(X\mathbin{\lrcorner}H),\\
D^\Gamma-D^\circ&=\frac34c(H),\\
P^\Gamma_X-P^\circ_X&=\frac14c(X\mathbin{\lrcorner}H) +\frac3{16}\gamma(X)c(H).
\end{aligned}$$

The geometric contraction is not automatically the Euler operator of a Hermitian matter action with general trace torsion. For fixed g and Gamma choose

$$\begin{align}
I_D=\int e\left[\frac i2\bigl(\bar\psi D^\Gamma\psi -(\nabla^\Gamma_\mu\bar\psi)\gamma^\mu\psi\bigr) -m\bar\psi\psi\right]d^4x,\qquad e=\sqrt{-\det g}.
\end{align}$$

Independent compactly supported spinor variations, or boundary data that kill the associated flux, use

$$\begin{align}
\frac1e\partial_\mu(e\bar\chi\gamma^\mu\psi) =(\nabla_\mu\bar\chi)\gamma^\mu\psi+ \bar\chi D^\Gamma\psi+t_\mu\bar\chi\gamma^\mu\psi.
\end{align}$$

Variation then gives $[i(D^\Gamma+t_\mu\gamma^\mu/2)-m]\psi=0$. The trace contribution to the geometric D cancels in this minimal symmetrized action, leaving the axial coupling. It should not be deleted before varying, and its cancellation does not identify the twistor operator P with the physical Dirac equation. Other matter couplings or boundary actions require their own derivation.

## 5. Horizontal Null Planes: the Frobenius Obstruction

Work now on an analytic complexification. The projective chiral spin bundle $\mathcal F=\mathbb P(S)$ has complex dimension five. At $(x,\ell)$ let $\mathscr D^\Gamma$ be the rank-two distribution consisting of Gamma-horizontal lifts of vectors in $\Pi_\ell=\ell\otimes S'$. This is a specified connection-dependent construction.

The obstruction can be read directly from the Cartan equations. On the spin-frame bundle let $\Theta_{\rm s}$ be the vector-valued solder form, distinct from the cotangent Liouville form in Section 2. Choose canonical horizontal fields $H_a$ with $\Theta_{\rm s}(H_a)=\mathbf e_a$ (constant model basis) and $\omega(H_a)=0$. Since $T=d\Theta_{\rm s}+\omega\wedge\Theta_{\rm s}$ and $\mathcal R=d\omega+\omega\wedge\omega$,

$$\begin{align}
\Theta_{\rm s}([H_a,H_b])=-T(H_a,H_b),\qquad \omega([H_a,H_b])=-\mathcal R(H_a,H_b).
\end{align}$$

After passing to the projective chiral bundle, the first term must stay in the selected null plane, and the unprimed curvature must act trivially on the projective line. Thus Frobenius is equivalent to both conditions

$$\begin{align}
T(X,Y)\in\Pi_\ell,\qquad \mathcal R^S(X,Y)\ell\subset\ell, \qquad X,Y\in\Pi_\ell.
\end{align}$$

They must hold throughout the open correspondence region, not just at one principal spinor at one point. Multiplication of a line representative by a scalar produces no projective vertical displacement; a genuine displacement or an outside-plane horizontal vector cannot be absorbed that way.

If the distribution is holomorphic, constant rank and involutive, holomorphic Frobenius supplies local leaf coordinates of complex dimension three. A global Hausdorff leaf space, suitable families of projective lines and a field transform are further questions.

For a torsion-free analytic conformal spin geometry the standard nonlinear-graviton integrability theorem applies [1,2]. For this fixed-unprimed plane family its condition is vanishing of the unprimed Weyl spinor $\Psi_{ABCD}$. The usual proof uses torsion-freeness and Levi-Civita curvature identities. It is not obtained by replacing that curvature with an arbitrary Riemann–Cartan curvature tensor. In real Lorentzian geometry the two Weyl spinors are conjugate, so vanishing of one forces conformal flatness; a complex or different-signature half-flat construction has different reality data.

## 6. Positive Special Case: Three-Form Torsion Preserves This Distribution

Suppose the torsion really is a three-form at the fixed metric, so $K_XY=T(X,Y)/2$. For $X,Y,Z\in\Pi_\ell$, three vectors in a complex two-plane are linearly dependent. Hence

$$\begin{align}
g(K_XY,Z)=\frac12H(Z,X,Y)=0.
\end{align}$$

Since $\Pi_\ell^\perp=\Pi_\ell$, this proves $K_X\Pi_\ell\subset\Pi_\ell$ whenever $X\in\Pi_\ell$. The difference of the induced Grassmannian horizontal lifts is the class of $K_XY$ in $TM/\Pi_\ell$, so it vanishes. Equivalently the spin-connection difference maps the unprimed line into itself. Projectively it follows that

$$\begin{align}
\mathscr D^\Gamma=\mathscr D^\circ.
\end{align}$$

This is an equality of the rank-two null-plane distributions, not of every horizontal direction of the two projective connections. Nor is it equality of their homogeneous line/amplitude connections: a nonzero radial line scaling is allowed. The covariant argument and a separate generic three-form/dual-spinor polynomial calculation agree on exactly that scope.

Under these hypotheses one may import the Levi-Civita integrability theorem through the proved identification. In particular nonzero three-form torsion does not by itself destroy the projective plane construction. The spinor operator in Section 4 still changes.

### A Concrete Flat-Metric, Curved-Connection Example

On Cartesian Minkowski space take $H_{123}=\tau$, all other components fixed by complete antisymmetry, and $\Gamma^\rho{}_{\mu\nu}=H^\rho{}_{\mu\nu}/2$. Tau is a real constant with inverse-length units. This connection is compatible, has the declared torsion and zero trace, but its curvature is not zero:

$$\begin{align}
R^1{}_{2\,12}=-\frac{\tau^2}{4}.
\end{align}$$

For $L=\partial_t+\partial_x$, $M=\partial_y+i\partial_z$, direct covariant component calculation gives $R(L,M)L=\tau^2M/4$ and $R(L,M)M=0$. The general distribution equality, not this one-plane sample alone, proves integrability for every chiral plane in the patch.

The ordinary flat projective incidence map is therefore a positive local construction for this distribution. In the dual convention of [AST-14](<AST-14.md>), it is

$$\begin{align}
(x,[\pi])\longmapsto[\omega^{A'},\pi_A],\qquad \omega^{A'}=i(x^{AA'}/L_0)\pi_A.
\end{align}$$

The positive reference length makes the affine coordinates dimensionless. For fixed pi, the kernel of its differential is exactly $\pi_A\delta x^{AA'}=0$; radial changes of pi only rescale the homogeneous output. This displays the familiar open flat twistor threefold without declaring the full torsionful connection flat.

Yet constant spinors in this Cartesian spin frame have $P^\Gamma_0\psi=(3\tau/16)\gamma_0\gamma^{123}\psi$. This matrix is invertible for nonzero tau, so that particular constant-spinor ansatz supplies no nonzero solutions. This is not an absence theorem for nonconstant torsionful twistor spinors.

## 7. Flat Metric and Flat Gamma Do Not Suffice

Use real coordinates $(t,x,y,z)$ on $\mathbb R^4$ and a real constant w. Choose the rotating orthonormal coframe

$$\begin{aligned}
e^0&=dt,\qquad e^3=dz,\\
e^1&=\cos(wt)dx+\sin(wt)dy,\\
e^2&=-\sin(wt)dx+\cos(wt)dy.
\end{aligned}$$

But set the full frame connection $\omega^\Gamma=0$. This is not the Levi-Civita connection in that coframe. A passive Lorentz change must transform coframe, connection and spinor together; setting a newly rotated frame connection to zero instead selects a different geometric connection.

The coordinate metric is Minkowski. Let $\mathsf e=(e^a{}_{\nu})$ be the coframe matrix, not the volume density e. The tetrad postulate gives $\Gamma_\mu=\mathsf e^{-1}\partial_\mu\mathsf e$, where $(\Gamma_\mu)^\rho{}_{\nu}=\Gamma^\rho{}_{\mu\nu}$. Only two coordinate components are nonzero:

$$\begin{align}
\Gamma^x{}_{ty}=w,\qquad \Gamma^y{}_{tx}=-w.
\end{align}$$

Dedicated covariant component evaluation gives

$$\begin{align}
Q^\Gamma=0,\quad R^\Gamma=0,\quad R^\circ=0,\qquad T^1=w\,e^0\wedge e^2,\quad T^2=-w\,e^0\wedge e^1.
\end{align}$$

The shared torsion trace is zero. This is not pure three-form torsion: $T_{012}=0$ whereas $T_{[012]}=-2w/3$. Its non-three-form part is nonzero for nonzero w, so Section 6 does not apply.

The dual frame is

$$\begin{aligned}
E_0&=\partial_t,\qquad E_3=\partial_z,\\
E_1&=\cos(wt)\partial_x+\sin(wt)\partial_y,\\
E_2&=-\sin(wt)\partial_x+\cos(wt)\partial_y.
\end{aligned}$$

It is Gamma-parallel, but $[E_0,E_2]=-wE_1$. The constant chiral null plane in this frame is spanned by

$$\begin{align}
L=E_0+E_1,\qquad M=E_2+iE_3.
\end{align}$$

Both vectors are null and mutually orthogonal. The dual unprimed $\pi_A=(1,-1)$ annihilates their soldering matrices, fixing the family rather than silently interchanging chiralities. Its horizontal base bracket is

$$\begin{align}
[L,M]=-wE_1,\qquad g([L,M],L)=-w.
\end{align}$$

For nonzero w it is outside the totally null plane. Thus the Gamma-horizontal distribution is not involutive even though the metric and the full connection are both flat. The missing obstruction is torsion, not another Weyl-curvature component.

### Actual Spinors and Actual Affine Curves

In this parallel spin frame any constant spinor has $\nabla^\Gamma\psi=0$ and hence $P^\Gamma\psi=0$. Nonzero torsion has not removed all geometric twistor spinors. By contrast the Levi-Civita spin connection is

$$\begin{align}
\omega^\circ_{12}=-w\,dt,\qquad \Omega^\circ_0=\frac w2\gamma^{12},\qquad P^\circ_0\psi=\frac{3w}{8}\gamma^{12}\psi
\end{align}$$

for a constant-frame spinor. Since $\gamma^{12}$ is invertible, those nonzero constant-frame spinors are not Levi-Civita twistor spinors when w is nonzero. Levi-Civita parallel spinors still exist explicitly: $\psi(t)=\exp[-t\Omega^\circ_0]\psi_0$. They are not being mistaken for constant components in the rotating frame. No complete classification of $\ker P^\Gamma$ is asserted.

There is also a Gamma-null autoparallel with tangent L. For nonzero w one parametrization is

$$\begin{aligned}
t&=s,\qquad z=z_0,\\
x&=x_0+\frac{\sin(ws)}w,\qquad y=y_0+\frac{1-\cos(ws)}w.
\end{aligned}$$

Its Gamma acceleration vanishes, but its Levi-Civita acceleration is $wE_2$, not a reparametrization term along L. Thus it is not a metric null geodesic and is not a ray in Section 2. No claim identifies this kinematic affine curve with the photon trajectories of an unspecified matter action.

All these rotating-frame fields are analytic. The obstruction is already local; it is not a global-topology or regularity failure. The limit w=0 restores the common torsion-free flat connection. Neither nonzero-torsion example is claimed to solve the vacuum or minimally Dirac-sourced Einstein–Cartan equations. A chosen spin density, stress tensor and action would have to satisfy those equations separately.

## 8. Penrose Fields Are a Separate Construction

The flat incidence geometry and worked massless contour fields of [AST-14](<AST-14.md>) use specified holomorphic bundles, domains, contours and metric/Levi-Civita field operators. Their existence on the Minkowski metric is not contradicted by choosing another background connection. For example a minimally coupled scalar gradient and Maxwell $F=dA$ have the action dependence described in [EC-02](<EC-02.md>): their standard metric propagation is not obtained by indiscriminately replacing every derivative by Gamma.

A spinor coupled through the Hermitian action above has a different torsion-sensitive equation. Even in the special three-form case where the projectivized null-plane distribution agrees with Levi-Civita, the operator contains $3c(H)/4$. An unchanged leaf space therefore does not prove an unchanged amplitude transport, weighted line connection, holomorphic field complex or Penrose transform for that operator. Suitable twisted transforms may exist under additional hypotheses; no universal one is constructed here.

The standard analytic Levi-Civita one-Weyl theorem is an explicit input, not a theorem about arbitrary torsionful curvature. For generic Riemann–Cartan data both Frobenius tests must be checked. For pure three-form torsion the proved projective identification allows the metric theorem to be used at fixed g; solving Einstein–Cartan dynamics can still change g and its Weyl curvature. A kinematic fixed-metric statement is not a claim that four-fermion interactions or backreaction never affect the geometry.

Verified local constructions here are metric null-ray contact geometry, the specified geometric spinor operators, their spin/trace normalizations, a positive skew-torsion projective identification, and the explicit flat-connection torsion obstruction. They do not provide a global complex twistor space for every Einstein–Cartan solution, a universal torsionful massless-field transform, an on-shell matter solution, or a quantization/anomaly/unification theorem. Boundary, action, gauge, analyticity and reality data remain part of each construction.
