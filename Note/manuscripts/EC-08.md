# Joint Gravity–Matter Quantization in Controlled Models

Status: DRAFT — bounded review recorded.

Joint quantization must specify more than a tensor product of a geometry space and a matter space. The gravitational constraint couples their variables, a physical inner product must be defined after imposing it, and a proposed clock must actually be monotonic on the sector being used. We carry out those steps for a flat homogeneous universe with a massless scalar. The resulting Wheeler–DeWitt model has explicit physical states and an exactly normalized packet. A separately assumed effective polymer Hamiltonian then gives a contrasting bounce calculation. The latter is an additional model, not a consequence of the first quantization or of scalar Einstein–Cartan torsion.

We use $c=\hbar=1$, $\kappa=8\pi G_{\rm N}>0$, Lorentzian signature $(-,+,+,+)$ and canonical brackets $\{q,p\}=+1$, consistent with the [shared conventions](<../CONVENTIONS.md>). Matter actions follow [the classical matter chapter](<EC-02.md>). Frequency and inner-product signs will be fixed explicitly rather than imported from the [background-field chapter](<EC-03.md>).

## 1. A Coupled System with Its Normalization Intact

Consider the metric and homogeneous real scalar

$$\begin{align}
ds^2=-N(t)^2dt^2+a(t)^2\delta_{ij}dx^i dx^j, \qquad a>0,\quad N>0,\quad\phi=\phi(t).
\end{align}$$

Time and comoving spatial coordinates have length units; $a$ and $N$ are dimensionless. Integrate over a comoving coordinate cell of volume $V_0$, with units of length cubed. Periodic spatial identifications avoid side-boundary fluxes and may describe a flat three-torus. For noncompact space, the cell is instead an infrared regulator for homogeneous extensive quantities. These interpretations should not be interchanged when discussing global volume.

Choose minimal Einstein–Cartan gravity and the scalar action

$$\begin{align}
I_\phi=-\frac12\int d^4x\sqrt{-g}\,g^{\mu\nu}\partial_\mu\phi\partial_\nu\phi.
\end{align}$$

This spinless matter action does not depend on the independent spin connection. The minimal classical Cartan equation therefore sets torsion to zero. We eliminate that classical auxiliary connection before reducing and quantizing; we are not quantizing propagating torsion modes. Quantum determinants associated with a regulated auxiliary-field integration would require a measure prescription, not a declaration that every such factor is identically constant.

With zero spatial curvature, zero cosmological constant and no scalar potential, the metric Ricci scalar is

$$\begin{align}
\overline R=6\left(\frac{\ddot a}{aN^2} +\frac{\dot a^2}{a^2N^2} -\frac{\dot a\dot N}{aN^3}\right).
\end{align}$$

Its Einstein–Hilbert contribution obeys the exact identity

$$\begin{align}
\frac{V_0Na^3}{2\kappa}\overline R =\frac{d}{dt}\left(\frac{3V_0a^2\dot a}{\kappa N}\right) -\frac{3V_0a\dot a^2}{\kappa N}.
\end{align}$$

Use the corresponding endpoint subtraction, equivalently the appropriate metric boundary term for fixed induced end metrics. The reduced action is then

$$\begin{align}
I=\int dt\,L, \qquad L=-\frac{3V_0a\dot a^2}{\kappa N} +\frac{V_0a^3\dot\phi^2}{2N}.
\end{align}$$

This is a genuinely coupled constrained mechanical system. The geometry and matter kinetic coefficients both depend on $a$, and the lapse is still varied. Here $\phi$ has inverse-length units, so both terms in $L$ have inverse-length units and $I$ is dimensionless.

The momenta and Hamiltonian are

$$\begin{aligned}
p_a&=-\frac{6V_0a\dot a}{\kappa N}, &p_\phi&=\frac{V_0a^3\dot\phi}{N},\\
H&=NC, &C&=-\frac{\kappa p_a^2}{12V_0a} +\frac{p_\phi^2}{2V_0a^3}.
\end{aligned}$$

For example, substitute $\dot a=-\kappa Np_a/(6V_0a)$ and $\dot\phi=Np_\phi/(V_0a^3)$ into $p_a\dot a+p_\phi\dot\phi-L$ to obtain exactly $NC$. The lapse has no velocity, so $p_N=0$ is a primary constraint; preserving it gives $C=0$. A full total Hamiltonian includes the multiplier of $p_N$. The homogeneous reduction has not proved closure of the inhomogeneous gravitational constraints.

Define physical cell volume, proper-time Hubble rate and scalar energy density by

$$\begin{align}
v=V_0a^3, \qquad H_{\rm cl}=\frac{\dot a}{Na}, \qquad\rho=\frac{p_\phi^2}{2v^2}.
\end{align}$$

Hamilton's equations and $C=0$ imply $H_{\rm cl}^2=\kappa\rho/3$. The momentum $p_a$ is dimensionless and $p_\phi$ has length units; $C$ has inverse-length units. Under a change of infrared cell $V_0\mapsto sV_0$ at fixed local homogeneous fields, $v,p_a,p_\phi$ all scale by $s$, while $H_{\rm cl}$ and $\rho$ do not. This checks the classical normalization. Comparing quantum states across regulators needs an additional prescription; leaving the numerical momenta unchanged would compare different local data.

## 2. A Scalar Clock and an Explicit Relational Observable

Set

$$\begin{align}
\alpha=\log(a/a_*), \qquad p_\alpha=ap_a, \qquad \beta=\sqrt{\frac6\kappa}\,\alpha, \qquad P_\beta=\sqrt{\frac\kappa6}\,p_\alpha,
\end{align}$$

where $a_*>0$ is a dimensionless reference value. The canonical one-form satisfies $p_a\,da=P_\beta\,d\beta$, so $\{\beta,P_\beta\}=1$. Both $\beta$ and $\phi$ have inverse-length units; their conjugate momenta have length units. Multiplying the constraint by the positive function $2v$ gives

$$\begin{align}
\widetilde C=2vC=p_\phi^2-P_\beta^2.
\end{align}$$

Classically this is a lapse redefinition: $NC=\widetilde N\widetilde C$ with $\widetilde N=N/(2v)$. On the constraint surface it changes the parametrization, not the gauge orbits. Quantizing after this rescaling is a choice; it does not prove equivalence of every ordering of the original $a$-dependent constraint.

The massless momentum is conserved, and

$$\begin{align}
\dot\phi=\frac{Np_\phi}{v}.
\end{align}$$

Thus $\phi$ is monotonic wherever $N,v>0$ and $p_\phi\ne0$. The $p_\phi=0$ sector has $P_\beta=0$ on the constraint and supplies no scalar clock. We exclude it from the clock construction. In the nonzero sector,

$$\begin{align}
\frac{d\beta}{d\phi}=-\frac{P_\beta}{p_\phi}.
\end{align}$$

An explicit gauge-invariant quantity is

$$\begin{align}
\mathcal O=\beta+\frac{P_\beta}{p_\phi}\phi, \qquad \{\mathcal O,\widetilde C\}=0, \qquad \{\mathcal O,P_\beta\}=1.
\end{align}$$

The first bracket vanishes because the contributions are $-2P_\beta$ and $+2P_\beta$; the momentum ratio itself commutes with the constraint. This identifies a reduced canonical pair rather than merely counting variables. On the branch $p_\phi>0$, one has $p_\phi=|P_\beta|$ and $\beta(\phi)=\beta(\phi_0)-\operatorname{sgn}(P_\beta)(\phi-\phi_0)$. Positive $P_\beta$ is the contracting branch, because positive expansion has $p_a<0$.

The classical volume satisfies

$$\begin{align}
v(\phi)=v(\phi_0) \exp\left[\pm\sqrt{\frac{3\kappa}{2}}(\phi-\phi_0)\right].
\end{align}$$

Each nonzero-momentum classical branch is monotonic. It has no bounce in this massless flat model.

## 3. A Specified Wheeler–DeWitt Quantization

After removing the pure multiplier pair $(N,p_N)$ using its primary constraint and gauge freedom, take joint kinematical wavefunctions on

$$\begin{align}
\mathcal H_{\rm kin}=L^2(\mathbb R_\beta\times\mathbb R_\phi,d\beta\,d\phi), \qquad \widehat P_\beta=-i\partial_\beta, \qquad\widehat p_\phi=-i\partial_\phi.
\end{align}$$

We choose the flat ordering of the rescaled constraint. It gives

$$\begin{align}
\widehat{\widetilde C}\,\psi =(-\partial_\phi^2+\partial_\beta^2)\psi=0, \qquad \partial_\phi^2\psi+\Theta\psi=0, \qquad\Theta=-\partial_\beta^2.
\end{align}$$

This is a wave equation on configuration variables, not the spacetime scalar field equation. On $L^2(d\beta)$, $\Theta$ is the nonnegative self-adjoint operator defined by Fourier multiplication by $k^2$, with domain consisting of functions for which $k^2\widetilde\psi(k)$ is square integrable. Its positive square root multiplies by $|k|$. This spectral definition specifies what the square root means.

Choose the positive canonical scalar-momentum sector:

$$\begin{align}
-i\partial_\phi\psi=+\sqrt\Theta\,\psi.
\end{align}$$

We call this the positive scalar-frequency sector here. Its modes are $e^{ik\beta+i|k|(\phi-\phi_0)}$, not $e^{-i\omega\phi}$. Written in the usual Schrödinger form, its relational generator is $i\partial_\phi\psi=-\sqrt\Theta\psi$. The minus sign is required by our branch, not an instability of the scalar energy density, which depends on $p_\phi^2$. Choosing the opposite scalar-momentum sector reverses this frequency convention.

The physical representation we use is

$$\begin{aligned}
\psi(\beta,\phi) &=\frac1{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{\mathbb R}dk\, F(k)e^{ik\beta+i|k|(\phi-\phi_0)},\\
\langle\psi_1,\psi_2\rangle_{\rm phys} &=\int d\beta\,\psi_1(\beta,\phi)^*\psi_2(\beta,\phi) =\int dk\,F_1(k)^*F_2(k).
\end{aligned}$$

The multiplier $e^{i|k|\Delta\phi}$ has unit modulus, so this norm is independent of the clock slice and defines unitary relational evolution in this particular model. Generic solutions are not square integrable over all clock values as elements of $\mathcal H_{\rm kin}$; imposing the constraint has supplied a different physical norm. The relational position operator evolves as $\widehat\beta(\phi)=\widehat\beta(\phi_0)-\operatorname{sgn}(\widehat P_\beta)\Delta\phi$ on a suitable differentiable spectral domain.

A Klein–Gordon-type norm is an alternative representation, but its sign and spectral weight must be changed deliberately. The background-field convention $+i\int\psi_1^*\overleftrightarrow{\partial_\phi}\psi_2\,d\beta$ is negative on our $e^{+i\omega\phi}$ modes. We do not use that unchanged form as a positive norm. Instead define

$$\begin{align}
(\Psi_1,\Psi_2)_{-\mathrm{KG}} =-i\int d\beta\,\Psi_1^*\overleftrightarrow{\partial_\phi}\Psi_2, \qquad \Psi(\beta,\phi) =\int\frac{dk}{\sqrt{4\pi|k|}}F(k)e^{ik\beta+i|k|\Delta\phi}.
\end{align}$$

For appropriate amplitudes excluding an infrared zero-mode problem, this gives exactly $\int F_1^*F_2\,dk$. It reverses the KG-type orientation/sign and rescales the solution by $(2|k|)^{-1/2}$ relative to $\psi$; it is not the assertion that the unchanged field and unchanged KG product equal the Schrödinger norm. Here $f\overleftrightarrow{\partial}g=f(\partial g)-(\partial f)g$. The current components

$$\begin{align}
\rho_{-\mathrm{KG}}=-i\Psi^*\overleftrightarrow{\partial_\phi}\Psi, \qquad j_{-\mathrm{KG}}=+i\Psi^*\overleftrightarrow{\partial_\beta}\Psi
\end{align}$$

obey $\partial_\phi\rho_{-\mathrm{KG}}+\partial_\beta j_{-\mathrm{KG}}=0$ on the wave equation. Conservation of the integrated norm is not a universal pointwise probability interpretation of this current. Our relational $\beta$ probability is defined in the explicitly chosen Schrödinger representation.

## 4. An Exact Packet and What It Does Not Establish

For $\sigma>0$ choose a one-chirality spectral amplitude

$$
F(k)=\begin{cases}
2\sigma^{3/2}k e^{-\sigma k}e^{-ik\beta_0},&k>0,\\
0,&k\le0.
\end{cases}
$$

Since $\int_0^\infty k^2e^{-2\sigma k}dk=1/(4\sigma^3)$, its physical norm is one. Here $\sigma$ has the same units as $\beta$, and $k$ has the conjugate length units. Evaluating the Fourier integral using $\int_0^\infty k e^{-zk}dk=z^{-2}$ for $\operatorname{Re}z>0$ gives

$$\begin{align}
\psi(\beta,\phi) =\sqrt{\frac{2\sigma^3}{\pi}}\frac1{(\sigma-iX)^2}, \qquad X=\beta-\beta_0+\phi-\phi_0, \qquad |\psi|^2=\frac{2\sigma^3}{\pi(\sigma^2+X^2)^2}.
\end{align}$$

Thus the packet translates without changing shape. Its explicitly finite moments are

$$\begin{aligned}
\langle\beta\rangle_\phi&=\beta_0-(\phi-\phi_0), &(\Delta\beta)^2&=\sigma^2,\\
\langle P_\beta\rangle&=\frac3{2\sigma}, &\langle P_\beta^2\rangle&=\frac3{\sigma^2}.
\end{aligned}$$

The momentum variance is $3/(4\sigma^2)$. Direct differentiation gives $\partial_\phi^2\psi=\partial_\beta^2\psi$ and $-i\partial_\phi\psi=-i\partial_\beta\psi$, as required on $k>0$. The negative-$k$ mirror packet describes the expanding chirality. Nothing in this chosen WDW constraint reflects one chirality into the other or forces a bounce.

The corresponding $-\mathrm{KG}$-normalized solution is also explicit:

$$\begin{align}
\Psi(\beta,\phi)=\frac{\sigma^{3/2}}{2(\sigma-iX)^{3/2}}, \qquad \rho_{-\mathrm{KG}} =\frac{3\sigma^4}{4(\sigma^2+X^2)^{5/2}}, \qquad\int d\beta\,\rho_{-\mathrm{KG}}=1.
\end{align}$$

The fractional power uses the analytic branch with $\operatorname{Re}(\sigma-iX)>0$. This packet's positive current density is a calculation for this state, not a positivity theorem for arbitrary superpositions.

There is a real observable-domain limitation. The volume function is

$$\begin{align}
v=V_0a_*^3\exp\left(\sqrt{\frac{3\kappa}{2}}\,\beta\right).
\end{align}$$

The rational packet has algebraic $\beta$ tails, so its exponential volume expectation diverges. It is a valid normalized state for the displayed $\beta$ and momentum observables, but not a state on which to quote a finite $\langle v\rangle$. In particular $v(\langle\beta\rangle)$ is not $\langle v\rangle$. A volume or density operator needs its own ordering and domain. Unitary evolution in an internal clock does not, by itself, prove geodesic completeness of a spacetime reconstructed from quantum data.

## 5. An Additional Effective Polymer Model

A bounce can be derived after adding specific effective dynamics. It must not be attributed retroactively to the WDW equation. Define

$$\begin{align}
v=V_0a^3, \qquad p_v=\frac{p_a}{3V_0a^2}, \qquad b=-\frac{\kappa\gamma}{2}p_v, \qquad \{b,v\}=\frac{\kappa\gamma}{2}, \qquad\gamma>0.
\end{align}$$

Classically $b=\gamma H_{\rm cl}$. The unmodified constraint in these variables is $C=-3vb^2/(\kappa\gamma^2)+p_\phi^2/(2v)$, independent of any new scale. Now assume the distinct effective Hamiltonian

$$\begin{align}
C_{\rm eff} =-\frac{3v}{\kappa\gamma^2\lambda^2}\sin^2(\lambda b) +\frac{p_\phi^2}{2v}, \qquad\lambda>0.
\end{align}$$

This replaces $b$ by $\sin(\lambda b)/\lambda$. The fixed $\lambda$ has length units, so $\lambda b$ is dimensionless. It is a physical regularization input, not a coordinate-cell interval. A proposed identification with an area gap would be further model input. We keep the classical Poisson brackets, neglect inverse-volume and quantum-moment corrections, and keep a flat homogeneous massless scalar with no anisotropy or inhomogeneous modes. This effective form is motivated by improved-dynamics loop cosmology [1,2]; it is not derived here from a full loop-gravity constraint or an arbitrary quantum state.

Hamilton's equations give

$$\begin{align}
\frac{\dot v}{3Nv}=H_{\rm eff} =\frac{\sin(\lambda b)\cos(\lambda b)}{\gamma\lambda}, \qquad \dot\phi=\frac{Np_\phi}{v}, \qquad\dot p_\phi=0.
\end{align}$$

In particular $b\ne\gamma H_{\rm eff}$ in general after modification. The constraint says

$$\begin{align}
\rho=\rho_c\sin^2(\lambda b), \qquad \rho_c=\frac3{\kappa\gamma^2\lambda^2},
\end{align}$$

so elimination of $b$ yields the modified Friedmann equation

$$\begin{align}
H_{\rm eff}^2=\frac\kappa3\rho\left(1-\frac\rho{\rho_c}\right), \qquad 0\le\rho\le\rho_c.
\end{align}$$

Because $p_\phi$ is constant, $\dot\rho=-6NH_{\rm eff}\rho$. Either differentiating the explicit $H_{\rm eff}(b)$ using Hamilton's equation, or using this continuity equation away from a turning point and extending smoothly, gives

$$\begin{align}
\frac1N\dot H_{\rm eff} =-\kappa\rho\left(1-\frac{2\rho}{\rho_c}\right).
\end{align}$$

For $p_\phi\ne0$, the point $\rho=\rho_c$ has $H_{\rm eff}=0$ and proper-time derivative $dH_{\rm eff}/d\tau=+\kappa\rho_c$. It is a bounce of this effective solution. The minimum volume is $v_b=|p_\phi|/\sqrt{2\rho_c}$.

On the selected $p_\phi>0$ branch and in proper time $N=1$, an exact solution is

$$\begin{aligned}
v(t)&=v_b\sqrt{1+3\kappa\rho_c(t-t_b)^2},\\
\phi(t)-\phi_b &=\sqrt{\frac2{3\kappa}}\, \operatorname{arsinh}\left(\sqrt{3\kappa\rho_c}(t-t_b)\right),\\
v(\phi)&=v_b\cosh\left(\sqrt{\frac{3\kappa}{2}}(\phi-\phi_b)\right).
\end{aligned}$$

Substitution checks both the Friedmann equation and $\dot\phi=p_\phi/v$. The scalar clock stays monotonic across the bounce. At $|\lambda b|\ll1$ the Hamiltonian approaches the classical one. Under $v,p_\phi\mapsto s(v,p_\phi)$ the effective constraint scales by $s$, whereas $\rho_c$, $\rho$ and $H_{\rm eff}$ remain unchanged. A fixed physical $\lambda$ is essential to that cell-scaling statement.

This bounce follows from the added sine Hamiltonian and its approximation assumptions. It is not a scalar spin–torsion bounce: the classical minimal scalar sector has zero torsion. Nor is it a theorem that every loop-cosmology state, every Einstein–Cartan model or an inhomogeneous spacetime is nonsingular or geodesically complete. The WDW packet in Section 4 and this effective trajectory are different constructions, not two automatically equivalent representations of a single proven quantum dynamics.

## 6. What Changes When the Matter Content Changes

Even a field called free in classical field theory is coupled to dynamical geometry through its kinetic coefficients and constraints. The massless scalar is especially tractable because its momentum is conserved and the rescaled constraint has constant coefficients.

For several homogeneous massless real scalars the constraint becomes

$$\begin{align}
\widetilde C=\sum_Ap_A^2-P_\beta^2.
\end{align}$$

Using $\phi^1$ as a clock requires $p_1\ne0$. Its branch equation is $p_1=+\sqrt{P_\beta^2-\sum_{A>1}p_A^2}$, only on the positive spectral region; its zero boundary is not the nonzero-clock sector. One cannot declare the square root positive on the entire unconstrained tensor-product space. A complex neutral scalar is two real fields, not automatically an electromagnetically charged clock.

A homogeneous mass or potential changes the problem more substantially. For $U(\phi)$,

$$\begin{align}
C=-\frac{3\kappa}{4}v p_v^2+\frac{p_\phi^2}{2v}+vU(\phi), \qquad \widetilde C=p_\phi^2-P_\beta^2+2v^2U(\phi).
\end{align}$$

Now $p_\phi$ is not generally constant, and the clock can turn. The fixed positive-frequency construction above does not transfer without additional analysis. For $U=m^2\phi^2/2$, the matter Hamiltonian at a frozen $v$ is an oscillator. But normalizing it at dynamical $v$ requires the full canonical transformation

$$\begin{align}
Q=\sqrt v\,\phi, \qquad P=\frac{p_\phi}{\sqrt v}, \qquad P_v=p_v-\frac{PQ}{2v}.
\end{align}$$

Indeed $p_\phi\,d\phi=P\,dQ-PQ\,dv/(2v)$. The joint constraint becomes

$$\begin{align}
C=-\frac{3\kappa v}{4}\left(P_v+\frac{PQ}{2v}\right)^2 +\frac12(P^2+m^2Q^2).
\end{align}$$

The oscillator's frozen-geometry spectrum has not diagonalized this coupled constraint. The momentum shift is the coupling that would be lost by treating $v$ as a numerical label throughout a joint quantization.

For a Maxwell field, use a general ADM split with spatial metric $h_{ij}$, determinant $h>0$, lapse $N$ and shift $\mathcal N^i$. Its momentum density conjugate to $A_i$ is

$$\begin{align}
E^i=\frac{\sqrt h}{N}h^{ij}\left(F_{0j}-\mathcal N^kF_{kj}\right).
\end{align}$$

The multiplier $A_0$ imposes the additional first-class Gauss constraint $\partial_iE^i=0$ in the absence of charges. This follows by integrating $E^i\partial_iA_0$ by parts with vanishing side flux or an appropriate fixed boundary term. Its lapse constraint contribution is

$$\begin{align}
\mathcal H_{\rm EM} =\frac{h_{ij}E^iE^j}{2\sqrt h} +\frac{\sqrt h}{4}h^{ik}h^{jl}F_{ij}F_{kl}, \qquad F_{ij}=\partial_iA_j-\partial_jA_i.
\end{align}$$

The momentum contribution can be represented on the Gauss surface by $F_{ij}E^j$. Thus gauge reduction/state choices are additional to the gravitational constraints. A single homogeneous classical vector generally has anisotropic stress and cannot simply be inserted into the isotropic ansatz without further assumptions. For massive Proca theory the primary and secondary constraints are second class rather than a Maxwell gauge redundancy; in a flat auxiliary canonical description they include $\pi^0=0$ and $\partial_i\pi^i+m^2A_0=0$. They leave a longitudinal mode, not another Gauss-invariant copy of Maxwell theory. These are changes of model, not solutions of all such fields in the scalar minisuperspace.

## 7. Fermions: Auxiliary Torsion Is Not a Free Joint Gaussian

With the minimal Hermitian Dirac action and the Clifford/orientation dictionary of [the matter chapter](<EC-02.md#L467-L508>), eliminating classical torsion gives

$$\begin{align}
\mathscr L_{\rm eff} =\mathscr L_D(\overline D) -\frac{3\kappa}{16}a_{5a}a_5^a, \qquad a_5^a=\bar\psi\gamma^a\gamma_5\psi.
\end{align}$$

The axial current is a spinor bilinear. Its square is quartic in the fermionic fields. At a fixed regulated geometry, take a symmetric invertible regulated auxiliary Hessian $M=M^T$, with commuting K and the Grassmann-even source J both treated as column vectors. Its quadratic auxiliary torsion form may be integrated by completing a square. Schematically,

$$\begin{align}
\int dK\,\exp\left[i\left(\frac12K^TMK+K^TJ(\psi)\right)\right]
 \ \propto\ (\det M)^{-1/2} \exp\left[-\frac i2J(\psi)^TM^{-1}J(\psi)\right],
\end{align}$$

with a chosen contour or $i0$ prescription. This is Gaussian in $K$, not free Gaussian in the self-consistent fermion. A determinant formula for a free Dirac operator would require the remaining fermion action to be quadratic, or would refer to a separately specified approximation or externally fixed source.

A dimensionless four-generator Berezin toy integral makes the distinction explicit without a continuum measure. Let $\eta_0,\ldots,\eta_3$ anticommute, choose the integral to select the coefficient of $\eta_0\eta_1\eta_2\eta_3$, and put $u=\eta_0\eta_1$, $w=\eta_2\eta_3$. Since $uw=wu$ and $(u+w)^2=2uw$,

$$\begin{align}
\exp[-m(u+w)-g\,uw] =1-m(u+w)+(m^2-g)uw, \qquad \int d\eta_3\,d\eta_2\,d\eta_1\,d\eta_0\,e^{-m(u+w)-g\,uw}=m^2-g.
\end{align}$$

The free result is $m^2$; the interaction has not disappeared merely because a preceding auxiliary integral was Gaussian. This finite illustration does not compute the spacetime Dirac determinant or a cosmological fermion state. A canonical spinor treatment additionally needs its first-order fermionic constraints/CAR, spin structure and gravitational/internal Gauss action. Spinor, Maxwell and scalar physical state spaces therefore cannot be obtained from one another by an unqualified tensor-factor substitution.

## 8. Kinematical Products, Physical States and Amplitudes

On a chosen finite graph and for a chosen compact gauge group, a possible kinematical space is a geometry link space tensored with regulated matter data. Imposing vertex gauge invariance gives schematically

$$\begin{align}
\mathcal H_{\rm kin}^{G} =\left[L^2(G^E,d\mu_H)\otimes\mathcal H_{\rm matter}\right]^{G^V}.
\end{align}$$

The superscript means invariance under the simultaneous gauge action on links and matter. In general this subspace is not the tensor product of separately invariant subspaces: nontrivial link and matter representations can combine into an invariant. For example, $U(1)$ weights $n$ and $-n$ have an invariant product even when $n\ne0$ and neither factor is invariant. Separately invariant factors, when present, give only a special subspace. With charged matter, the endpoint representations must combine with link representations into invariant tensors. Even after Gauss reduction, neither the spatial-diffeomorphism nor the Hamiltonian constraint has thereby been solved. A fixed graph, operator regulator and matter representation remain choices.

The homogeneous construction above goes further in a restricted model: it imposes an actual joint constraint, selects a clock sector and gives a physical inner product. Its relational amplitude is the distributional kernel

$$\begin{align}
K(\beta_2,\phi_2;\beta_1,\phi_1) =\int\frac{dk}{2\pi} e^{ik(\beta_2-\beta_1)+i|k|(\phi_2-\phi_1)}.
\end{align}$$

Integration over an intermediate $\beta$ gives kernel composition by the Fourier delta function. This amplitude describes evolution in the chosen scalar clock; it is not a matter correlator on a prescribed classical metric or a unique four-dimensional gravitational path integral.

A covariant amplitude on a finite complex instead requires a regulated measure, boundary data, gauge treatment and an explicit action or amplitude rule. Including dynamical matter changes that action and the constraints, and a fermionic contact term remains an interaction. Agreement between a proposed regulated covariant amplitude and a canonical physical inner product is a result to establish for the chosen models, not an identity following from equal Hilbert-space dimensions. Refinement limits, anomaly control and physical observable domains are further questions.

## 9. Scope of the Controlled Results

The flat massless-scalar reduction is a solved constrained model with declared lapse, endpoint term, canonical normalization, ordering, clock branch and physical inner product. Its packet calculation demonstrates relational propagation and finite moments for specific observables. The conserved KG-type current is given with a deliberate sign and spectral map; it is not substituted for the background-field product without translation.

The effective polymer bounce is a second, explicitly assumed dynamical model. Its Friedmann and clock solutions follow from the stated Hamiltonian and brackets, with no spin source. Neither this calculation nor a finite graph proves the full inhomogeneous constraint algebra, a regulator-independent continuum theory, a unique quantization of all matter, or quantum-gravity unitarity. No universal bounce, global geodesic-completeness theorem or full standard-model/gravity solution is inferred.
