# Stars, Collapse and Cosmology: Explicit Fluid Models

*Study draft.*

Hydrostatic equilibrium, radial stability, gravitational trapping and a cosmological bounce are different statements. Each requires a matter model and appropriate initial or boundary data. This chapter constructs a static spherical GR fluid, a cold ideal-fermion equation of state, two stellar calibrations and a separate spinless dust-collapse control. A short averaged spin-fluid example then shows exactly what a density correction does—and what it does not establish.

Use the [shared conventions](<../CONVENTIONS.md>): signature $(-,+,+,+)$, $c=\hbar=1$, $\kappa=8\pi G$ and the declared curvature sign. The equations below have zero cosmological constant. Matter density $\varepsilon$ includes rest energy unless a subtraction is explicitly stated. Macroscopic rotation is not the same as microscopic spin: an unpolarized gas of spin-one-half particles can be spherically symmetric. A rotating star needs additional geometry and is not described by the static ansatz used here.

## 1. A Conserved Spherical Perfect Fluid

Let r be areal radius and write

$$\begin{align}
ds^2=-e^{2\Phi(r)}dt^2+\frac{dr^2}{f(r)}+r^2d\Omega^2,\qquad f(r)=1-\frac{2GM(r)}r. \tag{1.1}
\end{align}$$

The fluid is at rest, $u=e^{-\Phi}\partial_t$, and its mixed stress is $T^\mu{}_\nu=\mathrm{diag}(-\varepsilon,p,p,p)$. Assume a regular static region with $f>0$, an isotropic stress and an equation of state closing the relation between p and $\varepsilon$.

The independent time and radial Einstein components are

$$\begin{align}
G^t{}_t=-\frac{2GM'}{r^2},\qquad G^r{}_r=-\frac{2GM}{r^3}+\frac{2f\Phi'}r.
\end{align}$$

Their equations, together with $\bar\nabla_\mu T^\mu{}_r=0$, give

$$\begin{align}
M'=4\pi r^2\varepsilon,\qquad \Phi'=\frac{G(M+4\pi r^3p)}{r^2f},\qquad p'=-(\varepsilon+p)\Phi'. \tag{1.2}
\end{align}$$

Combining the last two equations gives the Tolman–Oppenheimer–Volkoff equation. The factors $\varepsilon+p$ and $4\pi r^3p$ are part of relativistic gravity, not optional Newtonian corrections.

This derivation assumes isotropy. For a spherical anisotropic stress $\mathrm{diag}(-\varepsilon,p_r,p_t,p_t)$, conservation instead gives

$$\begin{align}
p_r'=-(\varepsilon+p_r)\Phi'+\frac{2(p_t-p_r)}r.
\end{align}$$

Specifying only a scalar pressure is then insufficient. Microscopic polarization or additional fields must be averaged consistently before choosing one of these stress models.

For a cold isentropic fluid with conserved proper number density n,

$$\begin{align}
d\varepsilon=\mu\,dn,\qquad p=n\mu-\varepsilon,\qquad dp=n\,d\mu. \tag{1.3}
\end{align}$$

Consequently (1.2) implies $\mu'+\mu\Phi'=0$, or $\mu e^\Phi=\mathrm{constant}$. The conserved particle number of a static configuration is

$$\begin{align}
N=4\pi\int_0^R\frac{n(r)r^2}{\sqrt{f(r)}}dr. \tag{1.4}
\end{align}$$

M is a gravitational mass function, whereas N uses proper spatial volume. Replacing this integral by a flat-volume density estimate changes the model.

### Regular Center and Surface

Choose central pressure $p_c$, obtain $\varepsilon_c$ from the EOS, and impose $M(0)=0$ and finite $\Phi(0)$. A smooth isotropic center has

$$\begin{align}
M=\frac{4\pi}{3}\varepsilon_c r^3+O(r^5),\qquad p=p_c-\frac{2\pi G}{3}(\varepsilon_c+p_c)(\varepsilon_c+3p_c)r^2+O(r^4),
\end{align}$$

$$\begin{align}
\Phi=\Phi_c+\frac{2\pi G}{3}(\varepsilon_c+3p_c)r^2+O(r^4). \tag{1.5}
\end{align}$$

The surface is the first radius R with $p(R)=0$. For a vacuum exterior without a surface layer, set $M(R)=M_\star$ and normalize time by $e^{2\Phi(R)}=1-2GM_\star/R$. The exterior is Schwarzschild. The surface density may vanish or remain finite for a self-bound EOS; a density jump is not automatically a thin shell. With $p(R)=0$, the required radial lapse derivative agrees with the vacuum value. These are GR matching conditions; a microscopic torsion/spin layer needs a separate construction.

## 2. Cold Ideal Spin-One-Half Fermions

The following EOS is a specific noninteracting, one-species, zero-temperature model with two spin states. Its microscopic local-density interpretation assumes wavelengths smaller than geometric and matter-variation scales; the numerical star treats it as a continuum constitutive input. Let $m_f>0$ be the fermion rest mass in natural units, $p_F$ its Fermi momentum and $x=p_F/m_f$. Then

$$\begin{align}
n=\frac{p_F^3}{3\pi^2},\qquad \varepsilon=\frac1{\pi^2}\int_0^{p_F}p^2\sqrt{p^2+m_f^2}\,dp,\qquad p=\frac1{3\pi^2}\int_0^{p_F}\frac{p^4}{\sqrt{p^2+m_f^2}}dp. \tag{2.1}
\end{align}$$

The energy density includes the occupied particles' rest energies. Evaluating the integrals gives

$$\begin{align}
\varepsilon=m_f^4\mathcal E(x),\qquad \mathcal E=\frac{x\sqrt{1+x^2}(1+2x^2)-\operatorname{arsinh}x}{8\pi^2},
\end{align}$$

$$\begin{align}
p=m_f^4\mathcal P(x),\qquad \mathcal P=\frac{x\sqrt{1+x^2}(2x^2-3)+3\operatorname{arsinh}x}{24\pi^2}. \tag{2.2}
\end{align}$$

The Fermi energy $\mu=m_f\sqrt{1+x^2}$ satisfies $d\varepsilon=\mu\,dn$ and $p=n\mu-\varepsilon$. Thus the EOS can be used consistently with number conservation, rather than supplying unrelated density and pressure formulas. Its adiabatic sound speed is

$$\begin{align}
c_s^2=\frac{dp}{d\varepsilon}=\frac{x^2}{3(1+x^2)}. \tag{2.3}
\end{align}$$

It is nonnegative and below one third. The limiting pressures are

$$\begin{align}
p=\frac{p_F^5}{15\pi^2m_f}+O(p_F^7/m_f^3)\quad(p_F\ll m_f),\qquad p\sim\frac{p_F^4}{12\pi^2},\quad \varepsilon\sim3p\quad(p_F\gg m_f).
\end{align}$$

In the nonrelativistic limit, $p=(3\pi^2)^{2/3}n^{5/3}/(5m_f)$, while $\varepsilon=m_fn+3p/2+\cdots$. The rest-mass density $m_fn$ and the total energy density are therefore not interchangeable across the relativistic regime.

The stellar integrations below are GR controls; the ideal Fermi EOS does not eliminate microscopic EC contact correlations by itself. Its use as an EC approximation would need a regime and closure in which those corrections are negligible. This is not an interacting neutron-matter or quark-matter EOS. Nuclear interactions, composition, beta equilibrium, phase boundaries and a crust are extra inputs. An electron gas supporting heavy ions also requires the ions' energy density and charge neutrality; merely replacing $m_f$ by the electron mass in this one-species gravitating model does not construct a white dwarf.

## 3. A Bounded Numerical Ideal-Fermion Star

A convenient integration variable is the cold enthalpy

$$\begin{align}
h=\log(\mu/m_f)=\frac12\log(1+x^2),\qquad h'=-\Phi'.
\end{align}$$

It reaches zero at the ideal-gas surface and avoids dividing the pressure equation by a small $dp/dx$. Define the non-reduced Planck mass and scales

$$\begin{align}
m_{\mathrm{Pl}}=G^{-1/2},\qquad R_0=\frac{m_{\mathrm{Pl}}}{m_f^2},\qquad M_0=\frac{m_{\mathrm{Pl}}^3}{m_f^2},\qquad r=R_0\xi,\quad M=M_0v.
\end{align}$$

Since $GM_0/R_0=1$, the dimensionless equations are

$$\begin{align}
\frac{dv}{d\xi}=4\pi\xi^2\mathcal E(h),\qquad \frac{dh}{d\xi}=-\frac{v+4\pi\xi^3\mathcal P(h)}{\xi^2(1-2v/\xi)},\qquad x(h)=\sqrt{e^{2h}-1}. \tag{3.1}
\end{align}$$

For the particle rest mass $M_b=m_fN=M_0w$ (baryonic mass in the neutron-mass calibration), add

$$\begin{align}
\frac{dw}{d\xi}=\frac{4\pi\xi^2}{\sqrt{1-2v/\xi}}\frac{x^3}{3\pi^2}. \tag{3.2}
\end{align}$$

At the center $v,w=O(\xi^3)$, while $h=h_c-2\pi(\mathcal E_c+3\mathcal P_c)\xi^2/3+O(\xi^4)$. Integration stops at $h=0$. The lapse is reconstructed by $\Phi(\xi)=\Phi(R)-h(\xi)$, fixing the remaining time-normalization constant at the Schwarzschild surface.

For the specified central value $x_c=0.5$, the resulting dimensionless configuration is

$$\begin{align}
\xi_R\simeq4.95023077,\qquad v_R\simeq0.321498273,\qquad w_R\simeq0.329871396,\qquad \frac{2v_R}{\xi_R}\simeq0.129892237.
\end{align}$$

To calibrate units only, take $m_{f\,\mathrm{SI}}=1.67492749804\times10^{-27}$ kg and restore

$$\begin{align}
m_{\mathrm{Pl,SI}}=\sqrt{\hbar c/G},\qquad M_{0\,\mathrm{SI}}=m_{\mathrm{Pl,SI}}^3/m_{f\,\mathrm{SI}}^2,\qquad R_{0\,\mathrm{SI}}=GM_{0\,\mathrm{SI}}/c^2.
\end{align}$$

Using $G=6.67430\times10^{-11}$ SI, $c=299792458$ m/s, $\hbar=1.054571817\times10^{-34}$ J s and $M_\odot=1.98847\times10^{30}$ kg gives $M_0=1.84810M_\odot$ and $R_0=2.72903$ km. The model star has

$$\begin{align}
M_\star\simeq0.59416M_\odot,\qquad R\simeq13.5093\,\mathrm{km},\qquad M_b\simeq0.60964M_\odot,
\end{align}$$

with $(M_b-M_\star)/M_b\simeq0.02538$. This is one noninteracting model configuration, not a prediction of an observed neutron-star mass, a maximum mass or a stability classification.

The calculation used regular-center seeds at $\xi_0=10^{-5}$ and $10^{-6}$ with higher-precision integration. The dimensionless radius and mass changed by less than $7\times10^{-14}$ and $5\times10^{-15}$ respectively. The final normalized mass/enthalpy ODE residuals sampled at 100 interior points were below $4\times10^{-21}$. These are numerical consistency checks, not a rigorous uniform error bound or an uncertainty estimate for real dense matter. A zero-density extension for $h<0$ was used only for surface-event location; no such region was included in the star.

## 4. An Exact Geometric Calibration and Its Limitation

For a constant energy density $\varepsilon_0$, choose radius R and compactness $C=2GM_\star/R$. Put $s_R=\sqrt{1-C}$ and $s(r)=\sqrt{1-Cr^2/R^2}$. The exact interior solution is

$$\begin{align}
M(r)=M_\star(r/R)^3,\qquad \varepsilon_0=\frac{3C}{8\pi GR^2},\qquad e^{\Phi(r)}=\frac{3s_R-s(r)}2,
\end{align}$$

$$\begin{align}
p(r)=\varepsilon_0\frac{s(r)-s_R}{3s_R-s(r)},\qquad \frac{p_c}{\varepsilon_0}=\frac{1-\sqrt{1-C}}{3\sqrt{1-C}-1}. \tag{4.1}
\end{align}$$

Substitution satisfies the mass, radial and angular Einstein equations and fluid conservation. The pressure is finite and nonnegative for $0<C<8/9$, vanishes at R, and diverges at the center as $C\uparrow8/9$.

With $C=0.4$ and $R=12$ km, restoring c in $C=2GM_\star/(c^2R)$ gives

$$\begin{align}
M_\star\simeq1.62528M_\odot,\quad \rho_0=\varepsilon_0/c^2\simeq4.46493\times10^{17}\,\mathrm{kg/m^3},
\end{align}$$

$$\begin{align}
p_c/\varepsilon_0\simeq0.170271,\quad p_c\simeq6.83278\times10^{33}\,\mathrm{Pa},\quad z_R=(1-C)^{-1/2}-1\simeq0.290994.
\end{align}$$

This is an exact geometric calibration, not a causal microscopic EOS: an incompressible interpretation has infinite $dp/d\varepsilon$. Its regular hydrostatic metric does not prove that an admissible material star with these properties exists or is dynamically stable.

The general Buchdahl compactness bound is a separate comparison theorem for regular static isotropic GR configurations with nonnegative pressure, nonnegative nonincreasing density and the usual vacuum matching. Under its finite-central-pressure hypotheses, $2GM_\star/R<8/9$. The constant-density limit illustrates the limiting pressure divergence but does not prove that theorem. Neither the theorem nor this example determines an EOS-specific maximum mass. Anisotropic or effective sources violating its hypotheses require a different analysis.

## 5. Equilibrium Is Not Radial Stability

A radial perturbation displaces a fluid element by $\xi_r(r)e^{i\omega t}$. In the conservative adiabatic problem, a mode with $\omega^2<0$ grows exponentially. The perturbation problem must use the chosen EOS and composition response, conserve the appropriate particle numbers, be regular at the center and satisfy the surface condition

$$\begin{align}
\Delta p=\delta p+\xi_r p'=0.
\end{align}$$

Typically $\xi_r=O(r)$ at a regular center. The adiabatic input is $\Gamma_1=(n/p)(\partial p/\partial n)$ at fixed entropy per particle and the relevant frozen composition; it need not equal a derivative along a different chemical-equilibrium sequence.

A turning point of mass along an appropriate fixed-EOS equilibrium sequence is a conditional stability diagnostic. Its use requires specified conservation constraints and a known starting branch. It is not the definition of radial stability, and static solutions can continue beyond a turning point while being unstable. Solving TOV, checking $R>2GM_\star$ or finding a causal EOS does not replace the radial eigenvalue problem. No radial spectrum was calculated for either calibration above.

## 6. Trapping and an Exact Spinless Collapse Control

### A Local Spherical Diagnostic

For a spherical metric $q_{ab}dx^adx^b+R(x)^2d\Omega^2$, define the Misner–Sharp mass by

$$\begin{align}
1-\frac{2Gm_{\mathrm{MS}}}R=q^{ab}\partial_aR\partial_bR. \tag{6.1}
\end{align}$$

The signs of the future null expansions, not just the density at a point, diagnose trapped spheres. An event horizon is instead defined using the entire future causal geometry and cannot in general be located by one instantaneous density threshold.

### Homogeneous Pressureless Dust

As a separate control model, take GR dust with no rotation, charge, pressure or spin source. Its interior is

$$\begin{align}
ds^2=-d\tau^2+a(\tau)^2(d\chi^2+\sin^2\chi\,d\Omega^2),\qquad 0\le\chi\le\chi_b,\qquad 0<\chi_b<\pi/2.
\end{align}$$

Here $a,\tau$ have length dimensions and $\chi$ is dimensionless. Starting from rest at $a_i$ gives the exact collapse

$$\begin{align}
a=\frac{a_i}{2}(1+\cos\eta),\qquad \tau=\frac{a_i}{2}(\eta+\sin\eta),\qquad 0\le\eta<\pi,
\end{align}$$

$$\begin{align}
\dot a^2+1=\frac{a_i}{a},\qquad \varepsilon=\frac{3a_i}{8\pi Ga^3},\qquad \ddot a=-\frac{a_i}{2a^2}. \tag{6.2}
\end{align}$$

This is not asserted to be the time evolution of the pressure-supported numerical star. It is a different, fully specified dust initial-value model.

The areal radius is $R=a\sin\chi$, and

$$\begin{align}
m_{\mathrm{MS}}(\chi)=\frac{a_i}{2G}\sin^3\chi.
\end{align}$$

At the boundary, match to a Schwarzschild exterior with $M=m_{\mathrm{MS}}(\chi_b)$. The boundary follows a radial geodesic with exterior specific energy $\cos\chi_b$ and satisfies

$$\begin{align}
\dot R_b^2=\cos^2\chi_b-1+\frac{2GM}{R_b}.
\end{align}$$

The induced metric is $-d\tau^2+R_b^2d\Omega^2$ on both sides. With the same outward normal, the angular extrinsic curvature is $K_{\theta\theta}=R_b\cos\chi_b$, while $K_{\tau\tau}=0$ because the boundary is geodesic. These are the standard no-layer GR matching conditions for this pressureless model. They are not a junction prescription for a new microscopic torsionful medium.

Choose future null vectors $l_\pm=\partial_\tau\pm a^{-1}\partial_\chi$, with $l_+\cdot l_-=-2$. Their expansions are

$$\begin{align}
\theta_\pm=\frac2R(\dot a\sin\chi\pm\cos\chi),\qquad \theta_+\theta_-=\frac4{R^2}\left(\frac{2Gm_{\mathrm{MS}}}R-1\right). \tag{6.3}
\end{align}$$

During collapse $\theta_-<0$. Both expansions become negative when $2Gm_{\mathrm{MS}}/R>1$. The boundary is marginal at $a/a_i=\sin^2\chi_b$, equivalently $\eta_h=\pi-2\chi_b$, before the singular collapse time $\tau_s=\pi a_i/2$.

Horizon crossing does not require infinite density. But this particular dust solution later does develop a curvature divergence:

$$\begin{align}
R_{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}R^{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta} =12\left[\left(\frac{\ddot a}a\right)^2+\left(\frac{\dot a^2+1}{a^2}\right)^2\right] =\frac{15a_i^2}{a^6}. \tag{6.4}
\end{align}$$

Comoving timelike geodesics reach the curvature blow-up in finite proper time. This supplies an explicit classical singular-collapse control without invoking a general singularity theorem.

Its proper rest energy is also not equal to an arbitrary multiple of the Schwarzschild mass. Direct volume integration gives

$$\begin{align}
E_{\mathrm{proper}}=\varepsilon\,2\pi a^3(\chi_b-\sin\chi_b\cos\chi_b) =\frac{3a_i}{4G}(\chi_b-\sin\chi_b\cos\chi_b),
\end{align}$$

whereas $M=a_i\sin^3\chi_b/(2G)$. The distinction is gravitational binding and proper volume, not a universal mass-to-particle-number conversion.

In minimal Einstein–Cartan theory, an actually spinless matter action has zero independent-connection source, so the algebraic connection equation sets torsion to zero. This dust control then remains a GR solution. It shows why the name of the gravitational theory alone does not force every matter model to bounce. It is not an extrapolation of realistic fermionic nuclear matter as pressureless dust.

## 7. A Consistent Averaged Spin-Fluid Input

The [averaged model in EC-01](<EC-01.md>) can be used as an explicitly declared constitutive approximation. Suppose a conserved density n and a fixed spin-average prescription give

$$\begin{align}
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{eff}}(n)=\varepsilon_0(n)-A n^2,\qquad A\ge0,\quad\text{constant}.
\end{align}$$

For example, writing the inherited closure as $s^2=\sigma n^2$ gives $A=\kappa\sigma/4$ in that model's normalization. This is not a coefficient inferred from a mean axial current: local spin correlations, state, averaging and composite definitions are additional inputs, as discussed in [EC-05](<EC-05.md>).

The cold first law fixes, rather than independently guesses, the pressure:

$$\begin{align}
p_{\mathrm{eff}}=n\frac{d\varepsilon_{\mathrm{eff}}}{dn}-\varepsilon_{\mathrm{eff}} =p_0(n)-A n^2. \tag{7.1}
\end{align}$$

If $A=A(n)$, there is an extra $-n^3A'(n)$ in the pressure. Keeping only $-A(n)n^2$ would violate this one-variable thermodynamic closure.

Number conservation gives $\dot n+3Hn=0$ in a homogeneous flow. Equation (7.1) then implies $\dot\varepsilon_{\mathrm{eff}}+3H(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{eff}}+p_{\mathrm{eff}})=0$. An effective TOV or cosmological calculation is consistent only when its source, pressure and conservation law refer to the same closure.

A further local-fluid admissibility test is

$$\begin{align}
c_{s\,\mathrm{eff}}^2=\frac{p_0'(n)-2An}{\varepsilon_0'(n)-2An}. \tag{7.2}
\end{align}$$

It must not simply be assumed positive and causal. For the dust toy $\varepsilon_0=m_fn$ with $m_f>0$, the ratio is $-2An/(m_f-2An)$. For $A>0$ and positive n, it is negative on $0<n<m_f/(2A)$, undefined at $n=m_f/(2A)$, and greater than one for $n>m_f/(2A)$; at $A=0$ it is zero. If this one-parameter closure is treated as a local adiabatic fluid, it is therefore not a stable causal matter model across the full density range. Additional spin dynamics or a different closure would have to supply the actual characteristic behavior. A formal homogeneous solution of this EOS is not a controlled inhomogeneous collapse prediction.

## 8. Density Extrema, Focusing and a Local Homogeneous Bounce

Keep the dust-plus-spin expression only as a declared background toy with $A>0$:

$$\begin{align}
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{eff}}=m_fn-An^2,\qquad p_{\mathrm{eff}}=-An^2.
\end{align}$$

Its different thresholds are

| Condition | Positive density |
|---|---|
| $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{eff}}+3p_{\mathrm{eff}}=0$ | $m_f/(4A)$ |
| $d\varepsilon_{\mathrm{eff}}/dn=0$ | $m_f/(2A)$ |
| $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{eff}}+p_{\mathrm{eff}}=0$ | $m_f/(2A)$ |
| $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{eff}}=0$, nonvacuum root | $m_f/A$ |

In this quadratic dust model with $A>0$, the density maximum and the NEC-saturation point coincide at $n=m_f/(2A)$, distinct from the nonvacuum energy zero at $n=m_f/A$. The coincidence follows from the cold single-variable first law, $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{eff}}+p_{\mathrm{eff}}=n\,d\varepsilon_{\mathrm{eff}}/dn$; for a generic such closure at positive n, NEC saturation identifies a stationary point, not necessarily a maximum or minimum. A multi-variable matter model must use its own thermodynamic constraints. For a perfect-fluid effective Einstein source, null convergence involves $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{eff}}+p_{\mathrm{eff}}$, whereas comoving timelike acceleration involves $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{eff}}+3p_{\mathrm{eff}}$. The complete strong energy condition also includes the former combination. A null-focusing theorem cannot be replaced by checking only the latter.

In spatially flat FLRW with no other source, $H^2=\kappa\varepsilon_{\mathrm{eff}}/3$. Its nonvacuum zero occurs at $n=m_f/A$, not at the density vertex. The acceleration equation gives positive $\ddot a/a$ there. Both equations and conservation are needed to interpret a turning point.

### Closed FLRW: Identify the Correct Turning Root

For a complete closed spatial three-sphere, a has length dimension and its conserved number is $N_{\mathrm{tot}}=2\pi^2n a^3$. Write $N_c=na^3$. The same background closure gives

$$\begin{align}
\dot a^2=F(a)=-1+\frac Ca-\frac D{a^4},\qquad C=\frac{\kappa m_fN_c}{3},\quad D=\frac{\kappa A N_c^2}{3}. \tag{8.1}
\end{align}$$

Here $[C]=L$, $[D]=L^4$. Turning radii obey $a^4-Ca^3+D=0$. The polynomial has its positive minimum at $3C/4$, so two distinct positive roots exist when

$$\begin{align}
0<D<\frac{27C^4}{256}. \tag{8.2}
\end{align}$$

At a root, eliminating D gives

$$\begin{align}
F'(a)=\frac{3C-4a}{a^2}.
\end{align}$$

The smaller root $a_-<3C/4$ has positive acceleration $\ddot a=F'(a_-)/2$ and is the local minimum of a. The larger root $a_+>3C/4$ has negative acceleration and is the maximum. This acceleration follows from the full fluid Einstein equations and extends continuously to a simple turning point; differentiating the squared constraint only where $\dot a\ne0$ is not a license to choose an arbitrary glued branch.

For $D/C^4\ll1$,

$$\begin{align}
a_-\sim(D/C)^{1/3},\qquad a_+=C-D/C^3+O(D^2/C^7). \tag{8.3}
\end{align}$$

The root close to the GR maximum C is not the small bounce root. At a closed turning point the density also satisfies $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{eff}}=3/(\kappa a^2)>0$, so the flat-space zero-density condition is not its turning equation.

This construction is a homogeneous closed-universe background model, not a matched stellar interior. Its negative pressure has not been joined to an exterior, and no microscopic EC surface layer or dynamical collapse solution has been constructed from it. Finite density and a regular local minimum of a do not by themselves establish bounded Weyl curvature in an inhomogeneous spacetime, geodesic completeness, a global event-horizon diagram or a new asymptotic region.

## 9. What a Stellar Evolution Claim Still Needs

A static mass–radius point requires an EOS and boundary conditions. Radial stability requires the perturbation problem. Collapse requires dynamical initial data, transport and an admissible matter evolution. Trapping is a local null-expansion statement, while an event horizon and a global singularity or extension concern the complete causal solution.

An interacting neutron or quark star cannot be selected from mass alone. Composition, interactions, phase matching, rotation and thermal history are extra physical inputs. Likewise a black-hole-to-child-universe relation cannot be inferred by combining a GR dust mass formula with an unrelated homogeneous spin-fluid turning point. The EOS, conservation laws, matching conditions and global continuation would all have to belong to one constructed solution.

The calculations here provide explicit controls and consistency tests, not that universal evolutionary chain.
