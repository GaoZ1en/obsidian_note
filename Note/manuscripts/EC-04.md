# The Standard Model on a Torsionful Background and Neutrino Mass Options

DRAFT — classical matter couplings, minimal Einstein–Cartan torsion, and tree-level neutrino-mass matching with explicit model assumptions.

The Standard Model specifies an internal gauge group, chiral representations and coupling constants. Spacetime geometry tells us how to place those fields on a curved background; it does not determine their gauge group or the number of generations. We take the observed three-generation representation pattern as input, construct the needed action terms, and calculate what minimal torsion changes. Neutrino masses require an additional choice of fields, operators or symmetries. Dirac versus Majorana describes a mass structure; type I, II and III label three particular heavy-field constructions of a Majorana mass operator.

The prerequisites are the [classical matter chapter](<EC-02.md>) and the [shared conventions](<../CONVENTIONS.md>). We work on an oriented, time-oriented spin manifold with a Lorentzian metric of signature $(-,+,+,+)$ and a metric-compatible connection. Torsion is allowed; nonmetricity is not introduced. A prescribed background connection and a connection varied in a gravitational action are distinguished below.

## 1. One Chiral-Field and Spinor Dictionary

Use $a,b$ for orthonormal Lorentz indices, $r$ for color, $p,q=1,2$ for weak-doublet indices, and $i,j=1,2,3$ for generations. The spinor matrices and connection are

$$\begin{align}
\{\gamma^a,\gamma^b\}=-2\eta^{ab},\qquad \gamma^{ab}=\frac12[\gamma^a,\gamma^b],\qquad \Omega_\mu=-\frac14\omega_{ab\mu}\gamma^{ab}, \tag{1.1}
\end{align}$$

with $\bar f=f^\dagger\gamma^0$, $\gamma_5=i\gamma^0\gamma^1\gamma^2\gamma^3$, and $P_{L,R}=(1\mp\gamma_5)/2$. These are the same Clifford-minus and spin-connection-minus signs as in EC-02; the spacetime metric used to contract tensors remains negative-time.

For representations, anomalies and mass matrices, it is economical to use only independent **left-handed** Grassmann fields. The charge-conjugate fields $u^c,d^c,e^c$ are left-handed conjugates of the physical right-handed singlets, not additional independent species. In the notation $(d_c,d_w,Y)$, one generation is

| Left-handed field | Representation | Electric charges of its weak components |
|---|---|---|
| $q_i=(u_{Li},d_{Li})$ | $(\mathbf3,\mathbf2,1/6)$ | $2/3,-1/3$ |
| $u_i^c$ | $(\overline{\mathbf3},\mathbf1,-2/3)$ | $-2/3$ |
| $d_i^c$ | $(\overline{\mathbf3},\mathbf1,1/3)$ | $1/3$ |
| $\ell_i=(\nu_{Li},e_{Li})$ | $(\mathbf1,\mathbf2,-1/2)$ | $0,-1$ |
| $e_i^c$ | $(\mathbf1,\mathbf1,1)$ | $1$ |

The Higgs field is $H\sim(\mathbf1,\mathbf2,1/2)$. Electric charge is normalized by

$$\begin{align}
Q_{\mathrm{em}}=T_3+Y. \tag{1.2}
\end{align}$$

The physical right-handed $u_R,d_R,e_R$ have the conjugate representations and opposite hypercharges to the corresponding fields with a superscript c. There are 15 independent Weyl multiplet components per generation, counting color and weak multiplicities, hence 45 in three generations. The neutrino already occurs in $\ell$.

We write the internal symmetry as $SU(3)_c\times SU(2)_L\times U(1)_Y$. The displayed fractional hypercharges are a Lie-algebra normalization. For a compact $U(1)$ with an integer charge lattice, one may use the integer generator $6Y$ and coupling $g'/6$ instead. Choosing a possible quotient by a common center is a further global specification, not something derived here.

### The Invariant Spinor Product in Mass Terms

Let $C=i\gamma^2\gamma^0$, so $C^T=-C$ in the EC-02 representation. For two left-handed Grassmann columns define

$$\begin{align}
\chi\xi:=\chi^T C\xi. \tag{1.3}
\end{align}$$

Restricted to the two nonzero Weyl components, $C$ is the invariant matrix $\epsilon_s=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\-1&0\end{pmatrix}$. Dotted conjugate components transform in the complex-conjugate spin representation, and the Hermitian-conjugate mass term supplies the conjugate expression. No alternative two-component kinetic convention is being introduced.

Antisymmetry of $C$ and anticommutation of the odd components give $\chi\xi=\xi\chi$. In particular, $\chi\chi=2\chi_1\chi_2$ need not vanish. A complex symmetric flavor matrix can therefore multiply a Majorana mass term. Commuting test spinors obey a different bilinear selection rule; their cancellations must not be applied to these fermions.

## 2. Gauge Transport and the Action Terms That Will Be Used

Take Hermitian generators $t_A$ of the color representation and $T_I$ of the weak representation, normalized in the fundamentals by $\operatorname{tr}(t_At_B)=\delta_{AB}/2$ and $T_I=\sigma_I/2$. On the conjugate color representation, the generators are $-t_A^*$. For a field in the table,

$$\begin{align}
\mathcal D_\mu f= \left(\partial_\mu+\Omega_\mu -ig_sG_\mu^At_A-igW_\mu^IT_I-ig'Y_f B_\mu\right)f. \tag{2.1}
\end{align}$$

Absent representations contribute zero generators. The spin connection acts on spinor indices and the gauge connection on internal indices; these actions commute on their tensor-product bundle.

Put $G=G^At_A$ and $W=W^IT_I$. Their field-strength two-forms are defined by exterior differentiation:

$$\begin{align}
\mathcal G=\mathrm dG-ig_sG\wedge G,\qquad \mathcal W=\mathrm dW-igW\wedge W,\qquad \mathcal B=\mathrm dB. \tag{2.2}
\end{align}$$

For example,

$$\begin{align}
\mathcal W^I{}_{\mu\nu} =\partial_\mu W^I_\nu-\partial_\nu W^I_\mu +g\epsilon^{IJK}W^J_\mu W^K_\nu. \tag{2.3}
\end{align}$$

Neither exterior derivative uses the affine connection. Replacing it by antisymmetrized full covariant derivatives would add a torsion term and change the gauge prescription. The coordinate **operator** commutator on a spinor is

$$\begin{align}
[\mathcal D_\mu,\mathcal D_\nu]f =\left(-\frac14R_{ab\mu\nu}\gamma^{ab} -ig_s\mathcal G^A{}_{\mu\nu}t_A -ig\mathcal W^I{}_{\mu\nu}T_I -ig'Y_f\mathcal B_{\mu\nu}\right)f. \tag{2.4}
\end{align}$$

If the second derivative is instead taken as a tensor Hessian with its derivative-direction lower index differentiated, its antisymmetrization additionally contains $-T^\lambda{}_{\mu\nu}\mathcal D_\lambda f$. This is the same distinction as in the affine-connection chapter.

The gauge and Higgs Lagrangians used here are

$$\begin{align}
\mathscr L_g=-\frac14\mathcal G^A{}_{\mu\nu}\mathcal G^{A\mu\nu} -\frac14\mathcal W^I{}_{\mu\nu}\mathcal W^{I\mu\nu} -\frac14\mathcal B_{\mu\nu}\mathcal B^{\mu\nu}, \tag{2.5}
\end{align}$$

$$\begin{align}
\mathscr L_H=-(\mathcal D_\mu H)^\dagger\mathcal D^\mu H-V(H),\qquad V=-\mu_H^2H^\dagger H+\lambda(H^\dagger H)^2, \tag{2.6}
\end{align}$$

where $\mu_H^2>0$, $\lambda>0$. The negative covariant scalar kinetic sign gives a positive time-derivative term with signature $(-,+,+,+)$. The symmetrized chiral fermion kinetic term is

$$\begin{align}
\mathscr L_f=\sum_f\frac i2\left[ \bar f\gamma^\mu\mathcal D_\mu f -(\mathcal D_\mu\bar f)\gamma^\mu f\right]. \tag{2.7}
\end{align}$$

The sum includes generations and each internal component once. It uses the independent chiral fields in the table, not a second copy of their charge conjugates. The symmetrized form matters when torsion is present: its connection-trace bookkeeping cannot be replaced by the torsion-free unsymmetrized action without checking a boundary identity.

The present minimal classical model includes no $R(\Gamma)H^\dagger H$ term or other nonminimal torsion operator. Such terms are possible additional model inputs and can change the Cartan equation. Their presence cannot be simulated by inserting the minimal torsion solution into an otherwise unvaried enlarged action.

## 3. Yukawa Contractions and the Broken-Phase Mass Matrices

Use a separate invariant tensor $\epsilon_{pq}=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\-1&0\end{pmatrix}$ on weak indices, and define $q_i\cdot H=\epsilon_{pq}q_i^pH^q$ and $\ell_i\cdot H=\epsilon_{pq}\ell_i^pH^q$. These are left-handed spinors. With spinor products understood as (1.3), the charged-fermion Yukawa terms are

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathscr L_Y=-\big[&
(Y_u)_{ij}(q_i\cdot H)^r u^c_{jr}
+(Y_d)_{ij}(H^\dagger_p q_i^{rp})d^c_{jr}\\
&+(Y_e)_{ij}(H^\dagger_p\ell_i^p)e^c_j
+\mathrm{h.c.}\big].
\end{aligned}
\tag{3.1}
$$

For the up term, $\mathbf3\otimes\overline{\mathbf3}$ supplies the color singlet, $\mathbf2\otimes\mathbf2$ supplies the weak singlet through $\epsilon$, and $1/6+1/2-2/3=0$. The down and charged-lepton hypercharge sums are $1/6-1/2+1/3=0$ and $-1/2-1/2+1=0$. The matrices $Y_f$ are arbitrary complex flavor couplings, not predictions of geometry.

Read tree-level masses about the constant broken-phase configuration

$$
H=\frac1{\sqrt2}\begin{pmatrix}0\\v+h\end{pmatrix},\qquad
v^2=\frac{\mu_H^2}{\lambda}.
\tag{3.2}
$$

This identifies local mass parameters; it is not a solution of every curved or time-dependent background problem. Substitution in (3.1) gives

$$\begin{align}
m_u=\frac v{\sqrt2}Y_u,\qquad m_d=\frac v{\sqrt2}Y_d,\qquad m_e=\frac v{\sqrt2}Y_e. \tag{3.3}
\end{align}$$

These are coefficients of the left-Weyl bilinears $u_Lm_uu^c$, $d_Lm_dd^c$ and $e_Lm_ee^c$. Choose unitary matrices satisfying $U_{fL}^Tm_fU_{fc}=D_f$, with $D_f$ nonnegative diagonal; singular-value decomposition guarantees their existence. Under $f_L=U_{fL}f'_L$ and $f^c=U_{fc}f'^c$, the mass bilinear is diagonal. The charged weak current compares the left rotations, giving $V_{\mathrm{CKM}}=U_{uL}^\dagger U_{dL}$.

The Higgs kinetic term gives

$$\begin{align}
\mathscr L_H\supset -\frac{(v+h)^2}{8}\left[g^2(W^1_\mu W^{1\mu}+W^2_\mu W^{2\mu}) +(gW^3_\mu-g'B_\mu)^2\right]. \tag{3.4}
\end{align}$$

The neutral mass matrix in $(W^3,B)$ has a zero eigenvector $(g',g)$ and a massive eigenvector $(g,-g')$. Thus

$$
\begin{aligned}
A_\mu&=\frac{g'W^3_\mu+gB_\mu}{\sqrt{g^2+g'^2}},&
Z_\mu&=\frac{gW^3_\mu-g'B_\mu}{\sqrt{g^2+g'^2}},\\
m_W^2&=\frac{g^2v^2}{4},&m_Z^2&=\frac{(g^2+g'^2)v^2}{4},\qquad
m_h^2=2\lambda v^2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{3.5}
$$

With $\tan\theta_W=g'/g$, the electromagnetic coupling is $e_{\mathrm{em}}=g\sin\theta_W=g'\cos\theta_W$. The mass terms have signs $-m_W^2W^+_\mu W^{-\mu}-m_Z^2Z_\mu Z^\mu/2$, as required by the chosen metric. These formulas are for the one-doublet minimal model. A scalar-triplet vacuum introduced below modifies the electroweak mass relations.

These are scalar Lagrangians. With $e=\sqrt{-g}$ denoting the volume density, the matter action is $I_m=\int\mathrm d^4x\,e(\mathscr L_g+\mathscr L_H+\mathscr L_f+\mathscr L_Y+\mathscr L_\nu)$, where $\mathscr L_\nu$ is absent in the minimal massless model and is selected in Sections 6–8.

## 4. Perturbative Anomaly Sums and the Separate Global Check

An anomaly calculation tests the consistency of quantizing the chosen chiral gauge theory. It does not derive that theory from torsion, and cancellation alone is not a proof of ultraviolet completeness. The usual four-dimensional perturbative gauge-anomaly formulas are established inputs here; their representation sums can be checked exactly.

All entries must be left-handed. With cubic color index $A(\mathbf3)=1$, $A(\overline{\mathbf3})=-1$, and quadratic indices $T(\mathbf3)=T(\mathbf2)=1/2$, one generation gives

$$\begin{align}
\mathcal A_{SU(3)^3}=2-1-1=0, \tag{4.1}
\end{align}$$

$$\begin{align}
\mathcal A_{SU(3)^2Y} =2\left(\frac16\right)\frac12 -\frac23\frac12+\frac13\frac12=0, \qquad \mathcal A_{SU(2)^2Y} =3\left(\frac16\right)\frac12-\frac12\frac12=0, \tag{4.2}
\end{align}$$

$$\begin{align}
\mathcal A_{Y^3} =6\left(\frac16\right)^3+3\left(-\frac23\right)^3 +3\left(\frac13\right)^3+2\left(-\frac12\right)^3+1=0, \tag{4.3}
\end{align}$$

$$\begin{align}
\mathcal A_{\mathrm{grav}^2Y} =6\left(\frac16\right)+3\left(-\frac23\right) +3\left(\frac13\right)+2\left(-\frac12\right)+1=0. \tag{4.4}
\end{align}$$

The fundamental weak representation is pseudoreal, so it has no perturbative cubic $SU(2)$ anomaly. Terms with a single traceless non-Abelian generator also vanish. The local charge sums above do not require choosing three generations: they already cancel per generation.

The familiar $SU(2)$ global anomaly is a different test. For the present doublet/singlet content, the number of left-handed weak doublets must be even. Color supplies three copies of $q$ and leptons one copy of $\ell$: there are four per generation, or twelve for three generations. The Higgs is bosonic and is not counted. This parity statement uses the standard four-dimensional spin-manifold $SU(2)$ anomaly criterion; no classification for every possible global quotient or background bundle is being proved.

A type-I singlet adds no gauge anomaly. A type-II scalar adds no fermionic anomaly. A type-III left-handed $(\mathbf1,\mathbf3,0)$ is in a real weak representation, has zero hypercharge, and has $2T(\mathbf3)=4$, even, so it also passes this global parity test. The representations below therefore preserve these stated consistency checks. Possible axial-current anomalies, torsion-dependent counterterms and a complete quantum treatment are outside this classical construction.

## 5. What Torsion Couples to, and How to Count the Total Spin Current

The gauge, Higgs and Yukawa terms just constructed contain no independent spin connection. The kinetic term (2.7) does. Write $\Gamma=\bar\Gamma+K$ and $\mathcal C_{ab\mu}=\omega_{ab\mu}-\bar\omega_{ab\mu}$. With the derivative-first contorsion slots, $\mathcal C_{abc}=K_{acb}$. Its contribution is

$$\begin{align}
\mathscr L_K=-\frac i8\sum_f\mathcal C_{abc} \bar f\{\gamma^c,\gamma^{ab}\}f. \tag{5.1}
\end{align}$$

The gamma anticommutator is completely antisymmetric in the three Lorentz labels. Thus only axial torsion couples in this minimal Hermitian action. Define

$$\begin{align}
J_5^a=\sum_{f\ \mathrm{independent\ Weyl}}\bar f\gamma^a\gamma_5f. \tag{5.2}
\end{align}$$

The sum runs over generations, color and weak components exactly once, including any added heavy fermions. For a multiplet $\ell$, it already includes both $\nu_L$ and $e_L$; no extra light-neutrino current is added. Since the fields in our table are left-handed, each summand equals $-\bar f\gamma^af$. Charge conjugation leaves the axial bilinear even when its Grassmann reordering sign is included, so using $u^c,d^c,e^c$ rather than the corresponding right-handed fields does not reverse their axial contribution.

There is one normalization change when rewriting independent Weyl fields as four-component Majorana fields. For $N=\chi+\chi^c$, the canonical Majorana action has one half of the corresponding Dirac kinetic term, and

$$\begin{align}
\bar N\gamma^a\gamma_5N=2\bar\chi\gamma^a\gamma_5\chi. \tag{5.3}
\end{align}$$

Consequently the contribution of that canonical Majorana field to (5.2) is $\tfrac12\bar N\gamma^a\gamma_5N$, not its full four-component bilinear. This prevents double counting the heavy Majorana fields in seesaw models. A commuting-Majorana cancellation is not a physical Grassmann identity and supplies no decoupling argument here.

### Prescribed Background Versus Dynamical Minimal Einstein–Cartan Theory

On a prescribed torsionful background, (5.1) is a coupling to given geometric data. To obtain an algebraic response, additionally choose

$$\begin{align}
I_g=\frac1{2\kappa}\int\mathrm d^4x\,eR(\Gamma),\qquad \kappa=8\pi G>0,\quad e=\sqrt{-g}, \tag{5.4}
\end{align}$$

and vary the metric-compatible spin connection with compact support. The Cartan equation sets the unsourced non-axial torsion components to zero. Parametrize the axial part by $T_{abc}=\epsilon_{abcd}s^d$, with $\epsilon_{0123}=+1$. Then

$$\begin{align}
R-\bar R=-\frac14T_{abc}T^{abc}=\frac32s_as^a,\qquad \mathscr L_K=\frac34s_aJ_5^a. \tag{5.5}
\end{align}$$

The connection-dependent scalar Lagrangian is

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathscr L_{\mathrm{tor}}
&=\frac{3}{4\kappa}s^2+\frac34s\cdot J_5\\
&=\frac{3}{4\kappa}(s+\kappa J_5/2)^2
-\frac{3\kappa}{16}J_5^2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{5.6}
$$

The currents are Grassmann-even, so this polynomial square completion is valid. Varying $s$ gives

$$\begin{align}
T_{abc}=-\frac\kappa2\epsilon_{abcd}J_5^d,\qquad \mathscr L_{\mathrm{contact}}=-\frac{3\kappa}{16}J_{5a}J_5^a. \tag{5.7}
\end{align}$$

The contact interaction contains cross-species terms $-3\kappa\sum_{f<g}a_f\cdot a_g/8$ when $J_5=\sum_fa_f$ with the correctly normalized summands. It is not a sum of isolated self-interactions. A gauge-singlet neutrino is not absent from (5.2): it still has a spin kinetic term.

This result assumes the minimal action specified above. Adding nonminimal curvature, torsion or matter operators changes the variation; a background torsion must not also be replaced by (5.7) unless its gravitational equation is actually imposed. The quartic contact term preserves the classical phase symmetries of the kinetic terms. It does not, by itself, establish a Majorana mass, a condensate or a cosmological bounce.

## 6. Massless, Dirac and Majorana Neutrinos

In the minimal renormalizable field content, there is no gauge-invariant neutrino mass term: $\nu_L$ belongs to a weak doublet, and neither a neutral right-handed partner nor the fields needed for a renormalizable Majorana construction have been supplied. This is not a claim that every single Weyl field must be massless. A gauge-singlet Weyl field can have an invariant Majorana bilinear as in (1.3).

A Dirac option adds left-handed singlets $n_A\sim(\mathbf1,\mathbf1,0)$, interpreted as conjugates of right-handed neutrinos, and

$$\begin{align}
\mathscr L_{\nu,D}=-y_{iA}(\ell_i\cdot H)n_A+\mathrm{h.c.}, \qquad m_D=\frac v{\sqrt2}y. \tag{6.1}
\end{align}$$

A chosen classical lepton-number symmetry with $L(\ell)=1$, $L(n)=-1$ and $L(H)=0$ forbids $n_An_B$ while permitting (6.1). The existence and smallness of these Yukawa couplings are model inputs. This classical symmetry statement is not an assertion that all Standard Model lepton-number currents are anomaly-free quantum symmetries.

Without specifying heavy fields, the leading lepton-number-violating operator made from the minimal fields is

$$\begin{align}
\mathscr L_5=-\frac12(C_5)_{ij}(\ell_i\cdot H)(\ell_j\cdot H)+\mathrm{h.c.}, \qquad C_5^T=C_5. \tag{6.2}
\end{align}$$

Spinor products use (1.3), and the weak contractions were defined in Section 3. The operator has dimension five; $[C_5]=\mathrm{mass}^{-1}$. Since $\ell\cdot H=v\nu_L/\sqrt2$ in the neutral vacuum,

$$\begin{align}
m_\nu^{(5)}=\frac{v^2}{2}C_5. \tag{6.3}
\end{align}$$

It multiplies $-\nu_Lm_\nu^{(5)}\nu_L/2+\mathrm{h.c.}$ in this left-Weyl convention. The matrix is complex symmetric, not generally Hermitian. Its nonnegative masses are obtained by Takagi factorization, $U_\nu^Tm_\nu^{(5)}U_\nu=D_\nu$. In the three-light-field approximation, the charged-current mixing matrix is $U_{eL}^\dagger U_\nu$; mixing with heavy fermions can add small nonunitarity corrections.

The next section constructs three possible sources of $C_5$. None is required by torsion geometry.

## 7. Type I: Heavy Singlets and a Controlled Matrix Seesaw

Add $r$ singlets $n_A$ with a symmetric invertible mass matrix $M_N$. Its Majorana mass term breaks the Dirac option's lepton-number assignment by two units:

$$\begin{align}
\mathscr L_I=-y_{iA}(\ell_i\cdot H)n_A -\frac12(M_N)_{AB}n_An_B+\mathrm{h.c.} \tag{7.1}
\end{align}$$

In the all-left basis $(\nu_L,n)$, the symmetric neutral mass matrix is

$$
\mathcal M_I=\begin{pmatrix}
0&m_D\\
m_D^T&M_N
\end{pmatrix},\qquad m_D=\frac v{\sqrt2}y.
\tag{7.2}
$$

The transpose, rather than a Hermitian adjoint, follows from the symmetric Grassmann mass bilinear. Put $\Theta=M_N^{-1}m_D^T$. Direct block multiplication gives

$$
S^T\mathcal M_I S=
\begin{pmatrix}
-m_DM_N^{-1}m_D^T&0\\
0&M_N
\end{pmatrix},\qquad
S=\begin{pmatrix}I&0\\-\Theta&I\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{7.3}
$$

This is an exact algebraic congruence, but $S$ is not unitary. Its light kinetic block becomes $I+\Theta^\dagger\Theta$. Thus the Schur complement is the zero-derivative mass coefficient, not an exact canonical light-particle spectrum for arbitrary mixing. At tree level and to leading order in inverse heavy mass,

$$\begin{align}
C_5^{I}=-yM_N^{-1}y^T,\qquad m_\nu^{(5)}=-m_DM_N^{-1}m_D^T. \tag{7.4}
\end{align}$$

For this expansion require $\|\Theta\|\ll1$ in the spectral operator norm, and require external momenta and characteristic inverse-length scales of the curved/torsionful background to be small compared with the smallest heavy singular mass. Kinetic-normalization corrections begin at order $\|\Theta\|^2$; higher-derivative operators carry further heavy-mass suppression. Relative errors can be large if different leading matrix contributions cancel. We do not replace this matrix power counting by an unproved universal relative pole-mass bound. The leading mass rank is at most $r$.

### An Exact Finite-Hierarchy Error Estimate for One Family

Take real $m_D=d$ and $M_N=M>0$. The signed eigenvalues are $(M\pm\sqrt{M^2+4d^2})/2$. A Majorana phase converts the negative light eigenvalue into the positive mass

$$\begin{align}
m_{\mathrm{light}}=\frac{\sqrt{M^2+4d^2}-M}{2} =\frac{d^2}{M}-\frac{d^4}{M^3}+\frac{2d^6}{M^5} +O\!\left(\frac{d^8}{M^7}\right). \tag{7.5}
\end{align}$$

It obeys $m_{\mathrm{light}}(M+m_{\mathrm{light}})=d^2$, so

$$\begin{align}
0\le\frac{d^2}{M}-m_{\mathrm{light}} =\frac{m_{\mathrm{light}}^2}{M} \le\frac{d^4}{M^3}. \tag{7.6}
\end{align}$$

This bound is exact for all real $d$ and positive $M$; it is useful as a small relative error estimate only when $|d|/M\ll1$. It makes explicit what is lost by replacing the true mass by $d^2/M$.

## 8. Type II and Type III: Two Different Triplet Constructions

### 8.1 Type II Uses a Scalar Triplet

Introduce a complex scalar $\Delta\sim(\mathbf1,\mathbf3,1)$, written as

$$
\Delta=\begin{pmatrix}
\Delta^+/\sqrt2&\Delta^{++}\\
\Delta^0&-\Delta^+/\sqrt2
\end{pmatrix},\qquad
\Delta\mapsto U\Delta U^\dagger
\quad\text{under }SU(2).
\tag{8.1}
$$

Its hypercharge is one. The trace kinetic norm gives one canonical kinetic term per component, and $\epsilon\Delta$ is a symmetric weak matrix. Choose

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathscr L_{II}={}&-\operatorname{tr}[(\mathcal D_\mu\Delta)^\dagger\mathcal D^\mu\Delta]
-V_\Delta\\
&-\left[\frac12(y_\Delta)_{ij}(\ell_i^p\ell_j^q)(\epsilon\Delta)_{pq}
+\mathrm{h.c.}\right],
\end{aligned}
\tag{8.2}
$$

where $y_\Delta^T=y_\Delta$, $M_\Delta^2>0$, $\mathcal D\Delta=\mathrm d\Delta-ig[W,\Delta]-ig'B\Delta$, and the additional scalar-potential terms are

$$\begin{align}
V_\Delta=M_\Delta^2\operatorname{tr}(\Delta^\dagger\Delta) +\left[\mu_\Delta H^T\epsilon\Delta^\dagger H+\mathrm{h.c.}\right]. \tag{8.3}
\end{align}$$

The parameter $\mu_\Delta$ has mass dimension one. For this illustrative classical potential, other allowed triplet quartics are set to zero as a model choice. The hypercharge sums in $\ell\ell\Delta$ and $HH\Delta^\dagger$ vanish. Assigning $L(\Delta)=-2$ makes the Yukawa term conserve lepton number, while the $\mu_\Delta$ term breaks it by two units.

In a constant neutral bosonic configuration, write $\delta=\langle\Delta^0\rangle$. Since $H^T\epsilon\Delta^\dagger H=-v^2\delta^*/2$, its potential is

$$\begin{align}
V(\delta)=M_\Delta^2|\delta|^2 -\frac{v^2}{2}(\mu_\Delta\delta^*+\mu_\Delta^*\delta). \tag{8.4}
\end{align}$$

Stationarity gives $\delta=\mu_\Delta v^2/(2M_\Delta^2)$. The neutral Yukawa coefficient in (8.2) is $y_\Delta\delta$, so in these explicit normalizations

$$\begin{align}
m_\nu^{II}=\frac{\mu_\Delta v^2}{2M_\Delta^2}y_\Delta, \qquad C_5^{II}=\frac{\mu_\Delta}{M_\Delta^2}y_\Delta. \tag{8.5}
\end{align}$$

The constant-background elimination also shifts the Higgs quartic to $\lambda_{\mathrm{eff}}=\lambda-|\mu_\Delta|^2/M_\Delta^2$. Choose it positive; the neutral minimum then has $v^2=\mu_H^2/\lambda_{\mathrm{eff}}$, not the unchanged minimal-doublet relation. If extra triplet quartics are included, the triplet denominator and vacuum equations change; for order-one couplings and $M_\Delta\gg v$, their leading relative corrections scale as $v^2/M_\Delta^2$.

The low-energy approximation further requires momenta/background scales small compared with $M_\Delta$ and $|\delta|/v\ll1$. For a characteristic external momentum or background inverse-length scale $E$, eliminating its kinetic term generates derivative corrections of order $E^2/M_\Delta^2$. Its nonzero weak-triplet vacuum changes electroweak mass relations at order $|\delta|^2/v^2$ and is constrained independently of the neutrino mass formula. The scalar has no minimal spin-connection source of the form (5.2).

### 8.2 Type III Uses Fermion Triplets

Instead add left-handed Grassmann fields $\Sigma_A^I\sim(\mathbf1,\mathbf3,0)$, with canonical kinetic terms, a symmetric invertible $M_\Sigma$, and the explicitly normalized interactions

$$\begin{align}
\mathscr L_{III}\supset -\frac12(M_\Sigma)_{AB}\Sigma_A^I\Sigma_B^I +(y_\Sigma)_{iA}(\ell_i^T\epsilon\sigma^I H)\Sigma_A^I +\mathrm{h.c.} \tag{8.6}
\end{align}$$

The transpose here contracts only the weak doublet; the spinor products remain (1.3). The three objects $\ell^T\epsilon\sigma^I H$ form a weak triplet, and $\mathbf3\otimes\mathbf3$ contains a singlet. At the neutral Higgs vacuum they are

$$\begin{align}
\frac v{\sqrt2}(-e_L,\ ie_L,\ -\nu_L).
\tag{8.7}
\end{align}$$

Thus the neutral term is $-(v/\sqrt2)y_\Sigma\nu_L\Sigma^3$, fixing $m_D^\Sigma=vy_\Sigma/\sqrt2$ without an unstated Clebsch factor. The neutral block has the form (7.2), and

$$\begin{align}
C_5^{III}=-y_\Sigma M_\Sigma^{-1}y_\Sigma^T,\qquad m_\nu^{(5),III}=-\frac{v^2}{2}y_\Sigma M_\Sigma^{-1}y_\Sigma^T. \tag{8.8}
\end{align}$$

The same small-mixing and low-background-scale requirements as in type I apply. Charged triplet components also mix with charged leptons; a complete spectrum and current fit must include them. The neutral coefficient alone is not that complete phenomenological solution. Each independent triplet Weyl component belongs in the total torsion current; a four-component Majorana rewrite again uses the half-bilinear rule (5.3).

## 9. What Survives When the Constructions Are Combined

All three seesaw mechanisms give a gauge-invariant dimension-five operator with a complex symmetric coefficient, but their heavy representations, scalar sectors and charged-particle signatures differ. Their couplings and heavy scales are additional inputs. The universal minimal torsion interaction is quartic in fermions; about a bosonic background without a fermion condensate it does not replace the tree-level bilinear matching just derived. A polarized background can modify fermion propagation through its specified axial source, but this is not a model-independent generation of Majorana mass.

The usual gauge kinetic terms, Higgs/Yukawa contractions, algebraic torsion response and heavy-mass expansion can therefore be used together only after their respective assumptions are fixed. Higher-dimensional operators, nonminimal curvature couplings, the QCD theta term, quantum counterterms and a full nonperturbative theory require additional specifications. The anomaly checks here are necessary consistency tests of the input representation pattern, not a proof of a complete quantum theory on arbitrary geometric backgrounds. No sterile-neutrino scale, neutrino mass ordering, thermal history or singularity-resolution result follows from this classical action alone.
