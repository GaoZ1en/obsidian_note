# The Standard Model and Quantum Geometry: A Regulated Joint Channel

Status: DRAFT — bounded review recorded.

Gauge invariance can bind a geometric representation to charged matter without making either factor separately invariant. Fermionic statistics impose an additional restriction: an invariant tensor in a tensor power need not survive in the exterior Fock space of identical fermions. We calculate both effects for a lepton–Higgs channel, then construct two normalized, jointly Gauss-invariant link states on which an explicit transport operator changes a kinematical area. The calculation is a finite regulated model, not a quantization of the complete Standard Model–gravity Hamiltonian.

The matter action and current dictionary are inputs from [EC-04](<EC-04.md>), the gravitational graph conventions from [EC-07](<EC-07.md>), and the distinction between joint kinematics and physical constraint solutions from [EC-08](<EC-08.md>). Physical spacetime has signature $(-,+,+,+)$; connection lower slots are derivative-first. We retain $\hbar$ in quantum evolution and geometric spectra, with $c=1$, $\kappa=8\pi G$ and constant real $\gamma\ne0$. The inherited spinor conventions are

$$\begin{align}
\{\gamma^a,\gamma^b\}=-2\eta^{ab},\qquad \Omega_\mu=-\frac14\omega_{ab\mu}\gamma^{ab},\qquad \gamma^{ab}=\frac12[\gamma^a,\gamma^b]. \tag{1}
\end{align}$$

The spatial spin doublets used below are restrictions to frame rotations of these Lorentz spinors. Positive inner products on the finite representation spaces do not change the spacetime signature.

## 1. The Empirical Input and Its Global Charge Convention

Geometry does not supply the observed internal group, generations, Higgs sector or Yukawa matrices. We adopt the minimal three-generation representation pattern of [EC-04](<EC-04.md>). It is useful here to display integer $U(1)$ charges $q=6Y$ rather than fractional hypercharges:

| Independent left-handed field | Color | Weak | $q=6Y$ |
|---|---|---|---:|
| $Q$ | $\mathbf3$ | $\mathbf2$ | $1$ |
| $u^c$ | $\overline{\mathbf3}$ | $\mathbf1$ | $-4$ |
| $d^c$ | $\overline{\mathbf3}$ | $\mathbf1$ | $2$ |
| $L$ | $\mathbf1$ | $\mathbf2$ | $-3$ |
| $e^c$ | $\mathbf1$ | $\mathbf1$ | $6$ |

The superscript $c$ labels a left-handed charge conjugate of a physical right-handed field, not an extra independent species. There are $6+3+3+2+1=15$ independent Weyl components per generation, hence 45 for three generations. The Higgs is a bosonic $(\mathbf1,\mathbf2,3)$ in this integer convention; it is a spatial-frame scalar. Neutrino mass extensions, including neutral singlet Weyl fields, are additional choices, not additional copies of the neutrino already in $L$.

For a $2\pi$-periodic $U(1)$ coordinate $\beta$, its character is $e^{iq\beta}$. To leave the continuum covariant derivative unchanged, the Abelian coupling is rescaled:

$$\begin{align}
-ig'YB_\mu=-i\frac{g'}6qB_\mu,\qquad Q_{\rm em}=T_3+\frac q6. \tag{2}
\end{align}$$

This makes every representation single-valued under $\beta\mapsto\beta+2\pi$. Writing fractional $Y$ with that same period but without changing the generator would not do so.

For definiteness take the compact product $G_0=SU(3)_c\times SU(2)_L\times U(1)_q$ in the regulated construction. The displayed fields also descend to quotients by subgroups of a common central $\mathbb Z_6$. Its generator is

$$\begin{align}
z=\left(e^{2\pi i/3}I_3,-I_2,e^{i\pi/3}\right). \tag{3}
\end{align}$$

For example, its phase on $Q$ is $e^{2\pi i/3}(-1)e^{i\pi/3}=1$, on $u^c$ it is $e^{-2\pi i/3}e^{-4\pi i/3}=1$, and on $L$ it is $(-1)e^{-i\pi}=1$. The same check gives one on $d^c,e^c,H$.

In fact these fields detect no larger central kernel. For a central element $(e^{2\pi i a/3}I_3,(-1)^bI_2,e^{i\beta})$, the $e^c$ representation requires $\beta=n\pi/3$; the Higgs then requires $b=n$ modulo 2, and $d^c$ requires $a=n$ modulo 3. There are exactly six such elements. Nevertheless choosing $G_0/\mathcal Z$, with $\mathcal Z$ trivial, $\mathbb Z_2$, $\mathbb Z_3$ or $\mathbb Z_6$, is a further specification of allowed bundles and line operators. The Lie algebra and this local field list do not select that global choice. The separate spatial-frame $SU(2)$ is not included in this internal quotient.

The Higgs potential and the arbitrary complex flavor matrices $Y_u,Y_d,Y_e$ remain the inputs specified in [EC-04](<EC-04.md>). Their gauge contractions are $(Q\cdot H)u^c$, $(H^\dagger Q)d^c$ and $(H^\dagger L)e^c$, with integer charge sums $1+3-4=0$, $1-3+2=0$ and $-3-3+6=0$. Their values, symmetry-breaking scale and the resulting masses and mixings are not graph-counting predictions. In particular, the neutral composite $L\cdot H$ used below is not the charged-lepton Yukawa contraction $H^\dagger L$.

## 2. Spatial and Weak Rotations Act on Different Factors

Choose one regulated left-handed lepton mode. This isolated finite channel is not, by itself, a continuum anomaly-free chiral gauge theory. Let $S=\mathbb C^2$ carry spatial-frame spin, $W=\mathbb C^2$ weak isospin, and $V=S\otimes W$ its four-dimensional one-particle space. Use $a,b=\uparrow,\downarrow$ for spin and $p,q=1,2$ for weak indices. A spatial rotation $g$, weak rotation $u$ and Abelian phase act by

$$\begin{align}
R_L(g,u,\beta)=e^{-3i\beta}(g\otimes u),\qquad R_H(g,u,\beta)=e^{3i\beta}u. \tag{4}
\end{align}$$

The Higgs has no factor $g$. With $s_i=\sigma_i/2$, the lepton generators are $S_i=s_i\otimes I$ and $T_i=I\otimes s_i$; thus $[S_i,T_j]=0$. Summing them would generate only a diagonal subgroup, not the two independent gauge actions. For example $(|\uparrow,2\rangle-|\downarrow,1\rangle)/\sqrt2$ is killed by $S_i+T_i$ but not by $S_3$ or $T_3$ individually. Calling that vector a product-group singlet would lose a Gauss condition.

Let an oriented link $e:v\to w$ carry a spatial holonomy $h_e$, a weak holonomy $U_e$ and an Abelian element $z_e\in U(1)$. Transport in these representations is

$$\begin{align}
K_L(e)=z_e^{-3}(h_e\otimes U_e),\qquad K_H(e)=z_e^3U_e. \tag{5}
\end{align}$$

Their endpoint laws involve independent transformations at both ends:

$$
\begin{aligned}
h_e&\mapsto g_w h_e g_v^{-1},\\
U_e&\mapsto u_w U_e u_v^{-1},\\
z_e&\mapsto e^{i\beta_w}z_e e^{-i\beta_v}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{6}
$$

Consequently $K_L\mapsto R_{L,w}K_LR_{L,v}^{-1}$, and likewise for $K_H$. These transporters are defined by $\mathcal P\exp(-\int\mathcal A)$ for the relevant anti-Hermitian connection. For internal matter, $\mathcal A$ includes $-igW^IT_I$ and $-i(g'/6)qB$; for the gravitational graph it is $A^i\tau_i$, $\tau_i=-i\sigma_i/2$, as in [EC-07](<EC-07.md>). This fixes the minus-holonomy/positive-flux-insertion convention without identifying the connections.

Equation (5) is our regulated spatial-frame-covariant prescription. The real Ashtekar–Barbero connection is not automatically the full four-dimensional Lorentz spin connection in (1). Deriving a physical chiral-fermion Hamiltonian from the continuum action requires a canonical reduction, treatment of fermionic second-class constraints and a regulator. Gauge covariance alone does not perform that derivation.

## 3. The Exterior Fock Space Removes a Tempting False Singlet

For identical fermions in this one regulated orbital, use the exterior Fock space

$$\begin{align}
\mathcal F_L=\bigoplus_{n=0}^4\Lambda^n V,\qquad \dim\Lambda^nV=\binom4n=(1,4,6,4,1). \tag{7}
\end{align}$$

Order modes as $1=\uparrow1$, $2=\uparrow2$, $3=\downarrow1$, $4=\downarrow2$. Creation operators satisfy $\{c_i,c_j^\dagger\}=\delta_{ij}$ and $\{c_i,c_j\}=0$. For any one-particle generator $M$, its Fock action is $d\Gamma(M)=\sum_{ij}c_i^\dagger M_{ij}c_j$; the CAR give $[d\Gamma(M),d\Gamma(N)]=d\Gamma([M,N])$. These normalized mode operators are dimensionless; cell-volume factors needed to approximate continuum fields have not been fixed. This finite construction retains fermionic statistics, not merely a tensor-power representation. On spin labels use $\epsilon_{\uparrow\downarrow}=1=-\epsilon_{\downarrow\uparrow}$, and on weak labels use $\epsilon_{12}=1=-\epsilon_{21}$, with diagonal components zero: these are separate tensors.

Write $|ij\rangle=c_i^\dagger c_j^\dagger|0\rangle$ for $i<j$. Six orthonormal two-particle states are arranged into two triplets:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{spin triplet, weak singlet:}\quad&
 |12\rangle,\quad\frac{|14\rangle-|23\rangle}{\sqrt2},\quad|34\rangle,\\
\text{spin singlet, weak triplet:}\quad&
 |13\rangle,\quad\frac{|14\rangle+|23\rangle}{\sqrt2},\quad|24\rangle.
\end{aligned}
\tag{8}
$$

For example, the spin raising operator takes $|34\rangle$ to $|14\rangle-|23\rangle$, fixing the relative sign. The spin Casimir is 2 on the first triplet and zero on the second; the weak Casimir does the reverse. Writing $C_S=\sum_i[d\Gamma(S_i)|_{n=2}]^2$ and $C_W=\sum_i[d\Gamma(T_i)|_{n=2}]^2$, their exact six-dimensional matrices satisfy $C_S+C_W=2I$ and $C_SC_W=0$. Hence

$$\begin{align}
\Lambda^2(S\otimes W)=(\mathbf3,\mathbf1)\oplus(\mathbf1,\mathbf3), \qquad q_{\Lambda^2V}=-6. \tag{9}
\end{align}$$

There is no simultaneous spin and weak singlet in this same-orbital two-lepton sector. One can see the obstruction before diagonalizing anything. The normalized tensor with coefficients $\epsilon_{ab}\epsilon_{pq}/2$ in $V\otimes V$ is symmetric under exchange of the two complete particles: both epsilon factors change sign. Its antisymmetrization $(1-P_{12})/2$ is zero. Thus “spin singlet times weak singlet” is not an allowed exterior state here. A different orbital/flavor sector can change the exchange symmetry; that would require adding that sector explicitly. Hypercharge neutrality would still require compensating fields or flux.

## 4. An Allowed Lepton–Higgs Channel and a Normalized Geometry–Matter Intertwiner

A lepton and a Higgs quantum are distinguishable, so their weak singlet is not removed by the preceding fermionic antisymmetrization. Keep one bosonic Higgs excitation and define the isometry $B:S\to V\otimes W_H$ by

$$\begin{align}
B|a\rangle=|F_a\rangle =\frac{|a,1\rangle_L|2\rangle_H-|a,2\rangle_L|1\rangle_H}{\sqrt2}, \qquad B^\dagger B=I_2. \tag{10}
\end{align}$$

It has one fermion, one boson and total integer charge $-3+3=0$. The identity $u\epsilon u^{\mathsf T}=(\det u)\epsilon=\epsilon$ for $u\in SU(2)$ proves weak invariance. Spatially it remains a doublet:

$$\begin{align}
\big[R_L(g,u,\beta)\otimes R_H(g,u,\beta)\big]B=Bg. \tag{11}
\end{align}$$

The continuum field expression $L\cdot H$ has mass dimension $5/2$ in natural units, but (10) is a normalized state in a chosen finite mode sector. No bound state, neutrino mass or additional renormalizable coupling has been deduced.

Supply a geometric boundary leg $S_r$ of spin $1/2$ at a vertex. Its dimensionless generator is $s_i$; the matter spin generator acts on $L$, not $H$. The normalized joint intertwiner is

$$\begin{align}
|I\rangle=\frac{|\uparrow\rangle_r|F_\downarrow\rangle
 -|\downarrow\rangle_r|F_\uparrow\rangle}{\sqrt2}.
\tag{12}
\end{align}$$

Equivalently its four nonzero coefficients are $+1/2,-1/2,-1/2,+1/2$ in the order obtained by expanding (10). Total spatial generators $s_i^{(r)}+S_i^{(L)}$ and total weak generators $T_i^{(L)}+T_i^{(H)}$ all annihilate it. The common kernel in $S_r\otimes V\otimes W_H$ is one-dimensional: spatial spin zero requires the unique singlet of $S_r\otimes S$, and weak spin zero requires the unique singlet of $W\otimes W_H$.

Neither $S_r$ nor the matter channel $B(S)$ has a spatial singlet separately. Nevertheless their product contains (12). In this actual example,

$$\begin{align}
\operatorname{Inv}(S_r\otimes B(S))\ne \operatorname{Inv}(S_r)\otimes\operatorname{Inv}(B(S)).
\tag{13}
\end{align}$$

Both factors on the right are zero; the left has dimension one. This is joint Gauss invariance, not a claim that adding arbitrary tensor factors produces a physical gravitational state. The single boundary leg is not a nondegenerate three-cell or a quantum tetrahedron.

## 5. A Complete Two-State Gauge-Invariant Transport Calculation

The channel in (10) gives an actual link operation, not just a dimension count. Transport both constituents along the same oriented link. Charge phases cancel and the weak epsilon contraction gives

$$\begin{align}
B^\dagger\big[K_L(e)\otimes K_H(e)\big]B=h_e. \tag{14}
\end{align}$$

For instance, take $h=e^{-i\theta\sigma_1/2}$, $U=e^{-i\eta\sigma_2/2}$, $z=e^{i\phi}$. At $\theta=\pi$, $\eta=\pi/2$, $\phi=\pi/3$, the result is $-i\sigma_1$: the composite spin is flipped, although its weak transport and Abelian phases have disappeared from this singlet channel. This does not make the Higgs a spatial spinor; its transport in (5) contains no $h$.

To impose the endpoint Gauss conditions rather than only state a covariance law, use two vertices $v,w$, one dynamical spatial link $h:v\to w$, and the fixed boundary leg $S_r$ attached at $v$. The far end of that boundary leg is retained as boundary data; no extra exterior vertex constraint is claimed solved. Keep exactly one composite $F$, located at either endpoint. The reduced matter space is $\mathbb C_v^2\oplus\mathbb C_w^2$. The spatial link space remains the **full** $L^2(SU(2),dh)$ with normalized Haar measure; it is not truncated to a fixed link spin.

A state is a function of $h$ valued in $S_r\otimes(\mathbb C_v^2\oplus\mathbb C_w^2)$. The finite-patch gauge action is

$$\begin{align}
(\mathcal U(g_v,g_w)\Psi)(h) =\left[g_v\otimes\operatorname{diag}(g_v,g_w)\right] \Psi(g_w^{-1}hg_v). \tag{15}
\end{align}$$

The weak and Abelian links may also be included with Haar measure; the chosen channel is constant in their link coordinates and already internally invariant. Restoring its constituents is performed by $B$ at the occupied vertex.

With $|s\rangle=(|\uparrow\downarrow\rangle-|\downarrow\uparrow\rangle)/\sqrt2$, define

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Psi_v(h)&=|s\rangle_{r,F_v},\\
\Psi_w(h)&=(I_r\otimes h)|s\rangle_{r,F_w}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{16}
$$

Position sectors make their inner product zero, while each has pointwise norm one. Haar normalization therefore gives $\langle\Psi_x,\Psi_y\rangle=\delta_{xy}$. They are exactly gauge invariant. For $\Psi_v$, (15) applies $g_v\otimes g_v$ to the singlet. For $\Psi_w$, the consequential cancellation is

$$\begin{align}
(g_v\otimes g_w)(I\otimes g_w^{-1}hg_v)|s\rangle =(I\otimes h)(g_v\otimes g_v)|s\rangle =(I\otimes h)|s\rangle. \tag{17}
\end{align}$$

Thus both endpoint conditions have been imposed, including the transformation of the link argument. The constant function in $\Psi_v$ has link spin zero; the matrix elements in $\Psi_w$ have link spin $1/2$.

Choose a real transfer energy $t$ and the operator

$$
(\widehat H_{\rm tr}\Psi)(h)
 =-t\left[I_r\otimes
 \begin{pmatrix}0&h^\dagger\\h&0\end{pmatrix}\right]\Psi(h).
\tag{18}
$$

This is a bounded self-adjoint multiplication operator with square $t^2I$. The endpoint laws make it commute with (15). It represents simultaneous transfer of the lepton and Higgs through the projected channel, not their individual continuum kinetic Hamiltonians. On the vacuum/one-composite sectors it can be realized by normalized composite creation/annihilation maps; those composite maps are not asserted to satisfy unrestricted canonical CAR.

Using $h^\dagger h=I$ gives the actual normalized matrix elements and proves there is no leakage:

$$
\widehat H_{\rm tr}\Psi_v=-t\Psi_w,\qquad
\widehat H_{\rm tr}\Psi_w=-t\Psi_v,\qquad
H_{\rm inv}=\begin{pmatrix}0&-t\\-t&0\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{19}
$$

Its eigenstates $(\Psi_v\pm\Psi_w)/\sqrt2$ have energies $\mp t$. These are eigenvalues of this chosen transfer model, not a solved gravitational scalar constraint. The absence of an $h$-dependent energy in this isolated link is expected from unitary transport, not evidence that all curvature effects vanish.

Take an embedded surface crossing only the dynamical link once transversely, away from true vertices. The [EC-07](<EC-07.md>) puncture/Casimir prescription gives

$$
a_0=|\kappa\gamma|\hbar,\qquad
A_* =a_0\frac{\sqrt3}{2},\qquad
A_{\rm inv}=\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\0&A_*\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{20}
$$

The boundary leg is not crossed by this surface. The eigenvalues follow from link spin 0 and $1/2$, not from a claim that the link retains a fixed spin under holonomy multiplication. In particular,

$$
[A_{\rm inv},H_{\rm inv}]
 =tA_*\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\-1&0\end{pmatrix}\ne0
\quad\text{if }t\ne0.
\tag{21}
$$

The positive-insertion flux operator provides the local counterpart. On smooth link functions put $L_if(h)=\left.\frac{d}{ds}f(he^{s\tau_i})\right|_0$ and $\widehat E_i=i\kappa\gamma\hbar L_i$. For the four-dimensional position/spin block in (18),

$$
[\widehat E_i,\widehat H_{\rm tr}]
 =-it\kappa\gamma\hbar
 \begin{pmatrix}0&-\tau_i h^\dagger\\h\tau_i&0\end{pmatrix},
\tag{22}
$$

with the spectator $I_r$ understood. The inverse holonomy differentiates as $L_i h^\dagger=-\tau_i h^\dagger$, fixing the top-right sign. Individual flux components need not preserve Gauss invariants; the Casimir area in (20) does preserve the displayed two-state space.

With an external model time $\tau$ and initial state $\Psi_v$, ordinary Schrödinger evolution evaluates to

$$\begin{align}
\Psi(\tau)=\cos(t\tau/\hbar)\Psi_v+i\sin(t\tau/\hbar)\Psi_w, \qquad \langle A\rangle_\tau=A_*\sin^2(t\tau/\hbar). \tag{23}
\end{align}$$

Here $t$ has energy units and $\tau$ time units, so the phase is dimensionless. This is a gauge-invariant matter–geometry correlation in a declared regulated model. The oscillation is not a cosmological bounce or a relational observable of full gravity. The surface remains an embedded, kinematical choice; no diffeomorphism or Hamiltonian constraint has been imposed. The coefficient $t$ and the same-site one-composite restriction are model choices, not values derived from the Higgs/Yukawa action.

## 6. What the Construction Does Not Replace

The empirical representation pattern must pass its own quantum consistency tests. Using the standard four-dimensional chiral-anomaly formulas as inputs, one generation in the integer convention gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal A_{SU(3)^3}&=2-1-1=0,\\
\mathcal A_{SU(3)^2q}&=\tfrac12(2-4+2)=0,\qquad
\mathcal A_{SU(2)^2q}=\tfrac12(3-3)=0,\\
\mathcal A_{q^3}&=6-192+24-54+216=0,\\
\mathcal A_{\mathrm{grav}^2q}&=6-12+6-6+6=0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{24}
$$

The separate familiar weak $SU(2)$ global-anomaly test counts four left-handed doublets per generation: three colors of $Q$ plus $L$. The bosonic Higgs is not counted. This uses the standard criterion on spin four-manifolds, not a classification of every global quotient/background. These cancellations already hold per generation; they do not select three generations. Omitting $e^c$, for example, leaves the integer cubic sum $-216$. Finite Gauss matrices do not replace a continuum chiral measure, anomaly analysis or control of fermion doubling.

For minimal metric-compatible Einstein–Cartan dynamics, [EC-04](<EC-04.md>) supplies the total axial source and the classically eliminated torsion interaction. In natural-unit action conventions,

$$\begin{align}
J_5^a=\sum_{f\ \mathrm{independent\ Weyl}}\bar f\gamma^a\gamma_5f, \qquad \mathscr L_{\rm contact}=-\frac{3\kappa}{16}J_{5a}J_5^a. \tag{25}
\end{align}$$

Each color, weak and generation component occurs once. If an independent Weyl field is rewritten as a canonically normalized four-component Majorana field $N$, its contribution is $\tfrac12\bar N\gamma^a\gamma_5N$, not the full doubled bilinear. This is not a commuting-spinor identity and does not make a sterile neutrino decouple from torsion. Adding Holst/nonminimal terms, quantizing before eliminating fermionic second-class constraints, or choosing nonmetricity requires its own action and reduction. There is no automatic spin lift of an arbitrary nonmetric connection, nor a universal substitution for the real gravitational canonical connection.

Nothing in the finite model derived the contact coefficient or a quantum measure for auxiliary torsion. The polynomial current square in (25) contains cross-species interactions and cannot generally be replaced by independent free-fermion determinants. A covariant amplitude needs a specified action, measure, regulator, boundary state, gauge fixing and a rule for interactions, as well as any refinement limit. Writing a formal partition symbol or matching a few finite coefficients does not establish equivalence to a canonical physical inner product.

The concrete results here are therefore the global charge bookkeeping, the exterior-state exclusion, the normalized joint intertwiner, and the evaluated invariant transfer/area matrices. They demonstrate how prescribed Standard Model representations can participate in regulated quantum geometry. They do not derive the Standard Model, neutrino masses, generation counts, continuum spectra or complete joint gravitational dynamics.
