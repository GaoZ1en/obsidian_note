# Canonical and Covariant Quantization of Gravity with Torsion

DRAFT — a specified classical reduction, explicit Gauss-invariant graph states, and bounded finite quantum constructions. The Hamiltonian constraint and a complete physical quantum theory are not solved here.

A connection, a canonical coordinate and a quantum holonomy are not interchangeable starting points. We first choose an action and its boundary conditions. We then distinguish eliminating algebraic torsion, changing gravitational canonical variables, imposing the internal rotation constraint, and constructing dynamical amplitudes. A finite spin-network calculation will make the kinematical construction concrete; a separate finite compact-group kernel will show what a genuine composition calculation does and does not establish.

Work on an oriented, time-oriented spin spacetime. The metric has signature $(-,+,+,+)$, the first lower coordinate-connection index is the derivative direction, and $\kappa=8\pi G>0$. Classical matter formulas use $c=\hbar=1$; $\hbar$ is restored explicitly in quantum formulas. The spinor dictionary is the one fixed in [the shared conventions](<../CONVENTIONS.md>) and [EC-02](<EC-02.md>):

$$\begin{align}
\{\gamma^I,\gamma^J\}=-2\eta^{IJ},\qquad \bar\psi=\psi^\dagger\gamma^0,\qquad \Omega_\mu=-\frac14\omega_{IJ\mu}\gamma^{IJ}. \tag{1.1}
\end{align}$$

Here $\gamma^{IJ}=[\gamma^I,\gamma^J]/2$ and $\gamma_5=i\gamma^0\gamma^1\gamma^2\gamma^3$. Capital indices are Lorentz-frame labels; $i,j,k=1,2,3$ will label spatial frame rotations. The fermions are Grassmann fields. No arbitrary nonmetric $GL$ connection is identified with a real $SU(2)$ canonical connection.

## 1. Choose the Action Before Choosing Its Canonical Variables

### 1.1 Minimal Einstein–Cartan Theory

Take a nondegenerate oriented tetrad $e^I$, a metric-compatible Lorentz connection $\omega^{IJ}=-\omega^{JI}$, and one Hermitian minimally coupled Dirac action. The bulk gravitational action is

$$\begin{align}
I_{\mathrm{EC}}= \frac1{4\kappa}\int_M\epsilon_{IJKL}e^I\wedge e^J\wedge R^{KL}(\omega) +I_D[e,\omega,\psi],\qquad \epsilon_{0123}=+1, \tag{1.2}
\end{align}$$

where $R^{IJ}=d\omega^{IJ}+\omega^I{}_K\wedge\omega^{KJ}=\tfrac12R^{IJ}{}_{\mu\nu}\,dx^\mu\wedge dx^\nu$ and $T^I=de^I+\omega^I{}_J\wedge e^J$. Frame-valued forms are local components patched by Lorentz transformations; the contracted action form is global. The coefficient is fixed by

$$\begin{align}
\epsilon_{IJKL}e^I\wedge e^J\wedge R^{KL} =2\sqrt{-g}\,R(\omega)\,d^4x. \tag{1.3}
\end{align}$$

The scalar on the right uses the independent metric-compatible connection, not its Levi-Civita replacement. The matter scalar Lagrangian is

$$\begin{align}
\mathscr L_D=\frac i2\left[ \bar\psi\gamma^\mu D_\mu\psi-(D_\mu\bar\psi)\gamma^\mu\psi\right] -m\bar\psi\psi. \tag{1.4}
\end{align}$$

The matter action is $I_D=\int_M\sqrt{-g}\,\mathscr L_D\,d^4x$. For the connection equation initially use compactly supported variations. The Cartan equation is algebraic. With $J_5^I=\bar\psi\gamma^I\gamma_5\psi$ and axial torsion $T_{IJK}=\epsilon_{IJKL}s^L$, the terms depending on $s$ are

$$\begin{align}
\mathscr L_s=\frac{3}{4\kappa}s_Is^I+\frac34s_IJ_5^I =\frac{3}{4\kappa}(s+\kappa J_5/2)^2 -\frac{3\kappa}{16}J_{5I}J_5^I. \tag{1.5}
\end{align}$$

The unsourced non-axial components vanish in this minimal theory. Thus

$$\begin{align}
T_{IJK}=-\frac\kappa2\epsilon_{IJKL}J_5^L,\qquad \mathscr L_{\mathrm{contact}}=-\frac{3\kappa}{16}J_5^2. \tag{1.6}
\end{align}$$

This is the EC-02 action normalization. For several independent fermions, $J_5$ is the correctly normalized total current, including its cross-species contact terms; a canonical four-component Majorana rewrite contributes half of its axial bilinear, as explained in [EC-04](<EC-04.md#L228-L300>). No new torsion wave equation has appeared.

The reduced bulk action is therefore Levi-Civita Einstein gravity plus the torsion-free Dirac action and (1.6). This is the prescription used for the gravitational canonical construction below. The remaining matter contact interaction is not silently removed when the connection is eliminated.

### 1.2 A Holst Term Is a Different Prescription with Spin Matter

For comparison, define the internal Lorentz dual on bivectors by

$$\begin{align}
(\star X)_{IJ}=\frac12\epsilon_{IJ}{}^{KL}X_{KL},\qquad \star^2=-1.
\end{align}$$

Write $\Sigma_{IJ}=e_I\wedge e_J$. One may instead choose

$$\begin{align}
I_\eta=\frac1{2\kappa}\int_M \big[(\star\Sigma)_{IJ}+\eta_H\Sigma_{IJ}\big]\wedge R^{IJ}+I_D, \tag{1.7}
\end{align}$$

with a real dimensionless parameter $\eta_H$. In vacuum, the internal operator $\star+\eta_H$ is invertible:

$$\begin{align}
(\star+\eta_H)^{-1}=\frac{\eta_H-\star}{1+\eta_H^2}. \tag{1.8}
\end{align}$$

For nondegenerate tetrads its connection equation therefore still gives zero torsion. With a spin current, however, the inverse acts on a nonzero source and changes the algebraic response. The Nieh–Yan identity makes the distinction visible:

$$\begin{align}
e_I\wedge e_J\wedge R^{IJ} =T_I\wedge T^I-d(e_I\wedge T^I). \tag{1.9}
\end{align}$$

The added term is not merely an irrelevant boundary term on arbitrary torsionful configurations. Its matter contact coefficients must be recomputed from the chosen Holst and possible nonminimal fermion action. We do not import (1.6) unchanged into (1.7). Nor do we identify $\eta_H$ with the canonical parameter introduced below without deriving the convention-dependent matching for that different action.

## 2. What Eliminating an Auxiliary Connection Means

In the rewritten minimal bulk action (1.5), $s^I$ has no time derivative. If retained as an auxiliary canonical coordinate, it has primary momentum $p_I=0$ and secondary equation

$$\begin{align}
\chi_I=\frac{3}{2\kappa}\eta_{IJ}s^J+\frac34\eta_{IJ}J_5^J=0. \tag{2.1}
\end{align}$$

With the source held fixed, $\{p_I,\chi_J\}=-3\eta_{JI}/(2\kappa)$ is invertible. The eight auxiliary conditions are second class, not an independent set of first-class gauge generators. This finite auxiliary-sector calculation illustrates the elimination; it is not a complete Dirac-constraint analysis of the unreduced tetrad, connection and fermion system.

Classical elimination and a functional integral also have different requirements. A finite convergent example is

$$\begin{align}
\int_{\mathbb R}dx\, \exp\left[-\frac{a x^2/2+jx}{\hbar}\right] =\sqrt{\frac{2\pi\hbar}{a}}
\exp\left[\frac{j^2}{2a\hbar}\right],\qquad a,\hbar>0. \tag{2.2}
\end{align}$$

The on-shell quadratic action is $-j^2/(2a)$, but the integral also has a determinant prefactor. If the quadratic coefficient depends on other fields, that factor matters. The real Lorentzian torsion form is indefinite and requires a regulated oscillatory prescription; (2.2) is not its unregulated functional integral. A quantum claim about integrating out torsion must state a measure, contour/regulator and treatment of the remaining constraints, not only perform the classical square completion.

In a time gauge, the velocity-independent contact potential contributes

$$\begin{align}
H_{\mathrm{contact}}[N]=\frac{3\kappa}{16} \int_\Sigma d^3x\,N\sqrt q\,J_5^2. \tag{2.3}
\end{align}$$

The sign is minus the contact Lagrangian sign; $J_5^2$ is Lorentzian and is not automatically positive. If $\xi=q^{1/4}\psi$ is a fermion half-density and $\mathfrak J_5^I=\sqrt q\,J_5^I$, the same expression is

$$\begin{align}
H_{\mathrm{contact}}[N]=\frac{3\kappa}{16} \int_\Sigma d^3x\,\frac{N}{\sqrt q}\, \mathfrak J_{5I}\mathfrak J_5^I. \tag{2.4}
\end{align}$$

This identity fixes the density and coupling coefficient. A complete canonical fermion reduction additionally handles its own primary second-class relations and any compensating gravitational-momentum shift induced by the half-density variables. Those steps are not replaced by declaring every fermion bracket equal to a bosonic one.

## 3. ADM Variables, Boundary Conditions and the Symplectic Sign

Assume a chosen spacelike foliation $M=[t_0,t_1]\times\Sigma$, with $\Sigma$ compact, oriented and without boundary. Let $N>0$ be the lapse, $N^a$ the shift, and $q_{ab}$ the positive spatial metric:

$$\begin{align}
ds^2=-N^2dt^2+q_{ab}(dx^a+N^a dt)(dx^b+N^b dt). \tag{3.1}
\end{align}$$

Define the extrinsic curvature by

$$\begin{align}
K_{ab}=\frac1{2N}\left(\dot q_{ab}-\mathcal L_{\vec N}q_{ab}\right). \tag{3.2}
\end{align}$$

Here $q=\det(q_{ab})$ and $K=q^{ab}K_{ab}$. This $K$ is extrinsic curvature, not the contorsion of the original connection. After minimal torsion elimination, add the metric endpoint term

$$\begin{align}
I_{\mathrm{end}}=-\frac1\kappa \left[\int_\Sigma\sqrt q\,K\,d^3x\right]_{t_0}^{t_1}, \tag{3.3}
\end{align}$$

and fix $q_{ab}$ at the endpoints. There is no spatial-boundary term under the stated closed-slice assumption. The resulting gravitational bulk action is

$$\begin{align}
I_{\mathrm{ADM}}=\frac1{2\kappa}\int dt\,d^3x\, N\sqrt q\left({}^{(3)}R+K_{ab}K^{ab}-K^2\right). \tag{3.4}
\end{align}$$

The sign in (3.3) belongs to (3.2). For instance, flat FLRW with $q_{ab}=a^2\delta_{ab}$ gives $K=3\dot a/(aN)$ and a bulk kinetic density $-3a\dot a^2/(\kappa N)$. This is a sign check, not a proof of the full canonical constraint algebra.

Differentiating (3.4) with respect to $\dot q_{ab}$ gives

$$\begin{align}
\pi^{ab}=\frac{\sqrt q}{2\kappa}(K^{ab}-Kq^{ab}),\qquad \mathcal H=\frac{2\kappa}{\sqrt q} \left(\pi^{ab}\pi_{ab}-\frac12\pi^2\right) -\frac{\sqrt q}{2\kappa}\,{}^{(3)}R. \tag{3.5}
\end{align}$$

The momentum trace is $\pi=q_{ab}\pi^{ab}$. Lapse and shift are multipliers for scalar and spatial constraints, rather than freely evolving configuration variables in this reduced action.

The canonical sign convention will be

$$\begin{align}
\Theta_{\mathrm{ADM}}=\int_\Sigma\pi^{ab}\,\delta q_{ab},\qquad \omega_{\mathrm{can}}=-\delta\Theta_{\mathrm{ADM}} =\int_\Sigma\delta q_{ab}\wedge\delta\pi^{ab},\qquad \iota_{X_H}\omega_{\mathrm{can}}=\delta H. \tag{3.6}
\end{align}$$

Here $\delta$ is the exterior derivative on field space, distinct from the spacetime derivative $d$. For one pair, $\dot q=\partial H/\partial p$, $\dot p=-\partial H/\partial q$ and $\{q,p\}=1$, with $\dot F=\{F,H\}$. A covariant-phase-space convention using $\Omega_{\mathrm{CPS}}=\delta\Theta$ instead has $\Omega_{\mathrm{CPS}}=-\omega_{\mathrm{can}}$ and $\iota_{X_H}\Omega_{\mathrm{CPS}}=-\delta H$. No equation of motion changes when that dictionary is respected.

## 4. From Triads to a Real Barbero Connection

Choose time gauge: the internal timelike tetrad direction is the future unit normal to the slice, and the spatial pullback of $e^0$ vanishes. This fixes local boosts and leaves spatial rotations; with a chosen spin lift their compact group is $SU(2)$. It is not a reduction of a generic $GL$ connection by notation alone.

Take an oriented spatial coframe $e_a{}^i$ with positive determinant. Define

$$\begin{align}
q_{ab}=e_a{}^ie_b{}^j\delta_{ij},\qquad E^a{}_i=\sqrt q\,e^a{}_i =\frac12\epsilon^{abc}\epsilon_{ijk}e_b{}^je_c{}^k, \qquad K_a{}^i=K_{ab}e^{bi}. \tag{4.1}
\end{align}$$

Then $E^a{}_iE^b{}_i=q q^{ab}$ and $\det E=q$. The geometric triad density $E$ is not rescaled by $1/(\kappa\gamma)$; that factor will occur in its conjugate momentum.

Passing from the metric to a triad adds three internal rotation directions. On the rotation-constraint surface $K_{ab}$ is symmetric, and direct variation gives

$$\begin{align}
\pi^{ab}\delta q_{ab} =-\frac1\kappa K_a{}^i\delta E^a{}_i =\frac1\kappa E^a{}_i\delta K_a{}^i -\frac1\kappa\delta(E^a{}_iK_a{}^i). \tag{4.2}
\end{align}$$

Thus $\Theta_{KE}=\kappa^{-1}\int E^a{}_i\delta K_a{}^i$ differs from the ADM potential by an exact field-space variation on the Gauss surface. The extended triad phase space carries the rotation constraint before quotienting those added directions.

Let $\Gamma_a{}^i[E]$ be the torsion-free spatial triad connection:

$$\begin{align}
de^i+\epsilon^i{}_{jk}\Gamma^j\wedge e^k=0. \tag{4.3}
\end{align}$$

It is the connection of the reduced spatial metric, not an arbitrary torsionful piece renamed $\Gamma$. For constant real $\gamma\ne0$, define

$$\begin{align}
A_a{}^i=\Gamma_a{}^i[E]+\gamma K_a{}^i. \tag{4.4}
\end{align}$$

Why is this canonical? Put $\Sigma_i=\tfrac12\epsilon_{ijk}e^j\wedge e^k$. Varying (4.3), contracting with $e_i$ and using the original torsion-free equation gives

$$\begin{align}
\Sigma_i\wedge\delta\Gamma^i =\frac12d(e_i\wedge\delta e^i). \tag{4.5}
\end{align}$$

Its integral vanishes on the closed slice. Consequently

$$\begin{align}
\Theta_{AE}=\frac1{\kappa\gamma}\int E^a{}_i\delta A_a{}^i =\Theta_{KE},\qquad \omega_{AE}=\frac1{\kappa\gamma}\int\delta A_a{}^i\wedge\delta E^a{}_i, \tag{4.6}
\end{align}$$

up to the exact ADM potential shift already displayed. The brackets are

$$\begin{align}
\{A_a{}^i(x),E^b{}_j(y)\}=\kappa\gamma\, \delta_a^b\delta^i_j\delta^{(3)}(x-y). \tag{4.7}
\end{align}$$

The momentum conjugate to $A$ is $E/(\kappa\gamma)$. With a spatial boundary, the boundary expression (4.5) must instead be retained or canceled by specified boundary data; the canonical argument is not automatic there.

This derivation uses the reduced gravitational action, not a claim that every unreduced Einstein–Cartan–fermion connection has bracket (4.7). Keeping torsion and fermion constraints before reduction requires their Dirac brackets and can require a shifted connection. Likewise, the classical canonicity of different real $\gamma$ choices does not prove unitary equivalence of all their quantum holonomy representations. Complex self-dual variables require a separate reality analysis and are not the real compact system considered here.

## 5. Constraints and One Finite Gauss-Algebra Calculation

Use $\tau_i=-i\sigma_i/2$, so $[\tau_i,\tau_j]=\epsilon_{ij}{}^k\tau_k$. For $A=A^i\tau_i$,

$$\begin{align}
F=dA+A\wedge A,\qquad F^i{}_{ab}=\partial_aA_b{}^i-\partial_bA_a{}^i +\epsilon^i{}_{jk}A_a{}^jA_b{}^k. \tag{5.1}
\end{align}$$

In vacuum a convenient constraint set is

$$\begin{align}
G[\lambda]=\frac1{\kappa\gamma}\int d^3x\,\lambda^iG_i, \qquad G_i=D_aE^a{}_i, \tag{5.2}
\end{align}$$

$$\begin{align}
D[\vec N]=\frac1{\kappa\gamma}\int d^3x\,N^a \left(F^i{}_{ab}E^b{}_i-A_a{}^iG_i\right), \tag{5.3}
\end{align}$$

$$\begin{align}
H[N]=\frac1{2\kappa}\int d^3x\, \frac{N E^a{}_iE^b{}_j}{\sqrt q} \left[\epsilon^{ij}{}_kF^k{}_{ab} -2(1+\gamma^2)K^i{}_{[a}K^j{}_{b]}\right]. \tag{5.4}
\end{align}$$

Here $D_aE^a{}_i=\partial_aE^a{}_i+\epsilon_{ij}{}^kA_a{}^jE^a{}_k$. Since $D_\Gamma E=0$, the Gauss equation is $G_i=\gamma\epsilon_{ij}{}^kK_a{}^jE^a{}_k=0$, equivalent to symmetry of $K_{ab}$ for a nondegenerate triad. Antisymmetrization includes the factor $1/2$. Equivalent constraint sets can differ by Gauss terms and multiplier redefinitions. In particular, the subtraction in (5.3) makes $D$ generate the ordinary spatial Lie derivative rather than only a gauge-covariant displacement. The curvature term in (5.4) does not set $F(A)=0$.

The coefficient $1+\gamma^2$ follows from the canonical transformation, rather than being an independent interaction coupling. For (4.3), the spatial frame-curvature convention is $R^{ij}=-\epsilon^{ij}{}_kF^k(\Gamma)$, hence

$$\begin{align}
\frac{E^a{}_iE^b{}_j}{\sqrt q}\epsilon^{ij}{}_kF^k{}_{ab}(\Gamma) =-\sqrt q\,{}^{(3)}R. \tag{5.5}
\end{align}$$

On the Gauss surface, the corresponding contraction of $D_\Gamma K$ vanishes because $K_{ab}$ is symmetric. The $\gamma^2 K\wedge K$ part of $F(A)$ combines with the explicit correction in (5.4), leaving $\sqrt q(K_{ab}K^{ab}-K^2)$ together with (5.5), exactly as in (3.5).

The Gauss generator gives

$$\begin{align}
\{A_a,G[\lambda]\}=-D_a\lambda,\qquad \{E^a,G[\lambda]\}=[\lambda,E^a]. \tag{5.6}
\end{align}$$

Matter adds its internal rotation moment map to $G$, and its energy/momentum to the other constraints. In a completed minimal-fermion reduction, (2.4) is among those scalar terms. We have not derived the entire fermionic canonical reduction or a quantum constraint operator for it.

### A Finite Closure Calculation, Not the Hypersurface-Deformation Algebra

For four legs, take commuting canonical coordinates $q_{A\alpha},p_{A\alpha}$, $A=1,\ldots,4$, $\alpha=1,2$, and $z_A=(q_A+ip_A)/\sqrt2$. Define

$$\begin{align}
J_A^i=\frac12z_A^\dagger\sigma_i z_A,\qquad C_i=\sum_AJ_A^i. \tag{5.7}
\end{align}$$

The canonical brackets give

$$\begin{align}
\{J_A^i,J_B^j\}=\delta_{AB}\epsilon^{ij}{}_kJ_A^k, \qquad\{C_i,C_j\}=\epsilon_{ij}{}^kC_k, \qquad\{J_A^2,C_i\}=0. \tag{5.8}
\end{align}$$

This is a finite classical model of the rotation moment map and closure condition. These commuting variables are not physical Grassmann fermions. The calculation checks internal Gauss algebra only. It is not a proof of the continuum hypersurface-deformation algebra, whose lapse brackets involve spatial derivatives and the metric-dependent structure function $q^{ab}$. Quantizing (5.8) does not solve (5.4).

## 6. Holonomies, Fluxes and the Kinematical Graph Space

Fix an oriented graph $\mathcal G$ embedded in the slice. For the fundamental connection $D=d+A$, define the actual parallel transporter by

$$\begin{align}
h_e[A]=\mathcal P\exp\left(-\int_e A\right),\qquad \frac{d}{ds}h_e(s)=-\dot e^aA_a(e(s))h_e(s),\quad h_e(0)=I. \tag{6.1}
\end{align}$$

This minus sign is a holonomy convention. Under $A\mapsto gAg^{-1}-(dg)g^{-1}$,

$$\begin{align}
h_e\longmapsto g(t_e)h_e g(s_e)^{-1}.
\tag{6.2}
\end{align}$$

Changing to a plus-exponent/right-transport convention changes insertion and endpoint formulas and must be translated, not combined silently with (6.1).

For a surface with oriented covector density $n_a$, the flux is $E_i(S)=\int_S n_aE^a{}_i\,d^2u$. At a single transverse interior intersection $p$ of $e$ and $S$, variation of (6.1) inserts a minus sign, while $\{E,A\}=-\kappa\gamma$. Thus

$$\begin{align}
\{E_i(S),h_e\}=\kappa\gamma\,\sigma(e,S)
\,h_{e_2}\tau_i h_{e_1}, \tag{6.3}
\end{align}$$

where $e=e_2\circ e_1$ and $\sigma=\pm1$ is the oriented intersection number. Endpoint, tangential and multiple-intersection cases need their own regulator conventions.

For a finite graph with $L$ edges, choose

$$\begin{align}
\mathcal H_{\mathcal G}=L^2(SU(2)^L,\prod_e dh_e), \tag{6.4}
\end{align}$$

with normalized Haar measure. Holonomies act by multiplication. For the flux, use a subdivided representative with $h_e=h_2h_1$ and choose $X_if(h_2,h_1)=\left.\frac{d}{ds}f(h_2e^{s\tau_i},h_1)\right|_{s=0}$, so $X_i(h_2h_1)=h_2\tau_i h_1$. This may require a graph refinement. On the corresponding smooth cylindrical domain, the regulated flux insertion is $i\hbar\kappa\gamma\,\sigma X_i$. The invariant derivative is anti-symmetric for Haar measure, so $iX_i$ has the Hermitian sign. Domains and self-adjoint closures remain operator questions; a formal differential expression is not their full analysis.

At the vertices, Haar averaging gives the compact Gauss projector

$$\begin{align}
(P_Gf)(h)=\int\prod_vdg_v\, f\!\left(\{g(t_e)h_eg(s_e)^{-1}\}\right). \tag{6.5}
\end{align}$$

Haar invariance proves $P_G^2=P_G=P_G^\dagger$. It imposes internal rotations, not the spatial-diffeomorphism or Hamiltonian constraints. Peter–Weyl theory decomposes each edge into matrix elements $D^j_{mn}(h_e)$; the vertex average retains invariant tensors, called intertwiners. Their contraction defines spin-network functions.

Subdividing an edge uses $h_e=h_{e_2}h_{e_1}$. Haar measure makes the map $f(h_e)\mapsto f(h_{e_2}h_{e_1})$ isometric. Building a continuum kinematical completion additionally specifies a graph category and a consistent family of such embeddings. The explicit calculation next needs only a fixed graph.

## 7. An Actual Invariant Graph State and a Finite Projector

### 7.1 Four Spin-One-Half Legs

In $V_{1/2}^{\otimes4}$, define the pair states

$$\begin{align}
|s\rangle=\frac{|\uparrow\downarrow\rangle-|\downarrow\uparrow\rangle}{\sqrt2},\quad |t_+\rangle=|\uparrow\uparrow\rangle,\quad |t_0\rangle=\frac{|\uparrow\downarrow\rangle+|\downarrow\uparrow\rangle}{\sqrt2},\quad |t_-\rangle=|\downarrow\downarrow\rangle.
\end{align}$$

Two orthonormal invariant tensors are

$$
\begin{aligned}
|I_0\rangle&=|s\rangle_{12}|s\rangle_{34},\\
|I_1\rangle&=\frac1{\sqrt3}\left(
|t_+\rangle_{12}|t_-\rangle_{34}
-|t_0\rangle_{12}|t_0\rangle_{34}
+|t_-\rangle_{12}|t_+\rangle_{34}\right).
\end{aligned}
\tag{7.1}
$$

Each is killed by $\mathbf J_{\mathrm{tot}}=\tfrac12\sum_{A=1}^4\boldsymbol\sigma^{(A)}$. They span the whole invariant space because

$$\begin{align}
V_{1/2}^{\otimes4}=2V_0\oplus3V_1\oplus V_2. \tag{7.2}
\end{align}$$

This gives an explicit Haar projector without a symbolic unevaluated group integral. If $C=\mathbf J_{\mathrm{tot}}^2$, its only eigenvalues are $0,2,6$, and

$$\begin{align}
P_0=\frac{(C-2I)(C-6I)}{12} =|I_0\rangle\langle I_0|+|I_1\rangle\langle I_1|. \tag{7.3}
\end{align}$$

The finite matrix has rank two, is Hermitian and idempotent, and obeys $\mathbf J_{\mathrm{tot}}P_0=0$. It is a projector onto this vertex's Gauss invariants, not onto solutions of the gravitational scalar constraint.

### 7.2 A Two-Node, Four-Edge Graph

Take two vertices $v,w$ joined by four edges directed from $v$ to $w$, all labelled $j=1/2$. With $A,B\in\{0,1\}$ define

$$\begin{align}
\Psi_{AB}(h_1,h_2,h_3,h_4) =4\langle I_A|\left(h_1\otimes h_2\otimes h_3\otimes h_4\right)|I_B\rangle. \tag{7.4}
\end{align}$$

The left $g(w)$ factors act trivially on the invariant bra and the right $g(v)^{-1}$ factors on the invariant ket, proving Gauss invariance at both vertices. Schur orthogonality, $\int dh\,\overline{D^{1/2}_{mn}(h)}D^{1/2}_{pq}(h)=\delta_{mp}\delta_{nq}/2$, shows that the prefactor $(\sqrt2)^4=4$ makes these four functions orthonormal. Their inner product is kinematical Haar inner product, not a physical transition amplitude.

For a concrete nontrivial evaluation, set

$$\begin{align}
h_1=e^{\theta\tau_3},\quad h_3=e^{\varphi\tau_3},\quad h_2=h_4=I, \qquad c_\theta=\cos(\theta/2),\quad s_\theta=\sin(\theta/2).
\end{align}$$

In the ordered intertwiner basis $(I_0,I_1)$, the four functions become

$$
\big[\Psi_{AB}\big]
=4\begin{pmatrix}
c_\theta c_\varphi&s_\theta s_\varphi/\sqrt3\\
s_\theta s_\varphi/\sqrt3&c_\theta c_\varphi+\frac23s_\theta s_\varphi
\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{7.5}
$$

At $\theta=\varphi=\pi$ this is $4\begin{pmatrix}0&1/\sqrt3\\1/\sqrt3&2/3\end{pmatrix}$, so different intertwiner labels are genuinely mixed by this holonomy configuration. A value of a normalized wavefunction at one configuration is not a probability or a physical evolution matrix.

A fermion spin leg can likewise participate in a spatial-rotation intertwiner, but it requires a graded matter factor and its own Gauss charge. This $SU(2)$ is the spatial frame-spin group, not the Standard Model weak group. The finite invariant tensor calculation does not construct a unique joint gravity–matter quantum state.

### 7.3 A Restricted Area Statement

Classically, the area density is $\sqrt{n_an_bE^a{}_iE^b{}_i}$. In the holonomy–flux representation above, choose a surface intersecting edges only at simple transverse interior punctures, with no genuine original-graph vertex or tangential intersections. An auxiliary bivalent subdivision used for insertion does not count as an additional puncture. Under the standard puncture/Casimir regularization,

$$\begin{align}
\widehat{\operatorname{Area}}(S) =\hbar\kappa|\gamma|\sum_{p}\sqrt{j_p(j_p+1)} =8\pi|\gamma|\ell_P^2\sum_p\sqrt{j_p(j_p+1)}, \qquad \ell_P^2=G\hbar. \tag{7.6}
\end{align}$$

For one fundamental edge, $-\sum_i\tau_i\tau_i=3I/4$, so one such puncture contributes $\hbar\kappa|\gamma|\sqrt3/2$. The factors of $i$ in the flux representation give a positive squared Casimir; anti-Hermitian generators are not treated as positive observables themselves.

This is a kinematical spectrum in a specified representation and intersection class. It is not a universal continuum measurement prediction or an automatically gauge-invariant observable under all gravitational constraints. A physical area needs a specified surface, often relationally defined, and control of the physical states/dynamics. No value of $\gamma$ is fixed by this one calculation.

## 8. Covariant Amplitudes: Why BF Is Not Already Four-Dimensional Gravity

In a covariant first-order treatment, integrate over tetrads and Lorentz connections only after choosing boundary data, gauge fixing, a measure and a regulator. The minimal action, the Holst action and nonminimal matter actions are different inputs. An algebraic classical elimination does not select all their quantum measures or physical boundary amplitudes.

A useful comparison is Lorentz-algebra BF theory,

$$\begin{align}
I_{\mathrm{BF}}[B,\omega]=\frac1{2\kappa}\int B_{IJ}\wedge R^{IJ}(\omega). \tag{8.1}
\end{align}$$

If $B$ is unrestricted, its equation is $R^{IJ}=0$ and the connection equation is $D_\omega B=0$. This is not vacuum four-dimensional Einstein gravity, which allows nonzero Weyl curvature. The gravitational nondegenerate branch restricts

$$\begin{align}
B_{IJ}=\frac12\epsilon_{IJKL}e^K\wedge e^L =(\star\Sigma)_{IJ}, \tag{8.2}
\end{align}$$

or $B=(\star+\eta_H)\Sigma$ for (1.7). Implementing such simplicity conditions, choosing the sector and handling degenerate configurations are substantive parts of a gravity model, not a relabelling of unconstrained BF variables.

A spin-foam amplitude further specifies representation data, face/edge/vertex weights, simplicity implementation, gauge volumes, boundary maps and a treatment of sums/refinement. Lorentzian bulk groups are noncompact; a Euclidean $\operatorname{Spin}(4)$ model is a different signature choice, not an unstated replacement for it. Shared $SU(2)$ boundary labels or recoupling identities alone do not prove equality with a canonical Hamiltonian projector, finiteness on arbitrary complexes, anomaly freedom or a unique continuum theory.

### A Finite Compact-Group Kernel with an Exact Composition Law

To see a real composition calculation without claiming it is gravity, use normalized Haar measure on $SU(2)$. Define a representation-cutoff kernel

$$\begin{align}
\delta_J(h)=\sum_{j=0,1/2,\ldots,J}(2j+1)\chi_j(h),\qquad K_J(g,h)=\delta_J(gh^{-1}). \tag{8.3}
\end{align}$$

This is a finite BF-type face regulator, not a four-dimensional gravitational amplitude. At $J=1/2$, write $g=g_0I+i\mathbf g\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma$ and $h=h_0I+i\mathbf h\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma$, with unit four-vectors. Then

$$\begin{align}
K_{1/2}(g,h)=1+4\sum_{\alpha=0}^3g_\alpha h_\alpha. \tag{8.4}
\end{align}$$

Haar measure is uniform on $S^3$, so $\int k_\alpha\,dk=0$ and $\int k_\alpha k_\beta\,dk=\delta_{\alpha\beta}/4$. Consequently

$$
\begin{aligned}
\int dk\,K_{1/2}(g,k)K_{1/2}(k,h)
&=1+16g_\alpha h_\beta\frac{\delta_{\alpha\beta}}4\\
&=K_{1/2}(g,h).
\end{aligned}
\tag{8.5}
$$

The integral of the kernel over one argument is one, and its diagonal value is five. As an integral operator on $L^2(SU(2))$, it projects onto the five matrix-element modes with $j=0$ or $1/2$: $1^2+2^2=5$. It is not the rank-two vertex projector (7.3). Nor is a finite $J$ kernel a delta distribution imposing exact flatness; its pointwise values can even be negative. The exact convolution law is a spectral-cutoff property, not evidence that an arbitrary regulated gravity amplitude solves all its constraints.

This calculation illustrates the distinction between a controlled finite kernel, a distributional BF face condition and the additional simplicity/dynamical work required for gravity. The identities of group recoupling theory do not by themselves supply that missing work.

## 9. Kinematics, Constraints and the Remaining Physical Problem

The graph Hilbert space and Gauss projector constructed above are genuine kinematical objects. They do not yet solve spatial diffeomorphisms, the scalar constraint or the construction of a positive physical inner product. A proposed Hamiltonian operator must specify domains, regulator, ordering, graph changes and the treatment of inverse-volume and contact-current products. Its constraint commutators must be compared with the appropriate classical algebra; one finite Gauss closure or minisuperspace computation is not that comparison.

Similarly, a covariant amplitude is not a physical projector merely because it uses the same spin labels as a boundary graph or satisfies a compact-group convolution identity. A chosen model needs independent dynamical, composition and continuum/refinement analysis. Algebraic torsion in minimal EC leaves an interaction to quantize, not an automatically solved new propagating sector. Added Holst/nonminimal terms require their own reduction and matter coefficients.

The bounded result is therefore precise: a minimal classical torsion prescription, a normalized real gravitational canonical transformation under explicit boundary/time-gauge assumptions, actual Gauss-invariant graph functions and finite projector/kernel calculations. Full gravitational dynamics and a complete gravity–matter quantum theory remain additional tasks.
